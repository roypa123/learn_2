## 1. What is this page?

In [Lesson 006](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md) you learned: English has **26 letters** but about **44 sounds**. Dictionaries show these sounds with **sound symbols** between slashes, like **/k/**.

This page is a **map** of those 44 sounds. **You do not need to learn it all today.** Come back to it many times. Use it like a dictionary for sounds.

The symbols here are the common British dictionary style. American dictionaries are very similar; differences are noted.

### How to read a symbol

- **/ /** — slashes mean "this is a sound, not a letter".
- **ː** — two little dots mean the sound is **long**: /iː/ is longer than /ɪ/.
- **ˈ** — a small mark **before** a part of a word means that part is **strong** (stressed): *apple* /ˈæpl/. You will learn about stress later.

## 2. Vowel sounds (20)

Remember: vowel sounds are made with an **open mouth**; the air flows freely ([Lesson 005](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).

### A. Short vowels (7)

| Symbol | Example words | Mouth tip |
|---|---|---|
| /ɪ/ | s**i**t, b**i**g, f**i**sh | short and relaxed; lips not stretched |
| /e/ | b**e**d, r**e**d, t**e**n | mouth a little open |
| /æ/ | c**a**t, b**a**g, h**a**t | mouth open wide, jaw down |
| /ʌ/ | c**u**p, s**u**n, m**o**ther | short "uh", mouth relaxed, middle |
| /ɒ/ | h**o**t, d**o**g, st**o**p | lips a little round (UK). **US:** often /ɑː/ |
| /ʊ/ | b**oo**k, p**u**t, g**oo**d | lips a little round, short |
| /ə/ | **a**bout, b**a**nan**a** (1st and 3rd a), doct**or** | the most common English sound! Very short, very weak. It is called **schwa**. |

### B. Long vowels (5)

| Symbol | Example words | Mouth tip |
|---|---|---|
| /iː/ | s**ee**, **ea**t, gr**ee**n | smile; long "ee" |
| /ɑː/ | c**ar**, f**a**ther, st**ar**t | mouth open, long "ah" |
| /ɔː/ | d**oor**, w**al**k, b**al**l | lips round, long "aw" |
| /uː/ | f**oo**d, bl**ue**, y**ou** | lips round and pushed forward, long "oo" |
| /ɜː/ | b**ir**d, w**or**k, l**ear**n | lips relaxed, long "er" |

> In American English, the **r** in words like *car, door, bird* is usually said. In British English, it is usually not said when no vowel follows.

### C. Two-part vowels — "gliding" sounds (8)

Your mouth **moves** from one position to another during these sounds.

| Symbol | Example words | Like saying… |
|---|---|---|
| /eɪ/ | d**ay**, c**a**ke, n**a**me | "e" → "ee" |
| /aɪ/ | m**y**, **I**, t**i**me | "a" → "ee" |
| /ɔɪ/ | b**oy**, t**oy**, n**oi**se | "aw" → "ee" |
| /aʊ/ | n**ow**, h**ou**se, **ou**t | "a" → "oo" |
| /əʊ/ | g**o**, h**o**me, n**o** | "uh" → "oo" (UK). **US:** /oʊ/ "o" → "oo" |
| /ɪə/ | **ear**, h**ere**, n**ear** | "i" → "uh" (mainly UK) |
| /eə/ | h**air**, wh**ere**, ch**air** | "e" → "uh" (mainly UK) |
| /ʊə/ | t**our**, p**ure** | "oo" → "uh" (rare; many people say /ɔː/) |

## 3. Consonant sounds (24)

Consonants are made when the **lips, tongue or teeth** block the air a little ([Lesson 005](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).

**Voiced or voiceless?** Put your fingers on your throat. 🤚
- **Voiceless:** no shaking → /p/, /t/, /k/, /f/, /s/…
- **Voiced:** your throat **shakes** (vibrates) → /b/, /d/, /g/, /v/, /z/…

Many consonants come in **pairs**: same mouth position, one voiceless, one voiced.

| Voiceless | Voiced | Mouth position | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| /p/ | /b/ | both lips close, then open | **p**en / **b**ed |
| /t/ | /d/ | tongue tip behind top teeth | **t**en / **d**og |
| /k/ | /g/ | back of tongue up | **c**at, **k**ey / **g**o |
| /f/ | /v/ | top teeth on bottom lip | **f**ish, **ph**one / **v**an |
| /θ/ | /ð/ | tongue tip **between** the teeth | **th**ink / **th**is |
| /s/ | /z/ | tongue near top teeth, air hisses | **s**un, **c**ity / **z**oo, i**s** |
| /ʃ/ | /ʒ/ | lips round, "sh" | **sh**e, **sh**op / televi**s**ion |
| /tʃ/ | /dʒ/ | "t" + "sh" / "d" + "zh" | **ch**air / **j**uice, **g**ym |

Other consonants:

| Symbol | Examples | Mouth tip |
|---|---|---|
| /h/ | **h**ouse, **h**ello | just air from the throat |
| /m/ | **m**oon | lips closed; sound through the nose |
| /n/ | **n**ose | tongue behind top teeth; sound through the nose |
| /ŋ/ | si**ng**, lo**ng**, thi**n**k | back of tongue up; sound through the nose. **Don't add a "g" sound** at the end of *sing*. |
| /l/ | **l**ion, **l**eg | tongue tip touches behind top teeth |
| /r/ | **r**ed, **r**ain | tongue tip goes up but does **not** touch; lips a little round |
| /w/ | **w**ater, **w**e | lips round and pushed forward, like "oo" |
| /j/ | **y**es, **y**ellow, **y**ou | like a quick "ee". ⚠️ The symbol **/j/** = the **y** sound, not the letter j! |

**Count:** 20 vowel sounds + 24 consonant sounds = **44 sounds**.

## 4. Sounds that many learners find difficult

Pairs of words that differ by **only one sound** are called **minimal pairs**. They are great for practice.

| Sounds | Minimal pairs | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| /ɪ/ vs /iː/ | sh**i**p / sh**ee**p · s**i**t / s**ea**t · l**i**ve / l**ea**ve | /iː/ is long, with a smile |
| /æ/ vs /e/ | m**a**n / m**e**n · b**a**d / b**e**d | /æ/ mouth opens wider |
| /æ/ vs /ʌ/ | c**a**t / c**u**t · h**a**t / h**u**t | /ʌ/ mouth more relaxed |
| /l/ vs /r/ | **l**ight / **r**ight · **l**ice / **r**ice | /l/ touches; /r/ does not touch |
| /v/ vs /w/ | **v**est / **w**est · **v**ine / **w**ine | /v/ teeth on lip; /w/ round lips, no teeth |
| /b/ vs /v/ | **b**oat / **v**ote · **b**an / **v**an | /b/ two lips; /v/ teeth on lip |
| /θ/ vs /s/ | **th**ink / **s**ink · **th**ick / **s**ick | tongue **between** the teeth for /θ/ |
| /θ/ vs /t/ | **th**ree / **t**ree · **th**in / **t**in | for /θ/ air keeps flowing; /t/ stops it |
| /ð/ vs /d/ | **th**ey / **d**ay · **th**en / **d**en | tongue between the teeth for /ð/ |

> 💡 Practise one pair per day. Say each word 5 times. Then ask someone (or a voice tool) to say one of the two words — can you hear which one it is?

## 5. The "th" sounds — a closer look

Many languages do not have **th**. Here is how to make it:

1. Put the **tip of your tongue** lightly **between your top and bottom teeth**. 👅
2. Blow air out gently.
3. **No shaking** in the throat → /θ/ as in **th**ink, **th**ank you, **th**ree.
4. **With shaking** in the throat → /ð/ as in **th**is, **th**e, mo**th**er.

It may feel strange. That is normal. Practise with a mirror.

## 6. Practice

**A. Voiced or voiceless? Put your hand on your throat.**

1. /s/ 2. /z/ 3. /p/ 4. /b/ 5. /f/ 6. /v/

**B. Which word has a different vowel sound?**

1. see / eat / sit / green
2. cat / bag / hat / cup
3. go / home / hot / no

**C. Say the minimal pairs. Then write a sentence using both words.**

Example: *ship / sheep* → *There is a sheep on the ship.* 🐑🚢

1. light / right
2. three / tree

## 7. Answers

**A.** 1. voiceless · 2. voiced · 3. voiceless · 4. voiced · 5. voiceless · 6. voiced

**B.**
1. **sit** /ɪ/ — the others have long /iː/.
2. **cup** /ʌ/ — the others have /æ/.
3. **hot** /ɒ/ — the others have /əʊ/.

**C.** Example answers:
1. *Turn **right** at the **light**.* (the traffic light)
2. *There are **three** birds in the **tree**.*
