# Dialogue 001 — Meeting Someone New

**ID:** D-001 · **Level:** 0 · **Use after:** [Lesson 015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)
**Grammar used (all taught already):** subjects ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)), verbs ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)), objects ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)), word order ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)), questions and short answers ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).

---

## 1. The situation

It is the **first day** of an evening English class. **Lucas** (from Brazil) sits down next to **Mei** (from China). They don't know each other. They start talking before the teacher arrives.

## 2. The dialogue

> **Lucas:** Hi! Is this seat free?
> **Mei:** Yes, it is. Please sit down.
> **Lucas:** Thanks. I'm Lucas.
> **Mei:** Nice to meet you, Lucas. I'm Mei.
> **Lucas:** Nice to meet you too. Sorry — how do you spell your name?
> **Mei:** M-E-I.
> **Lucas:** Where are you from, Mei?
> **Mei:** I'm from China. And you?
> **Lucas:** I'm from Brazil. I live here now. I work in a hotel.
> **Mei:** Oh, nice! Do you like your job?
> **Lucas:** Yes, I do. But it's very busy. What do you do?
> **Mei:** I'm a student. I study computer science.
> **Lucas:** Wow! Do you speak Portuguese?
> **Mei:** No, I don't. I speak Chinese and a little English.
> **Lucas:** Me too — a little English! That's why we're here. 😄
> **Mei:** Ha ha, yes! Oh — the teacher is here.
> **Lucas:** Great. See you after class!
> **Mei:** See you!

---

## 3. New words and expressions

| Word / phrase | Meaning in simple English |
|---|---|
| **seat** | a place to sit, a chair |
| **free** | here: nobody is using it (it can also mean "costs no money") |
| **Thanks.** | a short, relaxed *Thank you.* |
| **Nice to meet you.** | a polite thing to say when you meet a new person |
| **Nice to meet you too.** | the answer: "the same for me" |
| **Where are you from?** | which country or city do you come from? |
| **And you?** | a quick way to ask the same question back |
| **job** | the work you do to get money |
| **busy** | having a lot to do; no free time |
| **What do you do?** | ⚠️ means **"What is your job?"** — not "what are you doing now?" |
| **computer science** | the study of computers and programs |
| **Wow!** | a word that shows surprise 😮 |
| **That's why we're here.** | "That is the reason we are in this class." |
| **See you (after class)!** | goodbye (we will meet again later) |

## 5. Sentences analysed in depth

### D-001 line 1 — "Hi! Is this seat free?"

- **Hi!** — a relaxed greeting ([Lesson 001](../../00-foundations/01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). The **!** shows friendly energy.
- **Is this seat free?**
  - Statement: *This seat **is** free.* → question: ***Is** this seat free?* (Way 1: *be* moves.)
  - Subject: **this seat** (*this* = the one near me; full lesson: [Lesson 019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
  - **free** — describing word (*It is free* ✅).
- **Why say this?** It is polite to ask before you sit next to someone in a class, a bus, or a café.
- **Other ways:** *Is anyone sitting here?* · *Can I sit here?*

### D-001 line 5 — "Sorry — how do you spell your name?"

- **Sorry** — here it is not an apology for a big problem. It is a polite way to **interrupt** or ask for help ("excuse me").
- **how do you spell…** — Way 3 question: *How* + **do** + *you* + *spell* ([Lesson 015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **your name** — the **object** of *spell* (*spell what?*) ([Lesson 013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- **M-E-I** — letter names: "em – ee – eye" ([Lesson 003](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)). Careful: **E** = "ee", **I** = "eye"!

### D-001 line 9 — "I'm from Brazil. I live here now. I work in a hotel."

- Three **short** sentences — each with its own subject (**I**) and verb (**'m**, **live**, **work**) ([Lesson 010](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
- **I live here now.** — S – V – Place – Time (*here* = place, *now* = time) ([Lesson 014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)).
- **I work in a hotel.** — no object (*work what?* ✗). *In a hotel* = place.
- **Short sentences are good** in speaking. They are clear and easy to follow.

### D-001 line 11 — "Yes, I do. But it's very busy. What do you do?"

- **Yes, I do.** — short answer to *Do you like your job?* We repeat the helper **do**.
- **But** — a joining word that shows **contrast** (a different idea): *I like it* — **but** — *it's busy*. (Full lesson: [Lesson 054](../../01-beginner-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-054-and-but-or.md).) In speaking, starting a sentence with *But* is very common.
- **it's** — *it is*. **it** = the job (not the weather this time!) ([Lesson 011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **What do you do?** — two **do**s!
  - 1st **do** = the **helper** for the question.
  - 2nd **do** = the **main verb** (to do = to act, to work).
  - Meaning: *What is your job?* ⚠️ Not *What are you doing now?*
  - Answer: *I'm a student.* / *I'm a nurse.* / *I work in a bank.*

### D-001 line 14 — "No, I don't. I speak Chinese and a little English."

- **No, I don't.** — short answer (*don't* = *do not*).
- **I speak Chinese and a little English.** — S – V – O. The **object** is two things joined with **and**: *Chinese* + *a little English* ([Lesson 013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- **a little** — not much ([Lesson 002](../../00-foundations/01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)).
- Language names → capital letters ([Lesson 004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).

## 6. How the speakers keep the conversation going

Good conversations are like **ping-pong** 🏓: you answer, then you **send a question back**.

| Speaker answers… | …then asks back |
|---|---|
| *I'm from China.* | ***And you?*** |
| *Yes, I do. But it's very busy.* | ***What do you do?*** |

**Tip:** After you answer a question, ask **the same question** back with *And you?* or ask a **new** question about the other person.

## 7. Common mistakes in this kind of conversation

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Where you are from? | Where **are you** from? | *be* moves before the subject. |
| Where do you are from? | Where are you from? | No *do* with *be*. |
| Nice to meet you. — Me too. | Nice to meet you. — **Nice to meet you too.** / **You too.** | *Me too* sounds a little strange here. |
| What do you do? — I'm sitting. | — I'm a student. | *What do you do?* = your job. |
| Yes, I'm. | Yes, I **am**. | No contraction in positive short answers. |

## 8. Other natural replies

| Line | Other ways to say it |
|---|---|
| *Nice to meet you.* | *Pleased to meet you.* (a bit more formal) · *Good to meet you.* |
| *Where are you from?* | *Where do you come from?* |
| *What do you do?* | *What's your job?* · *What do you do for work?* |
| *See you!* | *Bye!* · *See you later!* · *Talk to you later!* |

## 9. Pronunciation and listening notes 🗣️👂

- *Nice to meet you* → "nice-tuh-MEE-chuh" (the **t** + **y** in *meet you* often join to make a "ch" sound).
- *Where are you from?* → "WHERE-uh-yuh FROM?" ↘ (wh-question, voice down)
- *Is this seat free?* ↗ (yes/no question, voice up)
- *What do you do?* → "WOT-d'yuh DOO?" ↘
- **Stress** the important words: *I **WORK** in a ho**TEL**.*

## 10. Check your understanding

1. Where is Mei from?
2. What is Lucas's job?
3. What does Mei study?
4. Does Mei speak Portuguese?
5. What does *What do you do?* mean?
6. Why are Lucas and Mei in the class?

## 11. Answers

1. **China.** — *I'm from China.*
2. **He works in a hotel.**
3. **Computer science.**
4. **No, she doesn't.** — note the short answer with **she** + **doesn't** ([Lesson 015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
5. **What is your job?**
6. **To learn English** — they both speak "a little English". (*That's why we're here.*)

## 12. Role-play 🎭

**Step 1.** Read the dialogue aloud with a partner (or say both parts yourself). Swap roles.

**Step 2.** Now make **your own** version. Change:
- the names,
- the countries,
- the jobs,
- the languages.

**Step 3.** Use these **useful questions**:
- *What's your name?* · *How do you spell that?*
- *Where are you from?* · *Where do you live?*
- *What do you do?* · *Do you like your job?*
- *Do you speak ___?*

**Step 4.** Remember the **ping-pong** rule: answer, then ask back. 🏓

## 13. Write it ✏️

Write **5 sentences** about the person you "met" in the role-play.
Example: *Her name is Mei. She is from China. She is a student. She studies computer science. She speaks Chinese.*

Check: subject + verb in every sentence ✅ · **-s** for *he/she* (*speaks*; and *study → stud**ies*** — the **y** changes to **ies**, full rule in [Lesson 043](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md)) ✅ · capitals for names, countries, languages ✅.
