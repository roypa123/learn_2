# Story 001 — A New Student

**Grammar reused:** *am/is/are* (L-018), *this/that* (L-019), questions and short answers (L-015), subject–verb–object (L-013, L-014), numbers (L-016), greetings and classroom English (L-017).
**New grammar:** none. A few new words are explained below.

---

## 1. The story

> ### A New Student
>
> **(1)** It is Monday morning. It is eight o'clock. The sun is bright, and the sky is blue.
>
> **(2)** Amira is a new student at Green Park School. She is fourteen years old. She is from Morocco, but she lives in Manchester now. Today is her first day at the new school. She is a little nervous.
>
> **(3)** Amira opens the door of Classroom 12. The room is big. There are many students. They look at her. Amira's face is red.
>
> **(4)** A tall woman smiles at her. "Good morning! Are you Amira?"
> "Yes, I am," says Amira.
> "Welcome! I'm Mrs Clark. I'm your English teacher. Please sit down."
>
> **(5)** Amira sits next to a girl with long black hair. The girl smiles. "Hi! I'm Sofia. Nice to meet you."
> "Nice to meet you too," says Amira. "Sorry — how do you spell your name?"
> "S-O-F-I-A."
>
> **(6)** "Where are you from, Sofia?"
> "I'm from Italy. And you?"
> "I'm from Morocco. I speak Arabic and French. My English isn't very good."
> "That's okay! My English isn't perfect either. We can learn together."
>
> **(7)** Mrs Clark writes a word on the board: *library*.
> Amira puts up her hand. "Excuse me, Mrs Clark. What does 'library' mean?"
> "Good question! A library is a room with many books. You can read books there or take them home. Our library is on the second floor."
>
> **(8)** At eleven o'clock, the bell rings. Sofia points to a door. "That's the café. Do you like hot chocolate?"
> "Yes, I do!" says Amira.
> "Great! Let's go."
>
> **(9)** Amira isn't nervous now. She has a new friend. She smiles. "This is a good school," she thinks.

---

## 2. Simple summary

Amira is 14. She is from Morocco and lives in Manchester. It is her first day at a new school, and she is nervous. Her teacher, Mrs Clark, is kind. Amira meets a girl called Sofia, from Italy. They talk and become friends. At the end, Amira is happy.

## 6. Sentence analysis

### Sentence (1): "It is Monday morning. It is eight o'clock."

- **It** — the empty subject for days and time ([Lesson 011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)). ❌ *Is Monday morning.*
- **eight o'clock** — *o'clock* = "of the clock", for **exact** hours: *8:00 = eight o'clock*. Not for 8:15.

### Sentence (2): "She is from Morocco, but she lives in Manchester now."

- Two parts joined by **, but** — the second idea is different from the first ([Lesson 018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md), EX-018-002).
- Part 1: **be** (*is from*). Part 2: a normal verb (*lives*, with **-s** for *she*).
- **now** — time; at the end (S – V – Place – **Time**) ([Lesson 014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)).

### Sentence (3): "Amira opens the door of Classroom 12."

- Subject: **Amira** · Verb: **opens** · Object: **the door of Classroom 12** (*opens what?*) ([Lesson 013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- **of Classroom 12** — tells *which* door. *Classroom 12* is a name → capital **C**.
- **12** — numbers for rooms are written as numerals and said "twelve" ([Lesson 016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).

### Sentence (4): "Good morning! Are you Amira?" — "Yes, I am."

- **Good morning** — before 12:00 ([Lesson 017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)).
- **Are you Amira?** — *You are Amira* → *Are you…?* (Way 1).
- **Yes, I am.** — short answer. ❌ *Yes, I'm.*

### Sentence (6): "My English isn't very good."

- Subject: **My English** · Verb: **isn't** (*is not*) · Description: **very good**.
- **very** makes *good* stronger. *Not very good* = a bit bad (a polite, soft way to say it).
- Sofia answers: *My English isn't perfect **either***. When we agree with a **negative** idea, we use **either** at the end, not *too*:
  - ✅ *I like tea. — I like tea **too**.* (positive)
  - ✅ *My English isn't good. — My English isn't perfect **either**.* (negative)
  - ❌ *My English isn't perfect too.* — a very common learner mistake.
  - Full lesson: [Lesson 246](../../02-intermediate-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-246-correlative-conjunctions.md) and combination C-033.

### Sentence (7): "Excuse me, Mrs Clark. What does 'library' mean?"

- **Excuse me** — before you interrupt ([Lesson 017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)).
- **What does … mean?** — **does** + base verb **mean** (no -s). ❌ *What means 'library'?*

### Sentence (8): "That's the café. Do you like hot chocolate?"

- **That's** — the café is **not near** (Sofia points) ([Lesson 019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
- **Do you like…?** — Way 3 question with **do** ([Lesson 015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)). Answer: *Yes, I do!*

### Sentence (9): "This is a good school," she thinks.

- **This** — the school she is in **now** (near).
- **she thinks** — after a quotation, we often write *she thinks / she says*. Note the **comma** inside the quotation marks: *"…good school," she thinks.* (Punctuation of speech: [Lesson 256](../../02-intermediate-grammar/punctuation/lesson-256-quotation-marks-and-direct-speech.md).)

## 7. Tense choice

The whole story uses the **present** (*is, opens, smiles*), as if we are watching it **now**, like a film 🎬. Stories are often told in the **past** (*was, opened*). You will learn the past in Level 2 ([Lesson 061](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-061-was-were.md)).

## 9. How the paragraphs connect

1. **Time and weather** — sets the scene.
2. **Who is Amira?** — introduces the main character and her feeling (*nervous*).
3. **The classroom** — the problem: she feels shy.
4. **The teacher** — the first kind person.
5. & 6. **Sofia** — a new friend; they find something in common (both learning English).
7. **The lesson** — Amira is brave enough to ask a question.
8. **Break time** — friendship grows.
9. **The end** — the feeling changes: *nervous → happy*.

## 12. Speaking practice 🗣️

1. Read the dialogue in paragraphs 4–6 aloud with a partner. One person is Amira; the other is Mrs Clark and Sofia.
2. **Role-play:** You are a new student. Introduce yourself to the class: name, age, country, languages.
3. **Talk about it:** *How do you feel on a first day? Nervous? Excited?* Use: *I'm…* / *I'm not…*

## 13. Writing practice ✏️

Write **5–6 sentences** about Sofia. Use the story.
Example start: *Sofia is a student at Green Park School. She is from…*

Then write **5 sentences** about **your** first day at a school or a job.

## 14. Sentence transformations

Change each sentence as shown.

1. *Amira is nervous.* → negative
2. *Sofia is from Italy.* → question
3. *The room is big.* → question + short answer (yes)
4. *She is a new student.* → change *She* to *They*
5. *That is the café.* → make it plural (*cafés*)

**Answers:**
1. *Amira **isn't** nervous.*
2. ***Is Sofia** from Italy?*
3. ***Is the room** big? — Yes, it is.*
4. ***They are** new student**s**.* (plural → *are*, *students*; no *a*)
5. ***Those are** the cafés.*
