# Lesson 016 — Numbers 0–100

## 1. What you will learn

- The numbers **0 to 100**, how to **say** them and how to **write** them.
- The difference between **-teen** (13, 14…) and **-ty** (30, 40…) — a very common listening problem.
- Spelling rules: the **hyphen** in *twenty-one*.
- Using numbers in sentences: **age**, **phone numbers**, **prices**, **How many…?**
- **Singular and plural** after numbers: *one book*, *two books*.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- Age: **I am ___ years old.** — not *I have ___ years* ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
- **-s** on a thing word means **more than one**: *books, friends* ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- Wh-questions: **question word + helper + subject…?** ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).

## 3. Numbers 0–20

These must be learned by heart. 11 and 12 are special words.

| Number | Word | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | zero | "ZEER-oh" |
| 1 | one | "wun" (⚠️ not "own") |
| 2 | two | "too" |
| 3 | three | "three" (tongue between teeth: /θ/) |
| 4 | four | "for" |
| 5 | five | "five" |
| 6 | six | "siks" |
| 7 | seven | "SEV-un" |
| 8 | eight | "ate" (the **gh** is silent) |
| 9 | nine | "nine" |
| 10 | ten | "ten" |
| 11 | eleven | "i-LEV-un" |
| 12 | twelve | "twelv" |
| 13 | thirteen | "thur-TEEN" |
| 14 | fourteen | "for-TEEN" |
| 15 | fifteen | "fif-TEEN" (not *fiveteen*) |
| 16 | sixteen | "siks-TEEN" |
| 17 | seventeen | "sev-un-TEEN" |
| 18 | eighteen | "ay-TEEN" (only **one t**: eigh**t**een) |
| 19 | nineteen | "nine-TEEN" |
| 20 | twenty | "TWEN-tee" |

> 💡 Notice the spellings: **thir**teen (not *threeteen*), **fif**teen (not *fiveteen*), **eigh**teen (one t).

## 4. The tens: 20–100

| Number | Word | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | twenty | "TWEN-tee" |
| 30 | thirty | "THUR-tee" |
| 40 | forty | "FOR-tee" ⚠️ no **u** (not *fourty*) |
| 50 | fifty | "FIF-tee" |
| 60 | sixty | "SIKS-tee" |
| 70 | seventy | "SEV-un-tee" |
| 80 | eighty | "AY-tee" |
| 90 | ninety | "NINE-tee" (keep the **e**: nin**e**ty) |
| 100 | one hundred / a hundred | "wun HUN-drid" |

## 5. Numbers between the tens: 21–99

**Tens word + hyphen ( - ) + ones word:**

> 21 = twenty**-**one · 35 = thirty**-**five · 48 = forty**-**eight · 99 = ninety**-**nine

The small line **-** is called a **hyphen**. It joins the two parts into one number word.

## 6. ⚠️ -teen or -ty? The big listening problem

13 and 30, 14 and 40, 15 and 50… sound similar! The difference is the **stress** (the strong part of the word):

| -teen (13–19): stress at the **END** | -ty (20–90): stress at the **START** |
|---|---|
| thir**TEEN** (13) | **THIR**ty (30) |
| four**TEEN** (14) | **FOR**ty (40) |
| fif**TEEN** (15) | **FIF**ty (50) |
| six**TEEN** (16) | **SIX**ty (60) |

Also: **-teen** has a long "ee" + **n**: /tiːn/. **-ty** is short: /ti/ (in American English the *t* often sounds like a quick **d**: "FOR-dee").

> 💡 **If you are not sure, ask!** *"Sorry, is that one-five or five-zero?"* — this is very normal, even for English speakers.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **number** | a word or sign for an amount: *3, three* |
| **digit** | one sign from 0 to 9. *45* has two digits. |
| **hyphen** | the short line **-** in *twenty-one* |
| **stress** | the strong (louder, longer) part of a word |
| **plural** | more than one: *books* |
| **singular** | just one: *book* |

## 7. Numbers and nouns: one book, two books

After **one** → singular (no -s). After **two or more** (and after **zero**) → plural (+ -s):

> **one** apple · **two** apple**s** · **zero** apple**s** · **twenty-one** apple**s**

⚠️ *Twenty-one* ends with "one", but it is still **more than one** → plural: *twenty-one book**s***.

Some plurals are irregular: *one child → two **children***; *one man → two **men*** ([Lesson 025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).

## 8. Saying phone numbers

Say each **digit** separately. **0** is usually "**oh**" (UK, US) or "**zero**". Pause in groups:

> 07 845 312 → "oh-seven, | eight-four-five, | three-one-two"

Two same digits together: **double** (UK) — *44* = "double four". Americans usually say "four four". Remember *double s* in spelling ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)).

## 9. Saying prices

> £5 = "five pounds" · $20 = "twenty dollars" · €1 = "one euro"
> $3.50 = "three dollars fifty" (or "three fifty")

The sign (£ $ €) is **written first**, but **said last**.

## 10. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-016-001 — "I am twenty-three years old."

- **Meaning in simple English:** My age is 23.
- **Situation:** A form or a new friend asks your age.
- **Main lesson:** Numbers with a hyphen; how to say age.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | am | twenty-three years old. |
  |---|---|---|
  | subject | verb (be) | age |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I am** — *be* is used for age in English ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
  - **twenty-three** — *twenty* + hyphen + *three*.
  - **years** — plural, because 23 is more than one.
  - **old** — completes the age phrase.
- **Shorter and natural:** *I'm twenty-three.* (You can leave out *years old*.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I have twenty-three years.* — English uses **be** for age.
  - ❌ *I am twenty-three year old.* — after 23 → **years**.
  - ❌ *I am twenty three years old.* — in writing, use a **hyphen**: *twenty-three*. (Or just write the number: *I am 23 years old.*)
  - ✅ **I am twenty-three years old.**
- **⚠️ Special case:** *a **twenty-three-year-old** woman* — when the age comes **before** a noun, we use hyphens and **year** (no -s). You'll see this later.
- **Question:** ***How old are you?*** (*How old* + *are* + *you* — Way 1, [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md).)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Subject + am/is/are + number + years old.**
- **Quick check:** Write your age in words.

### EX-016-002 — "How many students are in your class?" — "Fifteen."

- **Meaning in simple English:** What is the number of students in your class? — There are 15.
- **Situation:** A friend asks about your English class.
- **Main lesson:** **How many** + plural noun; careful listening for -teen.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | How many students | are | in your class? |
  |---|---|---|
  | question words + plural noun (subject) | verb (be) | place |

- **Word by word:**
  - **How many** — asks for a **number** of things you can count.
  - **students** — **plural** after *how many* (always!).
  - **are** — plural subject → **are** (not *is*).
  - **in your class** — place.
- **The answer:** *Fifteen.* (short, natural) / *There are fifteen students.* (full — you will learn *there are* in [Lesson 041](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-041-there-is-there-are.md)).
- **Listening tip:** *fif**TEEN*** (15) or ***FIF**ty* (50)? Listen for the **stress**. If unsure, ask: *"Fifteen — one-five?"*
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *How many student are in your class?* — after *how many* → plural.
  - ❌ *How much students…?* — *how much* is for things you can't count (*How much water?*). Full lesson: [Lesson 071](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-071-how-much-how-many.md).
  - ✅ **How many students are in your class?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **How many + plural noun + are + place?** → *How many books are on the table?*
- **Quick check:** Ask about the number of people in someone's family.

---

### EX-016-003 — "My phone number is 0 7 9 4 4 1 2 6 5 0 3."

- **Meaning in simple English:** These are the digits you need to call my phone.
- **Situation:** A new friend wants your number.
- **Main lesson:** Saying phone numbers digit by digit.
- **How to say it:** "**oh**-seven-nine, | **double four**, one, two, | six-five, **oh**-three" — or in the US style: "zero-seven-nine, four-four-one, two-six-five, zero-three".
- **Word by word:**
  - **My phone number** — subject (main word: *number*).
  - **is** — be.
  - **0 7 9 4 4…** — the digits. In writing, people use **numerals** (0, 7, 9) for phone numbers, not words.
- **Question:** ***What's your phone number?*** (*What is…?*)
- **Checking:** *Sorry, is that four-four or four-five?* — totally normal.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **My phone number is + digits.**
- **Quick check:** How do British speakers often say *33*?

---

### EX-016-004 — "This book costs twelve dollars fifty."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The price of this book is $12.50. 📕💵
- **Situation:** You are in a bookshop. Your friend asks the price.
- **Main lesson:** Saying prices.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | This book | costs | twelve dollars fifty. |
  |---|---|---|
  | subject | verb | price |

- **Word by word:**
  - **This book** — the book near me ([Lesson 019](lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
  - **costs** — *cost* = to have a price. **-s** because *this book* = one thing.
  - **twelve dollars fifty** — written **$12.50**. The **$** is written first but **said after** the number.
- **Question:** ***How much is this book?*** / ***How much does this book cost?*** (*How much* for prices — money is not counted one by one here.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *This book costs dollars twelve fifty.* — the number comes before *dollars*.
  - ❌ *This book cost twelve dollars fifty.* — one book → **costs**.
  - ✅ **This book costs twelve dollars fifty.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **This + thing + costs + price.**
- **Quick check:** Say *£8.99* in words.

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| fourty | **forty** | no *u* |
| ninty | **ninety** | keep the *e* |
| fiveteen | **fifteen** | special spelling |
| twenty one | **twenty-one** | hyphen |
| two book | two book**s** | plural after 2+ |
| I have 30 years. | I am 30 (years old). | age with *be* |
| How many water? | How **much** water? | *how many* = countable things |
| dollars twenty | twenty dollars | number first |

## 12. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

1. Say the pairs, stressing correctly: *thir**TEEN** – **THIR**ty · four**TEEN** – **FOR**ty · fif**TEEN** – **FIF**ty*.
2. Say your phone number in groups, with small pauses.
3. With a text-to-speech tool, type *13, 30, 14, 40* and listen.

## 13. Exercises

**A. Write the words.**

**B. Correct the spelling.**

**E. Correct the sentences.**

## 14. Answers (with explanations)

**A.** 1. **seventeen** · 2. **forty** · 3. **fifty-five** · 4. **ninety** · 5. **twelve** · 6. **eighty-three**

**B.** 1. **forty** · 2. **nineteen** (keep the *e*) · 3. **eighteen** (one *t*) · 4. **thirty-five** (hyphen)

**C.** 1. **cat** (one → singular) · 2. **cars** · 3. **days** (21 is more than one)

**D.**
1. ***How old are you?***
2. ***What's your phone number?***
3. ***How much is it?*** / ***How much does it cost?***

**E.**
1. *My brother **is** fifteen (**years old**).* — age uses *be*.
2. *How many **apples** do you want?* — plural after *how many*.

## 15. Quick revision sheet

- 0–12 are special words; 13–19 end in **-teen**; tens end in **-ty**.
- Spellings: **thir**teen, **fif**teen, **eigh**teen, **for**ty, **nine**ty.
- 21–99: **twenty-one** (hyphen).
- **-teen**: stress at the end. **-ty**: stress at the start.
- 1 → singular; 0, 2+ → plural.
- Phone numbers: digit by digit; **0** = "oh"; **44** = "double four" (UK).
- Prices: **twelve dollars fifty** ($12.50).
- **How old…? · How many + plural? · How much (price)?**

## 16. Memory trick

> **"-TEEN at the end, -ty at the start."** 🎯 *thir-TEEN* vs *THIR-ty*
