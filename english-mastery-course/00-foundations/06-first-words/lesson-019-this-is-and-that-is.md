# Lesson 019 — "This Is…" and "That Is…"

**ID:** L-019 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 018](lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)

⬅️ [Lesson 018](lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md) · ➡️ [Lesson 020](../07-review/lesson-020-foundations-review-and-test.md)

---

## 1. What you will learn

- **this** = near me 👇 · **that** = not near me 👉
- **these / those** for more than one (first look).
- **This is…** to show things and to **introduce people**.
- **What's this? / What's that?** and the answer **It's…**
- **That's…** to react: *That's great!*

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- **is** for one thing; **are** for more than one ([Lesson 018](lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- *This is a banana* ([Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)) · *This book costs twelve dollars fifty* ([Lesson 016](lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).
- **a / an** before one thing ([Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).

---

## 3. Near and far

| | One thing | More than one |
|---|---|---|
| **Near** me 👇 (I can touch it) | **this** | **these** |
| **Not near** me 👉 (over there) | **that** | **those** |

> ***This** is my bag.* (in my hand) · ***That** is your bag.* (over there, on the chair)
> ***These** are my keys.* · ***Those** are your keys.*

**Near / far can also be about time:**
- *this week* = the week we are in now
- *that day* = a day in the past (or future), not today

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **this** | the one near me |
| **that** | the one not near me |
| **these** | the ones near me (plural) |
| **those** | the ones not near me (plural) |
| **introduce** | tell people each other's names when they meet for the first time |
| **demonstrative** | the grammar name for *this, that, these, those* ("pointing words") |

---

## 4. Patterns

### Pattern A — Showing something

> **This is / That is + a/an + thing.** → *This is a pen. That is an orange.*
> **These are / Those are + plural.** → *These are pens. Those are oranges.*

### Pattern B — Asking

> **What's this?** / **What's that?** → **It's a/an ___.**
> **What are these?** / **What are those?** → **They're ___.**

⚠️ In the answer, we use **it** (one) or **they** (more than one) — not *this/that* again.

### Pattern C — Introducing people

> **This is + name / person.** → *Mum, **this is** my friend Leo.*

We use **this** (not *he* / *she*) when we introduce someone.

### Pattern D — Phone and voice messages

> *Hello, **this is** Anna.* (= I am Anna — on the phone)

### Pattern E — Reacting

> ***That's** great! / That's terrible! / That's true. / That's right.*

Here **that** = "the thing you just said".

### Short forms

- **that is → that's** ✅ (very common)
- **this is → ~~this's~~** — not written. Always write **this is** (in speech it sounds like "thiss-iz").
- **it is → it's** ✅

## 5. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-019-001 — "This is my phone. That is your phone."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The phone in my hand belongs to me. The phone over there (on the table) belongs to you. 📱
- **Situation:** You and your friend have the same phone. You are checking which is which.
- **Main lesson:** **this** (near) vs **that** (far).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | This / That | is | my / your phone. |
  |---|---|---|
  | subject (pointing word) | be | what it is |

- **Word by word:**
  - **This** — the phone **near me**. It is the **subject** ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
  - **is** — one thing → **is** ([Lesson 018](lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
  - **my phone** — the phone of me. ⚠️ No *a* with *my*: ❌ *a my phone*.
  - **That** — the phone **not near me**.
  - **your phone** — the phone of you. (Full lesson on *my/your/his…*: [Lesson 036](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-036-possessive-determiners.md).)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *This is a my phone.* — don't use *a* and *my* together.
  - ❌ *This are my phone.* — one thing → **is**.
  - ✅ **This is my phone. That is your phone.**
- **In natural speech:** *This is my phone. **That's** yours.* (*yours* = your one — full lesson: [Lesson 066](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-066-possessive-pronouns.md))
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **This is my + thing. That is your + thing.**
- **Quick check:** Point to something far away and say what it is.

### EX-019-002 — "What's that?" — "It's a bird."

- **Meaning in simple English:** What is the thing over there? — It is a bird. 🐦
- **Situation:** You are in the park with a child. The child sees something in a tree.
- **Main lesson:** Asking with **that**, answering with **it**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | What | 's | that? | — | It | 's | a bird. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | question word | be | subject | | subject | be | what it is |

- **Word by word:**
  - **What's** — *What is* ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)).
  - **that** — the thing far away.
  - **It's** — *It is*. We change **that → it** in the answer, because we already know which thing we mean.
  - **a bird** — one bird → **a**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *What is that? — That is bird.* — need **a**. (*That's a bird* is possible, but **It's a bird** is the most natural answer.)
  - ❌ *What that?* — the verb **is** is missing.
  - ✅ **What's that? — It's a bird.**
- **Plural version:** *What are **those**? — **They're** birds.*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **What's this/that? — It's a/an ___.**
- **Quick check:** Answer: *What are these?* (apples)

---

### EX-019-003 — "Mum, this is my friend Leo. Leo, this is my mum."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I am telling my mother the name of my friend, and telling my friend that this woman is my mother.
- **Situation:** You bring a friend home for the first time.
- **Main lesson:** **This is** to introduce people.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Mum, | this | is | my friend Leo. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | the person you talk to | subject | be | the person you introduce |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Mum,** — you are **talking to** your mother. When you say the name of the person you talk to, put a **comma** after it ([Lesson 017](lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md): *Good morning, Mrs Brown*). *Mum* = informal word for mother (US: **Mom**).
  - **this is** — introduces a person who is **here with you**. We don't say *he is* or *she is* in introductions.
  - **my friend Leo** — *friend* (small f) + name *Leo* (capital L) ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
  - **my mum** — when it's not used as a name, *mum* has a small letter.
- **What do they say next?** *Nice to meet you!* — *Nice to meet you too!* ([D-001](../../06-spoken-english/daily-conversations/dialogue-001-meeting-someone-new.md))
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Mum, he is my friend Leo.* — in introductions, use **this is**.
  - ❌ *Mum, this is Leo my friend.* — understandable, but *my friend Leo* (or *Leo, my friend*) is more natural.
  - ✅ **Mum, this is my friend Leo.**
- **Formal version:** *Mr Smith, **I'd like you to meet** my colleague, Dr Ahmed.* (later lessons)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person A, this is (my ___) Person B.**
- **Quick check:** Introduce your teacher, Mr Lee, to your father.

### EX-019-004 — "These are my keys, and those are your keys."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The keys here, near me, are mine. The keys over there are yours. 🔑
- **Situation:** You and a family member put your keys on a table by the door. You separate them.
- **Main lesson:** **these / those** + **are** (plural).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | These | are | my keys, | and | those | are | your keys. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | subject (near, plural) | be | | joining word | subject (far, plural) | be | |

- **Word by word:**
  - **These** — more than one, near me. Plural → **are**.
  - **keys** — plural (+ s).
  - **, and** — joins two complete ideas. A comma before *and* is common here.
  - **those** — more than one, not near me.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Plural subject → **are** ([Lesson 018](lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *This are my keys.* — plural → **These are**.
  - ❌ *These is my keys.* — plural → **are**.
  - ❌ *Those are my key.* — plural → **keys**.
  - ✅ **These are my keys, and those are your keys.**
- **Pronunciation ⚠️:** *this* /ðɪs/ (short **i**) vs *these* /ðiːz/ (long **ee**, and **z** sound). Practise: *this — these, this — these* ([sounds-001](../../08-pronunciation-and-listening/sounds/sounds-001-english-vowel-and-consonant-sounds.md): /ɪ/ vs /iː/).
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **These are my ___s, and those are your ___s.**
- **Quick check:** Make plural: *That is my shoe.*

### EX-019-005 — "I have a new job!" — "That's great!"

- **Meaning in simple English:** I got new work! — That is wonderful news!
- **Situation:** Your friend tells you good news.
- **Main lesson:** **That's** + describing word to **react**.
- **Word by word:**
  - **That** — here it means **"what you just said"** (the news). Not a thing you can see!
  - **'s** — *is*.
  - **great** — very good.
- **Other reactions:**
  - Good news: *That's great! / That's wonderful! / That's amazing!*
  - Bad news: *Oh no, that's terrible. / That's a shame.* (shame = a sad thing)
  - Agreeing: *That's right. / That's true.*
  - After "thank you": *That's okay.* (= no problem)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *This is great!* — possible (about something here now), but to react to news, people say ***That's** great!*
  - ❌ *It's great!* — possible too, but less common as a reaction.
  - ✅ **That's great!**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **That's + feeling word!**
- **Quick check:** Your friend says "I'm sick." What can you say?

## 6. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| This are my books. | **These** are my books. | plural |
| These is my book. | **This is** my book. | singular |
| This's my bag. | **This is** my bag. | no short form *this's* |
| What's this? — This is a pen. | — **It's** a pen. | use *it* in the answer |
| He is my friend Tom. (introducing) | **This is** my friend Tom. | introductions use *this is* |
| This is a my car. | This is my car. | no *a* + *my* |

## 7. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

- **th** in *this, that, these, those* = /ð/ — tongue between teeth, voice **on** (throat shakes) ([sounds-001](../../08-pronunciation-and-listening/sounds/sounds-001-english-vowel-and-consonant-sounds.md)).
- *this* (short) vs *these* (long + z).
- *that* /ðæt/ — open mouth wide; *that's* = "thats".
- When introducing, stress the **name**: *This is my friend **LEO**.*

## 8. Exercises

**A. this, that, these or those?**

1. ___ is my pen. (in my hand)
2. ___ are my shoes. (on my feet)
3. ___ is the station. (far away)
4. ___ are birds. (in the sky)

**B. Answer the questions.**

1. What's this? (an apple)
2. What are those? (cars)

**C. Introduce:**

1. your friend Mia to your brother.

**D. React.**

1. "I'm getting married!" → ___
2. "My cat is sick." → ___

**E. Correct the mistakes.**

1. This are my friends.
2. What's that? — That's a apple.
3. This's my house.

## 9. Answers (with explanations)

**A.** 1. **This** (near, one) · 2. **These** (near, plural) · 3. **That** (far, one) · 4. **Those** (far, plural)

**B.**
1. ***It's an apple.*** — *it* in the answer; **an** before a vowel sound.
2. ***They're cars.*** — plural → *they*.

**C.** ***(Brother's name), this is my friend Mia.*** — and maybe: *Mia, this is my brother ___.*

**D.**
1. ***That's wonderful!*** / *That's great!* / *Congratulations!* (a word for good news — "con-grat-yoo-LAY-shunz")
2. ***Oh no, that's terrible.*** / *That's a shame.*

**E.**
1. ***These** are my friends.* — plural.
2. *It's **an** apple.* — **an** before a vowel; *It's* is the more natural answer.
3. *This **is** my house.* — no short form *this's*.

## 10. Quick revision sheet

| | one | more than one |
|---|---|---|
| near 👇 | **this** is | **these** are |
| far 👉 | **that** is | **those** are |

- **What's this/that?** → **It's** a/an ___.
- **What are these/those?** → **They're** ___.
- Introduce: **This is** my friend ___.
- Phone: *Hello, **this is** Anna.*
- React: **That's** great / terrible / right.

## 11. Memory trick

> **"tHIS is HERE, tHAT is THERE."** — *this* is short and close; *that* points far away. 👇👉
