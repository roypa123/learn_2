## 3. Formal and informal

> **Formal** = careful and polite. Use it with people you don't know well, older people, teachers, at work, in offices.
> **Informal** = relaxed and friendly. Use it with friends, family, classmates, children.

**Neutral** words are fine almost everywhere.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **greeting** | what you say when you meet someone |
| **formal** | careful, polite, serious |
| **informal** | relaxed, friendly |
| **neutral** | okay in most situations |
| **polite** | kind and respectful in the way you speak |

## 4. Saying hello

| Greeting | When | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **Hello.** | any time | neutral |
| **Hi.** / **Hey!** | any time | informal (*Hey* is very informal) |
| **Good morning.** | before 12:00 | neutral / formal |
| **Good afternoon.** | 12:00 to about 6 pm | formal |
| **Good evening.** | after about 6 pm | formal |

> ⚠️ **Good night** is **not** a greeting! It means **goodbye** at night, or before going to bed. 🌙 ❌ *(arriving at a party at 9 pm)* "Good night!" → ✅ "Good evening!" / "Hi!"

## 5. How are you? — and answers

**How are you?** is a very common, friendly question after *hello*. Usually people expect a **short, positive** answer — and a question back.

| Question | Answers |
|---|---|
| **How are you?** | *I'm fine, thanks. And you?* · *Very well, thank you.* (formal) · *Good, thanks!* · *Not bad.* · *I'm okay.* |
| **How's it going?** (informal) | *Good, thanks. You?* · *Not bad!* |
| **What's up?** (very informal) | *Not much.* · *Nothing much.* |

- **Ping-pong rule** 🏓 ([D-001](../../06-spoken-english/daily-conversations/dialogue-001-meeting-someone-new.md)): answer, then ask back: *And you?* / *How about you?*
- With close friends you can be honest: *I'm a bit tired today.* With a shop worker, *Fine, thanks* is normal.

## 6. Saying goodbye

| Goodbye | Style |
|---|---|
| **Goodbye.** | neutral / formal |
| **Bye!** / **Bye-bye!** | informal |
| **See you!** / **See you later!** / **See you tomorrow!** | informal |
| **Have a nice day!** | neutral, friendly (shops, work) |
| **Good night!** | at night, or before bed |
| **Take care!** | friendly |

## 7. Polite words — the "magic words" ✨

| Situation | Say | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| You ask for something | **Please** | — |
| Someone helps you | **Thank you.** / **Thanks.** (informal) / **Thank you very much.** | **You're welcome.** / **No problem.** (informal) |
| You make a small mistake | **Sorry!** / **I'm sorry.** | **That's okay.** / **No problem.** |
| You want attention, or to pass someone | **Excuse me.** | — |
| You didn't hear | **Sorry?** / **Pardon?** (formal) | (they repeat) |

> 💡 **Excuse me** vs **Sorry**: *Excuse me* is **before** you disturb someone (*Excuse me, where is the station?*). *Sorry* is **after** you did something wrong (*Sorry, I'm late.*).

## 9. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-017-001 — "Good morning, Mrs Brown. How are you?" — "I'm very well, thank you. And you?"

- **Meaning in simple English:** A polite morning greeting to a teacher (Mrs Brown), and asking how she feels. She answers that she is good, and asks back.
- **Situation:** You arrive at school at 8:30. You meet your teacher in the corridor.
- **Main lesson:** A **formal** greeting with a title; the *How are you?* exchange.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Good morning, | Mrs Brown. | How | are | you? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | greeting | name with title | question word | verb (be) | subject |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Good morning** — formal/neutral greeting before 12:00.
  - **,** — a comma before the name of the person you are talking to.
  - **Mrs Brown** — **Mrs** (say "MISS-iz") = title for a married woman. Other titles: **Mr** ("MIS-ter") for a man, **Ms** ("miz") for any woman, **Miss** for a young/unmarried woman. Use the title + **family name** (*Mrs **Brown***), not the first name. In American English, people write a full stop: **Mrs.**, **Mr.**
  - **How are you?** — Way 1 question: *You are…* → *How **are** you?* ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
  - **I'm very well** — formal answer; *well* here means "healthy, good".
  - **thank you** — polite.
  - **And you?** — asking back (ping-pong 🏓).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Good morning, Mrs Anna.* — title + **family name**, not first name.
  - ❌ *How you are?* — question order: *How **are you**?*
  - ❌ *I'm very good, thanks you.* — *thank **you*** or *thank**s*** (not *thanks you*).
  - ✅ **Good morning, Mrs Brown. How are you?**
- **Informal version (with a friend):** *Hi, Sam! How's it going?* — *Good, thanks. You?*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Greeting, + title + family name. How are you?**
- **Quick check:** It's 3 pm. Greet Mr Lee formally.

### EX-017-002 — "What does 'busy' mean?"

- **Meaning in simple English:** Please explain the meaning of the word *busy*.
- **Situation:** You read the word *busy* in a text. You don't know it. You ask the teacher.
- **Main lesson:** Asking about the meaning of a word — a Way 3 question with **does**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | What | does | 'busy' | mean | ? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | question word | helper | subject (the word) | main verb | |

- **Word by word:**
  - **What** — asks for information.
  - **does** — helper. The subject (*the word "busy"*) = one thing, like **it** → **does** ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
  - **'busy'** — the word we ask about. Put it in **quotation marks** ( ' ' or " " ) in writing.
  - **mean** — main verb, **no -s** (the -s is in *does*).
- **The answer:** *"Busy" **means** "having a lot to do".* — in the statement, *means* has **-s**.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *Does your brother like football?* — same pattern ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *What means 'busy'?* — very common mistake! Use **does … mean**.
  - ❌ *What does 'busy' means?* — no -s after *does*.
  - ❌ *What is mean 'busy'?* — mixing *is* and *mean*.
  - ✅ **What does 'busy' mean?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **What does + "word" + mean?**
- **Quick check:** Ask about the meaning of *library*.

### EX-017-003 — "How do you say 'gato' in English?" — "Cat."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I know the word *gato* (Spanish). What is the English word for it? — It's *cat*. 🐱
- **Situation:** A Spanish-speaking learner wants an English word.
- **Main lesson:** Asking for an English word — Way 3 with **do**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | How | do | you | say | 'gato' | in English? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | question word | helper | subject | main verb | object | which language |

- **Word by word:**
  - **How** — asks about the way.
  - **do you** — here **you** means **people in general** (anyone), not only the listener. *How do you say…?* = *How do people say…?*
  - **say** — main verb.
  - **'gato'** — the object (*say what?* → 'gato') ([Lesson 013](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
  - **in English** — in the English language. Capital **E** ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Compare with *How do you spell that?*** ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)) — same pattern, different verb.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *How say 'gato' in English?* — *do you* is missing.
  - ❌ *How do you call 'gato' in English?* — common mistake; in English we **say** a word, not "call" it. (*What do you call this in English?* — pointing at a thing — **is** correct.)
  - ✅ **How do you say 'gato' in English?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **How do you say "___" in English?**
- **Quick check:** Ask for the English word for a word in your language.

### EX-017-004 — "Sorry, I don't understand. Can you speak more slowly, please?"

- **Meaning in simple English:** I'm sorry, but I can't follow what you said. Please talk at a slower speed.
- **Situation:** The teacher (or a shop worker, or a stranger) speaks very fast.
- **Main lesson:** A **negative** sentence with **don't**, and a polite request.
- **Sentence 1:**

  | Sorry, | I | don't | understand. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | polite word | subject | helper + not | main verb |

- **Sentence 2:**

  | Can | you | speak | more slowly, | please? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | helper | subject | main verb | how | polite word |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Sorry** — polite: you are not angry; you are asking for help.
  - **don't** — *do not*. To make a normal verb **negative** (not true), we use **do + not** before it. (Full lesson: [Lesson 044](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-044-present-simple-negative.md).)
  - **understand** — main verb: to know the meaning of something.
  - **Can you … please?** — polite request ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md), EX-015-002).
  - **more slowly** — *slowly* = not fast; *more* makes it "slower than before". *Slowly* describes **how** you speak (an adverb — [Lesson 074](../../01-beginner-grammar/adverbs/lesson-074-adverbs-of-manner.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I not understand.* — use **don't**: *I don't understand.*
  - ❌ *I no understand.* — same.
  - ❌ *I am not understand.* — *understand* is a normal verb → **don't**, not *am not*.
  - ✅ **I don't understand.**
  - ❌ *Can you speak more slow?* — common in speech, but correct English is **more slowly**.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I don't + verb.** → *I don't know. I don't remember.*
- **Quick check:** Correct: *I no know.*

## 10. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Good night! (arriving at 9 pm) | Good evening! | *Good night* = goodbye. |
| Thanks you. | Thank you. / Thanks. | |
| What means "X"? | What does "X" mean? | Way 3 question. |
| How do you call this in English? (a word) | How do you say "X" in English? | *say* a word. |
| I no understand. | I don't understand. | negative with *don't*. |
| Good morning, Mr John. | Good morning, Mr Smith. | title + family name. |
| — Thank you! — Please. | — Thank you! — **You're welcome.** | *Please* is for asking. |

## 11. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

- *How are you?* → "how-ah-YOU?" (stress on **you** when asking back: *And **YOU**?*)
- *Thank you* → /θ/ — tongue between teeth: "THANK-yoo" ([sounds-001](../../08-pronunciation-and-listening/sounds/sounds-001-english-vowel-and-consonant-sounds.md)).
- *I don't understand* → "I DOHN-un-der-STAND" (the **t** in *don't* is almost silent).
- *Mrs* → "MISS-iz" · *Ms* → "MIZ".

## 14. Quick revision sheet

- **Hello / Hi** — any time. **Good morning / afternoon / evening** — formal.
- **Good night** = goodbye (night). Not a greeting!
- **How are you?** → *Fine, thanks. And you?*
- **Thank you** → *You're welcome.* · **Excuse me** (before) · **Sorry** (after).
- Titles: **Mr / Mrs / Ms / Miss + family name.**
- Classroom: *What does X mean? · How do you say X in English? · I don't understand. · Can you repeat that, please?*

## 15. Memory trick

> **"Excuse me" opens the door 🚪, "Sorry" fixes the mistake 🩹, "Thank you" closes it with a smile 😊.**
