# Lesson 018 — First Sentences with "Am / Is / Are"

> This is a **first introduction**. The verb *be* gets three full lessons in Level 1: [L-029](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-029-be-positive.md) (positive), [L-030](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-030-be-negative.md) (negative), [L-031](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-031-be-questions-and-short-answers.md) (questions).

---

## 1. What you will learn

- **am, is, are** are three forms of one verb: **be**.
- **Which form** goes with which subject.
- **Short forms**: *I'm, he's, they're*.
- **Negative**: *I'm not, isn't, aren't*.
- **Questions**: *Are you…? Is he…?*
- What we use *be* for: names, age, jobs, countries, feelings, places, descriptions.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- *am, is, are* are **being verbs**. They work like **=** ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
- Subject pronouns: **I, you, he, she, it, we, they** ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- Questions with *be*: move it to the front — *Are you ready?* ([Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- You have used *be* in many sentences already: *I am Sam* (L-001), *My name is Ana* (L-003), *The bus is late* (L-009).

---

## 3. One verb, three forms

The base word is **be**. In the present, it has **three** forms:

| Subject | be | Short form | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **am** | I**'m** | *I'm a student.* |
| **you** (one person or many) | **are** | you**'re** | *You're late.* |
| **he** | **is** | he**'s** | *He's from Japan.* |
| **she** | **is** | she**'s** | *She's my sister.* |
| **it** | **is** | it**'s** | *It's cold.* |
| **we** | **are** | we**'re** | *We're hungry.* |
| **they** | **are** | they**'re** | *They're at home.* |

### The simple rule

> **I → am** · **one person or thing (he/she/it) → is** · **you, and more than one (we/they) → are**

| Subject | Form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *My mother* | **is** | = she |
| *The bus* | **is** | = it |
| *My parents* | **are** | = they |
| *Tom and I* | **are** | = we |
| *The children* | **are** | = they |

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **be** | the base (dictionary) form of *am / is / are* |
| **form** | one shape of a word (*am* is a form of *be*) |
| **short form** (contraction) | *I'm, she's, they're* |
| **negative** | a sentence that says something is **not** true |
| **positive** (affirmative) | a sentence that says something **is** true |

---

## 4. Negative: add "not"

Put **not** **after** *am / is / are*:

| Full | Short (two ways) |
|---|---|
| I am not | I**'m not** (⚠️ there is no *amn't*) |
| you are not | you**'re not** / you **aren't** |
| he / she / it is not | he**'s not** / he **isn't** |
| we / they are not | we**'re not** / we **aren't** |

> *I'm not tired.* · *She isn't at home.* · *They aren't students.*

## 5. Questions and short answers

Move **am / is / are** before the subject (Way 1 — [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)):

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| **Am** I late? | Yes, you **are**. | No, you **aren't**. |
| **Are** you a teacher? | Yes, I **am**. | No, I**'m not**. |
| **Is** she okay? | Yes, she **is**. | No, she **isn't**. |
| **Are** they here? | Yes, they **are**. | No, they **aren't**. |

## 6. What do we use "be" for?

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| name | *I **am** Sam.* |
| age | *She **is** twelve.* ([L-016](lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)) |
| job | *He **is** a doctor.* |
| country / nationality | *We **are** from Brazil. / We **are** Brazilian.* |
| feelings | *I **am** happy. / They **are** tired.* |
| place | *The keys **are** on the table.* |
| description | *The house **is** big.* |
| weather / time | *It **is** cold. / It **is** 5 o'clock.* |

## 7. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-018-001 — "I'm a nurse. I'm not a doctor."

- **Meaning in simple English:** My job is nurse (a person who takes care of sick people in a hospital). My job is not doctor.
- **Situation:** A patient thinks you are the doctor. You explain.
- **Main lesson:** **am** with **I**; positive and negative.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | 'm | (not) | a nurse / a doctor. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | subject | be (am) | negative word | job |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I'm** — *I am*. The apostrophe replaces **a** ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
  - **a nurse** — one job → **a** ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
  - **not** — makes the sentence negative. It goes **after** *am*.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I'm not doctor.* — still need **a**: *not **a** doctor*.
  - ❌ *I amn't a doctor.* — *amn't* doesn't exist in standard English.
  - ❌ *I no am a doctor.* — use **not**, after *am*.
  - ✅ **I'm a nurse. I'm not a doctor.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I'm a/an + job. I'm not a/an + job.**
- **Quick check:** Say your job (or *I'm a student*) and one job you are **not**.

---

### EX-018-002 — "My parents are from Ghana, but they live in London."

- **Meaning in simple English:** My mother and father were born in (or come from) Ghana. Now their home is in London.
- **Situation:** A friend asks about your family.
- **Main lesson:** **are** with a plural subject (*my parents* = they).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My parents | are | from Ghana, | but | they | live | in London. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | subject | be | where from | joining word | subject | verb | where |

- **Word by word:**
  - **My parents** — *parents* = mother and father. **Plural** → **are**.
  - **from Ghana** — country of origin. Capital **G** ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
  - **, but** — joins two ideas that are different ([D-001](../../06-spoken-english/daily-conversations/dialogue-001-meeting-someone-new.md)). A comma usually comes before *but* when it joins two sentences.
  - **they** — replaces *my parents* (so we don't repeat it).
  - **live** — normal verb. **No -s**, because *they* is plural.
- **Notice:** The first part uses **be** (*are from*); the second part uses a normal verb (*live*). Each part has its own subject + verb ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My parents is from Ghana.* — plural → **are**.
  - ❌ *My parents are live in London.* — don't put *are* before a normal verb ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md): ❌ *I am go*).
  - ✅ **My parents are from Ghana, but they live in London.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Subject + are from + country, but they live in + city.**
- **Quick check:** *My brother and sister ___ students.* (is / are)

---

### EX-018-003 — "Is Tom at work?" — "No, he isn't. He's at home. He's sick."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Is Tom at his job now? — No. He is in his house, because he is ill. 🤒
- **Situation:** You phone Tom's office. His colleague answers.
- **Main lesson:** Question, negative short answer, and two positive sentences with **is**.
- **Sentence parts (question):**

  | Is | Tom | at work? |
  |---|---|---|
  | be | subject | place |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Is Tom…?** — *Tom is at work* → ***Is** Tom at work?* (Way 1.)
  - **at work** — at his job / workplace. (Also: *at home, at school*. No *the*: ❌ *at the home*.)
  - **No, he isn't.** — short answer. *Tom* → **he** ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
  - **He's at home.** — *He is* at home.
  - **He's sick.** — *sick* = ill, not healthy (describing word).
- **⚠️ He's = He is** here. (Later you will see *he's* can also mean *he has*: *He's got a car.* The other words tell you which.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Tom is at work?* — possible in speech with a rising voice, but the normal question is *Is Tom at work?*
  - ❌ *No, he's not at work, he is at home, he is sick.* — commas joining full sentences ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)). Use full stops.
  - ❌ *He is at the home.* — *at home* has no *the*.
  - ✅ **Is Tom at work? — No, he isn't. He's at home. He's sick.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Is + person + at work/home/school? — No, he/she isn't. He's/She's at ___.**
- **Quick check:** Make a question: *Your sister is at school.*

---

### EX-018-004 — "We aren't tired. We're excited!"

- **Meaning in simple English:** We do not feel tired. We feel very happy and full of energy because something good is coming. 🤩
- **Situation:** It's midnight before a big trip. Your mother says "Go to sleep, you're tired!" You and your brother answer.
- **Main lesson:** **are** with **we**; negative *aren't*; feelings.
- **Word by word:**
  - **We aren't** — *we are not*. (Also correct: *We're not tired.*)
  - **tired** — needing sleep.
  - **We're** — *we are*. Sounds like "weer".
  - **excited** — very happy and eager because something good will happen.
  - **!** — strong feeling ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *We not tired.* — the verb **are** is missing.
  - ❌ *We are no tired.* — use **not**.
  - ✅ **We aren't tired. We're excited!**
- **⚠️ Spelling:** **we're** (we are) ≠ **were** (past of *are*: *We were tired yesterday* — [Lesson 061](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-061-was-were.md)) ≠ **where** (place question word).
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **We aren't + feeling. We're + feeling!**
- **Quick check:** Make negative: *They are happy.*

---

### EX-018-005 — "The flowers in your garden are beautiful."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The flowers that grow in your garden look very nice. 🌸
- **Situation:** You visit a friend's house. You see their garden.
- **Main lesson:** Choosing **is/are** with a long subject — look at the **main word**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The flowers in your garden | are | beautiful. |
  |---|---|---|
  | subject (main word: *flowers*) | be | description |

- **How to choose is/are:** Main word = **flowers** (plural) → **are**. *Garden* is closer to the verb, but it's not the main word ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md), EX-011-002).
- **Word by word:**
  - **flowers** — the coloured parts of plants. 🌷
  - **in your garden** — tells which flowers.
  - **beautiful** — describing word ([Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The flowers in your garden is beautiful.* — main word is plural → **are**.
  - ❌ *The flowers in your garden are beautifuls.* — describing words never take -s ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
  - ✅ **The flowers in your garden are beautiful.**
- **Change it:** *The **flower** in your garden **is** beautiful.* (one flower → is)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + plural noun + place + are + describing word.**
- **Quick check:** *The books on the table ___ mine.* (is / are)

## 8. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I is happy. / I are happy. | I **am** happy. | I → am |
| She are my friend. | She **is** my friend. | she → is |
| They is students. | They **are** students. | they → are |
| He tired. | He **is** tired. | missing verb |
| I am agree. / I am live here. | I agree. / I live here. | no *be* before a normal verb |
| I amn't sad. | I'm not sad. | no *amn't* |
| We not hungry. | We **aren't** hungry. | need *are* + not |
| Your welcome. | **You're** welcome. | you're = you are; your = belongs to you |

## 9. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Short forms are **normal in speaking** (and in informal writing):

| Full | Short | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I'm | "aim" |
| you are | you're | "yor" |
| he is | he's | "heez" |
| it is | it's | "its" |
| we are | we're | "weer" |
| they are | they're | "thair" (same as *there*!) |
| is not | isn't | "IZ-unt" |
| are not | aren't | "arnt" |

In **formal** writing (essays, official letters) use full forms more often.

## 10. Exercises

**A. am, is or are?**

1. I ___ from India.
2. My brother ___ a student.
3. You ___ very kind.
4. The children ___ in the garden.
5. It ___ hot today.
6. Ana and I ___ friends.

**B. Make negative (use short forms).**

1. He is at home.
2. They are hungry.
3. I am tired.

**C. Make questions and short answers.**

1. you / a student? (Yes)
2. your sister / at school? (No)
3. they / from Spain? (Yes)

**D. Correct the mistakes.**

1. My parents is teachers.
2. She not happy.
3. I am live in Paris.
4. The keys on the table is mine.

## 11. Answers (with explanations)

**A.** 1. **am** (I) · 2. **is** (= he) · 3. **are** (you) · 4. **are** (= they) · 5. **is** (it) · 6. **are** (= we)

**B.**
1. *He **isn't** at home.* / *He's not at home.*
2. *They **aren't** hungry.* / *They're not hungry.*
3. *I**'m not** tired.* (only one short form)

**C.**
1. ***Are you a student?*** — *Yes, I am.*
2. ***Is your sister at school?*** — *No, she isn't.*
3. ***Are they from Spain?*** — *Yes, they are.*

**D.**
1. *My parents **are** teachers.* — plural.
2. *She **isn't** happy.* — need the verb.
3. *I **live** in Paris.* — no *am* before a normal verb.
4. *The keys on the table **are** mine.* — main word *keys* is plural.

## 12. Quick revision sheet

| | + | − | ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | I'm | I'm not | Am I…? |
| he / she / it | he's | he isn't / he's not | Is he…? |
| you / we / they | you're | you aren't / you're not | Are you…? |

- I → **am** · one → **is** · you / many → **are**
- Look at the **main word** of a long subject.
- Don't use *am/is/are* before a normal verb (❌ *I am live*).
