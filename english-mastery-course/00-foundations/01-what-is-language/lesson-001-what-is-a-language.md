# Lesson 001 — What Is a Language?

**ID:** L-001 · **Level:** 0 (Absolute beginner) · **Needs before:** nothing. This is the first lesson.

---

## 1. What you will learn

By the end of this lesson, you will:

- Know what a **language** is.
- Know that a language has **sounds**, **words** and **rules**.
- Understand why we need rules (grammar).
- Read and understand your first 3 English sentences.

## 2. Why this lesson matters

Before we learn English, we should understand what we are learning. A language is like a tool. When you know how a tool works, you can use it better.

---

## 3. A simple start

Think about this:

You are hungry. You want an apple. How can you tell your friend?

- You can **point** at the apple. 👉🍎
- You can **draw** an apple.
- Or you can **say**: "I want an apple."

The last way is the fastest and clearest. That is **language**.

> **A language is a way for people to share ideas, feelings and information using words.**

We can share language in different ways:

| Way | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking | with our mouth | You say "Hello!" |
| Listening | with our ears | You hear "Hello!" |
| Writing | with a pen or keyboard | You write "Hello!" |
| Reading | with our eyes | You read "Hello!" |

These are called the **four skills**: speaking, listening, reading and writing. This course teaches all four.

---

## 4. What is inside a language?

Every language has three main parts. Think of building a house:

| Part of a language | Like in a house | Simple meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Sounds** | sand and stones | The noises we make with our mouth. |
| **Words** | bricks | Small pieces of meaning, like *apple*, *eat*, *happy*. |
| **Grammar (rules)** | the plan of the house | How we put words together in the right order. |

You need **all three**. Bricks without a plan make a pile, not a house. Words without grammar make a pile of words, not a clear message.

### Key words in this lesson

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **language** | a way of sharing ideas with words |
| **sound** | something you hear |
| **word** | a small piece of language that has a meaning |
| **sentence** | a group of words that gives a complete idea |
| **grammar** | the rules for putting words together |
| **meaning** | what something tells us; the idea inside it |

---

## 5. Why do we need grammar?

Look at these words:

> apple · eats · Tom · an

All the words are English. But they are in a strange order. It is hard to understand.

Now put them in the right order:

> **Tom eats an apple.**

Now it is clear! Tom is a person. He is eating. The thing he eats is an apple.

**Grammar told us the order.** That is why grammar is important.

Look at one more pair. The words are the **same**. Only the **order** changes.

> **The dog sees the cat.** 🐶 ➡️ 🐱 (the dog is looking)
>
> **The cat sees the dog.** 🐱 ➡️ 🐶 (the cat is looking)

Same words. Different order. **Different meaning.** In English, word order is very important. You will learn much more about this in [Lesson 014](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md) (word order).

---

## 6. Every language has different rules

You may already speak another language. That is great! It helps you.

But remember: **each language has its own rules.** English rules may be different from the rules in your language.

For example:

- In some languages, the action word comes at the **end** of the sentence.
- In English, the action word usually comes in the **middle**: *Tom **eats** an apple.*

So when English feels strange, do not worry. It is not wrong. It is just **different**.

> 💡 **Tip:** Do not always translate word by word from your language. Try to learn the English pattern.

## 7. Your first English sentences — explained slowly

You do not need to know grammar yet. We will look at each sentence like a puzzle. Do not try to remember every grammar word now. You will learn them all slowly in later lessons.

---

### EX-001-001 — "Hello."

- **Meaning in simple English:** A friendly word we say when we meet someone.
- **Situation:** You see your friend in the morning. You smile and say "Hello."
- **Main lesson:** One word can be a complete message.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Hello** — a greeting word. A *greeting* is what you say when you meet someone.
- **Why this is correct:** In real conversation, some short words can stand alone. "Hello." is complete because everyone understands its meaning.
- **Writing:** It starts with a capital (big) letter **H**. It ends with a full stop **.** You will learn about this in [Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md).
- **In natural speech:** People also say **"Hi."** (more relaxed) or **"Good morning."** (more polite).
- **Pronunciation:** heh-LOH. The second part is stronger: he-**LLO**.
- **Quick check:** What do you say when you meet a friend?

---

### EX-001-002 — "I am Sam."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The person speaking is called Sam. Sam is telling you their name.
- **Situation:** You meet a new person. You want to tell them your name.
- **Main lesson:** A sentence is a group of words with a complete idea.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | am | Sam. |
  |---|---|---|
  | the person speaking | a joining word | a name |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — means "me, the person who is talking". In English, **I** is always written with a capital (big) letter, even in the middle of a sentence.
  - **am** — a small joining word. It joins **I** with **Sam**. It works a little like "=" in maths: *I = Sam.* You will learn about "am / is / are" in [Lesson 018](../06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md).
  - **Sam** — a name. Names always start with a capital letter.
- **Why this is correct:** English sentences usually start with **who the sentence is about** (here: *I*). Then comes the joining word or action (*am*). Then comes more information (*Sam*).
- **Other versions (change the name):**
  - I am Maria.
  - I am Ali.
  - I am Chen.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Am I Sam.* — This order makes a **question**, not a statement. (You will learn questions in [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md).)
  - ❌ *I Sam.* — The joining word **am** is missing. In English we need it.
  - ❌ *i am Sam.* — **I** must always be a capital letter.
  - ✅ **I am Sam.**
- **In natural speech:** People often say **"I'm Sam."** The word *I'm* is a short way to say *I am*. Both are correct. *I'm* is more common when speaking.
- **Pronunciation:** "I'm" sounds like **"aim"**.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I am + your name.**
- **Quick check:** Write the sentence with your own name.

---

### EX-001-003 — "Tom eats an apple."

- **Meaning in simple English:** A person named Tom is eating an apple.
- **Situation:** You see Tom at lunch time. He has an apple in his hand. You tell someone what he is doing every day. (For example, he eats an apple every day.)
- **Main lesson:** Word order shows who does the action and what receives it.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Tom | eats | an apple. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **who** does the action | **the action** | **what** gets the action |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Tom** — a name. He is the person doing the action. Names start with a capital letter.
  - **eats** — the action. Eating is what Tom does. Notice the letter **-s** at the end: *eat → eats*. When we talk about one other person (like Tom), we add **-s**. Don't worry about this now. You will learn it in [Lesson 043](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md).
  - **an** — a small word that means "one". We use **an** before words that start with a vowel sound, like *apple*. You will learn **a / an** in [Lesson 032](../../01-beginner-grammar/articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md).
  - **apple** — a fruit. 🍎 It is the thing Tom eats.
- **Why this is correct:** In English, the normal order is: **who** → **action** → **what**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Tom an apple eats.* — The action word is at the end. This is correct in some languages, but not in English.
  - ❌ *An apple eats Tom.* — Now the apple is doing the eating! 😄 The meaning is crazy.
  - ✅ **Tom eats an apple.**
- **Writing:** Capital **T** at the start. Full stop **.** at the end.
- **Pronunciation:** "an apple" is joined together when we speak: **"a-napple"**.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + action + thing.**
  - Sara drinks water.
  - Ben reads a book.
  - My mother cooks rice.
- **Quick check:** In "Sara drinks water", who does the action? What is the action?

## 8. Common mistakes beginners make (and that's okay!)

| Thinking | Better thinking |
|---|---|
| "I must understand everything now." | You will understand slowly. It is normal to forget. We will review often. |
| "I will translate every word." | Learn whole English patterns, like **I am + name**. |
| "Grammar is boring and not useful." | Grammar is the plan of the house. It makes your message clear. |
| "My mistakes mean I am bad at English." | Mistakes mean you are **learning**. Everybody makes them. |

## 9. Pronunciation and listening

Say these out loud three times. Speak slowly first, then a little faster.

1. **Hello.** (he-LLO)
2. **I'm Sam.** (aim SAM)
3. **Tom eats an apple.** (TOM EATS a-NAP-ple)

If you can, use a text-to-speech tool (a computer or phone voice) to listen to these sentences. Copy the voice.

## 10. Guided practice

**A. Match the part of a language with its meaning.**

1. sounds — ( ) the rules for putting words in order
2. words — ( ) the noises we make with our mouth
3. grammar — ( ) small pieces of meaning, like *apple*

**B. Put the words in the correct order.**

1. am / I / Sam
2. eats / Tom / an apple
3. Ana / water / drinks

**C. Which sentence is correct? ✅ or ❌**

1. I am Ali.
2. Am I Ali.
3. i am Ali.
4. The cat sees the dog.

**D. Your turn.** Write one sentence: **I am ___.** (your name)

---

## 11. Answer key (with explanations)

**A.**
1. sounds → **the noises we make with our mouth.**
2. words → **small pieces of meaning, like *apple*.**
3. grammar → **the rules for putting words in order.**

**B.**
1. **I am Sam.** — First *who* (I), then the joining word (am), then the name (Sam).
2. **Tom eats an apple.** — First *who* (Tom), then the action (eats), then *what* (an apple).
3. **Ana drinks water.** — First *who* (Ana), then the action (drinks), then *what* (water).

**C.**
1. ✅ Correct.
2. ❌ This order is for a question. And a question ends with **?**, not **.** The statement is: *I am Ali.*
3. ❌ **I** must always be a capital letter: *I am Ali.*
4. ✅ Correct. (The cat is the one looking.)

**D.** Example answer: *I am Priya.* Check: Is **I** a capital letter? Does your name start with a capital letter? Is there a full stop at the end?

## 12. Quick revision sheet

- **Language** = a way to share ideas with words.
- **Four skills:** speaking, listening, reading, writing.
- **A language has:** sounds + words + grammar.
- **Grammar** = rules for putting words in order.
- **In English, order changes meaning:** *The dog sees the cat* ≠ *The cat sees the dog.*
- **Pattern 1:** I am + name. → *I am Sam.*
- **Pattern 2:** Person + action + thing. → *Tom eats an apple.*
