- A **language** is a way to share ideas with words.
- A language has **sounds**, **words** and **grammar** (rules).
- In English, **word order** changes meaning.

## 3. Where do people use English?

English is used in many, many places. Here are some:

| Place | How English is used | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 🌍 Travel | Asking for help in airports and hotels | "Where is the bus?" |
| 💼 Work | Emails, meetings, phone calls | "Thank you for your email." |
| 🎓 School | Books, classes, exams | "Open your book." |
| 💻 Internet | Websites, videos, apps, games | "Click here." |
| 🎬 Fun | Films, songs, TV | "I love this song!" |
| 🤝 Friends | Talking with people from other countries | "Where are you from?" |

Many people speak English as their **first language** (the language they learned as a baby). Even more people speak it as a **second** or **foreign** language. So when you speak English, you can talk with people from all over the world.

---

## 4. Different kinds of English

English is spoken in many countries: the UK, the USA, Canada, Australia, India, Nigeria, South Africa, Ireland, New Zealand and more.

People in different places:

- **sound** a little different (this is called an **accent**),
- sometimes **use different words**,
- sometimes **spell** a few words differently.

| British English 🇬🇧 | American English 🇺🇸 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| colour | color | 🎨 red, blue, green… |
| flat | apartment | a home inside a big building |
| holiday | vacation | time when you don't work or study |

> 💡 **Important:** None of these is "wrong". They are just different. In this course, we usually show the most common forms. When there is a difference, we tell you.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **first language** | the language you learned first, as a child |
| **foreign language** | a language from another country that you learn later |
| **accent** | the way a person's speech sounds, often because of where they are from |
| **spell** | to write the letters of a word in the correct order |

## 6. Useful sentences — explained slowly

### EX-002-001 — "I speak Spanish."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The person talking knows the Spanish language and can talk in it.
- **Situation:** Someone asks you about languages. You tell them which language you know.
- **Main lesson:** A sentence with **who + action + what**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | speak | Spanish. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who (the person talking) | the action | what (which language) |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — the person talking. Always a capital letter.
  - **speak** — the action. To *speak* means to talk using a language.
  - **Spanish** — the name of a language. Names of languages **always start with a capital letter** in English: *English, Spanish, Arabic, Hindi, Chinese.*
- **Remember from before 🔁:** In [Lesson 001](lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md) we saw "Tom eats an apple." It has the same order: **who → action → what.**
- **Why this is correct:** It follows the normal English order: **who** (I) → **action** (speak) → **what** (Spanish).
- **Other versions:**
  - I speak Arabic.
  - I speak Hindi and English. (**and** joins two things. More in [Lesson 054](../../01-beginner-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-054-and-but-or.md).)
  - I speak a little English. (**a little** = not much.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I speak spanish.* — Language names need a capital letter.
  - ❌ *I Spanish speak.* — The action must come before the "what".
  - ✅ **I speak Spanish.**
- **Pronunciation:** "speak" has a long **ee** sound: *speeek*.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I speak + language.**
- **Quick check:** Write which language you speak.

### EX-002-002 — "I am learning English."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Right now, in this part of my life, I am studying English. I do not know it fully yet.
- **Situation:** You meet someone. You want them to know that your English is still growing. Then they may speak more slowly. 😊
- **Main lesson:** A useful sentence to say about yourself.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | am | learning | English. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who | helping word | action | what |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — the person talking.
  - **am** — a **helping word** here. It helps the action word *learning*. Together, **am learning** means the action is happening **now, these days**.
  - **learning** — the action word *learn* + **-ing**. *Learn* means to get new knowledge.
  - **English** — the language. Capital **E**.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** In "I am Sam" (Lesson 001), **am** joined *I* and *Sam*. Here **am** does a slightly different job: it helps the action word. One word can have more than one job. You will learn this fully in [Lesson 056](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-056-present-continuous-positive.md). For now, just learn the whole sentence.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I learning English.* — The helping word **am** is missing.
  - ❌ *I am learn English.* — With **am**, the action word needs **-ing**: *learning*.
  - ✅ **I am learning English.**
- **In natural speech:** **"I'm learning English."** (*I'm* = *I am*.)
- **Pronunciation:** "learning" sounds like *LER-ning*.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I am learning + something.** → *I am learning Spanish. I am learning maths.*
- **Quick check:** Which word is missing in "I learning English"?

### EX-002-003 — "Please say that again."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I did not hear or understand. Please repeat it.
- **Situation:** Someone speaks fast. You did not understand. You ask them to say it one more time.
- **Main lesson:** Asking politely for help. This is one of the most useful sentences for a learner!
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Please | say | that | again. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | polite word | action | the thing (what they said) | one more time |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Please** — a word that makes a request polite (kind).
  - **say** — the action: to speak words.
  - **that** — here it means "the thing you just said".
  - **again** — one more time.
- **Why is there no "who" at the start?** This is an **instruction or request** (asking someone to do something). In English, requests often start with the action, because everyone knows who should do it: **you**, the listener. You will learn about this in [Lesson 052](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-052-imperatives.md).
- **Other polite versions:**
  - **Sorry?** (very short, very common)
  - **Can you say that again, please?** (a question — also very polite)
  - **Can you speak more slowly, please?**
- **Wrong version:**
  - ❌ *Again say please.* — Wrong order.
  - ✅ **Please say that again.**
- **Pronunciation:** "again" sounds like *a-GEN* (or *a-GAIN*). Both are correct.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Please + action + …** → *Please sit down. Please open the door.*
- **Quick check:** You did not understand. What can you say? (Give two ways.)

## 7. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I speak english. | I speak **E**nglish. | Language names start with a capital letter. |
| I learning English. | I **am** learning English. | You need the helping word **am**. |
| Say again. (rude-sounding) | **Please** say that again. / **Sorry?** | **Please** makes it polite. |

## 8. Speaking practice 🗣️

Say these out loud. Imagine you are meeting a new person.

> **A:** Hello!
> **B:** Hello! I'm Sam.
> **A:** I'm Ana. I speak Portuguese. I'm learning English.
> **B:** Me too!
> **A:** Sorry? Please say that again.
> **B:** Me too! I'm learning English too.

- **"Me too!"** = "The same is true for me."

Now say it again with **your** name and **your** language.

## 9. Exercises

## 10. Answers

**A.**
1. **am** — *I am Sam.* "am" joins I and a name.
2. **speak** — *I speak French.* We **speak** a language. "I am French" is also a real sentence, but it means you are **from France** (a different meaning).
3. **learning** — after **am**, the action word takes **-ing**.
4. **again** — "again" means one more time.

**B.**
1. *I speak **A**rabic.* — language names need a capital letter.
2. *I **am** learning English.* — the helping word **am** was missing.
3. ***I** am learning English.* — **I** is always a capital letter.

**C.** Your own answers. Check: capital letter at the start, capital **I**, capital letter for names and languages, full stop at the end.
