# Lesson 013 — The Object: Who or What Receives the Action

**ID:** L-013 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md), [Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)

⬅️ [Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md) · ➡️ [Lesson 014](lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)

---

## 1. What you will learn

- What an **object** is: the person or thing that **receives** the action.
- How to find the object with a question test.
- Some verbs **need** an object; some verbs **don't take** one.
- Object pronouns: **me, you, him, her, it, us, them** (first look).
- Why *My father is **a doctor*** has no object.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- **Subject** = who/what the sentence is about, usually the **doer**. It comes **before the verb** ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Verb** = the action or state — the **engine** ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
- In *Tom eats an apple* ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)), we said: **who → action → what**. Today, the "what" gets its name: the **object**.

---

## 3. What is an object?

> **The object is the person or thing that receives the action of the verb.**

Think of throwing a ball ⚾:

- **Who throws?** → the **subject** (the doer)
- **throws** → the **verb** (the action)
- **What gets thrown?** → the **object** (the receiver)

```
   SUBJECT   →   VERB    →   OBJECT
   Tom           eats        an apple.
   (doer)        (action)    (receiver)
```

| Sentence | Subject | Verb | Object |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Sara reads **books**.* | Sara | reads | books |
| *I love **you**.* | I | love | you |
| *We watch **TV**.* | We | watch | TV |
| *The dog wants **food**.* | The dog | wants | food |

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **object** | the person or thing that receives the action |
| **receiver** | the one who gets something, or gets the action |
| **object pronoun** | a short word used as an object: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them* |
| **transitive verb** | a verb that **takes** an object: *eat (something)*, *love (someone)* |
| **intransitive verb** | a verb that **does not take** an object: *sleep, laugh, arrive* |

(You do not need to remember *transitive* and *intransitive* now. Full lesson later: [Lesson 096](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-096-verbs-with-two-objects.md) and Level 3.)

## 4. How to find the object — the question test

**Step 1.** Find the **subject** and the **verb**.
**Step 2.** Ask: **Subject + verb + what?** or **Subject + verb + who(m)?**
**Step 3.** The answer is the **object**.

| Sentence | Question | Object |
|---|---|---|
| *My brother plays the guitar.* | My brother plays **what**? | **the guitar** |
| *Ana calls her mother every day.* | Ana calls **who**? | **her mother** |
| *They sell fresh bread.* | They sell **what**? | **fresh bread** |

> 💡 In formal English the question word for a person-object is **whom** (*Ana calls whom?*). In everyday English, people say **who**. Both are understood. More in [Level 5](../../CURRICULUM_MAP.md).

## 5. Not every sentence has an object

### Verbs that usually take an object

> eat (*an apple*) · read (*a book*) · love (*my family*) · want (*water*) · make (*a cake*) · buy (*shoes*) · have (*a car*)

*I want.* ❌ — We wait: "...want **what**?" → *I want **water**.* ✅

### Verbs that don't take an object

> sleep · laugh · arrive · cry · sit · go · come · die

*The baby sleeps.* ✅ — Complete! You cannot "sleep something".

### Verbs that can do both

> *I **eat**.* ✅ (no object — but often it sounds incomplete) · *I **eat** rice.* ✅
> *She **sings**.* ✅ · *She **sings** a song.* ✅
> *We **read** every evening.* ✅ · *We **read** stories every evening.* ✅

### After "be": no object

> *My father **is** a doctor.* — *a doctor* is **not** an object. Nothing "receives" an action. *Is* works like **=**: *my father = a doctor*.

This part after *be* is called a **complement** (it *completes* the meaning). You will learn the full name later ([Lesson 055](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-055-sentence-patterns.md)). For now: **no action → no receiver → no object.**

### Words after the object (where, when) are not objects

> *I eat rice **every day**.* — Object: *rice*. *Every day* tells **when**. It is not the object.
> *She lives **in Paris**.* — No object! *In Paris* tells **where**. (*Lives what?* doesn't work.)

## 6. What can be an object?

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| A noun | *I like **music**.* |
| A noun with helper words | *She has **a big black cat**.* |
| A name | *I know **Tom**.* |
| An object pronoun | *Tom knows **me**.* |
| Two things with *and* | *I like **tea and coffee**.* |

### Object pronouns (first look)

When the object is a pronoun, English uses a **different form** from the subject:

| Subject form | Object form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **me** | *Tom helps **me**.* |
| you | **you** | *I love **you**.* |
| he | **him** | *I know **him**.* |
| she | **her** | *We like **her**.* |
| it | **it** | *I want **it**.* |
| we | **us** | *They help **us**.* |
| they | **them** | *I see **them**.* |

> ❌ *Tom helps **I**.* · ❌ ***Me** help Tom.*
> ✅ ***I** help Tom.* (subject) · ✅ *Tom helps **me**.* (object)

Full lesson: [Lesson 028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md).

## 7. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-013-001 — "Sara reads books."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Sara often reads books. It is something she does (maybe a hobby). 📚
- **Situation:** Someone asks: "What does Sara like doing in her free time?"
- **Main lesson:** The basic pattern **subject → verb → object**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Sara | reads | books. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **subject** (doer) | **verb** (action) | **object** (receiver) |

- **Find each part:**
  - Verb: **reads** (action; *they read* ✅).
  - Subject: *Who reads?* → **Sara**.
  - Object: *Sara reads what?* → **books**.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Sara** — a name; one person → verb gets **-s**: *read**s***.
  - **reads** — action verb. **ea** here says /iː/: "reedz" ([Lesson 006](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)).
  - **books** — more than one book. No *a* needed for "books in general".
- **Remember from before 🔁:** One person subject → **-s** on the verb ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md), *Maria sings*).
- **Swap subject and object:** ❌ *Books read Sara.* — Now the books are reading Sara! 😄 The meaning breaks. **Position decides the job.**
- **Other versions:**
  - Sara reads **a book**. (one book)
  - Sara reads **the newspaper**.
  - Sara reads **them**. (pronoun object)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Name + verb-s + object.** → *Ali drinks coffee. Mia plays tennis.*
- **Quick check:** Find the object: *Ben drives a taxi.*

---

### EX-013-002 — "Tom helps me."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Tom gives help to me. 🤝
- **Situation:** You are new at work. Someone asks: "Who helps you with the computer?" You answer.
- **Main lesson:** An **object pronoun** (*me*).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Tom | helps | me. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **subject** | **verb** | **object** (pronoun) |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Tom** — subject, the doer.
  - **helps** — *help* + **-s** (one person).
  - **me** — the **object form** of **I**. I am the receiver of the help.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *My brother and **I** play chess* ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)) — there **I** was in the **subject** place. Here the person-talking is in the **object** place → **me**.
- **Turn it around:**
  - ***I** help Tom.* — now I am the doer → **I**.
  - *Tom helps **me**.* — I am the receiver → **me**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Tom helps I.* — **I** is only for the subject.
  - ❌ *Tom me helps.* — the object goes **after** the verb in English. (In some languages it goes before. Not in English!)
  - ✅ **Tom helps me.**
- **Other versions:** *Tom helps **us**. / Tom helps **her**. / Tom helps **them**.*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + verb-s + me/him/her/us/them.**
- **Quick check:** Choose: *My mother loves (I / me).*

### EX-013-003 — "My mother makes delicious cakes for my birthday."

- **Meaning in simple English:** On my birthday, my mother bakes (makes) cakes that taste very good. 🎂
- **Situation:** You are telling a friend about your birthday traditions.
- **Main lesson:** A **long object**, and extra information after it that is **not** the object.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My mother | makes | delicious cakes | for my birthday. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **subject** | **verb** | **object** | extra (why / for what) |

- **Find each part:**
  - Verb: **makes**.
  - Subject: *Who makes?* → **My mother**.
  - Object: *My mother makes what?* → **delicious cakes**.
  - *for my birthday* — tells **for what occasion**. It is **not** the object. (Ask: *makes what?* → not "for my birthday".)
- **Word by word:**
  - **makes** — *make* + **-s**. *Make* = create, build, cook something new.
  - **delicious** — describing word: tastes very good 😋. It comes **before** *cakes* ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
  - **cakes** — main word of the object.
  - **for** — shows the reason or occasion.
  - **my birthday** — the day you were born, each year.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Describing words never take -s: *delicious cakes* not ~~deliciouses~~ ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My mother makes for my birthday delicious cakes.* — In English, the object usually comes **right after** the verb. Put the extra information **after** the object.
  - ❌ *My mother make delicious cakes…* — one person → **makes**.
  - ✅ **My mother makes delicious cakes for my birthday.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Subject + verb + object + for + occasion.** → *My father buys flowers for my mother's birthday.*
- **Quick check:** What is the object in *We cook a big dinner for the family*?

---

### EX-013-004 — "The baby sleeps in the afternoon."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Every afternoon, the baby sleeps. 👶😴
- **Situation:** A babysitter asks about the baby's day.
- **Main lesson:** A sentence **with no object**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The baby | sleeps | in the afternoon. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **subject** | **verb** | when (not an object) |

- **Is there an object?** Ask: *The baby sleeps **what**?* — That question doesn't make sense. You cannot "sleep something". So there is **no object**.
- **Word by word:**
  - **sleeps** — a verb that **does not take an object**.
  - **in the afternoon** — tells **when**. The *afternoon* is the time after 12:00 and before the evening.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** A sentence only needs **subject + verb** ([Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)). *The baby sleeps* is already complete.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The baby sleeps the afternoon.* — needs **in**: *in the afternoon*.
  - ❌ *The baby sleep in the afternoon.* — one baby → **sleeps**.
  - ✅ **The baby sleeps in the afternoon.**
- **Other no-object sentences:** *The children laugh.* ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)) · *The bus arrives at 8.* · *She cries.*
- **Quick check:** Which sentence has an object? (a) *He runs every morning.* (b) *He runs a small shop.* *(Hint: here **run a shop** = manage a business!)*

## 8. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I want. | I want **water**. | *want* needs an object. |
| Tom helps I. | Tom helps **me**. | Object form of I = me. |
| I him know. | I know **him**. | Object goes **after** the verb. |
| I like very much music. | I like music **very much**. | Don't put words between the verb and its object. |
| She plays every day the piano. | She plays the piano every day. | Object right after the verb; time after. |
| I sleep the bed. | I sleep **in** the bed. | *sleep* has no object; use *in*. |

> 💡 **Golden rule:** In English, **don't separate the verb from its object.** Put time and place **after** the object.

## 9. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Object pronouns are usually **weak** (said quickly and softly). The **verb** is strong:

> *Tom **HELPS** me.* · *I **LOVE** you.* · *I **KNOW** him* → often sounds like "I **KNOW**-im" (the **h** in *him* almost disappears).

## 10. Exercises

**A. Find the subject (S), verb (V) and object (O).**

1. The cat drinks milk.
2. My friends play basketball.
3. Lisa loves her grandmother.
4. We need a new car.

**B. Object or no object? If there is one, write it.**

1. The children laugh.
2. I drink tea every morning.
3. She lives in Spain.
4. My uncle sells cars.

**C. Choose the correct pronoun.**

1. My teacher helps (we / us).
2. (She / Her) likes (they / them).
3. I know (he / him).

**D. Correct the word order.**

1. I like very much chocolate.
2. He reads every evening the newspaper.
3. Ana her mother calls.

## 11. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **S** The cat · **V** drinks · **O** milk
2. **S** My friends · **V** play · **O** basketball
3. **S** Lisa · **V** loves · **O** her grandmother
4. **S** We · **V** need · **O** a new car

**B.**
1. **No object.** *Laugh what?* ✗
2. **Object: tea.** *Every morning* = when.
3. **No object.** *In Spain* = where. (*Lives what?* ✗)
4. **Object: cars.**

**C.**
1. **us** — object place.
2. **She** (subject) … **them** (object).
3. **him** — object place.

**D.**
1. *I like chocolate **very much**.* — don't separate verb and object.
2. *He reads **the newspaper** every evening.* — object first, then time.
3. *Ana **calls her mother**.* — the verb comes before the object in English.

## 12. Quick revision sheet

- **Object** = the receiver of the action.
- Find it: **Subject + verb + what/who?**
- Order: **Subject → Verb → Object** (S-V-O).
- Some verbs need an object (*want, make*); some don't take one (*sleep, laugh, arrive*).
- After **be** (*is, am, are*) there is **no object** — it's a complement.
- Object pronouns: **me, you, him, her, it, us, them**.
- Don't put time/place words between the verb and the object.
