# Lesson 015 — What Makes a Question?

## 1. What you will learn

- The two main kinds of questions: **yes/no questions** and **question-word questions**.
- **Three ways** English makes questions:
  1. Move **am / is / are** before the subject.
  2. Move a **helping verb** (*can, will…*) before the subject.
  3. Add **do / does** (when there is no helping verb).
- **Short answers**: *Yes, I am. / No, I can't.*
- How your **voice** changes in questions.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- A **question** asks for information and ends with **?** ([Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md), [Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
- **Helping verbs** (*am, is, are, can, will, do*) help the main verb ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
- In statements, the order is **S – V – O** ([Lesson 014](lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)).
- You already know some questions: *What's your name?* · *How do you spell that?* ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)) · *Where is the station?* ([Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)) · *Are you ready?* ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).

Today you learn **the system** behind all of them.

## 3. Two kinds of questions

| Kind | The answer is… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes/no question** | *yes* or *no* | *Are you hungry?* → *Yes.* / *No.* |
| **Question-word question** (wh-question) | information | *Where do you live?* → *In Cairo.* |

### The question words

| Question word | Asks about | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | a thing, information | *What is this?* |
| **Who** | a person | *Who is that man?* |
| **Where** | a place | *Where is the bank?* |
| **When** | a time | *When is the party?* |
| **Why** | a reason | *Why are you sad?* |
| **How** | the way / condition | *How are you?* |
| **Which** | a choice | *Which bag is yours?* |
| **Whose** | owner | *Whose phone is this?* |

Most start with **wh-**, so they are called **wh-questions** (even *how*!). Full lessons: [Lesson 047](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-047-question-words-what-who-where.md), [Lesson 048](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-048-question-words-when-why-how-which-whose.md).

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **yes/no question** | a question you can answer with *yes* or *no* |
| **wh-question** | a question that starts with a question word (*what, where…*) |
| **short answer** | a short reply: *Yes, I am.* / *No, she isn't.* |
| **inversion** | swapping the subject and the verb: *You are* → *Are you?* |
| **intonation** | how your voice goes up ↗ and down ↘ |

## 4. How to make a yes/no question — 3 ways

### Way 1 — The verb is am / is / are → move it before the subject

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| *You **are** ready.* | ***Are** you ready?* |
| *She **is** a teacher.* | ***Is** she a teacher?* |
| *The shop **is** open.* | ***Is** the shop open?* |
| *I **am** late.* | ***Am** I late?* |

This swap is called **inversion**.

### Way 2 — There is a helping verb (can, will, am + -ing…) → move the helping verb

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| *You **can** swim.* | ***Can** you swim?* |
| *He **will** come.* | ***Will** he come?* |
| *They **are** playing.* | ***Are** they playing?* |

Only the **helping verb** moves. The **main verb stays** after the subject.

### Way 3 — No *be*, no helping verb → add **do** or **does**

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| *You **like** coffee.* | ***Do** you **like** coffee?* |
| *They **live** here.* | ***Do** they **live** here?* |
| *She **likes** coffee.* | ***Does** she **like** coffee?* |

- Use **does** for **he / she / it / one person** (like *Maria, my father*).
- Use **do** for **I / you / we / they / more than one**.
- After **does**, the main verb **loses its -s**: *She like**s*** → *Does she **like**?*

Here **do/does** has no meaning by itself. It's a **helper** that makes the question. Remember *How **do** you spell that?* ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)). (Full lesson: [Lesson 045](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-045-present-simple-questions.md).)

### Decision chart

```
Is the verb am/is/are?  ── yes ──▶  Move it to the front.        Are you ready?
        │ no
Is there a helping verb (can, will…)?  ── yes ──▶  Move the helper.   Can you swim?
        │ no
Add do / does at the front.                                     Do you like tea?
```

## 5. How to make a wh-question

**Put the question word at the front**, then use the **same order as a yes/no question**:

| Yes/no question | Wh-question |
|---|---|
| *Is the station near?* | ***Where** is the station?* |
| *Can you come?* | ***When** can you come?* |
| *Do you live in Rome?* | ***Where** do you live?* |
| *Does she work here?* | ***Why** does she work here?* |

**Pattern:** **Question word + (helping) verb + subject + (main verb) …?**

> ⚠️ **Special case (later):** when the question word **is** the subject, there is no *do*: ***Who** lives here?* (not ~~Who does live here?~~). Full lesson: [Lesson 086](../../01-beginner-grammar/basic-sentences/lesson-086-subject-and-object-questions.md).

## 6. Short answers

English speakers often answer yes/no questions with a **short answer**: *Yes/No + subject + the same helping verb*.

| Question | Yes | No |
|---|---|---|
| *Are you ready?* | Yes, I **am**. | No, I'**m not**. |
| *Is she a teacher?* | Yes, she **is**. | No, she **isn't**. |
| *Can you swim?* | Yes, I **can**. | No, I **can't**. |
| *Do you like coffee?* | Yes, I **do**. | No, I **don't**. |
| *Does he live here?* | Yes, he **does**. | No, he **doesn't**. |

- *isn't* = is not · *can't* = cannot · *don't* = do not · *doesn't* = does not ([contractions, Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- Just *Yes.* or *No.* is also okay, but it can sound a little short or cold. The short answer sounds more natural and polite.
- ⚠️ Don't contract a positive short answer: ❌ *Yes, I'm.* → ✅ *Yes, I am.*

---

## 7. Your voice in questions (intonation)

| Question | Voice at the end |
|---|---|
| Yes/no question | usually goes **up** ↗ — *Are you **rea**dy?* ↗ |
| Wh-question | usually goes **down** ↘ — *Where do you **live**?* ↘ |

**In casual speech**, people sometimes make a question just with the voice: *You're **ready**?* ↗ — this is a statement with question intonation. It often shows surprise, or checks something. In writing (especially formal), use the normal question order.

## 8. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-015-001 — "Is she a teacher?" — "Yes, she is."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I want to know: is her job teaching? — Yes, it is.
- **Situation:** You see a woman with many books outside a school. You ask your friend about her.
- **Main lesson:** Way 1 — **is** moves before the subject.
- **Statement → question:**

  | | 1 | 2 | 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Statement | She | **is** | a teacher. |
  | Question | **Is** | she | a teacher? |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Is** — the verb *be*. It moves to the **front**. Capital **I** — first word.
  - **she** — the subject. Now small **s**, because it's not the first word.
  - **a teacher** — one job → **a** ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
  - **?** — question mark.
- **Short answer:** *Yes, she **is**.* / *No, she **isn't**.* — We repeat **is**, not the whole sentence.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *Are you ready?* ([Lesson 010](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)) works the same way.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *She is a teacher?* — possible in speech with rising voice (surprise), but not the normal question.
  - ❌ *Is a teacher she?* — the subject goes right after **is**.
  - ❌ *Does she is a teacher?* — with **be**, don't add *do/does*.
  - ✅ **Is she a teacher?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Is + he/she/it/name + …?** → *Is Tom at home? Is it cold?*
- **Quick check:** Make a question: *He is your brother.*

---

### EX-015-002 — "Can you help me, please?" — "Yes, of course."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I'm asking politely if you are able and willing to help me. — Yes, I'm happy to help.
- **Situation:** Your bag is very heavy. You ask a person near you.
- **Main lesson:** Way 2 — move the **helping verb** (*can*).
- **Statement → question:**

  | | Helper | Subject | Main verb | Object |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Statement | | You | **can** help | me. |
  | Question | **Can** | you | help | me, please? |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Can** — helping verb. It moves to the front. In a question like this, *Can you…?* is a **polite request** (asking someone to do something).
  - **you** — subject.
  - **help** — main verb. It **stays** after the subject. No -ing, no -s after *can*.
  - **me** — object pronoun ([Lesson 013](lesson-013-the-object.md)).
  - **please** — politeness. Comma before it at the end.
- **Answers:**
  - *Yes, of course.* / *Sure!* (very natural for requests)
  - *Yes, I can.* (more about ability)
  - *Sorry, I can't.* (refusing politely)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *Can you say that again, please?* ([Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Do you can help me?* — **can** is already a helper; don't add *do*.
  - ❌ *Can you to help me?* — no **to** after *can*.
  - ❌ *Can you helps me?* — no **-s** after *can*.
  - ✅ **Can you help me, please?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Can you + verb + …, please?** → *Can you open the door, please?*
- **Quick check:** Make a question: *You can speak French.*

---

### EX-015-003 — "Does your brother like football?" — "No, he doesn't."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I want to know if your brother enjoys football. — No, he doesn't enjoy it.
- **Situation:** You have two tickets for a football match. You want to invite your friend's brother.
- **Main lesson:** Way 3 — add **does**; the main verb loses **-s**.
- **Statement → question:**

  | | Helper | Subject | Main verb | Object |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Statement | | Your brother | like**s** | football. |
  | Question | **Does** | your brother | **like** | football? |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Does** — added helper, because *likes* is not *be* and there is no other helper. We use **does** (not *do*) because *your brother* = **he** (one person).
  - **your brother** — subject.
  - **like** — main verb, **without -s**. The **-s** has moved into **does** (do + **es**)! Only one of them gets the -s.
  - **football** — object.
- **Short answer:** *No, he **doesn't**.* — we change *your brother* to **he** ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)) and repeat the helper (*does* → *doesn't*).
- **Remember from before 🔁:** One person → **-s** on the verb ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)). In questions, that -s goes to **does**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Does your brother likes football?* — **two** -s endings. Only *does* has it.
  - ❌ *Do your brother like football?* — one person → **does**.
  - ❌ *Likes your brother football?* — in modern English, normal verbs don't move to the front.
  - ✅ **Does your brother like football?**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Does + he/she/name + verb (no -s) + …?**
- **Quick check:** Make a question: *Ana speaks Spanish.*

---

### EX-015-004 — "Where do you live?" — "I live in Lagos."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Tell me the place where your home is. — My home is in the city of Lagos.
- **Situation:** You meet someone new at a language class.
- **Main lesson:** A **wh-question**: question word + **do** + subject + main verb.
- **Build it step by step:**
  1. Statement: *You live in Lagos.*
  2. Yes/no question (Way 3): *Do you live in Lagos?*
  3. Replace the place with **Where** and put it at the front: ***Where** do you live?*
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Where | do | you | live | ? |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | question word | helper | subject | main verb | |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Where** — asks about place.
  - **do** — helper for *you* (not *does*).
  - **you** — subject.
  - **live** — main verb.
- **The answer:** *I live in Lagos.* (full) or just *In Lagos.* (short, natural in conversation — [Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)). You can't answer *yes/no* to a wh-question.
- **Voice:** goes **down** at the end ↘.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Where you live?* — **do** is missing. (Common in fast casual speech, but not correct.)
  - ❌ *Where do you live in?* — *in* is not needed here.
  - ❌ *Where does you live?* — *you* → **do**.
  - ✅ **Where do you live?**
- **In natural speech:** *Where d'you live?* — *do you* becomes short "d'you".
- **Change it:** *Where **does** she live?* · *What **do** you study?* · *When **do** they work?*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Wh-word + do/does + subject + verb?**
- **Quick check:** Ask where Tom works.

## 9. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You are ready? | Are you ready? | Move **are** before the subject. |
| Do you are happy? | Are you happy? | With *be*, no *do*. |
| Do you can swim? | Can you swim? | *can* is already a helper. |
| Does she likes tea? | Does she **like** tea? | Only *does* takes the -s. |
| Where you live? | Where **do** you live? | Add *do*. |
| What is your name. | What is your name**?** | Question mark. |
| Yes, I'm. | Yes, I **am**. | No contraction in positive short answers. |

## 10. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

1. Yes/no questions go **up** ↗: *Is she a **TEACH**er?* ↗ · *Can you **HELP** me?* ↗
2. Wh-questions go **down** ↘: *Where do you **LIVE**?* ↘
3. Weak forms in fast speech:
   - *Do you* → "d'you" / "dyuh"
   - *Does he* → "duzzy"
   - *Can you* → "k'n you"

## 11. Exercises

**A. Make yes/no questions.**

1. You are tired.
2. He can drive.
3. They live near the school.
4. She works in a hospital.
5. It is cold.

**B. Short answers.**

1. Are you a student? — Yes, ___.
2. Can she swim? — No, ___.
3. Do they like pizza? — Yes, ___.
4. Does he speak English? — No, ___.

**C. Make wh-questions.**

1. (Where) you / work
2. (What) she / study
3. (When) the bus / is (← be!)

**D. Correct the mistakes.**

1. Does Tom plays tennis?
2. Do you can come tomorrow?
3. Where she lives?
4. Is your sister a nurse. 

## 12. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. ***Are you tired?*** — Way 1 (be).
2. ***Can he drive?*** — Way 2 (helper).
3. ***Do they live near the school?*** — Way 3; *they* → **do**.
4. ***Does she work in a hospital?*** — Way 3; *she* → **does**; *works* → **work**.
5. ***Is it cold?*** — Way 1.

**B.**
1. Yes, **I am**. (not *I'm*)
2. No, **she can't**.
3. Yes, **they do**.
4. No, **he doesn't**.

**C.**
1. ***Where do you work?***
2. ***What does she study?*** — *she* → does.
3. ***When is the bus?*** — with **be**, no *do*.

**D.**
1. *Does Tom **play** tennis?* — only *does* has -s.
2. ***Can you** come tomorrow?* — no *do* with *can*.
3. *Where **does she live**?* — add *does*; *lives* → *live*.
4. *Is your sister a nurse**?*** — question mark.

## 13. Quick revision sheet

- **Yes/no questions** → answer *yes/no*. **Wh-questions** → answer with information.
- **Way 1:** *be* → move it: *Are you…?*
- **Way 2:** helper → move it: *Can you…?*
- **Way 3:** no helper → add **do/does**: *Do you…? Does she…?* (main verb without -s)
- **Wh-question:** wh-word + the yes/no question order: *Where do you live?*
- **Short answers:** *Yes, I am. / No, she doesn't.*
- Voice: yes/no ↗ · wh ↘

## 14. Memory trick

> **"Be or a helper? Jump to the front! Nothing to jump? Call DO for help!"** 🦘
