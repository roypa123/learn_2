# Lesson 014 — Why Word Order Matters (Subject–Verb–Object)

## 1. What you will learn

- Why English uses **word order** to show meaning.
- The basic English order: **S – V – O** (Subject – Verb – Object).
- The longer order: **S – V – O – Place – Time**.
- Which parts can **move** (time words) and which parts **cannot** (subject, verb, object).
- How moving one small word (*only*) can change the meaning.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- **Subject** = the doer, before the verb ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Verb** = the action/state, the engine ([Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
- **Object** = the receiver, right after the verb ([Lesson 013](lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- *The dog sees the cat* ≠ *The cat sees the dog* ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)).

---

## 3. Why is order so important in English?

In some languages, words change their **endings** to show who is the doer and who is the receiver. Then you can move the words around, and the meaning stays the same.

**English nouns don't do this.** The word *dog* looks the same when it is the doer and when it is the receiver. So how do we know who does what?

> **In English, the POSITION of a word shows its job.**

```
Position 1           Position 2        Position 3
(before the verb)    (the verb)        (after the verb)
SUBJECT = doer       VERB              OBJECT = receiver
```

| | Subject | Verb | Object | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | The dog | sees | the cat. | 🐶 looks at 🐱 |
| B | The cat | sees | the dog. | 🐱 looks at 🐶 |

Same words. Different positions. **Different meaning.**

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **word order** | the order of words in a sentence |
| **position** | the place of a word in a sentence (1st, 2nd, 3rd…) |
| **S-V-O** | Subject – Verb – Object, the basic English order |
| **adverbial** | a word or group that tells **where, when, how** (*at home, every day, slowly*). Full lessons on adverbs: [Lesson 051](../../01-beginner-grammar/adverbs/lesson-051-adverbs-of-frequency.md) onward. |

---

## 4. English compared with other languages

Languages put the parts in different orders:

| Order | Example languages (simplified) | Like saying… |
|---|---|---|
| **S-V-O** | English, Spanish, Chinese, French | *I eat rice.* |
| **S-O-V** | Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Turkish, Persian | *I rice eat.* |
| **V-S-O** | Classical Arabic, Welsh, Irish | *Eat I rice.* |

If your language is S-O-V or V-S-O, be careful: your brain may want to use that order in English. **Practise the English order until it feels natural.**

> 💡 Many languages are more flexible than this table shows. That's fine — the point is: **English is strict.**

## 5. The longer order: S – V – O – Place – Time

Many sentences also say **where** and **when**. The normal order is:

| Subject | Verb | Object | Place (where?) | Time (when?) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | meet | my friends | at the café | on Saturdays. |
| She | does | her homework | in her room | every evening. |
| We | play | football | in the park | after school. |

**Memory tip:** **Place before Time** → think "**P**lace comes before **T**ime, like **P** before **T** in the alphabet." 🔤

### What can move?

- **Time** can move to the **front** of the sentence (often with a comma):
  > ***On Saturdays**, I meet my friends at the café.* ✅
- **Subject, verb and object** **stay together** in their order:
  > ❌ *I meet at the café my friends.* (place between verb and object)
  > ❌ *My friends I meet.* (object before subject)

Remember the golden rule from [Lesson 013](lesson-013-the-object.md): **don't separate the verb from its object.**

## 7. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-014-001 — "Lions chase zebras." / "Zebras chase lions."

- **Meaning in simple English:**
  - Sentence 1: Lions run after zebras (to catch them). 🦁➡️🦓 — this happens in nature.
  - Sentence 2: Zebras run after lions. 🦓➡️🦁 — this is very strange! It doesn't happen.
- **Situation:** A nature film about Africa.
- **Main lesson:** Swapping subject and object **changes who does the action**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | | Subject (doer) | Verb | Object (receiver) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Lions | chase | zebras. |
  | 2 | Zebras | chase | lions. |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Lions / Zebras** — animals; **-s** = more than one.
  - **chase** — run after someone or something to catch it. **No -s** because the subjects are plural.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Subject before verb ([Lesson 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)); object after verb ([Lesson 013](lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- **Why this matters:** The words are exactly the same. **Only the order** tells you the meaning. If you put words in the wrong place, people may understand the **opposite** of what you mean.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Doer + verb + receiver.**
- **Quick check:** In *The teacher asks the students*, who asks?

---

### EX-014-002 — "I meet my friends at the café on Saturdays."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Every Saturday, I see my friends. We go to a café (a small place where people drink coffee and eat cake). ☕
- **Situation:** A new classmate asks what you do at weekends.
- **Main lesson:** The full order **S – V – O – Place – Time**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | meet | my friends | at the café | on Saturdays. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **S** | **V** | **O** | **Place** | **Time** |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — subject.
  - **meet** — verb: come together with someone.
  - **my friends** — object (*I meet who?* → my friends).
  - **at the café** — place. **at** is used for a point or a particular building/place ([Lesson 049](../../01-beginner-grammar/prepositions/lesson-049-prepositions-of-place-in-on-under.md)). The **é** in *café* is from French; in English it is often written *cafe* too.
  - **on Saturdays** — time. We use **on** with days. **Saturdays** with **-s** = every Saturday. (Full lesson: [Lesson 050](../../01-beginner-grammar/prepositions/lesson-050-prepositions-of-time-at-on-in.md).)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Capital letter for days: **S**aturday ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I meet at the café my friends on Saturdays.* — place between verb and object.
  - ❌ *I meet my friends on Saturdays at the café.* — understandable, and people do say it, but the more usual order is **place before time**.
  - ❌ *I my friends meet at the café.* — object before the verb.
  - ✅ **I meet my friends at the café on Saturdays.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **S + V + O + at/in + place + on/every + time.**
  → *She does yoga at the gym every morning.*
- **Quick check:** Put in order: *every day / English / at school / We / study*

---

### EX-014-003 — "On Saturdays, I meet my friends at the café."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Same meaning as EX-014-002.
- **Situation:** Same — but now you want to **focus on the time**. For example, someone said: "What do you do on Saturdays?"
- **Main lesson:** **Time** can move to the **front**. S-V-O stays together.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | On Saturdays, | I | meet | my friends | at the café. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **Time** (moved) | **S** | **V** | **O** | **Place** |

- **What changed?** Only the **time** part moved. **S-V-O did not change.**
- **The comma:** When time goes at the front, we often put a **comma** after it. With a short time word, the comma is optional: *Today I am tired.* / *Today, I am tired.* Both are fine.
- **Capital letter:** The sentence now starts with **O**n.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *On Saturdays meet I my friends at the café.* — in English (unlike German or Dutch), the subject does **not** move after the verb here.
  - ❌ *At the café, I meet my friends on Saturdays.* — not wrong, but it moves the focus to the place; less common.
  - ✅ **On Saturdays, I meet my friends at the café.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Time, + S + V + O + place.** → *Every morning, I drink coffee at home.*
- **Quick check:** Move the time to the front: *We play football in the park after school.*

### EX-014-004 — "Only Ana speaks French." / "Ana speaks only French."

- **Meaning in simple English:**
  1. *Only Ana speaks French.* — Ana is the **one person** (in this group) who can speak French. Nobody else can. 🙋‍♀️
  2. *Ana speaks only French.* — French is the **one language** Ana speaks. She doesn't speak any other language. 🇫🇷
- **Situation:** A group of travellers is talking about languages.
- **Main lesson:** Moving **one small word** can change the meaning. The word **only** affects the word **next to it**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | | Subject | Verb | Object |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | **Only** Ana | speaks | French. |
  | 2 | Ana | speaks | **only** French. |

- **Word by word:**
  - **only** — "no one / nothing else". It goes just **before** the word it is about.
  - **speaks** — *speak* + **-s** (Ana = one person).
  - **French** — a language → capital **F** ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Which word is "only" about?**
  - (1) *only* + **Ana** → limits the **person**.
  - (2) *only* + **French** → limits the **language**.
- **In natural speech:** People often say *Ana **only speaks** French*, with *only* before the verb, to mean (2). In speech, the **stress** (strong word) makes the meaning clear: *Ana only speaks **FRENCH***. In careful writing, put *only* right next to the word it is about.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Only + the word you want to limit.**
- **Quick check:** What's the difference? *Only I eat fish.* / *I eat only fish.*

## 8. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I rice eat. | I eat rice. | English is S-V-O. |
| I like very much football. | I like football very much. | Don't split verb and object. |
| She plays every day the piano. | She plays the piano every day. | Object, then time. |
| Yesterday went I to the market. | Yesterday I went to the market. | Subject stays before the verb. |
| a car red | a red car | Describing word before the noun. |

## 9. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

In S-V-O sentences, the **verb** and the **object** usually get the strongest stress:

> *I **MEET** my **FRIENDS** at the ca**FÉ** on **SAT**urdays.*

When **time** comes first, make a small pause at the comma:

> *On **SAT**urdays, | I **MEET** my **FRIENDS**…*

## 10. Exercises

**A. Put the words in the correct order.**

1. coffee / drinks / my father
2. the guitar / plays / Leo / every evening
3. in the library / study / we / on Mondays
4. a / dog / have / small / I

**B. Who does the action?**

1. The girl helps the boy. → Who helps?
2. The police catch the thief. → Who catches?

**C. Correct the word order.**

1. I watch every night TV.
2. My sister the dishes washes.
3. They play in the garden football.

**D. Move the time to the front.**

1. I go swimming every Sunday.
2. We visit our grandparents in summer.

**E. Explain the difference.**

1. *Only Tom drinks milk.* / *Tom drinks only milk.*

## 11. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. ***My father drinks coffee.*** — S-V-O.
2. ***Leo plays the guitar every evening.*** — S-V-O-Time.
3. ***We study in the library on Mondays.*** — S-V-Place-Time (no object here: *study* without an object).
4. ***I have a small dog.*** — S-V-O; describing word before noun.

**B.**
1. **The girl** — she is before the verb.
2. **The police** — before the verb. (*the thief* = a person who steals; here the receiver.)

**C.**
1. *I watch **TV every night**.* — object right after the verb.
2. *My sister **washes the dishes**.* — S-V-O.
3. *They play **football in the garden**.* — object first, then place.

**D.**
1. ***Every Sunday**, I go swimming.*
2. ***In summer**, we visit our grandparents.* — note the small **w** in *we* now (not the first word any more).

**E.** *Only Tom drinks milk* → Tom is the one person who drinks milk; others don't. *Tom drinks only milk* → milk is the one drink Tom has; he doesn't drink anything else.

## 12. Quick revision sheet

- In English, **position = job**.
- Basic order: **S – V – O**.
- Longer order: **S – V – O – Place – Time**.
- **Time** can move to the front: *On Saturdays, I…*
- **Never** split the verb and object.
- Small words like **only** change meaning depending on where they go.
