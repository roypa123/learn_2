# Lesson 009 — What Is a Sentence?

**ID:** L-009 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md), [Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)

⬅️ [Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md) · ➡️ [Lesson 010](lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)

## 1. What you will learn

- What a **sentence** is: a **complete idea**.
- The two things almost every sentence needs: **who/what** + **verb**.
- How to tell a **complete sentence** from an **incomplete group of words**.
- The **4 kinds of sentences**: telling, asking, instructing, and showing strong feeling.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- **Words** are small pieces of meaning ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- **Thing words** = **nouns** (*child, school*). **Action words** = **verbs** (*run, eat, is*). **Describing words** = **adjectives** (*happy, big*) ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **Am, is, are** are verbs too — they are "being" words, not busy actions ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).

---

## 3. What is a sentence?

> **A sentence is a group of words that gives a complete idea.**

"Complete" means: when you hear it, you **understand the full message**. You do not wait for more. You do not think "...and? what about it?"

Compare:

| Words | Complete? | What do you think when you hear it? |
|---|---|---|
| *The big red bus.* | ❌ No | "The big red bus... what about it? What happened?" |
| *The big red bus is late.* | ✅ Yes | "Ah, okay. I understand." |
| *Every morning.* | ❌ No | "Every morning... what?" |
| *I run every morning.* | ✅ Yes | "Okay, I understand." |

An incomplete group of words is not a full sentence. In grammar, when someone **writes** it as if it were a sentence, it is called a **fragment** (a broken piece). You will learn more in [Lesson 147](../../02-intermediate-grammar/sentence-types/lesson-147-sentence-boundaries-and-fragments.md).

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **sentence** | a group of words with a complete idea |
| **complete** | full; nothing important is missing |
| **fragment** | a piece of a sentence written alone; it is not complete |
| **statement** | a sentence that tells something |
| **question** | a sentence that asks something |
| **instruction** (command) | a sentence that tells someone to do something |
| **exclamation** | a sentence that shows strong feeling |

---

## 4. What does a sentence need?

Almost every English sentence needs **two parts**:

| Part 1: **Who or what?** | Part 2: **What does it do? / What is it?** |
|---|---|
| *Birds* | *fly.* |
| *The bus* | *is late.* |
| *My sister* | *plays football.* |
| *I* | *know the answer.* |

- **Part 1** is the person or thing the sentence is **about**. In grammar, this is called the **subject**. (Full lesson: [Lesson 011](lesson-011-the-subject.md).)
- **Part 2** must have a **verb** (an action word or a being word like *is*). (Full lesson: [Lesson 012](lesson-012-the-verb.md).)

### Simple rule

> **No verb → usually not a sentence.**

| ❌ No verb | ✅ With a verb |
|---|---|
| *My brother a doctor.* | *My brother **is** a doctor.* |
| *The children in the park.* | *The children **are** in the park.* / *The children **play** in the park.* |
| *A very good idea.* | *That **is** a very good idea.* |

> ⚠️ **In conversation**, people often use short answers without a verb: *"Where are you going?" — "Home."* This is normal in speaking because the question gives the missing part. But in careful **writing**, use complete sentences.

## 5. The 4 kinds of sentences

| Kind | Job | Ends with | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Statement** (telling) | gives information | **.** | *The shop is open.* |
| 2. **Question** (asking) | asks for information | **?** | *Is the shop open?* |
| 3. **Instruction** (command or request) | tells someone to do something | **.** (or **!**) | *Open the door, please.* |
| 4. **Exclamation** (strong feeling) | shows surprise, happiness, anger… | **!** | *What a beautiful day!* |

- **Statements** are the most common kind.
- In **instructions**, the "who" (*you*) is usually not said — but everyone understands it. *Open the door* = *(You) open the door.* That is why it is still complete. Remember *Please say that again* ([Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)).
- **Questions** often change the word order: *The shop is open.* → ***Is** the shop open?* More in [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md).

## 6. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-009-001 — "The big red bus is late."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The bus did not come at the correct time. It is coming after the time it should. 🚌⏰
- **Situation:** You are waiting at the bus stop with a friend. The bus should be here at 8:00. It is 8:15. You say this.
- **Main lesson:** A group of words becomes a **complete sentence** when we add the verb part.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The big red bus | is | late. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **who/what** (subject) | **verb** (being word) | describing word |

- **Word by word:**
  - **The** — helper word, pointing to the bus we are waiting for.
  - **big**, **red** — two describing words (adjectives). They come **before** the thing word ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
  - **bus** — thing word (noun).
  - **is** — the verb. It joins *the bus* and *late* (like "="). **This word makes the sentence complete.**
  - **late** — describing word: after the correct time.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Two describing words can go together: *big red bus*. Usually the **size** word comes before the **colour** word. (You will learn the full order of describing words much later.)
- **Fragment vs sentence:**
  - ❌ *The big red bus.* — only Part 1 (who/what). No verb → incomplete.
  - ✅ *The big red bus **is late**.* — Part 1 + Part 2 → complete.
- **Other versions:**
  - The bus **is early**. (before the correct time)
  - The bus **is here**.
  - The train **is late** again! (an exclamation — strong feeling 😠)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The big red bus late.* — the verb **is** is missing.
  - ❌ *The red big bus is late.* — not wrong in meaning, but it sounds strange; we usually say size before colour.
  - ✅ **The big red bus is late.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + (describing words) + thing + is + describing word.**
- **Quick check:** Is "My new phone." a complete sentence? How can you make it complete?

### EX-009-002 — "Where is the station?"

- **Meaning in simple English:** Please tell me the place of the train station. I want to go there. 🚉
- **Situation:** You are in a new city. You want to take a train. You ask someone on the street.
- **Main lesson:** A **question** sentence.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Where | is | the station | ? |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | question word (asks about place) | verb | who/what (subject) | question mark |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Where** — a question word that asks about a **place**. 📍
  - **is** — the verb. In this question, it comes **before** *the station*.
  - **the station** — the thing we ask about. (A *station* = a building where trains or buses stop.)
  - **?** — question mark, because this is a question.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** In *What's your name?* ([Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)) the question word *What* also came first, then *is* ('s).
- **Compare the statement and the question:**
  - Statement: *The station **is** near.* → subject first, then **is**.
  - Question: *Where **is** the station?* → question word, then **is**, then subject.
- **Polite version:** **Excuse me, where is the station, please?** (*Excuse me* = polite words to start talking to a stranger.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Where the station is?* — In a direct question, **is** comes before *the station*.
  - ❌ *Where is the station.* — a question needs **?**.
  - ✅ **Where is the station?**
- **In natural speech:** **Where's the station?** (*Where's* = *Where is*.)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Where is + the + place?** → *Where is the bank? Where is the toilet?*
- **Quick check:** Ask where the hospital is.

### EX-009-003 — "Close the window, please."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I am asking you to shut the window. 🪟
- **Situation:** The room is cold. Your friend is near the window. You ask them.
- **Main lesson:** An **instruction** (request) is a complete sentence, even though the "who" is not said.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | (You) | Close | the window | , please. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | hidden subject | verb (action) | what | polite word |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Close** — the action: to shut. Capital **C** (first word).
  - **the window** — the thing to close.
  - **, please** — polite word. It can go at the **start** (*Please close the window.*) or at the **end** with a comma before it (*Close the window, please.*). Both are correct.
- **Why is it complete?** Because everybody understands **who** must do it: **you**, the listener. English does not need to say *you* in instructions.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *Please say that again* ([Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)) is the same kind of sentence.
- **Being polite:** An instruction without *please* can sound **rude** or bossy, especially to adults or strangers. Add *please*, or use a question: *Can you close the window, please?*
- **Other versions:**
  - Open the door, please.
  - Sit down, please.
  - **Don't** close the window. (= do not; a negative instruction. More in [Lesson 052](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-052-imperatives.md).)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *You close the window please.* — not wrong, but it can sound like an angry order. The normal request is *Close the window, please* or *Can you close the window, please?*
  - ❌ *The window, please.* — understandable in some situations, but it is not a complete sentence (no verb).
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Action + thing, please.**
- **Quick check:** Who is the hidden "who" in "Open the door"?

---

### EX-009-004 — "What a beautiful day!"

- **Meaning in simple English:** Today the weather is very nice, and I feel happy about it! ☀️
- **Situation:** You open the door in the morning. The sun is shining. The sky is blue.
- **Main lesson:** An **exclamation** shows strong feeling.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | What | a | beautiful | day | ! |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | exclamation word | helper word | describing word | thing word | exclamation mark |

- **Word by word:**
  - **What** — here it is **not** a question! In this pattern, *What a ...!* shows a strong feeling.
  - **a** — before *beautiful* (consonant sound **b**, [Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
  - **beautiful** — describing word: very nice to look at.
  - **day** — thing word.
  - **!** — **exclamation mark**. It shows strong feeling.
- **Special note:** This exclamation has **no verb**, but it is still accepted as complete, because the pattern **What a + describing word + thing!** is a fixed, normal English pattern. (The full version would be *What a beautiful day **it is**!*)
- **Other versions:**
  - What a good idea!
  - What **an** interesting book! (*an* before a vowel sound)
  - **How** beautiful! (another pattern)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *What beautiful day!* — **a** is missing.
  - ❌ *What a beautiful day?* — it is not a question; use **!**.
  - ✅ **What a beautiful day!**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **What a/an + describing word + thing!**
- **Quick check:** Make an exclamation about a very good cake.

## 7. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| My brother a doctor. | My brother **is** a doctor. | A sentence needs a verb. |
| Every morning. (written alone) | I walk every morning. | Fragment: no who/what, no verb. |
| Where the station is? | Where **is the station**? | Question word order. |
| What a nice day? | What a nice day**!** | Exclamation, not question. |
| Close the window! (to a stranger) | Close the window, **please**. | Be polite. |

## 8. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Your **voice** changes with the kind of sentence:

| Kind | Voice | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | voice goes **down** at the end ↘ | *The bus is late.* ↘ |
| Question with *where / what* | usually goes **down** ↘ | *Where is the station?* ↘ |
| Yes/no question | usually goes **up** ↗ | *Is the shop open?* ↗ |
| Exclamation | strong, with feeling | *What a beautiful DAY!* |

## 9. Exercises

**A. Complete sentence (✅) or not complete (❌)?**

1. The children are happy.
2. A very old house.
3. In the kitchen.
4. Sit down, please.
5. My father cooks every Sunday.

**B. Make these complete. Add a verb (*is* or *are*).**

1. My mother a teacher.
2. The keys on the table.

**C. Which kind? Statement (S), Question (Q), Instruction (I), Exclamation (E).**

1. Where is my bag?
2. Turn off the light.
3. What a funny film!
4. The shop is closed.

**D. Correct the mistakes.**

1. Where the bank is?
2. What a good idea?
3. The bus late.

## 10. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. ✅ — who (*The children*) + verb (*are*) + describing word.
2. ❌ — no verb. Example: *That is a very old house.*
3. ❌ — no who/what and no verb. Example: *Mum is in the kitchen.*
4. ✅ — an instruction. The hidden "who" is *you*.
5. ✅ — who (*My father*) + verb (*cooks*).

**B.**
1. *My mother **is** a teacher.* — one person → **is**.
2. *The keys **are** on the table.* — more than one key → **are**. *(You will learn is/are fully in [Lesson 018](../06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md).)*

**C.** 1. **Q** · 2. **I** · 3. **E** · 4. **S**

**D.**
1. *Where **is the bank**?* — in a question, **is** comes before the thing.
2. *What a good idea**!*** — exclamation mark.
3. *The bus **is** late.* — the verb was missing.

## 11. Quick revision sheet

- A **sentence** = a complete idea.
- It usually needs **who/what (subject)** + **verb**.
- No verb → usually **not** a sentence (a **fragment**).
- 4 kinds: **statement (.)**, **question (?)**, **instruction (.)**, **exclamation (!)**.
- Instructions hide the "who": *(You) close the window.*
- In conversation, short answers like *"Home."* are normal.

## 12. Memory trick

> **Who + Verb = Sentence.** 🙋 + 🏃 = ✅
