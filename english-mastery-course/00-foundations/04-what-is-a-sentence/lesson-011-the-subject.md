# Lesson 011 — The Subject: Who or What the Sentence Is About

**ID:** L-011 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md), [Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md), [Lesson 010](lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)

---

## 1. What you will learn

- What the **subject** of a sentence is.
- How to **find** the subject with a simple question test.
- A subject can be **one word** or **a group of words**.
- Where the subject usually goes: **before the verb**.
- Special subjects: the **hidden "you"** in instructions, and **"it"** for weather and time.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- A sentence usually needs **two parts**: **who/what** + **verb** ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Thing words** are called **nouns** (*cat, teacher, school*). **Action/being words** are called **verbs** (*run, eat, is*) ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- In instructions, the "who" is hidden: *(You) close the window* ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).

Today, the **who/what** part gets its real name: the **subject**.

---

## 3. What is a subject?

> **The subject is the person or thing the sentence is about. Usually, it is the one who does the action — or the one we describe.**

| Sentence | Subject | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ***Maria** sings.* | Maria | Maria does the action (sings). |
| ***The baby** sleeps.* | The baby | The baby does the action. |
| ***The bus** is late.* | The bus | We describe the bus (it is late). |
| ***I** know the answer.* | I | I am the one who knows. |

Think of a sentence as a short film 🎬. The **subject** is the **main character** of that film.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **subject** | the person or thing the sentence is about; usually the "doer" |
| **doer** | the one who does the action |
| **pronoun** | a short word used instead of a noun: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they* (full lesson: [Lesson 027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md)) |
| **noun phrase** | a group of words built around one noun: *the big red bus* |

---

## 4. How to find the subject — the question test

**Step 1.** Find the **verb** (the action or being word).
**Step 2.** Ask: **"Who** + verb?" or **"What** + verb?"
**Step 3.** The answer is the **subject**.

| Sentence | Step 1: verb | Step 2: question | Step 3: subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| *My dog barks at night.* | barks | **Who/what** barks? | **My dog** |
| *The children play in the park.* | play | **Who** plays? | **The children** |
| *Ana is a nurse.* | is | **Who** is a nurse? | **Ana** |
| *The keys are on the table.* | are | **What** is on the table? | **The keys** |

> 💡 Use **who** for people and **what** for things and animals. (People often use *who* for pets too. 🐶)

---

## 5. What can be a subject?

### A. A name

> ***Tom** eats an apple.* · ***London** is big.*

### B. A noun (often with a small helper word before it)

> ***Birds** fly.* · ***The teacher** is here.* · ***My sister** plays football.*

### C. A pronoun

> ***I** am tired.* · ***She** speaks Arabic.* · ***They** live in Rome.*

The subject pronouns are: **I, you, he, she, it, we, they**. Remember *She is from Egypt* in [Lesson 010](lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md). (Full lesson: [Lesson 027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md).)

### D. A long group of words

The subject can be **many words**, but they all work together as **one subject**:

> ***The old man with the black dog** lives next door.*

Who lives next door? → **The old man with the black dog.** All 7 words together = the subject.

### E. Two subjects joined with "and"

> ***Ali and Sara** are friends.* · ***My brother and I** play chess.*

---

## 6. Where does the subject go?

In **statements**, the subject usually comes **before the verb**:

```
   SUBJECT   →   VERB   →   the rest
   My dog        barks       at night.
```

This is a very strong rule in English. Many other languages can drop the subject or move it. **English almost always needs a subject**, and it usually comes first.

> ❌ *Is cold.* → ✅ ***It** is cold.*
> ❌ *Am happy.* → ✅ ***I** am happy.*

(In very relaxed speech or text messages, people sometimes drop *I*: "*Can't come, sorry!*" But in normal sentences, always use the subject.)

In **questions**, a helping word can come **before** the subject — you will learn this in [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md):

> *The shop is open.* → *Is **the shop** open?* (the subject is still *the shop*)

---

## 7. Two special subjects

### Special 1 — The hidden "you" in instructions

> *Sit down, please.* → who should sit? **You.** The subject is hidden.

### Special 2 — "It" for weather, time, and distance

English needs a subject, even when there is no real "doer". So we use **it**:

> ***It** is cold.* (weather) · ***It** is 5 o'clock.* (time) · ***It** is Monday.* (day) · ***It** is far.* (distance)

Here *it* does not mean any real thing. It just **fills the subject place**. Many learners forget it: ❌ *Is raining.* → ✅ ***It** is raining.*

## 8. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-011-001 — "Maria sings."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Maria is a person who sings (maybe she sings well, or she sings as a hobby). 🎤
- **Situation:** Someone asks: "What does Maria do at the party?" You answer.
- **Main lesson:** The simplest subject: one name.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Maria | sings. |
  |---|---|
  | **subject** | **verb** |

- **Find the subject (question test):**
  1. Verb → **sings**.
  2. Who sings?
  3. **Maria** → the subject.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Maria** — a name (capital **M**). She is the doer.
  - **sings** — *sing* + **-s** because the subject is **one person** (not *I* or *you*). Remember *My sister play**s*** ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)). (Full lesson: [Lesson 043](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md).)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Two words can be a complete sentence: *Birds fly* ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- **Change the subject:**
  - **She** sings. (pronoun instead of the name)
  - **The children** sing. (more than one → no -s)
  - **I** sing. (I → no -s)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Sings Maria.* — the subject must come **before** the verb in a statement.
  - ❌ *Maria sing.* — one person → **sings**.
  - ✅ **Maria sings.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Name + verb-s.**
- **Quick check:** What is the subject in *Ahmed cooks*?

### EX-011-002 — "The old man with the black dog lives next door."

- **Meaning in simple English:** There is an old man. He has a black dog. His home is the house beside my house. 🏠👴🐕
- **Situation:** A new neighbour asks you about the people on your street.
- **Main lesson:** A **long subject**: many words working as one.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The old man with the black dog | lives | next door. |
  |---|---|---|
  | **subject** (7 words) | **verb** | where |

- **Find the subject (question test):**
  1. Verb → **lives**.
  2. Who lives next door?
  3. **The old man with the black dog** → the subject.
- **Inside the subject:**
  - **man** — the **main word** of the subject (a noun). The sentence is about **the man**, not about the dog!
  - **The old** — helper word + describing word before *man* ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
  - **with the black dog** — extra information about the man. It tells us **which** man.
- **Why "lives" and not "live"?** The main word is **man** (one person) → **lives**. Even though *dog* is closer to the verb, the verb agrees with **man**. This idea is called **subject–verb agreement** (full lesson much later: [Lesson 193](../../02-intermediate-grammar/subject-verb-agreement/lesson-193-subject-verb-agreement-basics.md)).
- **Word by word (rest):**
  - **lives** — has his home.
  - **next door** — in the house beside this one.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Describing word before thing word: *old man, black dog* ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **Make it shorter with a pronoun:** ***He** lives next door.* — **He** replaces all 7 words!
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The old man with the black dog he lives next door.* — **he** repeats the subject. Use one subject only.
  - ❌ *The old man with the black dog live next door.* — the main word is *man* (one) → **lives**.
  - ✅ **The old man with the black dog lives next door.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + describing word + person + with + the + thing + verb-s …**
  → *The young woman with the red bag works here.*
- **Quick check:** In *The girl with the long hair is my sister*, what is the main word of the subject?

### EX-011-003 — "It is very hot today."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The weather today is very warm. ☀️🥵
- **Situation:** You go outside in summer. The sun is strong.
- **Main lesson:** **It** as an "empty" subject for weather.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | It | is | very hot | today. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **subject** (empty "it") | **verb** | describing words | when |

- **Find the subject:** Verb → **is**. What is very hot? → **It** (the weather).
- **Word by word:**
  - **It** — fills the subject place. It does not mean a real thing here; it points to the weather in general.
  - **is** — being verb.
  - **very hot** — *very* makes *hot* stronger ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
  - **today** — this day.
- **Why do we need "it"?** Because **English sentences need a subject**. In some languages, you can say just "is hot". In English, you **cannot**.
- **Other "it" sentences:**
  - **It** is windy. 🌬️ / **It** is sunny. ☀️
  - **It** is 7 o'clock. ⏰
  - **It** is my birthday! 🎂
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Is very hot today.* — no subject.
  - ❌ *Today is very hot.* — ✅ actually **correct** too! Here *Today* is the subject. Both are fine; *It is very hot today* is more common.
  - ✅ **It is very hot today.**
- **In natural speech:** **It's** very hot today. (*It's* = *It is*.)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **It is + weather word (+ today).**
- **Quick check:** Correct this: *Is cold.*

---

### EX-011-004 — "My brother and I play chess every Sunday."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Each Sunday, my brother and I play the game of chess together. ♟️
- **Situation:** Someone asks what you do at the weekend.
- **Main lesson:** **Two subjects** joined with **and**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My brother and I | play | chess | every Sunday. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **subject** (two people) | **verb** | what | when |

- **Find the subject:** Verb → **play**. Who plays chess? → **My brother and I**.
- **Word by word:**
  - **My brother and I** — two people = one subject. Together they are like **we**.
  - **play** — **no -s**, because the subject is **two people** (like *we*).
  - **chess** — a board game.
  - **every Sunday** — each Sunday. Capital **S** for the day ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Why "my brother and I" — not "I and my brother"?** Both are understandable, but in English it is **polite** and normal to put **yourself last**: *my brother and I*, *Sara and I*.
- **Why "I" and not "me"?** In the **subject** position, use **I**. (*Me* is used in other positions: *He helps **me**.* Full lesson: [Lesson 028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md).) A quick test: remove *my brother and* → *I play chess* ✅ / ~~Me play chess~~ ❌.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Me and my brother play chess.* — very common in casual speech, but in careful English use *My brother and I*.
  - ❌ *My brother and I plays chess.* — two people → **play**.
  - ✅ **My brother and I play chess every Sunday.**
- **Shorter version:** ***We** play chess every Sunday.*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + and + I + verb …**
- **Quick check:** Choose: *Sara and (I / me) are students.*

## 9. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Is raining. | **It** is raining. | English needs a subject. |
| Am a student. | **I** am a student. | Same. |
| My mother she is a doctor. | My mother is a doctor. | Do not repeat the subject with a pronoun. |
| Me and Tom are friends. | Tom and **I** are friends. | Use **I** as a subject; put yourself last. |
| The man with the dogs live here. | The man with the dogs **lives** here. | The main word is *man* (one). |
| Sings Maria. | Maria sings. | Subject before verb. |

## 10. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

When listening, try to **catch the subject first** — it tells you who the sentence is about. Subjects are usually at the start, before the verb.

Listen (or read aloud) and say the subject:

1. *My teacher speaks three languages.* → **My teacher**
2. *The shop on the corner opens at nine.* → **The shop on the corner**
3. *It's cold.* → **It**

Note: *It's* sounds like "its" — short and quick.

## 11. Exercises

**A. Underline (write) the subject.**

1. The baby cries at night.
2. They live in a big house.
3. My best friend from school is a doctor.
4. Tom and Lisa work in a bank.
5. It is 10 o'clock.

**B. Add a subject.**

1. ___ is very cold today.
2. ___ am hungry.
3. ___ are my friends.

**C. Correct the mistakes.**

1. Is very windy.
2. My father he works in a hospital.
3. Me and Ali play football.
4. The girl with the cats live here.

**D. Replace the subject with a pronoun (he, she, it, we, they).**

1. **My mother** cooks dinner.
2. **The children** are in the garden.
3. **My friend and I** like music.

## 12. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **The baby** — *Who cries?*
2. **They** — *Who lives…?*
3. **My best friend from school** — *Who is a doctor?* (Main word: *friend*.)
4. **Tom and Lisa** — two people joined with *and*.
5. **It** — the empty "it" for time.

**B.**
1. **It** is very cold today. — weather → *it*.
2. **I** am hungry. — *am* goes only with *I*.
3. **They** (or *You* / *These people*) are my friends.

**C.**
1. ***It** is very windy.* — add the subject.
2. *My father works in a hospital.* — remove the extra *he*.
3. ***Ali and I** play football.* — subject → *I*; yourself last.
4. *The girl with the cats **lives** here.* — main word = *girl* (one).

**D.**
1. **She** cooks dinner.
2. **They** are in the garden.
3. **We** like music.

## 13. Quick revision sheet

- **Subject** = who or what the sentence is about (usually the doer).
- **Test:** find the verb → ask **Who/What + verb?** → the answer is the subject.
- A subject can be: a name, a noun, a pronoun, a long group, or two things with *and*.
- In statements: **subject before verb**.
- English **needs** a subject: use **it** for weather, time, days, distance.
- Instructions have a hidden **you**.
- Don't repeat the subject: ❌ *My mother she…*

## 14. Memory trick

> **The subject is the star ⭐ of the sentence — and the star comes first.**
