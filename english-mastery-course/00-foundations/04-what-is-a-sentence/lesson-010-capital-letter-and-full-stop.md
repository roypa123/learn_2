# Lesson 010 — Writing a Sentence: Capital Letter and Full Stop

**ID:** L-010 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md), [Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)

## 1. What you will learn

- Every written sentence has a **start** and an **end**.
- **Start** = a **capital letter**. **End** = **.** or **?** or **!**
- How to write **two or more sentences** one after another.
- How to fix sentences that **run together** with no full stop.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- A **sentence** is a complete idea: **who/what + verb** ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- 4 kinds: statement, question, instruction, exclamation ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- Capital letters are used for the first word, **I**, and names ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).

---

## 3. The start and the end of a sentence

When we **speak**, we show the end of a sentence with our **voice**: we stop for a moment, and our voice often goes down ↘.

When we **write**, we cannot hear the voice. So we use **signs**:

```
 Capital letter  →  [ the sentence ]  →  end mark
       T              he cat is black       .
```

> **A written sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop (.), a question mark (?) or an exclamation mark (!).**

These signs are called **punctuation marks**.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| **punctuation** | the small signs we use in writing | . , ? ! ' |
| **full stop** (British) / **period** (American) | ends a statement or instruction | **.** |
| **question mark** | ends a question | **?** |
| **exclamation mark** (US: **exclamation point**) | ends a sentence with strong feeling | **!** |

### Which end mark?

| Kind of sentence ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)) | End mark | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | **.** | *I like tea.* |
| Instruction | **.** (or **!** if strong) | *Sit down, please.* / *Stop!* |
| Question | **?** | *Do you like tea?* |
| Exclamation | **!** | *What a nice surprise!* |

## 4. Rules for writing sentences

### Rule 1 — After an end mark, the next sentence starts with a capital

> I am tired**.** **I** want to sleep**.**
> It is late**.** **W**here is Tom**?**

### Rule 2 — One sentence = one end mark

Use **one** end mark at the end. Not in the middle.

> ❌ *My sister. Is a doctor.* — The first part is not complete.
> ✅ *My sister is a doctor.*

### Rule 3 — Don't run sentences together

Two complete ideas need **two** sentences (or a joining word — you will learn this in [Lesson 054](../../01-beginner-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-054-and-but-or.md)).

> ❌ *I live in Rome I am a student.* — two ideas, no end mark between them.
> ✅ *I live in Rome. I am a student.*

### Rule 4 — No space before the end mark; one space after it

> ✅ *I like tea. You like coffee.*
> ❌ *I like tea . You like coffee .*

### Rule 5 — Use "!" only sometimes

Too many exclamation marks look like shouting, or not serious. In normal writing, use **.** for most sentences. Use **!** for real strong feeling. Do not write **!!!** in formal writing.

### Rule 6 — A comma does not end a sentence

A comma (**,**) is a small pause **inside** a sentence. After a comma, the next word is usually **small** (not a capital) — unless it is a name or **I**.

> ✅ *Today is Monday, and it is cold.* ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md))
> ❌ *I live in Rome, I am a student.* — two complete sentences joined only with a comma. This is a common mistake. (Full lesson: [Lesson 148](../../02-intermediate-grammar/sentence-types/lesson-148-run-on-sentences-and-comma-splices.md).)

## 5. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-010-001 — "I am tired. I want to sleep."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I have no energy. I need to sleep now. 😴
- **Situation:** It is 11 o'clock at night. You worked all day. You tell your family.
- **Main lesson:** Two sentences: full stop at the end of the first, capital letter at the start of the second.
- **Sentence 1:**

  | I | am | tired | . |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who | verb (being) | describing word | full stop |

- **Sentence 2:**

  | I | want | to sleep | . |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who | verb (wish) | what I want | full stop |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I am tired** — *tired* is a describing word (adjective) ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)). *I am* = like "I =".
  - **.** — the first idea is complete. Stop.
  - **I** — capital: new sentence (and **I** is always capital anyway).
  - **want** — a verb: to wish to have or do something.
  - **to sleep** — *to* + action word. After *want*, we often use **to + action**: *I want **to eat**. I want **to go**.* (Full lesson: [Lesson 082](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-082-want-to-and-would-like.md).)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Each sentence has **who + verb** ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I am tired I want to sleep.* — no end mark between two complete ideas.
  - ❌ *I am tired, I want to sleep.* — a comma cannot join two complete sentences (in careful writing).
  - ❌ *I am tired. i want to sleep.* — capital **I** needed.
  - ✅ **I am tired. I want to sleep.**
  - ✅ *I am tired, **so** I want to sleep.* — also correct: the joining word **so** connects them. (You will learn **so** in [Lesson 081](../../01-beginner-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-081-because-and-so.md).)
- **In natural speech:** *I'm tired. I wanna sleep.* — *wanna* is a very informal spoken form of *want to*. You will hear it, but **do not write it** in normal writing.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I am + feeling. I want to + action.** → *I am hungry. I want to eat.*
- **Quick check:** Where does the full stop go? *I am hungry I want to eat*

### EX-010-002 — "My name is Leo. I am ten years old. I live in Rome."

- **Meaning in simple English:** A boy is telling you three facts about himself: his name, his age, and his city.
- **Situation:** A boy is writing the first lines of a letter to a new friend.
- **Main lesson:** Writing several sentences in a row.
- **The three sentences:**

  | # | Sentence | Starts with | Ends with |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | My name is Leo. | **M** (capital) | **.** |
  | 2 | I am ten years old. | **I** (capital) | **.** |
  | 3 | I live in Rome. | **I** (capital) | **.** |

- **Word by word (new parts):**
  - **Leo**, **Rome** — names → capitals in the **middle** of the sentence too ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
  - **ten years old** — the way to say age in English. We say **I am** + number + **years old**. (Not *I have ten years* — that is a common mistake for speakers of some languages.)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *I live in London* ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)) has the same pattern.
- **Count the capital letters:** **M**y, **L**eo, **I**, **I**, **R**ome → 5.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My name is Leo I am ten years old I live in Rome.* — one long line, no end marks.
  - ❌ *My name is Leo. I have ten years. I live in Rome.* — wrong way to say age.
  - ✅ **My name is Leo. I am ten years old. I live in Rome.**
- **In natural speech:** *I'm ten.* (short and very common)
- **Pattern you can reuse (for introducing yourself):**
  - My name is ___ **.**
  - I am ___ years old **.**
  - I live in ___ **.**
- **Quick check:** Write these three sentences about yourself.

### EX-010-003 — "Are you ready? The taxi is here!"

- **Meaning in simple English:** I am asking if you can go now. The car you called to take you somewhere has arrived! 🚕
- **Situation:** You and your friend are going to the airport. Your friend is still in the bathroom. The taxi is waiting outside.
- **Main lesson:** Different end marks in a row: **?** then **!**
- **Sentence 1:**

  | Are | you | ready | ? |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | verb (comes first in this question) | who | describing word | question mark |

- **Sentence 2:**

  | The taxi | is | here | ! |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who/what | verb | where | exclamation mark |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Are you ready?** — a yes/no question. The verb **are** comes **before** *you*. Compare the statement: *You are ready.* The order changes for the question. (Full lesson: [Lesson 015](../05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md).)
  - **ready** — prepared, able to start now.
  - **The** — capital **T**: new sentence after **?**.
  - **taxi** — a car you pay to drive you somewhere.
  - **here** — in this place.
  - **!** — shows the feeling: *hurry up!* (A full stop is also correct here — it is just calmer.)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Voice goes **up** at the end of a yes/no question ↗ ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Are you ready. The taxi is here!* — a question needs **?**.
  - ❌ *Are you ready? the taxi is here!* — after **?**, the next sentence starts with a capital.
  - ✅ **Are you ready? The taxi is here!**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Are you + describing word?** → *Are you hungry? Are you okay?*
- **Quick check:** What does a sentence after a question mark start with?

### EX-010-004 — Fixing a run-on text

**Text with mistakes:**

> ❌ *this is my friend sara she is from egypt she speaks arabic and english*

**How to fix it — step by step:**

1. **Find each complete idea** (who + verb):
   - *this is my friend sara* → who: *this*, verb: *is*
   - *she is from egypt* → who: *she*, verb: *is*
   - *she speaks arabic and english* → who: *she*, verb: *speaks*
2. **Put an end mark** after each idea. They are statements → **.**
3. **Capital letter** at the start of each sentence.
4. **Capital letters** for names: *Sara*, *Egypt*, *Arabic*, *English* ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md), rules 3–5).

**Correct text:**

> ✅ **This is my friend Sara. She is from Egypt. She speaks Arabic and English.**

- **New word:** **she** — means a girl or woman we already talked about (here: Sara). It is used so we don't repeat *Sara, Sara, Sara*. (Full lesson: [Lesson 027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md).)
- **Count:** 3 sentences · 3 full stops · 7 capital letters (**T**his, **S**ara, **S**he, **E**gypt, **S**he, **A**rabic, **E**nglish).
- **Quick check:** Why does *She* have a capital letter, but *she* in the middle of a sentence would not? *(Answer: here it is the first word of a sentence. Unlike **I**, the word **she** is not always a capital.)*

## 6. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I like cats i have two cats. | I like cats. **I** have two cats. | Two ideas → two sentences. |
| I like cats, I have two cats. | I like cats. I have two cats. | A comma can't join two sentences. |
| where is my phone. | **W**here is my phone**?** | Capital start; **?** for a question. |
| I am happy!!! | I am happy! / I am happy. | One mark is enough. |
| I have twenty years. | I **am** twenty **years old**. | Age uses *be*. |
| Hello . | Hello. | No space before the end mark. |

## 7. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

When you read aloud:

- At **.** — stop for a short moment. Voice goes down ↘.
- At **?** (yes/no question) — voice goes up ↗.
- At **!** — say it with feeling.
- At **,** — a very short pause.

Read EX-010-002 and EX-010-003 aloud, using these pauses.

## 8. Exercises

**A. Add the end mark (. ? !).**

1. Where is the bus
2. I am a student
3. What a big house
4. Are you okay
5. Open your books, please

**B. Rewrite with capital letters and end marks.**

1. my brother is tall he plays basketball
2. is this your bag it is very heavy
3. hello my name is kim i am from korea

**C. Find and correct the mistake.**

1. I am tired, I want to go home.
2. My Father is a teacher.
3. I have nine years.

## 9. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **?** — a question (*Where*).
2. **.** — a statement.
3. **!** — an exclamation (*What a …!*).
4. **?** — a yes/no question (*Are you…*).
5. **.** — an instruction.

**B.**
1. ***M**y brother is tall**.** **H**e plays basketball**.*** — 2 ideas → 2 sentences.
2. ***I**s this your bag**?** **I**t is very heavy**.*** — first a question, then a statement.
3. ***H**ello**.** **M**y name is **K**im**.** **I** am from **K**orea**.*** — *Hello.* is a one-word greeting sentence ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). *(Also correct: **Hello! My name is Kim.** — the "!" shows a friendly, happy greeting.)*

**C.**
1. *I am tired**.** **I** want to go home.* — two complete sentences; a comma cannot join them. (Or: *I am tired, **so** I want to go home.*)
2. *My **f**ather is a teacher.* — *father* is an ordinary word here, not a name.
3. *I **am** nine **years old**.* — age uses *am / is / are*.

## 10. Quick revision sheet

- Sentence **start** → capital letter.
- Sentence **end** → **.** (statement, instruction) · **?** (question) · **!** (strong feeling).
- After an end mark → next sentence starts with a **capital**.
- Two complete ideas → two sentences (not a comma).
- No space before **. ? !** · one space after.
- Age: **I am ___ years old.**
