# Lesson 012 — The Verb: The Action or State

**ID:** L-012 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md), [Lesson 011](lesson-011-the-subject.md)

## 1. What you will learn

- What a **verb** is, and why every sentence needs one.
- Three kinds of verbs: **action**, **being**, and **having/thinking/feeling** (state) verbs.
- A verb can show **time**: now, before, later.
- **Main verbs** and **helping verbs** — a first look.
- The common mistake *"I am go"*.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- **Verbs** are action words (*run, eat*) and being words (*am, is, are*) ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **No verb → usually not a sentence** ([Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- The **subject** is who/what the sentence is about; it usually comes **before the verb** ([Lesson 011](lesson-011-the-subject.md)).

---

## 3. What is a verb?

> **A verb is a word that tells us what the subject does, what the subject is, or what the subject has, thinks or feels.**

If the subject is the **star** of the sentence ⭐ ([Lesson 011](lesson-011-the-subject.md)), the verb is the **engine** 🚂. Without an engine, the train does not move. Without a verb, the sentence does not work.

| Sentence | Subject | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| *Birds **fly**.* | Birds | fly |
| *My father **is** a doctor.* | My father | is |
| *We **have** two cats.* | We | have |
| *I **know** the answer.* | I | know |

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **verb** | a word for an action or a state |
| **action verb** | a verb for something you **do**: *run, eat, write* |
| **state verb** | a verb for something that **is**, or a feeling or thought: *be, have, like, know, want* |
| **main verb** | the verb with the main meaning |
| **helping verb** (auxiliary verb) | a verb that helps the main verb: *am, is, are, do, does, can, will…* |
| **tense** | the form of a verb that shows **time** (now, past, future) |

---

## 4. Three kinds of verbs

### Kind 1 — Action verbs 🏃 (what you **do**)

> run · walk · eat · drink · write · read · cook · play · open · close · sing · laugh · sleep

You can usually **see** or **hear** the action.

### Kind 2 — Being verbs 🟰 (what something **is**)

The verb **be** has the forms **am, is, are** (and later *was, were*):

> *I **am** tired.* · *She **is** a teacher.* · *They **are** at home.*

It does not show an action. It **joins** the subject with information about it — like an **=** sign.

### Kind 3 — Having, thinking and feeling verbs 💭 (states)

> have · know · like · love · want · need · think · understand · remember

*I **know** the answer.* — You cannot "see" knowing. It is in the mind. But it is still a verb.

> 💡 Kinds 2 and 3 are called **state verbs** (a *state* = how something is). Later you will learn they behave a little differently ([Lesson 190](../../02-intermediate-grammar/tenses/lesson-190-stative-verbs.md)).

---

## 5. How to find the verb — three tests

| Test | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Subject test** | Put *I, you* or *they* before the word. Does it make sense? | *they **cook*** ✅ · *they **kitchen*** ❌ |
| **2. Time test** | Can you change it to show **yesterday**? | *cook → cook**ed*** ✅ · *happy → ~~happied~~* ❌ |
| **3. "What does the subject do / is it?"** | Ask about the subject. | *Tom eats an apple.* What does Tom do? → **eats** |

## 6. Verbs show time

This is one of the most important jobs of a verb. The **form** of the verb changes to show **when** ([Lesson 007](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md), Idea 3: *a word can change its form*).

| Time | Example | Verb form |
|---|---|---|
| Now / usually | *I **walk** to school.* | walk |
| Before (past) | *I **walked** to school yesterday.* | walk**ed** |
| Later (future) | *I **will walk** to school tomorrow.* | **will** walk |

These forms are called **tenses**. English has many tenses. You will learn them **one by one**, starting with the present simple in [Lesson 042](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-042-present-simple-positive.md). Today, just notice: **the verb carries the time**.

## 7. Main verbs and helping verbs — a first look

Sometimes the verb is **two (or more) words**:

> *I **am learning** English.* ([Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md))
> *She **can swim**.*
> *I **will walk** to school.*
> ***Do** you **like** tea?*

| Helping verb | Main verb | Together they mean… |
|---|---|---|
| **am** | learning | an action happening now |
| **can** | swim | ability (she is able to) |
| **will** | walk | the future |
| **do** | like | (helps make a question) |

- The **main verb** gives the **main meaning** (learn, swim, walk, like).
- The **helping verb** adds **extra information** — time, ability, question… — or helps the grammar.

> ⚠️ **be, have, do** can be **main verbs** or **helping verbs**:
> - *She **is** a teacher.* → **is** = main verb (it's the only verb).
> - *She **is** teaching.* → **is** = helping verb (main verb = *teaching*).
>
> Full lesson: [Lesson 046](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-046-do-as-main-and-helping-verb.md) and later lessons.

## 8. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-012-001 — "The children laugh."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The young people are making happy sounds because something is funny. 😂
- **Situation:** A clown does a funny trick at a party. The children laugh. (We could also say *are laughing* for an action at this moment — you'll learn that later.)
- **Main lesson:** An action verb.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The children | laugh. |
  |---|---|
  | subject | **verb** (action) |

- **Find the verb:**
  - Subject test: *they laugh* ✅
  - Time test: *laugh → laughed* ✅
- **Word by word:**
  - **The children** — *children* = more than one **child** ([Lesson 008](../03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)). It is an unusual plural (not *childs*). (Full lesson: [Lesson 025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md).)
  - **laugh** — action verb. **No -s**, because the subject is **more than one**.
- **Pronunciation:** *laugh* = "laf" (UK: "lahf"). The **gh** sounds like **f** ([Lesson 006](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)).
- **Other versions:**
  - The baby **laughs**. (one → **-s**)
  - The children **laughed**. (past)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The children laughs.* — more than one → no -s.
  - ❌ *The children.* — no verb → not a sentence.
  - ✅ **The children laugh.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + plural subject + verb.**
- **Quick check:** What is the verb in *Dogs bark*?

---

### EX-012-002 — "My father is a doctor."

- **Meaning in simple English:** My father's job is doctor. He helps sick people. 👨‍⚕️
- **Situation:** A friend asks: "What does your father do?" (= what is his job?)
- **Main lesson:** A **being** verb (*is*) as the main verb.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My father | is | a doctor. |
  |---|---|---|
  | subject | **verb** (being) | what he is |

- **Word by word:**
  - **My father** — subject.
  - **is** — the main verb. Like **=**: *my father = a doctor*. We use **is** with one person (not *I* or *you*). (Full lesson: [Lesson 018](../06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md).)
  - **a doctor** — **a** is needed for **one** job: *a doctor, a teacher, **an** engineer* ([Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
- **Remember from before 🔁:** ❌ *My brother a doctor* was a fragment in [Lesson 009](lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md). The verb **is** fixes it.
- **Why is "is" a verb?** Time test: *is → **was*** (past: *My father **was** a doctor* — maybe he doesn't work now). The form changes for time → it's a verb.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My father doctor.* — no verb.
  - ❌ *My father is doctor.* — for one job, English needs **a**.
  - ✅ **My father is a doctor.**
- **In natural speech:** *My dad's a doctor.* (*dad* = informal word for father; *'s* = is.)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + is + a/an + job.**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *My sister is engineer.*

---

### EX-012-003 — "I walked to school yesterday."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The day before today, I went to school on foot. 🚶‍♀️🏫
- **Situation:** A friend asks: "Did you take the bus yesterday?" You answer: "No, I walked."
- **Main lesson:** The verb shows **time** (the past).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | walked | to school | yesterday. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | subject | **verb** (past) | where to | when |

- **Word by word:**
  - **walked** — *walk* + **-ed**. The **-ed** shows the action happened **before now** (the past). (Full lesson: [Lesson 062](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-062-past-simple-regular-verbs.md).)
  - **to school** — **to** shows the direction (where I went).
  - **yesterday** — the day before today. It also tells us "past", but the **verb** must show it too.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Subject → verb → the rest ([Lesson 011](lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Compare time:**
  - *I **walk** to school.* (usually, every day)
  - *I **walked** to school yesterday.* (past)
  - *I **will walk** to school tomorrow.* (future)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I walk to school yesterday.* — *yesterday* = past, so the verb must be past: **walked**.
  - ✅ **I walked to school yesterday.**
- **Pronunciation:** *walked* — the **l** is silent and **-ed** sounds like **t**: "wokt". (More about -ed sounds: [Lesson 063](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-063-ed-spelling-and-sounds.md).)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I + verb-ed + … + yesterday.** → *I cooked dinner yesterday. I played football yesterday.*
- **Quick check:** Make it past: *I play tennis.* → *I ___ tennis yesterday.*

---

### EX-012-004 — "I am cooking dinner."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Right now, at this moment, I am making the evening meal. 🍳
- **Situation:** Your friend calls you on the phone. You are in the kitchen. They ask: "What are you doing?"
- **Main lesson:** A **helping verb** (*am*) + a **main verb** (*cooking*).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | am | cooking | dinner. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | subject | **helping verb** | **main verb** | what |

- **Word by word:**
  - **am** — helping verb. Here it is **not** "=" (I am not = cooking). It helps *cooking* show an action happening **now**.
  - **cooking** — main verb: *cook* + **-ing**. It carries the real meaning (to cook).
  - **dinner** — the evening meal.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** *I am learning English* ([Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)) uses the same pattern: **am + verb-ing**. (Full lesson: [Lesson 056](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-056-present-continuous-positive.md).)
- **Compare "am" as main verb and helping verb:**
  - *I **am** hungry.* → **am** = main verb (only verb, "=").
  - *I **am** cooking.* → **am** = helping verb; **cooking** = main verb.
- **Wrong versions (very common!):**
  - ❌ *I am cook dinner.* — after helping **am**, the main verb needs **-ing**.
  - ❌ *I cooking dinner.* — the helping verb **am** is missing.
  - ✅ **I am cooking dinner.** / ✅ *I cook dinner.* (different meaning: usually, as a habit)
- **In natural speech:** ***I'm** cooking dinner.*
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I am + verb-ing + thing.**
- **Quick check:** Find the helping verb and the main verb: *She is reading a book.*

## 9. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| My sister very tall. | My sister **is** very tall. | A sentence needs a verb. |
| I am go to school every day. | I **go** to school every day. | Don't put **am** before a normal verb (no -ing). |
| I am agree. | I agree. | *agree* is already the verb. |
| I cooking now. | I **am** cooking now. | -ing needs a helping verb. |
| I walk there yesterday. | I **walked** there yesterday. | Past time → past verb. |
| My father is doctor. | My father is **a** doctor. | One job → a/an. |

> 💡 The mistake **"I am go"** is one of the most common for learners. Remember: **am/is/are + verb** needs **-ing** (*I am going*). Without -ing, use the verb alone (*I go*).

## 10. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Main verbs are usually **stressed** (said strongly). Helping verbs are usually **weak**:

> *I'm **COOK**-ing **DIN**-ner.* · *She can **SWIM**.* (can sounds like "kn")

Listen for the **main verb** — it carries the meaning.

## 11. Exercises

**A. Find the verb.**

1. The sun shines.
2. My mother works in a bank.
3. They are happy.
4. We have a new car.
5. I like chocolate.

**B. Action verb (A), being verb (B) or state verb — having/thinking/feeling (S)?**

1. jump 2. is 3. know 4. eat 5. want 6. are

**C. Helping verb (H) and main verb (M).**

1. She is sleeping.
2. I can swim.
3. They will come.

**D. Correct the mistakes.**

1. I am work in a hotel.
2. He sleeping now.
3. My brother a student.
4. We play football yesterday.

## 12. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **shines** — *What does the sun do?*
2. **works** — action.
3. **are** — being verb.
4. **have** — having verb.
5. **like** — feeling verb.

**B.** 1. **A** · 2. **B** · 3. **S** · 4. **A** · 5. **S** · 6. **B**

**C.**
1. **is** (H) + **sleeping** (M)
2. **can** (H) + **swim** (M)
3. **will** (H) + **come** (M)

**D.**
1. *I **work** in a hotel.* — a normal fact → verb alone. (Or *I **am working** in a hotel* — for a temporary situation now.)
2. *He **is** sleeping now.* — -ing needs **is**.
3. *My brother **is** a student.* — missing verb.
4. *We **played** football yesterday.* — past time → past verb.

## 13. Quick revision sheet

- **Verb** = action (*run*), being (*am/is/are*), or state (*have, know, like*).
- Every sentence needs a verb — it is the **engine**.
- Find it: *I/they +* word ✅ · can change for time ✅.
- Verbs show **time**: *walk / walked / will walk*.
- **Helping verb + main verb**: *am cooking, can swim, will walk*.
- **be, have, do** can be main or helping verbs.
- ❌ *I am go* → ✅ *I go* / *I am going*.

## 14. Memory trick

> **Subject = the star ⭐. Verb = the engine 🚂. No engine, no train.**
