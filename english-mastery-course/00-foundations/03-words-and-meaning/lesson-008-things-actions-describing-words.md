# Lesson 008 — Three Kinds of Words: Things, Actions and Describing Words

**ID:** L-008 · **Level:** 0 · **Needs before:** [Lesson 007](lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)

⬅️ [Lesson 007](lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md) · ➡️ [Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)

## 1. What you will learn

- Words do different **jobs**.
- Three big groups of words:
  1. **Thing words** (names of people, animals, places, things) → later called **nouns**.
  2. **Action words** (what someone does) → later called **verbs**.
  3. **Describing words** (what something is like) → later called **adjectives**.
- Simple **tests** to find each kind.
- The **same word** can do **different jobs** in different sentences.

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- A **word** is a small piece of language with a meaning ([Lesson 007](lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- One word can have more than one meaning; the **context** (the words around it) helps ([Lesson 007](lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- In *Please turn on the **light*** and *My bag is **light***, the word *light* did two different jobs.

## 3. Words have jobs

Think about a football team ⚽. Every player has a job: one player stops the ball, others run and score. A team needs different players.

A sentence is the same. **Every word has a job.** Look:

> **The** · **happy** · **child** · **runs.**

| Word | Its job |
|---|---|
| **child** | names a **person** (thing word) |
| **runs** | tells the **action** (action word) |
| **happy** | tells what the child is **like** (describing word) |
| **The** | a small **helper** word; it points to the child |

Grammar books call these word groups **parts of speech** or **word classes**. English has about 9 main groups. Today we learn the **three biggest**. The others come later.

### Key words

| Easy name | Grammar name | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| thing word | **noun** | names a person, animal, place, thing or idea | *mother, cat, school, phone, love* |
| action word | **verb** | tells what someone does (or what happens) | *run, eat, read, sleep* |
| describing word | **adjective** | tells what a person or thing is like | *big, happy, red, old* |

You do not need to remember the grammar names today. But try! They will be used a lot later.

## 4. Group 1 — Thing words (nouns)

A thing word gives a **name** to:

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| 👩 people | mother, teacher, doctor, Ana |
| 🐱 animals | cat, horse, bird |
| 🏫 places | school, park, London, kitchen |
| 📱 things | phone, table, apple, car |
| 💭 ideas and feelings (you cannot touch them) | love, time, music, help |

### Test for a thing word

> **Can you put "a", "an" or "the" before it?**
> *a* **cat** ✅ · *the* **school** ✅ · *the* **music** ✅
> *a* **run**? Sometimes... (see Section 7) · *a* **happy** ❌ (alone, this doesn't work)

> **Can you put "my" before it?** *my* **phone** ✅ · *my* **mother** ✅

Full lessons on nouns start at [Lesson 021](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md).

## 5. Group 2 — Action words (verbs)

An action word tells what someone **does**:

> run 🏃 · eat 🍽️ · sleep 😴 · write ✍️ · open 🚪 · play ⚽ · cook 🍳

Some verbs are not "busy" actions. They tell what happens **in the mind** or a **state**:

> know · like · want · think · **be** (am / is / are)

You met **know** and **like** in [Lesson 006](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md), and **am / is** in many sentences. These are verbs too!

### Test for an action word

> **Can you put "I", "you" or "they" before it — and it makes sense?**
> *I* **eat**. ✅ · *They* **run**. ✅ · *I* **table**. ❌ · *They* **happy**. ❌

> **Can it change its form for time?** *play → play**ed***, *eat → ate*. Verbs show **when** something happens.

Full lesson: [Lesson 012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md).

## 6. Group 3 — Describing words (adjectives)

A describing word tells what a person or thing is **like**:

| What it describes | Examples |
|---|---|
| size | big, small, tall, short |
| colour | red, blue, green, black |
| feeling | happy, sad, tired, angry |
| age | old, new, young |
| other | hot, cold, good, bad, beautiful, easy |

### Test for a describing word

> **Can you put "very" before it?** *very* **big** ✅ · *very* **happy** ✅ · *very* **cat** ❌
> **Can it go in "It is ___." or "He is ___."?** *It is* **cold**. ✅ · *He is* **tall**. ✅

> ⚠️ Not every adjective works with *very* (for example, *very dead* is strange). But the test works for most common ones.

**Where do describing words go?** Usually:

- **before** a thing word: *a **red** car*, *the **happy** child*
- **after** *am / is / are*: *The car is **red**.*

In English, a describing word **does not change** for more than one thing: *a red car → two **red** cars* (not ~~reds~~). Full lesson: [Lesson 038](../../01-beginner-grammar/adjectives/lesson-038-what-is-an-adjective.md).

## 7. The same word can do different jobs

This is a very important idea in English. **The job of a word depends on the sentence.**

| Word | As a thing word (noun) | As an action word (verb) |
|---|---|---|
| **water** | *I drink **water**.* | *I **water** the plants.* (= give water to) |
| **cook** | *My dad is a good **cook**.* (a person who cooks) | *My dad **cooks** dinner.* |
| **play** | *We watched a **play**.* (a show in a theatre) | *Children **play** in the park.* |
| **run** | *I went for a **run**.* | *I **run** every morning.* |

| Word | As a describing word (adjective) | As a thing word (noun) |
|---|---|---|
| **light** | *The bag is **light**.* (not heavy) | *Turn on the **light**.* |
| **cold** | *It is **cold**.* | *I have a **cold**.* (a small illness 🤧) |

**How do you know the job?** Look at the **position** and the words around it:

- After **a / the / my** → probably a **thing word**.
- After **I / you / they** → probably an **action word**.
- After **very**, or after **is** → probably a **describing word**.

## 8. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-008-001 — "The happy child runs."

- **Meaning in simple English:** One child feels good, and the child is running. 😀🏃
- **Situation:** You see a child in the park running and laughing.
- **Main lesson:** Finding a thing word, an action word and a describing word in one sentence.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The | happy | child | runs. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | helper word | **describing word** | **thing word** (person) | **action word** |

- **Word by word:**
  - **The** — helper word; it points to one child we can see. Capital **T** (first word).
  - **happy** — describing word. Test: *very happy* ✅. It tells what the child is like.
  - **child** — thing word (a person). Test: *a child* ✅. A *child* = a young person.
  - **runs** — action word. Test: *They run* ✅. The **-s** is there because it is **one** child ([Lesson 007](lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md): *My sister play**s***).
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Word order: **who → action** ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). Here the "who" is *the happy child* — three words together.
- **Why this order?** In English, the **describing word comes before the thing word**: *happy child*, not *child happy*.
- **Other versions:**
  - The **tired** child **sleeps**.
  - The **small** dog **barks**.
  - The **old** man **walks**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *The child happy runs.* — the describing word must come **before** *child*.
  - ❌ *The happy child run.* — one child → **runs**.
  - ✅ **The happy child runs.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + describing word + thing word + action word(-s).**
- **Quick check:** In "The small dog barks", which word is the describing word?

---

### EX-008-002 — "Ali cooks big dinners for his family."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Ali makes large meals for the people in his family. 🍲
- **Situation:** Someone is talking about Ali. He loves cooking.
- **Main lesson:** Many thing words in one sentence; a describing word before a thing word.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Ali | cooks | big dinners | for his family. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who | action | what | for whom |

- **Find the word types:**

  | Word | Type | Test |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ali | thing word (a person's name) | a name of a person |
  | cooks | action word | *I cook* ✅ |
  | big | describing word | *very big* ✅ |
  | dinners | thing word | *a dinner* ✅ |
  | for | helper word (shows who gets something) | — |
  | his | helper word (belongs to him) | — |
  | family | thing word | *my family* ✅ |

- **Word by word (important points):**
  - **cooks** — *cook* + **-s** (one person, Ali).
  - **big dinners** — **dinners** has **-s** (more than one dinner). But **big** has **no -s**. Describing words **never** take -s in English.
  - **his** — belongs to a man or boy (Ali). More in [Lesson 036](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-036-possessive-determiners.md).
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Names start with a capital letter: **A**li ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Ali cooks bigs dinners.* — describing words do not take -s.
  - ❌ *Ali cooks dinners big.* — the describing word goes **before** the thing word.
  - ✅ **Ali cooks big dinners for his family.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + action-s + describing word + thing words.** → *Sara makes beautiful cakes.*
- **Quick check:** Find the three thing words in this sentence.

---

### EX-008-003 — "I drink water. I water the plants."

- **Meaning in simple English:**
  1. I put water in my mouth and swallow it. 🥤
  2. I give water to the plants so they can grow. 🪴
- **Situation:** You are describing your morning: first you drink a glass of water; then you go into the garden.
- **Main lesson:** The **same word** (*water*) can be a **thing word** or an **action word**.
- **Sentence 1:**

  | I | drink | water. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | **action word** | **thing word** |

  - **water** here comes **after** the action *drink*. It is the **thing** I drink. Test: *the water* ✅.
- **Sentence 2:**

  | I | water | the plants. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | **action word** | thing words |

  - **water** here comes **right after "I"**. It is the **action**. Test: *I water* ✅ — it means "give water to".
  - **the plants** — **plants** = living green things that grow. **-s** = more than one.
- **How do we know?** **Position!** After *I* → action word. After an action word → thing word.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** English word order is very important ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). Here the order even tells us the **type** of word.
- **Other pairs:**
  - *I like this **book**.* (thing) / *I **book** a table at the restaurant.* (action = reserve)
  - *Give me a **call**.* (thing) / *I **call** my mother.* (action)
- **Quick check:** In "We **play** football", is *play* a thing word or an action word?

## 9. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a car red | a **red car** | describing word before the thing word |
| two bigs houses | two **big** houses | describing words never take -s |
| The child happy. | The child **is** happy. | after the thing word, we need **is** before the describing word |
| "*water* is always a thing word" | it can be an action word too | the job depends on the sentence |

## 10. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️

In English sentences, the **important words** (thing words, action words, describing words) are usually said **stronger** and **louder**. Small helper words are **weaker**.

> the **HAP**-py **CHILD** **RUNS**.
> a-li **COOKS** **BIG** **DIN**-ners for his **FAM**-i-ly.

Say these sentences. Make the big words strong.

## 11. Exercises

**A. Thing (T), action (A) or describing (D)?**

1. table
2. sleep
3. green
4. teacher
5. write
6. tired
7. music
8. open

**B. Put the words in the correct order.**

1. car / a / red
2. dog / the / small / barks
3. old / a / house

**C. Thing word or action word? (look at the bold word)**

1. I **call** my friend every day.
2. Thank you for your **call**.
3. They **play** in the garden.
4. We watched a **play** at the theatre.

**D. Correct the mistakes.**

1. I have two news phones.
2. She has a bag beautiful.

## 12. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **T** — *a table* ✅
2. **A** — *I sleep* ✅
3. **D** — *very green* ✅ / *It is green* ✅
4. **T** — *a teacher* ✅ (a person)
5. **A** — *I write* ✅
6. **D** — *very tired* ✅
7. **T** — *the music* ✅ (an idea / thing you can hear, but not touch)
8. **A** — *I open (the door)* ✅. *(Careful: **open** can also be a describing word: "The door is **open**." Here, alone, both are possible!)*

**B.**
1. **a red car** — helper word → describing word → thing word.
2. **The small dog barks.** — capital **T** at the start.
3. **an old house** — **old** starts with a vowel sound, so **a** changes to **an** ([Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)). *(Very good if you noticed this!)*

**C.**
1. **action** — after *I*.
2. **thing** — after *your*.
3. **action** — after *They*.
4. **thing** — after *a*.

**D.**
1. *I have two **new** phones.* — describing words never take -s. (*news* is a different word: information about events.)
2. *She has a **beautiful bag**.* — the describing word comes before the thing word.

## 13. Quick revision sheet

| Group | Grammar name | Job | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thing words | **noun** | names people, animals, places, things, ideas | *a / the / my* + word |
| Action words | **verb** | tells what someone does, or a state | *I / they* + word |
| Describing words | **adjective** | tells what something is like | *very* + word · *It is* + word |

- Describing word goes **before** a thing word: *a **big** house*.
- Describing words **never** take -s.
- **The same word can have different jobs**: *I drink **water**. / I **water** the plants.*

## 14. Memory trick

> **Noun = Name. Verb = Do. Adjective = Describe.** 🏷️🏃🎨
