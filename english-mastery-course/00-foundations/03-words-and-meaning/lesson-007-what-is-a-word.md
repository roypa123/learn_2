- What a **word** is.

## 3. What is a word?

> **A word is a small piece of language that has a meaning. You can say it or write it by itself.**

- **dog** 🐶 — a word. It has a meaning: an animal.
- **run** 🏃 — a word. It has a meaning: move fast with your legs.
- **happy** 😀 — a word. It has a meaning: feeling good.
- **d**, **o**, **g** — these are **letters**, not words. Alone, they have no meaning. Together, in this order, they make the word **dog**.

### Letters → word → sentence

```
letters        d + o + g          →  dog
word           dog                →  one idea
sentence       The dog is happy.  →  a complete idea
```

Words are the **bricks** of language 🧱 (remember the house picture in [Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)).

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **word** | a small piece of language with a meaning |
| **space** | the empty place between two written words |
| **meaning** | the idea a word gives you |
| **form** | the shape of a word; how it is written or said (*book* / *books*) |
| **dictionary** | a book or app that gives the meanings of words |
| **contraction** | two words joined into one short form: *I am → I'm* |

## 4. How can I see where a word starts and ends?

### In writing: spaces

We put a **space** between words.

> I␣like␣music.

That is **3 words**: *I*, *like*, *music*. Without spaces — *Ilikemusic* — it is very hard to read.

### In speaking: no spaces!

When people speak, they do **not** stop between words. The words join together like a chain:

> *I like it* → sounds like "**I-lai-kit**"

This is why listening is difficult at first. It is normal. You will practise it in the listening lessons. With time, your ears will learn to find the words.

## 5. Big ideas about words

### Idea 1 — Some words are very short, some are long

| Letters | Words |
|---|---|
| 1 | **I**, **a** |
| 2 | **is**, **in**, **on**, **to** |
| 3 | **cat**, **sun**, **run** |
| long | **information**, **beautiful**, **international** |

Short words like **a, the, is, in, to, and** are the **most used words in English**. They are small but very important.

### Idea 2 — One word can have more than one meaning

| Word | Meaning 1 | Meaning 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **light** | 💡 a lamp: *Turn on the light.* | not heavy: *The bag is light.* |
| **letter** | a written sign (A, B, C) | a message you send by post ✉️ |
| **watch** | ⌚ a small clock on your wrist | to look at something for some time: *watch TV* |
| **right** | correct ✅ | the opposite of left ➡️ |

How do you know which meaning? **Look at the other words around it.** The other words are called the **context**.

### Idea 3 — A word can change its form

The **main idea** stays the same, but the word changes a little:

| Base word | Changed forms |
|---|---|
| book | book**s** (more than one) |
| play | play**s**, play**ed**, play**ing** |
| happy | happi**er**, **un**happy, happi**ness** |

These endings and beginnings (**-s, -ed, -ing, un-**) give **extra information**: how many, when, or the opposite meaning. You will learn them step by step.

A group of words from the same base word is called a **word family**: *happy, unhappy, happiness, happily*.

### Idea 4 — Two words can join to make one short form (contractions)

| Full form | Short form | |
|---|---|---|
| I am | I**'**m | the **'** replaces the letter **a** |
| it is | it**'**s | replaces **i** |
| do not | don**'**t | replaces **o** |
| what is | what**'**s | replaces **i** |

A short form like this is called a **contraction**. When you count words, *I'm* is written as one word, but it **means two words**. Remember the **apostrophe ( ' )** from [Lesson 003](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md).

### Idea 5 — Some ideas need two (or more) words

> **ice cream** 🍦 · **bus stop** 🚏 · **post office** 🏤 · **good morning**

*Ice cream* is written as **two** words, but it is **one** thing. Later you will learn that these are called **compound nouns** ([Lesson 090](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-090-compound-nouns.md)).

## 6. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-007-001 — "Birds fly."

- **Meaning in simple English:** Birds can move through the air with their wings. 🐦
- **Situation:** A child asks: "What do birds do?" You answer.
- **Main lesson:** Even **two words** can make a complete sentence.
- **Count the words:** **2** — *Birds* (1), *fly* (2).
- **Word by word:**
  - **Birds** — animals with wings and feathers. The **-s** shows **more than one** bird. Capital **B** because it is the first word.
  - **fly** — the action: move through the air.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** A sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)). Here, **y** in *fly* works like a vowel and says "eye" ([Lesson 005](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
- **Why this is complete:** It tells us **who** (birds) and **what they do** (fly). That is enough for a complete idea. You will learn the names for these two parts — **subject** and **verb** — in [Lessons 011](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md) and [012](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md).
- **Other versions:**
  - Fish swim. 🐟
  - Babies cry. 👶
  - Dogs bark. 🐶
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Birdsfly.* — no space between the words.
  - ❌ *Fly birds.* — wrong order for this meaning.
  - ✅ **Birds fly.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Animals/people + action.**
- **Quick check:** How many words are in "Dogs bark."?

### EX-007-002 — "My sister plays football every day."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I have a sister. Each day, she plays the game of football. ⚽
- **Situation:** You are telling a friend about your family's hobbies.
- **Main lesson:** Counting words; seeing a word that changed its form (*plays*); two words for one idea (*every day*).
- **Count the words:** **6** — My (1) sister (2) plays (3) football (4) every (5) day (6).
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My sister | plays | football | every day. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | who | action | what | when / how often |

- **Word by word:**
  - **My** — belongs to me.
  - **sister** — a girl or woman who has the same parents as you.
  - **plays** — *play* + **s**. *Play* = do a game or sport. The **-s** is added because we talk about **one other person** (my sister). Remember *Tom eat**s*** in [Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)? Same rule. (Full lesson: [Lesson 043](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md).)
  - **football** — one word made from *foot* + *ball*. (In the USA, this game is called **soccer**.)
  - **every day** — two words together = each day. 📅
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Order **who → action → what** ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). Capital **M** for the first word ([Lesson 004](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Watch out — *every day* vs *everyday*:**
  - **every day** (two words) = each day: *I walk **every day**.*
  - **everyday** (one word) = normal, usual: *my **everyday** clothes.*
  - In this sentence we need **every day** (two words).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My sister play football every day.* — with one other person, add **-s**: *plays*.
  - ❌ *My sister plays football everyday.* — here we need two words: *every day*.
  - ✅ **My sister plays football every day.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Person + action-s + thing + every day.** → *My brother reads books every day.*
- **Quick check:** Which word changed its form in this sentence? What was added?

### EX-007-003 — "Please turn on the light. My bag is light."

- **Meaning in simple English:**
  1. Make the lamp work, so the room is bright. 💡
  2. My bag is not heavy. It is easy to carry. 🎒
- **Situation:** (1) The room is dark. (2) A friend wants to help you carry your bag. You say: "No thanks, my bag is light."
- **Main lesson:** The **same word** (*light*) can have **different meanings**. The **context** (words around it) tells you which.
- **Word by word (first sentence):**
  - **Please** — polite word.
  - **turn on** — two words, one idea: make a machine or lamp start working. (The opposite is **turn off**.)
  - **the light** — the lamp. Here *light* is a **thing**.
- **Word by word (second sentence):**
  - **My bag** — the bag that belongs to me.
  - **is** — joining word (like "=").
  - **light** — not heavy. Here *light* **describes** the bag.
- **How do we know the meaning?**
  - After **turn on the**, we expect a **thing** → *light* = lamp.
  - After **My bag is**, we expect a word that **describes** the bag → *light* = not heavy.
  - In [Lesson 008](lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md) you will learn more about **thing words** and **describing words**.
- **Pronunciation:** *light* — the **gh** is silent ([Lesson 006](../02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)): "lite".
- **Quick check:** In "This box is light", does *light* mean a lamp or not heavy?

## 7. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ilikemusic. | I like music. | Put spaces between words. |
| I walk everyday. | I walk **every day**. | "every day" = each day (2 words). |
| Thinking every word has only one meaning | Look at the **context** | *light, watch, right* have more than one meaning. |
| I'am happy. | I'**m** happy. / I **am** happy. | The apostrophe replaces the **a** of *am*. |

## 8. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Say these sentences **slowly**, word by word. Then say them **naturally**, joining the words:

| Slow | Natural (joined) |
|---|---|
| I · like · it. | "I-lai-kit." |
| Turn · it · on. | "Tur-ni-ton." |
| What · is · this? | "What's this?" |

## 9. Exercises

**A. How many words?**

1. I am happy.
2. The children play in the park.
3. I'm tired.
4. Good morning, everyone.

**B. Put spaces between the words.**

1. Iliveinasmalltown.
2. Shereadsbooks.

**C. Which meaning? (a) or (b)**

1. I can't find my **watch**. — (a) a small clock (b) look at
2. Your answer is **right**. — (a) correct (b) the opposite of left
3. I **watch** films at night. — (a) a small clock (b) look at

**D. Write the full form.**

1. I'm
2. it's
3. don't

**E. every day or everyday?**

1. I drink coffee ___.
2. These are my ___ shoes.

## 10. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. **3** (I · am · happy)
2. **6** (The · children · play · in · the · park)
3. **2** written words (I'm · tired) — but *I'm* means **two** words (I am), so the meaning has 3 words.
4. **3** (Good · morning · everyone). The comma is **not** a word; it is a punctuation mark.

**B.**
1. **I live in a small town.**
2. **She reads books.**

**C.**
1. **(a)** — "my watch" is a thing you can lose.
2. **(a)** — an answer can be correct or wrong.
3. **(b)** — *I watch* is an action.

**D.**
1. **I am** · 2. **it is** (or *it has*, but you will learn that later) · 3. **do not**

**E.**
1. **every day** — each day.
2. **everyday** — normal, for usual days.

## 11. Quick revision sheet

- A **word** = a small piece of language with a meaning.
- Letters → words → sentences.
- In writing, **spaces** separate words. In speech, words **join** together.
- One word can have **many meanings** — the **context** decides.
- Words can change **form**: *book → books*, *play → played*.
- **Contractions** join two words: *I am → I'm*.
- Some ideas need **two words**: *ice cream, every day*.
