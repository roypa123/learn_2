## 3. Two groups of letters

We divide the 26 letters into two groups.

### Vowel letters (5)

> **a · e · i · o · u**

When you say a vowel sound, your mouth is **open**. The air comes out **freely**. Nothing stops it. Try it: say "aaaa", "oooo". Your tongue and lips do not block the air.

### Consonant letters (21)

> **b c d f g h j k l m n p q r s t v w x y z**

When you say a consonant sound, something **blocks or squeezes** the air a little: your **lips**, your **tongue**, or your **teeth**. Try it:

- Say "**b**" — your lips close, then open. 👄
- Say "**t**" — your tongue touches behind your top teeth.
- Say "**s**" — air squeezes between your tongue and teeth: "sssss" 🐍.

### The special letter: y

**y** can work as a **consonant** or a **vowel**:

| Word | y works like… | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **y**es, **y**ellow | a consonant | it starts the word with a "y" sound |
| m**y**, fl**y** | a vowel | it sounds like "eye" |
| bab**y**, happ**y** | a vowel | it sounds like "ee" |

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **vowel** | a letter (a, e, i, o, u) or sound made with an **open** mouth |
| **consonant** | a letter or sound where the lips, tongue or teeth **block** the air a little |
| **syllable** | a "beat" of a word; each beat has a vowel sound: *ap-ple* (2 beats) |

## 4. Why do vowels and consonants matter?

### Reason 1 — Every word has a vowel sound

Almost every English word has at least one vowel sound. The vowel is like the **heart** of each beat (syllable).

| Word | Beats (syllables) | Vowels |
|---|---|---|
| cat | 1: cat | **a** |
| ap-ple | 2: ap · ple | **a**, **e** |
| ba-na-na | 3: ba · na · na | **a**, **a**, **a** |
| sky | 1: sky | **y** (works as a vowel) |

Clap your hands for each beat: *ba* 👏 *na* 👏 *na* 👏.

### Reason 2 — "a" or "an"? (first look)

**a** and **an** both mean **one**. We use them before a word for one thing.

- Use **a** before a word that starts with a **consonant sound**: **a** **b**anana, **a** **c**at, **a** **d**og.
- Use **an** before a word that starts with a **vowel sound**: **an** **a**pple, **an** **e**gg, **an** **o**range.

Why? Say "a apple" — there are two vowel sounds next to each other: *a-a*. It is hard to say. "**An** apple" is smoother: the **n** joins them → *a-napple*.

> ⚠️ **It is the SOUND, not the letter, that matters.** A few words break the "letter" rule:
> - **an hour** — the **h** is silent; the word starts with the vowel sound "ow".
> - **a university** — the word starts with a "y" sound ("you-niversity"), which is a consonant sound.
>
> Don't worry about these now. You will study them in [Lesson 032](../../01-beginner-grammar/articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md).

### Reason 3 — Spelling rules

Many English spelling rules depend on vowels and consonants. For example, how to add **-s**, **-ing** or **-ed** to words. You will meet these later (Lessons [024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md), [043](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md), [063](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-063-ed-spelling-and-sounds.md)).

## 5. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-005-001 — "I have an orange."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I own one orange (the fruit 🍊). It is with me.
- **Situation:** Your friend is hungry. You say you have a fruit.
- **Main lesson:** **an** before a vowel sound.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | have | an orange. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | action / owning | what (one orange) |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — the person talking. Always capital ([Lesson 004](lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md), Rule 2).
  - **have** — means "own" or "hold". (More in [Lesson 040](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-040-have-and-have-got.md).)
  - **an** — means "one". We use **an** because **orange** starts with the vowel letter **o** and a vowel sound.
  - **orange** — a round fruit. The word has 2 beats: **or · ange**.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Order: **who → action → what**, just like *Tom eats an apple* ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)).
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *I have a orange.* — **orange** starts with a vowel sound → use **an**.
  - ❌ *I have orange.* — For **one** fruit, we need **an**.
  - ✅ **I have an orange.**
- **Pronunciation:** "an orange" joins together → *a-NOR-inj*.
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I have an + vowel-sound word.** → *I have an egg. I have an idea.* (💡 **idea** = a thought, a plan in your head.)
- **Quick check:** Why do we say *an orange* and not *a orange*?

### EX-005-002 — "This is a banana."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The thing near me is one banana. 🍌
- **Situation:** You are showing a fruit to a child or a friend.
- **Main lesson:** **a** before a consonant sound.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | This | is | a banana. |
  |---|---|---|
  | the thing near me | joining word | what it is |

- **Word by word:**
  - **This** — the thing **near** me. (More in [Lesson 019](../06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md).)
  - **is** — joining word (like "="). *This = a banana.*
  - **a** — means "one". We use **a** because **banana** starts with the consonant letter **b** (a consonant sound).
  - **banana** — a long yellow fruit. 3 beats: **ba · na · na**.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** **is** also joined the parts in *My name **is** Ana* ([Lesson 003](lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)).
- **Other versions:**
  - This is **a** book. (b = consonant)
  - This is **a** pen. (p = consonant)
  - This is **an** egg. (e = vowel)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *This is an banana.* — **b** is a consonant sound → **a**.
  - ❌ *This a banana.* — the joining word **is** is missing.
  - ✅ **This is a banana.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **This is a / an + thing.**
- **Quick check:** *This is ___ umbrella.* — a or an?

### EX-005-003 — "An elephant and a mouse are friends."

- **Meaning in simple English:** One elephant 🐘 and one mouse 🐭 like each other. They are friends.
- **Situation:** A sentence from a children's story.
- **Main lesson:** **an** and **a** in the same sentence; we choose for **each** word.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | An elephant and a mouse | are | friends. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who (two animals) | joining word | what they are |

- **Word by word:**
  - **An** — **capital A** (first word of the sentence). **an** because **elephant** starts with the vowel **e**.
  - **elephant** — a very big grey animal with a long nose (trunk).
  - **and** — joins the two animals.
  - **a** — before **mouse** (m = consonant).
  - **mouse** — a very small animal.
  - **are** — a joining word. We use **are** (not **is**) because we are talking about **two** animals. (You will learn *am / is / are* in [Lesson 018](../06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md).)
  - **friends** — more than one friend. The **-s** means "more than one". (More in [Lesson 023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md).)
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Capital letter for the first word ([Lesson 004](lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md), Rule 1).
- **Why this is correct:** Each animal gets its own **a / an**, chosen by **its own first sound**.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *An elephant and an mouse are friends.* — **mouse** starts with a consonant → **a**.
  - ❌ *An elephant and a mouse is friends.* — two animals → **are**.
  - ✅ **An elephant and a mouse are friends.**
- **Quick check:** Choose: ___ egg and ___ cake.

## 6. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a apple | **an** apple | vowel sound → an |
| an book | **a** book | consonant sound → a |
| a egg | **an** egg | vowel sound → an |
| "y is always a consonant" | **y** can be a vowel too (*my, happy*) | it depends on the sound |

## 7. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️

1. Say the 5 vowels with your mouth open: **a, e, i, o, u**.
2. Feel the consonants: **b** (lips), **t** (tongue), **s** (teeth).
3. Clap the beats: *cat* (1), *apple* (2), *banana* (3), *elephant* (3: el · e · phant).
4. Say smoothly: **an apple** (a-napple), **an egg** (a-negg), **an orange** (a-norange).

## 8. Exercises

**A. Vowel (V) or consonant (C)?**

1. e
2. k
3. o
4. s
5. u

**B. a or an?**

1. ___ cat
2. ___ apple
3. ___ ice cream
4. ___ dog
5. ___ umbrella
6. ___ house

**C. How many beats (syllables)?**

1. dog
2. water
3. computer

**D. Correct the sentences.**

1. I have a egg.
2. This is an pen.

## 9. Answers (with explanations)

**A.** 1. **V** · 2. **C** · 3. **V** · 4. **C** · 5. **V**

**B.**
1. **a** cat — **c** is a consonant sound.
2. **an** apple — **a** is a vowel sound.
3. **an** ice cream — **i** is a vowel sound ("eye").
4. **a** dog — **d** is a consonant.
5. **an** umbrella — **u** here is a vowel sound ("um").
6. **a** house — **h** is a consonant sound here (we hear the "h").

**C.**
1. dog → **1** (dog)
2. water → **2** (wa · ter)
3. computer → **3** (com · pu · ter)

**D.**
1. *I have **an** egg.* — **egg** starts with a vowel sound.
2. *This is **a** pen.* — **pen** starts with a consonant sound.

## 10. Quick revision sheet

- **Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u — open mouth, free air.
- **Consonants:** the other 21 letters — lips, tongue or teeth block the air.
- **y** can be both.
- Every beat (syllable) has a vowel sound.
- **a** + consonant sound: *a cat* · **an** + vowel sound: *an apple*.
- The **sound** decides, not only the letter (*an hour*, *a university*).
