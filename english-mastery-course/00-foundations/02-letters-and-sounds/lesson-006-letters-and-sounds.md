# Lesson 006 — Letters Are Not Sounds: Spelling vs Speaking

## 1. What you will learn

- The difference between a **letter** (what you see) and a **sound** (what you hear).
- Why English has **26 letters** but about **44 sounds**.
- One letter can make **different sounds**.
- One sound can be written with **different letters**.
- **Silent letters**: letters we write but do not say.
- A first look at **sound symbols** like **/k/** (you will learn more in the pronunciation section).

## 2. Remember from before 🔁

- Each letter has a **name**: C = "see", G = "jee" ([Lesson 003](lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)).
- We said: the **name** of a letter is not always the **sound** in a word. Today we learn this properly.
- **Vowels** (a, e, i, o, u) are made with an open mouth; **consonants** block the air a little ([Lesson 005](lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).

## 3. Letters and sounds are different things

| | A letter | A sound |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | a sign you **see** and **write** | a noise you **hear** and **say** |
| Example | **c** | the "k" noise at the start of *cat* |
| How many in English? | **26** | about **44** (it depends a little on the accent) |

In some languages, every letter always makes the same sound. If you can read the letters, you can say the word.

**English is not like this.** English spelling is old. The way people speak changed over hundreds of years, but the spelling often stayed the same. So, sometimes, the spelling and the sound do not match.

> 💡 **This is not your fault!** Even children who speak English as their first language find spelling difficult. The answer: **learn the sound of every new word, not only its letters.**

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **sound** | a noise you hear when someone speaks |
| **silent letter** | a letter we write but do not say: the **k** in *know* |
| **pronounce** / **pronunciation** | how you say a word / the way a word is said |
| **sound symbol** | a special sign that shows one sound, written between slashes: **/k/** |

### Sound symbols — a first look

Because letters do not always show the sound, teachers and dictionaries use **sound symbols**. We write them between two slashes **/ /**.

- **/k/** = the sound at the start of *cat*, *key*, *school*.
- **/s/** = the sound at the start of *sun*, *city*.
- **/iː/** = the long "ee" sound in *see*, *eat*. (The **ː** means "long".)

These symbols are part of the **IPA** (International Phonetic Alphabet) — a list of symbols for the sounds of all languages. You do **not** need to learn them all now. Dictionaries use them, so it is useful to know they exist. More in [sounds-001](../../08-pronunciation-and-listening/sounds/sounds-001-english-vowel-and-consonant-sounds.md).

## 4. Idea 1 — One letter, different sounds

| Letter | Sound 1 | Sound 2 | Sound 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **c** | **c**at → /k/ | **c**ity → /s/ | |
| **g** | **g**o → /g/ | **g**ym → /dʒ/ (like "j") | |
| **a** | c**a**t → short "a" | c**a**ke → "ay" | c**a**r → "ah" |
| **o** | h**o**t → short "o" | h**o**me → "oh" | d**o** → "oo" |
| **s** | **s**un → /s/ | i**s** → /z/ | |

**A helpful pattern for c and g:** before **e, i, y**, they often make the "soft" sound:

- c + e / i / y → /s/: **c**ity, **c**entre, bi**c**ycle
- g + e / i / y → "j": **g**ym, **g**iant, oran**g**e

(But there are exceptions: **g**ive, **g**et, **g**irl use the hard /g/.)

## 5. Idea 2 — One sound, different spellings

The **long "ee" sound /iː/**:

> s**ee** · **ea**t · m**e** · k**ey** · p**ie**ce · mach**i**ne

The **/k/ sound**:

> **c**at · **k**ey · ba**ck** · s**ch**ool · **q**ueen (qu = /kw/)

The **/f/ sound**:

> **f**ish · **ph**one · lau**gh**

## 6. Idea 3 — Silent letters

Some letters are written but **not said**. In the table, the silent letter is in **bold**.

| Word | Silent letter | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| **k**now | k | "no" |
| **k**nife | k | "nife" |
| **w**rite | w | "rite" |
| **h**our | h | "our" |
| lam**b** | b | "lam" |
| ha**l**f | l | "haf" (UK: "hahf") |
| We**d**n**e**sday | the first **d** and the second **e** | "WENZ-day" |
| cas**t**le | t | "KAS-ul" (UK: "KAH-sul") |

> 💡 Remember [Lesson 005](lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md): we say **an hour** because the **h** is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound.

## 7. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-006-001 — "The cat likes the city."

- **Meaning in simple English:** The cat is happy in the city. It enjoys living there.
- **Situation:** Your cat moved with you from a village to a big city. It is happy there.
- **Main lesson:** The same letter **c** makes two different sounds.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | The cat | likes | the city. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | action / feeling | what |

- **Word by word (with sounds):**
  - **The** — a small word that points to one known thing. (More in [Lesson 033](../../01-beginner-grammar/articles-and-determiners/lesson-033-the-article-the.md).) The **th** makes one sound, not two.
  - **cat** — **c** = /k/ (hard sound), because **a** comes after it.
  - **likes** — *like* + **s**. *Like* means to enjoy something or feel good about it. The **e** at the end of *like* is **silent** — but it changes the **i** to say "eye".
  - **city** — **c** = /s/ (soft sound), because **i** comes after it. And the **y** at the end says "ee".
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Order: **who → action → what** ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). And **y** can work as a vowel ([Lesson 005](lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
- **Why this is important:** If you read **c** always as "k", you would say "kity". ❌ That is wrong. Look at the next letter.
- **Wrong pronunciation:**
  - ❌ "The **s**at likes the **k**ity."
  - ✅ "The **k**at likes the **s**ity." (/k/ … /s/)
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **The + animal/person + likes + the + thing.** → *The dog likes the park. The baby likes the music.*
- **Quick check:** In *centre* and *cup*, which **c** sounds like /s/?

### EX-006-002 — "I know the answer."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I have the correct information. I can answer the question.
- **Situation:** In class, the teacher asks a question. You put up your hand. ✋
- **Main lesson:** Silent letters.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | know | the answer. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | action (in the mind) | what |

- **Word by word (with sounds):**
  - **I** — "eye". Always a capital.
  - **know** — the **k** is silent. It sounds exactly like **no**! The **w** is also not said as a separate sound. *To know* = to have information in your head.
  - **the** — points to one specific answer (the answer to this question).
  - **answer** — the **w** is silent: "AN-ser". An *answer* is what you say or write after a question.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** A **sentence** gives a complete idea ([Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md)). Capital **I** ([Lesson 004](lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Interesting:** *know* and *no* sound **the same** but have **different spellings and meanings**. Words like this are called **homophones** ("same sound"). Other pairs: *write / right*, *hour / our*, *see / sea*.
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ Saying "**k**-now" with a "k" sound.
  - ❌ Writing *I no the answer.* — **no** is a different word (the opposite of *yes*).
  - ✅ **I know the answer.**
- **Natural speech:** Often people just say **"I know!"**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I know + thing / person.** → *I know her. I know the way.*
- **Quick check:** Which two letters are silent in "know"... and which one in "answer"?

### EX-006-003 — "Please eat the green beans."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I am asking you nicely to eat the green vegetables. 🫛
- **Situation:** A parent at dinner. The child is not eating the vegetables.
- **Main lesson:** One sound, different spellings: the long **/iː/** sound.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Please | eat | the green beans. |
  |---|---|---|
  | polite word | action | what |

- **Word by word (with sounds):**
  - **Please** — **ea** = /iː/. The **e** at the end is silent. The **s** sounds like /z/: "pleez".
  - **eat** — **ea** = /iː/: "eet".
  - **the** — points to the beans on the plate.
  - **green** — **ee** = /iː/: the colour of grass. 🟢
  - **beans** — **ea** = /iː/; the **s** = /z/: "beenz". *Beans* are a type of vegetable. The **s** means "more than one".
- **Count:** 4 words have the **same** /iː/ sound — but it is written **ea** (please, eat, beans) and **ee** (green).
- **Remember from before 🔁:** Sentences that ask someone to do something can start with the action ("Please say that again" in [Lesson 002](../01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)).
- **Watch out:** **ea** does not *always* sound /iː/: *br**ea**d* (short "e"), *gr**ea**t* ("ay"). This is why we learn each word's sound.
- **Wrong pronunciation:**
  - ❌ "ple-ase" or "e-at" (saying two separate vowel sounds)
  - ✅ "pleez", "eet"
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Please + action + thing.** → *Please drink the water. Please read the book.*
- **Quick check:** Find the /iː/ sound in: *sea, bed, tree, read.*

## 8. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reading every letter | Learn the **sound** of each word | English has silent letters |
| "kity" for *city* | "sity" | c before i = /s/ |
| "k-nife" | "nife" | silent k |
| *I no him.* | *I **know** him.* | know / no sound the same, mean different things |
| Wed-nes-day (3 parts) | "WENZ-day" | silent letters |

## 9. How to learn the sound of a new word

1. Listen to it (a teacher, a video, or a text-to-speech tool).
2. Look in a dictionary: it shows the sound symbols, e.g. *know* /nəʊ/ (UK) or /noʊ/ (US).
3. Say it out loud 5 times.
4. Write it once, and say it while writing.

## 10. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️

Say these pairs. Feel the difference:

- **c**at /k/ — **c**ity /s/
- **g**o /g/ — **g**ym "j"
- **s**un /s/ — i**s** /z/

Say these words without the silent letter: **know, knife, write, hour, lamb, answer.**

## 11. Exercises

**A. Which sound does c make? Write /k/ or /s/.**

1. car
2. city
3. cup
4. centre
5. bicycle

**B. Find the silent letter.**

1. knee
2. wrong
3. hour
4. climb

**C. Which word has a different vowel sound?**

1. see / tea / bread / me
2. cake / name / day / cat

**D. Choose the correct word.**

1. I (no / know) the answer.
2. Please (right / write) your name.
3. This is (our / hour) house.

## 12. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. car → **/k/** (a after c)
2. city → **/s/** (i after c)
3. cup → **/k/** (u after c)
4. centre → **/s/** (e after c)
5. bicycle → **/s/** for the first c, because **y** comes after it: "BY-si-kul". *(The second c comes before **l**, so it is hard: /k/.)*

**B.**
1. **k**nee → "nee"
2. **w**rong → "rong"
3. **h**our → "our"
4. clim**b** → "clime"

**C.**
1. **bread** — *ea* here is a short "e". The others have the long /iː/.
2. **cat** — short "a". The others say "ay".

**D.**
1. **know** — to have information. (*no* is the opposite of *yes*.)
2. **write** — to put words on paper. (*right* = correct, or the opposite of left.)
3. **our** — belongs to us. (*hour* = 60 minutes.)

## 13. Quick revision sheet

- **Letter** = what you see. **Sound** = what you hear.
- 26 letters, about 44 sounds.
- One letter → different sounds: **c**at / **c**ity.
- One sound → different letters: s**ee**, **ea**t, m**e**.
- Silent letters: **k**now, **w**rite, **h**our, lam**b**.
- Sound symbols go between slashes: **/k/**, **/iː/**.
- Always learn **how a new word sounds**.

## 14. Memory trick

> **"See it, hear it, say it."** 👀👂🗣️ — for every new word.
