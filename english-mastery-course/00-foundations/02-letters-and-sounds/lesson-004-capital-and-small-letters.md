## 3. Two forms of every letter

Every English letter has two forms:

| Capital (big) | Small |
|---|---|
| A | a |
| B | b |
| G | g |
| Q | q |
| R | r |

- **Capital letters** are also called **uppercase** letters or **big letters**.
- **Small letters** are also called **lowercase** letters.

Most writing uses **small letters**. We use **capital letters** only in special places.

### Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **capital letter** (uppercase) | the big form: **A, B, C** |
| **small letter** (lowercase) | the small form: **a, b, c** |
| **name** (proper noun) | the special name of one person, place or thing: *Ana, Paris, Monday* |

> The grammar word for a special name is **proper noun**. You do not need this word yet. You will learn it in [Lesson 022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md).

## 4. When do we use a capital letter?

### Rule 1 — The first word of a sentence

> **T**he cat is black.
> **M**y name is Ana.

Every sentence starts with a capital letter. (A **sentence** is a group of words with a complete idea. More in [Lesson 009](../04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md).)

### Rule 2 — The word "I"

> Today **I** am happy.
> My friend and **I** are learning English.

The word **I** (meaning *me, the person talking*) is **always** a capital letter. Even in the middle of a sentence. This is special to English.

### Rule 3 — Names of people

> **S**am, **M**aria, **A**li, **M**r **S**mith

### Rule 4 — Names of places (cities, countries, rivers, streets…)

> **L**ondon, **I**ndia, **B**razil, the **N**ile, **P**ark **S**treet

### Rule 5 — Languages and nationalities

> **E**nglish, **S**panish, **A**rabic
> She is **F**rench. (= she is from France)

### Rule 6 — Days of the week and months

> **M**onday, **T**uesday, **W**ednesday, **T**hursday, **F**riday, **S**aturday, **S**unday
> **J**anuary, **F**ebruary, **M**arch, **A**pril, **M**ay, **J**une, **J**uly, **A**ugust, **S**eptember, **O**ctober, **N**ovember, **D**ecember

> ⚠️ But **seasons** usually use small letters: *spring, summer, autumn (US: fall), winter.*

### Rule 7 — Important words in titles (names of books, films, songs)

> *The **L**ion **K**ing*, *Harry **P**otter*

(Small words like *the, of, and, in* in the middle of a title usually stay small: *The Lord **of** the Rings*.)

### When NOT to use capitals

Ordinary words in the middle of a sentence use small letters:

> ❌ I have a **B**ig **D**og.
> ✅ I have a **b**ig **d**og.

> 💡 **Writing everything in CAPITALS** (LIKE THIS) is possible on signs, but in messages it can look like **shouting**. Avoid it in normal writing.

## 5. Letters that look similar ⚠️

Some small letters look like each other. Write them carefully.

| Letters | Tip |
|---|---|
| **b** and **d** | **b** has its round part on the **right**; **d** has its round part on the **left**. Think of the word **bed**: the **b** and **d** are like the two ends of a bed. 🛏️ |
| **p** and **q** | **p** round part on the right; **q** round part on the left. |
| **m** and **n** | **m** has two "hills", **n** has one. |
| **u** and **n** | **u** is open at the top, **n** is open at the bottom. |
| **I** (capital i) and **l** (small L) | In some fonts they look almost the same. Read the whole word to decide. |
| **O** (letter) and **0** (zero) | The letter O is usually rounder; zero is often thinner. |

## 6. Sentences explained slowly

### EX-004-001 — "I live in London."

- **Meaning in simple English:** My home is in the city of London.
- **Situation:** Someone asks where you live. You tell them your city.
- **Main lesson:** Capital letters for **I** and for **place names**.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | I | live | in London. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who | action | where |

- **Word by word:**
  - **I** — the person talking. **Capital** (Rule 1: first word, and Rule 2: always capital).
  - **live** — the action. *To live in* a place = to have your home there.
  - **in** — a small word showing **place**: inside an area. (More in [Lesson 049](../../01-beginner-grammar/prepositions/lesson-049-prepositions-of-place-in-on-under.md).)
  - **London** — the name of a city. **Capital L** (Rule 4).
- **Count the capital letters:** **I**, **L** → 2 capitals.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** The order is **who → action → more information**, like "Tom eats an apple" in [Lesson 001](../01-what-is-language/lesson-001-what-is-a-language.md).
- **Other versions:**
  - I live in **C**airo.
  - I live in **T**okyo.
  - I live in a small town. (No capital for *small* or *town* — they are ordinary words.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *i live in london.* — **I** and **London** need capitals.
  - ❌ *I Live In London.* — **live** and **in** are ordinary words; they should be small.
  - ✅ **I live in London.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **I live in + place.**
- **Quick check:** Write the sentence with your city.

### EX-004-002 — "My friend Maria is from Spain."

- **Meaning in simple English:** I have a friend. Her name is Maria. She was born in Spain, or her family is from Spain.
- **Situation:** You are telling someone about your friend.
- **Main lesson:** Capitals at the start, for a person's name and for a country.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | My friend Maria | is | from Spain. |
  |---|---|---|
  | who (the person we talk about) | joining word | where she comes from |

- **Word by word:**
  - **My** — belongs to me. **Capital M** because it is the first word (Rule 1).
  - **friend** — a person you like and know well. Small letter: it is an ordinary word.
  - **Maria** — the friend's name. **Capital M** (Rule 3).
  - **is** — a joining word (like "="). *Maria = from Spain.*
  - **from** — shows where someone or something started or was born.
  - **Spain** — a country. **Capital S** (Rule 4).
- **Count the capitals:** **M**y, **M**aria, **S**pain → 3.
- **Remember from before 🔁:** "My name **is** Ana" ([Lesson 003](lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)) also uses **is** to join.
- **Other versions:**
  - My friend Maria is **S**panish. (Rule 5: nationality → capital.)
  - My friend Maria speaks **S**panish. (Rule 5: language → capital.)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *My Friend Maria is from Spain.* — **friend** is an ordinary word; no capital.
  - ❌ *my friend maria is from spain.* — needs 3 capitals.
  - ✅ **My friend Maria is from Spain.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Name + is from + country.**
- **Quick check:** Why does *friend* have a small letter but *Maria* has a capital letter?

---

### EX-004-003 — "Today is Monday, and it is very cold."

- **Meaning in simple English:** This day is Monday. The weather is very cold.
- **Situation:** You are talking about the day and the weather.
- **Main lesson:** Capitals for days. No capitals for ordinary words in the middle.
- **Sentence parts:**

  | Today is Monday | , and | it is very cold. |
  |---|---|---|
  | first idea | joining word | second idea |

- **Word by word:**
  - **Today** — this day. **Capital T** (first word, Rule 1).
  - **is** — joining word.
  - **Monday** — a day of the week. **Capital M** (Rule 6).
  - **,** — a **comma**. A small pause in writing, before *and* here. (Commas come later, in [Lesson 087](../../01-beginner-grammar/punctuation/lesson-087-commas-in-lists-and-capital-letters.md).)
  - **and** — a joining word. It joins two ideas. (More in [Lesson 054](../../01-beginner-grammar/conjunctions/lesson-054-and-but-or.md).)
  - **it** — here it means "the weather". English uses **it** to talk about weather: *It is cold. It is raining.*
  - **very** — makes a word stronger: cold → **very** cold.
  - **cold** — not warm. 🥶
- **Count the capitals:** **T**oday, **M**onday → 2. (*it* in the middle is small!)
- **Wrong versions:**
  - ❌ *Today is monday, and It is very cold.* — *Monday* needs a capital; *it* in the middle does not.
  - ✅ **Today is Monday, and it is very cold.**
- **Pattern you can reuse:** **Today is + day.** / **It is + weather word.** → *Today is Friday. It is hot.*
- **Quick check:** Why is *it* small in this sentence?

## 7. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| i am happy. | **I** am happy. | 1 and 2 |
| my sister lives in paris. | **M**y sister lives in **P**aris. | 1 and 4 |
| I speak english on mondays. | I speak **E**nglish on **M**ondays. | 5 and 6 |
| I like Summer. | I like **s**ummer. | seasons are usually small |
| I have a New Phone. | I have a **n**ew **p**hone. | ordinary words are small |

## 9. Exercises

**A. Capital or small? Rewrite correctly.**

1. tom lives in new york.
2. my birthday is in june.
3. today i am tired.
4. she speaks arabic and french.
5. I Have A Red Car.

**B. Which word needs a capital letter?**

1. dog / china / book
2. tuesday / table / tree
3. water / winter / william

**C. Find the mistake (b/d, p/q).**

1. I have a reb pen. (should be *red*)
2. The ped is big. (should be *bed*)

---

## 10. Answers (with explanations)

**A.**
1. ***T**om lives in **N**ew **Y**ork.* — first word + person's name (Tom); place name has two words, so both get capitals (New York).
2. ***M**y birthday is in **J**une.* — first word; month.
3. ***T**oday **I** am tired.* — first word; **I** is always capital.
4. ***S**he speaks **A**rabic and **F**rench.* — first word; languages.
5. *I **h**ave **a** **r**ed **c**ar.* — only **I** needs a capital here. The other words are ordinary.

**B.**
1. **china** → **China** (a country). *(Note: "china" with a small c is also an ordinary English word for plates and cups made from a special white material — but here we mean the country.)*
2. **tuesday** → **Tuesday** (a day).
3. **william** → **William** (a name). *winter* is a season → small.

**C.**
1. *reb* → *re**d*** — the round part of **d** is on the left.
2. *ped* → ***b**ed* — the tall line of **b** goes **up**; the line of **p** goes **down**, below the writing line.

## 11. Quick revision sheet

Use a **capital letter** for:

1. the first word of a sentence
2. **I**
3. names of people
4. names of places
5. languages and nationalities
6. days and months (not seasons)
7. important words in titles

Everything else → **small letters**.

## 12. Memory trick

> **Capitals are for STARTS, "I", and NAMES.** 😊
> - **Start** of a sentence.
> - The word **I**.
> - **Names** of everything special: people, places, languages, days, months, titles.
