# Lesson 027 — What Is a Pronoun? Subject Pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)

## 1. Learning objectives

1. Say what a **pronoun** is and **why** we use it.
2. Know the 7 **subject pronouns**: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they*.
3. Choose the right pronoun for people, animals, things, groups and ideas.
4. Match the pronoun with the **verb**: *I am / he is / they are*, *she works / they work*.
5. Avoid the 3 big mistakes: **repeating** the subject, **dropping** the subject, **unclear** pronouns.
6. Use **singular *they*** and **empty *it*** (weather, time).

## 2. Why this topic matters

Pronouns are among the **most common words** in English: *I, you, it, he, they* appear in almost every conversation. They make language short and smooth. Without them we'd say: *"Maria is my friend. Maria lives in Rome. Maria works in a bank."* — very repetitive! With pronouns: *"Maria is my friend. **She** lives in Rome. **She** works in a bank."*

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- The **subject** is who/what the sentence is about, usually **before the verb** ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- English **needs** a subject: ***It** is cold* ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- *I am · he/she/it is · you/we/they are* ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- Singular → *it*, plural → *they* ([L-023](../nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).
- You've already used pronouns many times — now you learn the full system.

## 4. Simple introduction

A **pronoun** is like a **nickname** for a noun. Once everyone knows who or what we are talking about, we use the short word instead of repeating the noun.

> *My **brother** is 20. **He** is a student.* → **He** = my brother
> *I bought a **phone**. **It** is very fast.* → **It** = the phone

## 5. Definitions

> **A pronoun is a word that takes the place of a noun (or a noun phrase).**
>
> **A subject pronoun** is a pronoun used as the **subject** of a sentence — the doer, before the verb.

"Pro-noun" = "for a noun" (it stands **for** a noun).

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **pronoun** | a word that replaces a noun | *she, it, they* |
| **subject pronoun** | a pronoun in the subject position | ***They** live here.* |
| **refer to** | point to / mean | *"He" refers to my brother.* |
| **reference** | the noun a pronoun points to (also called the **antecedent**) | *my brother* |
| **person** (grammar) | 1st = the speaker (*I, we*) · 2nd = the listener (*you*) · 3rd = others (*he, she, it, they*) | |

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 The 7 subject pronouns

| | Singular (one) | Plural (more than one) |
|---|---|---|
| **1st person** (the speaker) | **I** | **we** |
| **2nd person** (the listener) | **you** | **you** |
| **3rd person** (others) | **he** (male) · **she** (female) · **it** (thing, animal, idea) | **they** |

**Notes:**
- **I** is always a **capital** letter ([L-004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **you** is the **same** for one person and many people. *Are **you** ready?* (to one friend, or to a whole class). Informal plurals exist: *you guys* (US, informal), *you all / y'all* (southern US), *you lot* (UK, informal).
- **we** includes the speaker: *we* = I + other people.
- **they** is the plural for **everything**: people, animals and things.

### 7.2 Choosing the right pronoun

| Noun | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| *my father, Tom, the man, my son* | **he** |
| *my mother, Ana, the woman, my daughter* | **she** |
| *the phone, the city, the idea, the weather* | **it** |
| *a dog, a cat* (unknown animal) | **it** |
| *my dog Max* (a pet we know) | **he** (people often use *he/she* for their pets) |
| *the baby* (sex unknown) | **he / she** if you know; *it* is possible but can sound cold; ***they*** is common |
| *my friends, the children, the books* | **they** |
| *Tom and I, my wife and I* | **we** |
| *you and Tom* | **you** (plural) |
| *a person, someone, the teacher* (we don't know if man or woman) | **they** (singular *they* — §7.6) |

### 7.3 Pronoun + verb agreement

The pronoun decides the verb form:

| Pronoun | *be* | Normal verb (present) |
|---|---|---|
| I | **am** | work |
| you | **are** | work |
| he / she / it | **is** | work**s** |
| we | **are** | work |
| they | **are** | work |

> ⚠️ **he / she / it** are the only ones with **-s** on normal verbs: *She work**s**. It rain**s**.* (Full lesson: [L-043](../verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md).)
> ⚠️ Singular **they** still uses **are** and no -s: *Someone called. **They are** waiting.*

### 7.4 Where do subject pronouns go?

Same place as a subject noun: **before the verb** in statements; **after the helper** in questions:
> ***She** is a nurse.* · *Is **she** a nurse?* · *Where does **she** work?*

### 7.5 Three big mistakes

**Mistake 1 — Repeating the subject** (very common for speakers of some languages)
> ❌ *My sister **she** lives in Paris.* → ✅ *My sister lives in Paris.* / ✅ ***She** lives in Paris.*
> Use the noun **or** the pronoun — **not both**.

**Mistake 2 — Dropping the subject**
> ❌ *Is raining.* → ✅ ***It** is raining.*
> ❌ *Am tired.* → ✅ ***I** am tired.*
> English always needs a subject (except instructions: *Sit down*).

**Mistake 3 — Unclear pronoun**
> ⚠️ *Tom met Ali. **He** was happy.* — Who was happy? Tom or Ali? Unclear!
> ✅ *Tom met Ali. **Tom** was happy.* / *Tom was happy to meet Ali.*
> When there are two possible nouns, repeat the noun or change the sentence.

### 7.6 Singular "they"

When we **don't know** if a person is male or female, or **don't want to say**, or the person **prefers** it, English uses **they** for **one** person:
> *Someone left **their** bag. I hope **they** come back for it.*
> *Every student must bring **their** book.*
> *Alex is my new colleague. **They** are from Canada.* (if Alex uses *they*)

This is correct, standard English (used for centuries, and accepted by modern dictionaries and style guides). The verb is still **are / no -s**.

### 7.7 "It" without a real meaning

**it** fills the subject place for weather, time, days, distance, and situations ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)):
> ***It**'s raining. **It**'s 7 o'clock. **It**'s Monday. **It**'s 5 km to the station. **It**'s nice to meet you.*

And **it** for identifying who someone is (on the phone, at the door):
> *"Who's at the door?" — "**It**'s Maria."* (not ~~She is Maria~~ here)

### 7.8 "They" for people in general / organisations

> ***They** say it will rain tomorrow.* (= people in general / the weather reporters)
> ***They**'re building a new hospital.* (= some company or the government)
> *The bank is closed. **They** open at 9.* (= the people who work there)

## 8. Basic sentence patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Noun… . Pronoun + verb… | *My aunt is a chef. **She** cooks very well.* |
| Pronoun + *be* + noun/adjective | ***We** are students. **It** is cold.* |
| Helper + pronoun + verb? | *Do **they** speak English?* |
| Short answer | *Is your brother here? — Yes, **he** is.* |

## 9. Rules and exceptions

| Rule | Note |
|---|---|
| *I* always capital. | Other pronouns only at the start of a sentence. |
| *you* = singular and plural. | Informal plural forms: *you guys, you all*. |
| *he/she* for people; *it* for things/animals. | Pets often *he/she*; ships/countries sometimes *she* (old-fashioned/poetic). |
| *they* = plural. | Also singular *they* for one unknown/non-binary person. |
| Don't repeat or drop the subject. | Instructions have a hidden *you*. |

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-027-001 — "This is my friend Ana. She is from Brazil."

- **Meaning:** I'm introducing my friend Ana. Ana comes from Brazil.
- **Situation:** At a party, introducing a friend.
- **Main lesson:** A noun first, then a **pronoun** for the same person.
- **Reference:** **She** → *my friend Ana* (female, singular).
- **Why not repeat *Ana*?** *Ana is from Brazil* is correct, but repeating the name every time sounds unnatural.
- **Verb:** *she* → **is**.
- **Remember 🔁:** *This is…* for introductions ([L-019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *This is my friend Ana. He is from Brazil.* (wrong gender) · ❌ *Ana she is from Brazil.* (repeated subject)
- **Pattern:** **Name/noun … . He/She + verb …**
- **Quick check:** *This is my brother. ___ is a pilot.*

---

### EX-027-002 — "I am a student, and you are a teacher."

- **Meaning:** My job is student; your job is teacher.
- **Situation:** A child playing "school" with a parent.
- **Main lesson:** 1st person (**I**) and 2nd person (**you**) with their *be* forms.
- **Verbs:** *I* → **am** · *you* → **are**.
- **Capital I:** always, even in the middle (*…and **I** am…*).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I is a student.* · ❌ *You is a teacher.* · ❌ *i am a student.*
- **Natural speech:** *I'm a student, and you're a teacher.*
- **Pattern:** **I am a/an … · You are a/an …**
- **Quick check:** Write two sentences: one with *I*, one with *you*.

---

### EX-027-003 — "My phone is old. It doesn't work well."

- **Meaning:** My phone is not new, and it has problems. 📱
- **Situation:** Explaining why you want a new phone.
- **Main lesson:** **it** for a **thing**.
- **Reference:** **It** → *my phone*.
- **Verb:** *it* → **doesn't** (does not) — he/she/it form ([L-044](../verbs/lesson-044-present-simple-negative.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *He doesn't work well.* (a phone is not a person) · ❌ *Doesn't work well.* (missing subject — OK only in very casual text messages)
- **Pattern:** **Thing … . It + verb …**
- **Quick check:** *I have a new car. ___ is red.*

---

### EX-027-004 — "My parents live in a village. They have a big garden."

- **Meaning:** My mother and father's home is in a small town; they own a large garden. 🏡
- **Situation:** Describing your family.
- **Main lesson:** **they** for **people** (plural).
- **Reference:** **They** → *my parents*.
- **Verb:** *they* → **have** (no -s).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My parents they live…* · ❌ *They has a big garden.*
- **Pattern:** **Plural noun … . They + verb (no -s) …**
- **Quick check:** *My cousins are funny. ___ make me laugh.*

---

### EX-027-005 — "Tom and I are neighbours. We live on the same street."

- **Meaning:** Tom and I live next to each other, on one street.
- **Situation:** Explaining how you know Tom.
- **Main lesson:** **we** = *I* + others.
- **Reference:** **We** → *Tom and I*.
- **Why *Tom and I* (not *me and Tom*)?** In the subject position, use **I**; put yourself **last** ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Verb:** *we* → **are**, **live**.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Me and Tom are neighbours.* (informal speech only) · ❌ *Tom and I is neighbours.*
- **Pattern:** **X and I … . We + verb …**
- **Quick check:** *My sister and I like music. ___ go to concerts.*

---

### EX-027-006 — "Are you ready, children?" — "Yes, we are!"

- **Meaning:** The teacher asks a group of children if they can start. They all answer "yes".
- **Situation:** A teacher before a school trip.
- **Main lesson:** **you** as a **plural** (talking to many people); the answer with **we**.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Are you…?** — *you* here means all the children.
  - **, children** — the people you are talking to (comma before their name/group — [L-019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
  - **we are** — the children answer about themselves as a group.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Yes, you are!* (the children are talking about themselves → *we*) · ❌ *Yes, we're!* (no contraction in positive short answers — [L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md))
- **Informal:** *Are **you guys** ready?*
- **Quick check:** A teacher asks the class "Are you tired?" The class answers "No, ___."

---

### EX-027-007 — "It's raining, and it's very cold."

- **Meaning:** Rain is falling, and the weather is very cold. 🌧️🥶
- **Situation:** Looking out of the window in the morning.
- **Main lesson:** **empty *it*** for weather ([§7.7](#77-it-without-a-real-meaning)).
- **Word by word:** **It's** = It is. *it* doesn't refer to any real thing; it fills the subject place.
- **is raining** — action now ([L-056](../verbs/lesson-056-present-continuous-positive.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Is raining.* · ❌ *The weather is raining.* · ❌ *Rains.*
- **Pattern:** **It's + weather word / It's + verb-ing.**
- **Quick check:** Say what the weather is like today.

---

### EX-027-008 — "Someone called you. They didn't leave a message."

- **Meaning:** A person phoned you (I don't know who). That person didn't leave a message.
- **Situation:** You answer your roommate's phone.
- **Main lesson:** **singular *they*** for an unknown person ([§7.6](#76-singular-they)).
- **Reference:** **They** → *someone* (one person, sex unknown).
- **Verb:** *didn't* = did not (past negative — [L-065](../verbs/lesson-065-past-simple-negative-and-questions.md)).
- **Why not *he* or *she*?** You don't know who it was. *He or she* is possible but long and formal.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Someone called you. It didn't leave a message.* (*it* is not for people)
- **Pattern:** **someone / a person / the customer … . They …**
- **Quick check:** *A student left their phone. I hope ___ come back for it.*

---

### EX-027-009 — "Who's at the door?" — "It's Grandma!"

- **Meaning:** Who is knocking? — Grandmother is here!
- **Situation:** Someone knocks; a child runs to look.
- **Main lesson:** **It's + person** to identify someone ([§7.7](#77-it-without-a-real-meaning)).
- **Why *it* and not *she*?** When we **identify** who someone is (door, phone, photo), English uses *it*: *It's me. It's Tom. It's the postman.*
- **After that, use *she/he*:** *It's Grandma! **She**'s got a cake!*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *She is Grandma!* (sounds strange here) · ❌ *Is Grandma!*
- **On the phone:** *Hello, **it's** Ana.* (also: *This is Ana* — [L-019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md))
- **Quick check:** Your friend Leo calls. What does he say first?

---

### EX-027-010 — "My sister lives in Paris. She works in a museum."

- **Meaning:** My sister's home is in Paris, and her job is in a museum. 🏛️
- **Situation:** Talking about family abroad.
- **Main lesson:** **Don't repeat the subject** ([§7.5](#75-three-big-mistakes)).
- **Correct structure:** Sentence 1 uses the **noun** (*my sister*), sentence 2 uses the **pronoun** (*she*).
- **Verb:** *she* → **works** (-s).
- **Wrong (very common!):** ❌ *My sister she lives in Paris.* — two subjects for one verb.
- **Pattern:** **Noun + verb… . Pronoun + verb…**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *My father he is a doctor.*

---

### EX-027-011 — "The bank is closed now. They open at nine."

- **Meaning:** The bank isn't open at the moment; the bank (its workers) will open at 9:00.
- **Situation:** You arrive at the bank too early.
- **Main lesson:** **they** for the **people of an organisation** ([§7.8](#78-they-for-people-in-general--organisations)).
- **Reference:** *The bank* (building) → **is** · *They* = the people who work at the bank → **open**.
- **Also possible:** *It opens at nine.* (*it* = the bank as a building/business). Both are natural.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *They opens at nine.*
- **Pattern:** **The shop/bank/school … . They + verb …**
- **Quick check:** *The museum is great. ___ have a café on the roof.*

---

### EX-027-012 — "Is your dog friendly?" — "Yes, he is. He loves people!"

- **Meaning:** Does your dog like strangers? — Yes, he likes everyone. 🐶
- **Situation:** In a park, before you pet someone's dog.
- **Main lesson:** **he/she** for a **pet** we know ([§7.2](#72-choosing-the-right-pronoun)).
- **Why *he*?** Owners usually call their pets *he* or *she*, like family members. *It* is used for animals in general or unknown animals.
- **Short answer:** *Yes, he is.* (not *Yes, it is* — the owner thinks of the dog as "he")
- **Verb:** *he* → **loves**.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Yes, he's.* (no contraction in short positive answers)
- **Quick check:** You see a cat in the street (no owner). *___ is very small.*

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| My mother she is a nurse. | My mother is a nurse. / She is a nurse. | don't repeat the subject |
| Is cold today. | **It** is cold today. | subject needed |
| i am happy. | **I** am happy. | capital I |
| He is a nice phone. | **It** is a nice phone. | things → it |
| They is my friends. | They **are** my friends. | they → are |
| She work in a bank. | She work**s** in a bank. | she → -s |
| Me and my brother are… | My brother and **I** are… | subject → I |
| The phone, it is broken. | The phone is broken. | don't repeat |
| Someone called. It… | Someone called. **They**… | people → they |

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** Where are **you** from?
> **B:** **I**'m from Ghana. And **you**?
> **A:** **We**'re from Peru — my husband and **I**.
> **B:** Oh nice! Is **it** hot in Peru?
> **A:** **It** depends. In Lima, **it**'s not very hot.

- *we* = A + her husband · *it* in *Is it hot in Peru* = the weather · *It depends* = the answer is "sometimes yes, sometimes no".

## 15. Story-based example

> *Ravi has a small dog called Coco. **She** is very clever. Every morning, Ravi and Coco walk to the park. **They** meet other dogs there. **It** is Coco's favourite place. When **it** rains, **they** stay at home and **Ravi** reads, but Coco is not happy!*

- *She* = Coco (pet → *she*) · *They* = Ravi and Coco · *It is Coco's favourite place* = the park · *it rains* = empty *it* · **Ravi** repeated at the end to be clear (after *they*, a new single subject).

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

- Pronouns are usually **weak** (unstressed) in speech: *i'm GOing · he's a DOCtor* → "hiz-a-DOC-tor".
- Contractions: *I'm* "aim" · *you're* "yor" · *he's* "heez" · *she's* "sheez" · *it's* "its" · *we're* "weer" · *they're* "thair".
- **Stress** a pronoun only for **contrast**: *I'm tired, but **HE** isn't.*
- Listening tip: *he's / his*, *they're / there / their* sound the same or almost the same. Use the context.

## 18. Guided practice

Replace the repeated nouns with pronouns:
*"My uncle has a farm. My uncle has cows and a horse. The horse is old. My uncle and I ride the horse on Sundays."*

1. 2nd *My uncle* → **He**
2. *The horse is old* → keep (*the horse* is new information here, after *cows and a horse* — two possible "it" references would be unclear).
3. *My uncle and I* → **We**
✅ *My uncle has a farm. **He** has cows and a horse. The horse is old. **We** ride **it** on Sundays.* (*it* = the horse — object pronoun: [L-028](lesson-028-object-pronouns.md))

|---|---|---|
| 1st | I (am) | we (are) |
| 2nd | you (are) | you (are) |
| 3rd | he / she / it (is, verb-s) | they (are) |
