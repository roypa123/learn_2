# Lesson 028 — Object Pronouns (me, you, him, her, it, us, them)

## 1. Learning objectives

1. Know the 7 **object pronouns**: *me, you, him, her, it, us, them*.
2. Choose **subject or object** form by **position**: *I* help *him* / *He* helps *me*.
3. Use object pronouns **after verbs** and **after prepositions** (*with me, for her*).
4. Use them in **short answers** and informal speech: *Me too! · It's me.*
5. Handle **two people** together correctly: *between you and **me***, *for Tom and **me***.
6. Put pronouns in the right place with **two objects** and with **phrasal verbs**: *Give **it** to me · Turn **it** off*.

## 2. Why this topic matters

English nouns don't change for subject/object — but **pronouns do** ([L-021 §7.6](../nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#76-nouns-dont-change-for-doer-or-receiver)). Choosing *I* or *me*, *he* or *him*, *they* or *them* is something you do **in almost every sentence**. Mistakes like *He loves I*, *Give to me it*, *between you and I*, *Turn off it* are very common.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- The **object** receives the action; it comes **after the verb**: *Tom eats **an apple*** ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- First look at object pronouns: *Tom helps **me*** ([L-013 §6](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md#6-what-can-be-an-object)).
- Subject pronouns: *I, you, he, she, it, we, they* ([L-027](lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md)).
- **Golden rule:** don't put words between the verb and its object ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).

## 4. Simple introduction

Look at the same two people in two sentences:

> ***I** love **him**.* → I am the lover; he receives the love.
> ***He** loves **me**.* → he is the lover; I receive the love.

The **same people** — but the words change: *I ↔ me*, *he ↔ him*. The form shows the **job**: doer (subject) or receiver (object).

## 5. Definition

> **An object pronoun** replaces a noun that is in an **object position**: after a verb, after a preposition, or alone in a short answer.

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **object pronoun** | a pronoun used as an object | *me, him, them* |
| **preposition** | a small word for place, time, etc.: *with, for, to, at, about…* | *with **her*** |
| **direct object** | the thing/person that receives the action directly | *Give **the book** to Ali.* |
| **indirect object** | the person who receives the direct object | *Give **Ali** the book.* |
| **phrasal verb** | verb + small word with a special meaning | *turn off, pick up* |

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 Subject and object pronouns side by side

| Subject (before the verb) | Object (after the verb / preposition) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I | **me** | *She knows **me**.* |
| you | **you** | *I know **you**.* |
| he | **him** | *We like **him**.* |
| she | **her** | *I called **her**.* |
| it | **it** | *I want **it**.* |
| we | **us** | *They visit **us**.* |
| they | **them** | *I see **them**.* |

- **you** and **it** are the **same** in both columns.
- **her** is also a possessive word (*her bag*) — [L-036](lesson-036-possessive-determiners.md). Context tells you which.

### 7.2 Position 1 — After the verb

> *I love **you**.* · *Can you help **me**?* · *I don't know **them**.* · *Call **her**!*

### 7.3 Position 2 — After a preposition

After **every** preposition (*with, for, to, at, from, about, without, near, behind, between, like…*) use the **object** form:

> *Come **with me**.* · *This is **for you**.* · *Look **at him**.* · *I'm thinking **about her**.* · *Sit **next to us**.* · *I can't live **without them**.*

> ❌ *This gift is for I.* → ✅ *This gift is for **me**.*

### 7.4 Position 3 — Alone (short answers and informal speech)

When a pronoun stands **alone** (no verb after it), English normally uses the **object** form:

| Situation | Natural (informal/neutral) | Very formal (rare) |
|---|---|---|
| *I'm hungry. — **Me too**.* | ✅ | *So am I.* (also very common, neutral) |
| *Who wants cake? — **Me**!* | ✅ | *I do.* |
| *Who's there? — **It's me**.* | ✅ | *It is I.* (very old-fashioned) |
| *She's taller than **me**.* | ✅ (normal) | *…than **I am**.* (also correct) |

> 💡 *She's taller than **I*** (without *am*) is grammatically defended by some, but sounds stiff. Use *than me* or *than I am*.

### 7.5 Two people together: "you and me" or "you and I"?

**Test:** remove the other person and see what sounds right.

| Sentence | Test | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| *Tom and (I / me) are friends.* | *__ am a friend* → **I** | *Tom and **I** are…* (subject) |
| *She invited Tom and (I / me).* | *She invited __* → **me** | *…Tom and **me**.* (object) |
| *This is between you and (I / me).* | *between __* → **me** | *between you and **me*** |
| *A photo of my wife and (I / me).* | *a photo of __* → **me** | *…my wife and **me*** |

> ⚠️ ***between you and I*** is a famous mistake — many native speakers say it because they think *I* sounds "more correct". After a preposition, it's **me**.

Politeness: put the other person **first**: *Tom and me*, not *me and Tom* (in careful English).

### 7.6 Two objects: Give **me** the book / Give **it** to me

Some verbs (*give, send, show, tell, buy, bring, lend*) can have **two objects** ([L-096](../verbs/lesson-096-verbs-with-two-objects.md)):

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + **person** + thing | *Give **me** the book.* · *Send **her** a message.* |
| verb + thing + **to/for** + person | *Give the book **to me**.* · *Buy a gift **for her**.* |
| when the **thing** is a pronoun → use pattern 2 | ✅ *Give **it to me**.* · ✅ *Send **them to her**.* |

> ❌ *Give me it.* — heard in British speech, but **Give it to me** is the safe, standard form.
> ❌ *Give to me the book.* — wrong order.

### 7.7 Phrasal verbs: Turn **it** off (not ~~turn off it~~)

With many phrasal verbs (*turn on/off, pick up, put on, take off, throw away, wake up*), a **pronoun object goes in the middle**:

| With a noun (both OK) | With a pronoun (only one way) |
|---|---|
| *Turn off **the light**. / Turn **the light** off.* | ✅ *Turn **it** off.* ❌ *Turn off it.* |
| *Pick up **the children**. / Pick **the children** up.* | ✅ *Pick **them** up.* ❌ *Pick up them.* |
| *Put on **your coat**. / Put **your coat** on.* | ✅ *Put **it** on.* ❌ *Put on it.* |

(Full lessons on phrasal verbs: Level 3, [L-143](../../02-intermediate-grammar/phrasal-verbs/lesson-143-what-are-phrasal-verbs.md).)

### 7.8 "Let me…" / "Let's…"

> ***Let me** help you.* = Allow me to help you / I'll help you.
> ***Let's** go!* = **Let us** go = I suggest we go together.

*Let* is always followed by an **object** pronoun: ❌ *Let I help.*

### 7.9 Singular "them"

After singular *they* ([L-027 §7.6](lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#76-singular-they)), the object is **them**:
> *Someone called. I don't know **them**.* · *Ask the teacher — **they** can help. Talk to **them** after class.*

## 8. Basic sentence patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + verb + **object pronoun** | *I love **you**.* |
| Subject + verb + noun + preposition + **object pronoun** | *I made a cake for **them**.* |
| Verb (instruction) + **object pronoun** | *Help **me**! Call **her**.* |
| Short answer | *— **Me** too. / — Not **me**!* |
| Verb + **it/them** + to/for + person | *Give **it** to **him**.* |
| Phrasal verb + **pronoun** + particle | *Turn **it** on.* |

## 9. Rules and exceptions

| Rule | Note |
|---|---|
| After verbs and prepositions → object form. | — |
| Subject position → subject form. | *Tom and **I** are…* |
| Alone → object form (*Me too*). | Formal: *So am I / I do.* |
| *than me* / *than I am*. | *than I* = very formal. |
| Pronoun + phrasal verb → in the middle. | *Turn it off.* |
| Thing pronoun + person → *it to me*. | *Give me it* = informal UK. |

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-028-001 — "I love you." / "You love me."

- **Meaning:** I have deep feelings for you. / You have deep feelings for me. ❤️
- **Situation:** Between family members or partners.
- **Main lesson:** *I* (subject) ↔ *me* (object). *you* is the same in both.
- **Parts:** *I* (S) · *love* (V) · *you* (O) | *You* (S) · *love* · *me* (O)
- **Why *me*?** After the verb → object form.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *You love I.* · ❌ *Me love you.*
- **Natural speech:** *I love you* — stress on **love** (or on **you** in reply: *I love **you** too*).
- **Pattern:** **Subject pronoun + verb + object pronoun.**
- **Quick check:** *My dog loves (I / me).*

---

### EX-028-002 — "Do you know Tom?" — "Yes, I know him well."

- **Meaning:** Have you met Tom? — Yes, I know Tom very well.
- **Situation:** At a party, someone asks about a mutual friend.
- **Main lesson:** *Tom* (object) → **him**.
- **Reference:** *him* → Tom (male, singular, object position).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I know he well.* · ❌ *I know well him.* (don't put *well* between verb and object)
- **Pattern:** **I know + him/her/them.**
- **Quick check:** *Do you know Ana? — Yes, I know ___.*

---

### EX-028-003 — "Where's Ana? I need to talk to her."

- **Meaning:** Where is Ana? I must speak with her.
- **Situation:** At work, looking for a colleague.
- **Main lesson:** Object pronoun **after a preposition** (*to*).
- **Parts:** *I* · *need to talk* · *to her* (preposition + object pronoun)
- **Note:** *her* here = object (not possessive), because nothing follows it (compare *her bag*).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *talk to she*
- **Pattern:** **preposition + me/him/her/us/them**
- **Quick check:** *I'm going to the cinema with (they / them).*

---

### EX-028-004 — "The children are hungry. Can you feed them?"

- **Meaning:** The children want food. Can you give them something to eat?
- **Situation:** A parent asks a babysitter.
- **Main lesson:** *they* (subject) / **them** (object) for the same people.
- **Reference:** *them* → the children.
- **feed** = give food to.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Can you feed they?*
- **Pattern:** **plural noun … . verb + them**
- **Quick check:** *My keys! I can't find (they / them).*

---

### EX-028-005 — "This is a great book. Read it!"

- **Meaning:** I recommend this book strongly. 📖
- **Situation:** Talking to a friend in a bookshop.
- **Main lesson:** **it** as object (same form as subject).
- **Parts:** (*You*) *Read* (instruction) · *it* (object → the book)
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Read him!* (a book is not a person) · ❌ *It read!* (the object goes **after** the verb)
- **Pattern:** **Instruction + it/them.**
- **Quick check:** *I bought a new phone, but I don't like ___.*

---

### EX-028-006 — "I'm tired." — "Me too."

- **Meaning:** I feel tired. — I also feel tired.
- **Situation:** Two friends after a long walk.
- **Main lesson:** A pronoun **alone** → object form ([§7.4](#74-position-3--alone-short-answers-and-informal-speech)).
- **Why *me* (not *I*)?** When the pronoun stands alone in speech, English uses *me*: *Me too. Not me! Who, me?*
- **Neutral alternative:** *So am I.* (very common too — Level 4: short responses)
- **Negative version:** *I'm not tired. — **Me neither**.* (or *Neither am I.*)
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I too.* (sounds very old-fashioned) · ❌ *Me also.* (unusual)
- **Quick check:** *I love pizza. — ___ ___!*

---

### EX-028-007 — "Who's there?" — "It's me, Sam!"

- **Meaning:** Who is at the door? — I am Sam.
- **Situation:** Someone knocks at your door.
- **Main lesson:** *It's + object pronoun* to identify someone ([L-027 §7.7](lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#77-it-without-a-real-meaning), [§7.4](#74-position-3--alone-short-answers-and-informal-speech)).
- **Why *me*?** *It's me* is the normal modern form. *It is I* = very old-fashioned.
- **Same:** *Is that you? · It's him! · It's us!*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I am me.* · ❌ *Is me.*
- **Quick check:** You see your brother in an old photo. *Look! It's ___!*

---

### EX-028-008 — "Please come with us to the beach."

- **Meaning:** We invite you to go to the beach together with us. 🏖️
- **Situation:** Inviting a new friend.
- **Main lesson:** **us** after the preposition *with*.
- **Parts:** *Please come* (instruction) · *with us* (prep + object pronoun) · *to the beach* (place)
- **Wrong:** ❌ *come with we*
- **Pattern:** **come/go + with + me/us/them**
- **Quick check:** *Can you sit next to (we / us)?*

---

### EX-028-009 — "She invited my brother and me to her wedding."

- **Meaning:** She asked me and my brother to come to her wedding. 💒
- **Situation:** Telling a friend about an invitation.
- **Main lesson:** Two objects joined by *and* → still **me** ([§7.5](#75-two-people-together-you-and-me-or-you-and-i)).
- **Test:** remove *my brother and* → *She invited **me*** ✅ → so: *my brother and **me***.
- **Politeness:** the other person first: *my brother and me*.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *She invited my brother and I.* (very common, but incorrect) · ❌ *She invited me and my brother.* (informal; less polite)
- **Compare (subject):** *My brother and **I** went to her wedding.*
- **Quick check:** *The teacher helped Ana and (I / me).*

---

### EX-028-010 — "Can you give me that bag? — Sure, here you are. Give it to Tom later."

- **Meaning:** Please pass me that bag. — OK, take it. Later, give the bag to Tom.
- **Situation:** Packing the car with a friend.
- **Main lesson:** Two objects: *give **me** that bag* (person + thing) / *give **it to** Tom* (thing pronoun + *to* + person) ([§7.6](#76-two-objects-give-me-the-book--give-it-to-me)).
- **Why *it to Tom*, not *Tom it*?** When the **thing** is a pronoun (*it/them*), put it first and use **to**.
- **here you are** = a phrase when you give someone something.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Give to me that bag.* · ❌ *Give Tom it.*
- **Pattern:** **Give me + noun / Give it to + person**
- **Quick check:** Put in order: *it / me / to / show*

---

### EX-028-011 — "The TV is too loud. Turn it down, please."

- **Meaning:** The TV volume is very high. Make it quieter, please. 📺🔉
- **Situation:** Someone is watching TV while you study.
- **Main lesson:** Pronoun **in the middle** of a phrasal verb ([§7.7](#77-phrasal-verbs-turn-it-off-not-turn-off-it)).
- **Word by word:** **turn down** = make the sound lower (opposite: *turn up*). **it** = the TV.
- **With a noun:** *Turn down the TV.* ✅ / *Turn the TV down.* ✅
- **With a pronoun:** *Turn **it** down.* ✅ only. ❌ *Turn down it.*
- **Pattern:** **verb + it/them + particle**
- **Quick check:** *Your shoes are dirty. Take ___ ___.*

---

### EX-028-012 — "Let's ask the teacher. They can help us."

- **Meaning:** I suggest we ask the teacher. The teacher can help us.
- **Situation:** Two students can't do an exercise.
- **Main lesson:** *Let's* (= *let us*); singular *they* for "the teacher"; **us** as object.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Let's ask** — a suggestion for "you and me" ([§7.8](#78-let-me--lets)).
  - **They** — singular *they* (we don't say if the teacher is a man or a woman) ([L-027](lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#76-singular-they)).
  - **can help us** — *us* = object (the two students).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Let's to ask.* · ❌ *They can help we.*
- **Quick check:** *Let ___ help you.* (I / me)

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| She loves I. | She loves **me**. | object position |
| This is for she. | This is for **her**. | after a preposition |
| Me and Tom went home. | Tom and **I** went home. | subject position |
| between you and I | between you and **me** | after a preposition |
| She invited Ali and I. | She invited Ali and **me**. | object position |
| Give to me the book. | Give **me** the book. / Give the book to me. | order |
| Give me it. | Give **it to me**. | pronoun thing first |
| Turn off it. | Turn **it** off. | pronoun in the middle |
| Let I help. | Let **me** help. | *let* + object |
| I too. | **Me too.** / So am I. | alone → object |
| I like very much her. | I like **her** very much. | don't split verb + object |

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** Is that your new jacket? I love **it**!
> **B:** Thanks! My sister gave **it** to **me**.
> **A:** That's nice of **her**. Can I try **it** on?
> **B:** Sure. Here — put **it** on.

- *gave it to me* (thing pronoun + *to*) · *nice of her* (after preposition) · *try it on*, *put it on* (pronoun in the middle of phrasal verbs).

## 15. Story-based example

> *Every Sunday, Grandpa calls **us**. **We** talk to **him** for an hour. Last week, **he** sent **me** a letter and some photos. I put **them** on my wall. I love **them** — and I love **him**!*

- *us, him, me, them, him* — object pronouns after verbs and prepositions · *he sent me a letter* (person + thing) · *I put them on my wall* (*them* = the photos).

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

Object pronouns are usually **weak** and **joined** to the verb:

| Written | Sounds like (fast speech) |
|---|---|
| *help him* | "HEL-pim" (the **h** disappears) |
| *tell her* | "TEL-er" |
| *call them* | "CALL-em" (sometimes written *'em* in very informal text) |
| *give it to me* | "GI-vi-tuh-me" |
| *let me* | "LEM-me" |

**Listening tip:** because *him* and *her* lose their **h**, *help him* and *helped* can sound similar. Listen to the whole sentence.
