## 1. Learning objectives

1. Know the **most common irregular plurals** — the ones you will use every day.
2. Group them into **5 types** so they are easier to remember.
3. Use them with correct **agreement** (*The children **are**…*, *These people **live**…*).
4. Pronounce tricky ones correctly (*women* = "WIM-in").
5. Recognise a few **academic** plurals (*data, criteria, analyses*) for later reading.

## 2. Why this topic matters

The most common irregular plurals — **people, children, men, women, feet, teeth** — are some of the most frequent nouns in English. You will hear and use them every day. Mistakes like *childs*, *peoples* (meaning "persons"), *mans*, *foots* are very noticeable.

The good news: there aren't many of them. About **20 words** cover almost everything a learner needs.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- **Regular plural** = + s / + es / y → ies / f → ves ([L-024](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)).
- **Irregular plural** = a plural not made in the regular way ([L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).
- When the noun is plural, change its partners: *a → (none)*, *this → these*, *is → are*, *it → they*, verb without -s ([L-023 §7.2](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#72-agreement-make-everything-match)).
- You already met *children* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)), *people* ([L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md) bank), *fish* ([L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md)).

## 4. Simple introduction

Why are some plurals strange? Because English is **very old**. A thousand years ago, Old English made plurals in many different ways. Most words later changed to the simple **-s**, but a few **very common** words kept their old forms. Because people used them all the time, they never changed. That's why the irregular ones are often the **everyday** words: man, woman, child, foot, tooth, mouse.

## 5. Definition

> **An irregular plural** is a plural form that doesn't follow the normal -s / -es rules. You have to **learn it as a new word**.

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **irregular** | not following the usual rule |
| **vowel change** | the plural changes a vowel inside the word: *f**oo**t → f**ee**t* |
| **same form** | singular and plural look the same: *one sheep, two sheep* |
| **person / people** | one human / more than one human |

---

## 7. The five types of irregular plurals

### Type 1 — The vowel inside changes

| Singular | Plural | Say the plural |
|---|---|---|
| man | m**e**n | "men" |
| woman | wom**e**n | ⚠️ "**WIM**-in" |
| foot | f**ee**t | "feet" |
| tooth | t**ee**th | "teeth" |
| goose | g**ee**se | "geese" |
| mouse | m**ice** | "mice" |

> ⚠️ **woman / women**: the spelling changes only one letter (a → e), but the **sound** of the first part changes completely: *woman* "WUM-un" → *women* "WIM-in".

**Words with -man** follow the same pattern:
> police**man** → police**men** · fire**man** → fire**men** · business**man** → business**men** · sales**woman** → sales**women**

(Many people now prefer neutral words: *police officers, firefighters, businesspeople, salespeople*.)

⚠️ But **human → humans** (not *humen*) — *human* is not made from *man*. Also *German → Germans*.

### Type 2 — Add **-en** or **-ren**

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| child | child**ren** |
| ox | ox**en** |

*Children* is the important one. *Ox* (a large farm animal) is rare.

### Type 3 — Singular and plural are the **same**

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| one sheep 🐑 | two sheep |
| one fish 🐟 | two fish |
| one deer 🦌 | two deer |
| one aircraft ✈️ | two aircraft |
| one series | two series |
| one species | two species |

**How do you know if it's singular or plural?** Look at the **partners**: *a sheep is…* / *the sheep **are**…* / *this fish* / *these fish*.

⚠️ *fishes* exists, but it means **different kinds** of fish (in science). For everyday use: *I caught three **fish***.

### Type 4 — A completely different word

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| person | **people** |
| (die — very rare) | dice 🎲 |

**person → people** is extremely important:
> *one **person**, two **people**, many **people**, a lot of **people***
> ✅ *People **are** friendly here.* ❌ *Peoples are…* ❌ *People is…*

- *persons* exists, but it's **formal** (signs, laws): *"Lift: max. 8 persons."*
- *peoples* (with -s) means **nations / ethnic groups**: *the peoples of Africa*. Rare for learners.
- *die → dice* 🎲: today many people say *dice* for one **or** more; *one die* is correct but less common.

### Type 5 — Words from Latin and Greek (academic — first look)

These come from old languages and keep their old plurals. You'll see them in science, school and news. **You don't need to use them yet** — just recognise them.

| Singular | Plural | Note |
|---|---|---|
| cactus 🌵 | cacti / cactuses | both used |
| crisis | crises | "CRY-seez" |
| analysis | analyses | "a-NAL-i-seez" |
| criterion | criteria | |
| phenomenon | phenomena | |
| medium | media | *the media* = TV, newspapers… |
| datum (rare) | data | *data* is used as plural **or** uncountable: *The data **is/are** correct.* |

---

## 8. Agreement still applies

Irregular plurals work **exactly like regular plurals** with their partners:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| ***This** man **is** tall.* | ***These** men **are** tall.* |
| ***A** child **plays**.* | *Children **play**.* |
| *The person **is** kind. **He/She**…* | *The people **are** kind. **They**…* |
| *My tooth **hurts**.* | *My teeth **hurt**.* |
| *The sheep **is** white.* (one) | *The sheep **are** white.* (many) |

## 9. Common traps

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| childs / childrens | children | -ren, and never add -s again |
| mans, womans | men, women | vowel change |
| foots, tooths | feet, teeth | vowel change |
| mouses | mice | (*mouses* is sometimes used for computer mice, but *mice* is safer) |
| sheeps, fishs | sheep, fish | same form |
| peoples (= persons) | people | person → people |
| people is | people **are** | *people* is plural |
| a people | a person | *people* is plural |
| humen | humans | *human* is regular |

### Possessives (first look)

Irregular plurals that **don't end in -s** take **'s** like singular nouns:
> *the **children's** toys · the **men's** room · **people's** opinions · **women's** football*
(Full lesson: [L-037](lesson-037-possessive-s.md).)

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-025-001 — "My children are at school."

- **Meaning:** My kids (sons/daughters) are at school now. 🏫
- **Situation:** A colleague asks: "Where are your kids today?"
- **Main lesson:** *child → children* (Type 2) + plural agreement.
- **Parts:** **My children** (subject, plural) · **are** · **at school**
- **Word by word:**
  - **children** — plural of *child*. Already plural → **no -s**: ❌ *childrens*.
  - **are** — plural verb.
  - **at school** — no *the* (like *at home, at work* — [L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- **Singular:** *My child **is** at school.*
- **Pronunciation:** *child* "chyld" (long i) → *children* "CHIL-dren" (short i!).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My childs are at school.* · ❌ *My children is at school.*
- **Pattern:** **My children + are/plural verb…**
- **Quick check:** How many children are in your family?

---

### EX-025-002 — "There are three men and two women in the room."

- **Meaning:** In the room, I can count 3 men and 2 women.
- **Situation:** Describing a meeting.
- **Main lesson:** Type 1 vowel change: *man → men*, *woman → women*.
- **Parts:** **There are** (+ plural — [L-041](../basic-sentences/lesson-041-there-is-there-are.md)) · **three men and two women** · **in the room**
- **Pronunciation:** *men* "men" · *women* "**WIM**-in" (⚠️ not "wo-men").
- **Wrong:** ❌ *three mans* · ❌ *two womans* · ❌ saying *women* like *woman*
- **Pattern:** **There are + number + irregular plural.**
- **Quick check:** Say aloud: *one woman, two women* — can you hear the difference?

---

### EX-025-003 — "Many people live in big cities."

- **Meaning:** A large number of humans have their homes in big cities. 🏙️
- **Situation:** A geography lesson.
- **Main lesson:** *person → people* (Type 4) — and **people** takes a plural verb.
- **Parts:** **Many people** (subject, plural) · **live** (no -s) · **in big cities**
- **Word by word:**
  - **Many** — a large number of (+ plural) ([L-070](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-070-much-many-a-lot-of.md)).
  - **people** — plural of *person*. Never *peoples* for this meaning.
  - **live** — plural subject → no -s.
  - **cities** — regular plural (y → ies — [L-024](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Many peoples live…* · ❌ *Many people lives…* · ❌ *Many persons live…* (too formal)
- **Pattern:** **People + plural verb.**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *People is very friendly here.*

---

### EX-025-004 — "He is a nice person. They are nice people."

- **Meaning:** One kind man / several kind humans.
- **Situation:** Talking about new neighbours.
- **Main lesson:** *a person* (singular) vs *people* (plural) — and all partners change.
- **Compare:**

  | He | is | **a** nice | **person**. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | They | are | nice | **people**. |

- *a* disappears; *nice* (adjective) stays the same ([L-023 §7.8](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#78-describing-words-dont-change)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *He is a nice people.* · ❌ *They are nice persons.* (very formal/strange)
- **Pattern:** **a + adjective + person / adjective + people**
- **Quick check:** Make plural: *She is a friendly person.*

---

### EX-025-005 — "My feet hurt after the long walk."

- **Meaning:** Both of my feet feel pain because I walked a lot. 🦶😣
- **Situation:** You come home after a day of sightseeing.
- **Main lesson:** *foot → feet* (Type 1) + plural verb.
- **Parts:** **My feet** (subject, plural) · **hurt** (no -s) · **after the long walk**
- **Word by word:**
  - **feet** — plural of *foot*. You have two feet.
  - **hurt** — feel pain. Plural subject → *hurt* (not *hurts*).
  - **walk** — noun here: *a long walk* ([L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md)).
- **Singular:** *My **foot hurts**.* (one foot → -s on verb)
- **⚠️ Measuring:** *feet* is also a unit of length (1 foot ≈ 30 cm): *He is six **feet** tall* (or *six **foot** tall* — informal).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My foots hurt.* · ❌ *My feet hurts.*
- **Pattern:** **My + irregular plural + plural verb.**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *I have two foot.*

---

### EX-025-006 — "Brush your teeth before bed."

- **Meaning:** Clean your teeth with a toothbrush before you go to sleep. 🪥
- **Situation:** A parent talking to a child.
- **Main lesson:** *tooth → teeth* (Type 1).
- **Parts:** (*You*) **Brush** (instruction) · **your teeth** (object) · **before bed**
- **Word by word:**
  - **teeth** — plural of *tooth*. "teeth" with long /iː/ and /θ/ (tongue between teeth! 😁).
  - **before bed** — before going to bed. No *the*.
- **Interesting:** *tooth**brush*** (one word — compound noun; the first part stays **singular**: not *teethbrush*).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Brush your tooths.* · ❌ *Brush your teeths.*
- **Quick check:** How many teeth does an adult have? (Answer: usually 32.)

---

### EX-025-007 — "Do you have any mice in your house?"

- **Meaning:** Are there any small grey animals 🐭 living in your house?
- **Situation:** A friend hears noises in the walls.
- **Main lesson:** *mouse → mice* (Type 1) in a question.
- **Parts:** **Do you have** · **any mice** (object, plural) · **in your house**
- **any** + plural in questions ([L-069](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-069-some-and-any.md)).
- **Computer mouse:** for two computer mice, both *mice* and *mouses* are heard; *mice* is more common in writing.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *any mouses* (for animals) · ❌ *any mice's*
- **Pattern:** **Do you have any + plural…?**
- **Quick check:** Plural of *mouse* and *house*? (One is irregular!)

---

### EX-025-008 — "The farmer has fifty sheep and a few cows."

- **Meaning:** The farmer owns 50 sheep and a small number of cows. 🐑🐄
- **Situation:** Describing a farm.
- **Main lesson:** Type 3 — *sheep* is the **same** in singular and plural; *cows* is regular.
- **Parts:** **The farmer** · **has** · **fifty sheep and a few cows**
- **How do we know *sheep* is plural?** The number **fifty**.
- **a few** — a small number of (+ plural) ([L-093](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-093-a-few-a-little.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *fifty sheeps*
- **Agreement:** *The sheep **are** in the field.* (many) / *The sheep **is** sick.* (one)
- **Pattern:** **number + sheep/fish/deer** (no -s)
- **Quick check:** *I saw three (deer / deers).*

---

### EX-025-009 — "We caught five fish, but we only ate two."

- **Meaning:** We got five fish from the water, and ate two of them. 🎣
- **Situation:** Telling someone about a fishing trip.
- **Main lesson:** *fish* — same form for plural (Type 3).
- **Parts:** **We** · **caught** (past of *catch*) · **five fish** · **but** · **we only ate two** (past of *eat*)
- **fish vs fishes:** everyday counting → **fish**. *fishes* = different species (science).
- **fish** is also uncountable as **food**: *I like **fish**.* ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md))
- **Wrong:** ❌ *five fishs* · ❌ *five fishes* (for the same kind)
- **Quick check:** *There are many (fish / fishs) in the lake.*

---

### EX-025-010 — "The children's toys are all over the floor."

- **Meaning:** The toys that belong to the children are everywhere on the floor. 🧸
- **Situation:** A tired parent describes the living room.
- **Main lesson:** Irregular plural + **'s** (possessive, first look).
- **Parts:** **The children's toys** (subject; head = *toys*, plural) · **are** · **all over the floor**
- **Word by word:**
  - **children's** — *children* doesn't end in -s, so we add **'s** (like *Tom's*). ❌ *childrens'* ([L-037](lesson-037-possessive-s.md)).
  - **toys** — head noun → **are**.
  - **all over** — everywhere on.
- **Same pattern:** *the **men's** room, **women's** clothes, **people's** ideas*.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The childrens toys* · ❌ *The children toys*
- **Quick check:** Write "the shoes that belong to the women".

---

### EX-025-011 — "Two police officers are talking to the people in the street."

- **Meaning:** Two police workers are speaking with the people outside. 👮👮
- **Situation:** Something happened on your street.
- **Main lesson:** Neutral modern word (*police officers*) instead of *policemen*; *people*.
- **Parts:** **Two police officers** (subject) · **are talking** · **to the people in the street**
- **Word by word:**
  - **police officers** — regular plural (*officer + s*). *Policemen* (irregular, Type 1) is also used, but *police officers* includes men and women.
  - **are talking** — action now ([L-056](../verbs/lesson-056-present-continuous-positive.md)).
  - **the people** — specific people (in the street).
- **Note:** *the police* itself is always **plural**: *The police **are** here.*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Two policemans* · ❌ *The police is here.*
- **Quick check:** Plural of *fireman*? A neutral word for it?

---

### EX-025-012 — "The data shows that more women than men study medicine."

- **Meaning:** The numbers/information tell us: the number of women studying medicine is higher than the number of men.
- **Situation:** A news report about universities.
- **Main lesson:** A **Latin plural** (*data*) and Type 1 plurals in one sentence.
- **Parts:** **The data** (subject) · **shows** · **that more women than men study medicine** (what it shows)
- **Word by word:**
  - **data** — originally the plural of *datum*. Today, most people use it as **uncountable** (*The data **shows***); scientists often use it as plural (*The data **show***). **Both are accepted.**
  - **more women than men** — comparing numbers ([L-072](../adjectives/lesson-072-comparative-adjectives.md)).
  - **study** — plural subject (*women*) → no -s.
  - **medicine** — the study of how to treat illnesses.
- **Why advanced?** The part after *that* is a whole sentence inside the sentence (a noun clause — Level 5). Just notice the plurals for now.
- **Quick check:** Find the two irregular plurals in the sentence.

## 11. Common mistakes

See the table in [§9](#9-common-traps). The top five to remember:

1. ❌ *childs / childrens* → ✅ **children**
2. ❌ *peoples are* / *people is* → ✅ **people are**
3. ❌ *womans* → ✅ **women** ("WIM-in")
4. ❌ *foots / tooths* → ✅ **feet / teeth**
5. ❌ *sheeps / fishs* → ✅ **sheep / fish**

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** How many **people** are in your family?
> **B:** Five. My parents, me and two **children** — my son and my daughter.
> **A:** Oh nice! How old are the **children**?
> **B:** Seven and ten. They have very big **feet** — new shoes every year! 😄

## 15. Story-based example

> *Old Mr Green has a farm with ten **sheep**, two **geese** and a lot of **mice** in the barn. Every Sunday, the **children** from the village visit. The **women** bring cakes, and the **men** help with the animals.*

Irregular plurals: sheep (same), geese, mice, children, women, men.

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

| Singular | Plural | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| woman "WUM-un" | women "**WIM**-in" | the first vowel changes completely |
| child "chyld" | children "CHIL-dren" | long i → short i |
| foot "fut" | feet "feet" | short → long |
| tooth "tooth" | teeth "teeth" | /θ/ at the end |
| goose "goos" | geese "gees" | /s/ (not /z/) |
| mouse "mows" | mice "myce" | |
| person "PUR-sun" | people "PEE-pul" | |

## 18. Guided practice

Make plural: *This woman has a sore tooth. She is a nice person.*
1. *This → These* · 2. *woman → women* · 3. *has → have* · 4. *a sore tooth → sore teeth* · 5. *She → They* · 6. *is → are* · 7. *a nice person → nice people*
✅ ***These women have sore teeth. They are nice people.***
