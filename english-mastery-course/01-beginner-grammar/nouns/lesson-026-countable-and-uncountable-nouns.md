## 1. Learning objectives

1. Know what **countable** and **uncountable** nouns are.
2. Use the **4 differences**: *a/an*, plural, verb, and quantity words.
3. Learn the **common groups** of uncountable nouns (liquids, food, materials, ideas, "group" words).
4. Count uncountable things with **containers and pieces**: *a glass of water, a piece of advice*.
5. Know that some nouns can be **both**, with different meanings (*coffee / a coffee*).

## 2. Why this topic matters

Countable/uncountable decides many small words around the noun: *a* or *some*? *is* or *are*? *many* or *much*? -s or no -s? Mistakes like *an advice, informations, many money, a homework, furnitures* are among the **most common** learner errors in English.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- **Plural** = more than one, usually + s ([L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).
- *a/an* only with **singular** nouns ([L-023 §7.3](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#73-aan-only-with-singular)).
- You already met some uncountable nouns: *water, information, advice, news, music, money, milk, rice, bread* ([L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md), [L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).
- *How many* + plural ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).

## 4. Simple introduction

Imagine you are in a kitchen.

- 🍎🍎🍎 You can count **apples**: one apple, two apples, three apples. → **countable**
- 💧 Can you count **water**? "One water, two waters…"? No! You count **glasses of water** or **litres of water**. Water itself is a mass, like a liquid you pour. → **uncountable**

> 💡 **Countable = you can put a number before it.** *three eggs ✅ · three rices ❌*

## 5. Definitions

> **A countable noun** names something we see as **separate units** we can count: *one book, two books*.
>
> **An uncountable noun** names something we see as a **mass** or an **idea** — not separate units. It has **no plural** and we can't use a number directly: ~~*two informations*~~.

⚠️ This is about how **English** sees the thing, not always about real life. *Rice* is made of little grains you *could* count, but English treats *rice* as uncountable. And a word that is uncountable in English (*information, furniture, advice*) may be countable in **your** language. So learn them!

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **countable** (C) | can be counted; has a plural | *a chair, two chairs* |
| **uncountable** (U) | can't be counted with a number; no plural | *water, furniture* |
| **mass** | a lot of something without separate pieces | *sand, milk* |
| **container** | a thing that holds something | *a cup, a bottle, a bag* |
| **unit / piece** | one counted part | *a slice, a piece, a loaf* |
| **quantity word** (quantifier) | a word for "how much / how many" | *some, many, much, a lot of* |

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 The 4 differences

| | Countable (*book*) | Uncountable (*water*) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. a / an** | ✅ *a book* | ❌ ~~*a water*~~ → *some water* / *water* |
| **2. plural** | ✅ *books* | ❌ ~~*waters*~~ |
| **3. verb** | *The book **is**… / The books **are**…* | always singular: *The water **is**…* |
| **4. quantity words** | *many books, a few books, How many books?* | *much water, a little water, How much water?* |
| (both) | *some books, a lot of books, the books* | *some water, a lot of water, the water* |

**Easy test:** Can you say "**three** + noun-**s**"?
> *three chairs* ✅ → C · *three furnitures* ❌ → U

### 7.2 Common groups of uncountable nouns

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| 💧 **Liquids** | water, milk, juice, coffee, tea, oil, soup |
| 🍚 **Food as a mass** | rice, bread, sugar, salt, cheese, meat, butter, flour, pasta |
| 🪵 **Materials** | wood, paper, glass, plastic, gold, sand |
| 🌧️ **Weather** | rain, snow, wind, sunshine, weather |
| 💭 **Ideas / feelings** | love, happiness, help, fun, time, luck, music, knowledge |
| 📚 **Study & work words** | information, advice, news, homework, research, work, knowledge |
| 🧳 **"Group" words** (many items seen as one mass) | furniture, luggage, baggage, money, traffic, equipment, jewellery |

> ⚠️ **The tricky ones for learners** (countable in many other languages):
> **information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework, equipment, research, bread, money, work, traffic**
> ❌ *an information · advices · a furniture · luggages · homeworks · a bread · moneys*

**news** looks plural but is **uncountable singular**: *The news **is** good.* ([L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md))

### 7.3 How to count uncountable things: containers and pieces

Use **a [unit] of + uncountable noun**. The **unit** is countable, so it takes *a* and plural -s.

| Unit | + uncountable |
|---|---|
| **a glass of** | water, milk, juice |
| **a cup of** | tea, coffee |
| **a bottle of** | water, oil, wine |
| **a bowl of** | soup, rice |
| **a slice of** | bread, cheese, cake, pizza |
| **a loaf of** | bread |
| **a piece of** | paper, cake, furniture, advice, information, news, luggage |
| **a kilo of / a litre of** | rice, sugar / milk, water |
| **a bag of** | rice, flour, sugar |
| **a bar of** | chocolate, soap |

> *two glass**es** of water · three slice**s** of bread · a few piece**s** of advice*
> The **unit** becomes plural; the uncountable noun **never** does. ❌ *two glasses of waters*

**a piece of** is the most useful: it works with almost any uncountable noun.

### 7.4 Nouns that can be both — with different meanings

Some nouns are uncountable for the **mass/general idea**, but countable for **one unit / one kind / one occasion**:

| Uncountable (U) | Countable (C) |
|---|---|
| *I like **coffee**.* (the drink in general) | *Two **coffees**, please.* (two cups — in a café) |
| *I eat **chicken**.* (the meat) | *They have three **chickens**.* (the birds 🐔) |
| *I need some **paper**.* (material) | *I bought **a paper**.* (a newspaper) |
| *I don't have **time**.* | *I called you three **times**.* (occasions) |
| *Glass breaks easily.* (material) | *a **glass** of water* (a cup) / *my **glasses*** (for eyes) |
| *She has long **hair**.* (all of it) | *There's **a hair** in my soup!* (one single hair) |
| *Is there **room** for me?* (space) | *The house has five **rooms**.* |
| *I have **experience** in teaching.* (knowledge) | *It was **an** amazing **experience**.* (an event) |
| *I like **cake**.* (in general) / *a slice of **cake*** | *She made **two cakes**.* (whole cakes) |

**fish** — as food: U (*I like fish*); as animals: C (*three fish*) ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).

### 7.5 Countable / uncountable pairs with similar meanings

| Uncountable | Countable |
|---|---|
| work | a job, jobs |
| travel | a trip, a journey |
| furniture | a chair, a table, a bed |
| luggage / baggage | a bag, a suitcase |
| money | a coin, a note (US *bill*), a dollar |
| advice | a suggestion, a tip |
| homework | an exercise, a task |
| bread | a loaf, a roll |

> ❌ *I have a work.* → ✅ *I have **a job**.* / *I have **work** to do.*
> ❌ *How was your travel?* → ✅ *How was your **trip**?*

### 7.6 Quantity words — first look

| Only countable | Both | Only uncountable |
|---|---|---|
| many, a few, few, How many? | some, any, a lot of, lots of, no, the | much, a little, little, How much? |
| number + noun (*three*) | | |

> *How **many** apples? · How **much** milk?*
> *There are **a lot of** cars. · There is **a lot of** traffic.*

In positive sentences, people usually say **a lot of** instead of *much*: *I have **a lot of** work* (more natural than *I have much work*). Full lessons: [L-069](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-069-some-and-any.md), [L-070](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-070-much-many-a-lot-of.md), [L-093](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-093-a-few-a-little.md).

## 8. Basic sentence patterns

| Countable | Uncountable |
|---|---|
| *I have **an** apple.* | *I have **some** milk.* |
| *The apples **are** fresh.* | *The milk **is** fresh.* |
| *How **many** apples do you want?* | *How **much** milk do you want?* |
| *There **are** some eggs.* | *There **is** some cheese.* |
| *I need two bottles.* | *I need two bottles **of water**.* |

## 9. Rules and exceptions (summary)

| Rule | Note |
|---|---|
| U nouns: no *a/an*, no -s, singular verb. | Some nouns are C **and** U with different meanings (§7.4). |
| Count U nouns with *a [unit] of*. | The unit gets -s: *two cups of tea*. |
| *many* + C, *much* + U. | *a lot of, some, any* go with both. |
| *news* = U singular. | ✅ *The news is…* |
| Café language: *a coffee, two teas* | = cups of — normal and correct when ordering. |

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-026-001 — "I'd like a glass of water, please."

- **Meaning:** I want one glass of water. 💧
- **Situation:** At a restaurant.
- **Main lesson:** Counting an uncountable noun with a **container**.
- **Parts:** **I'd like** (= I would like — polite *want*, [L-082](../verbs/lesson-082-want-to-and-would-like.md)) · **a glass of water** · **please**
- **Why not *a water*?** *water* is uncountable. We count the **glass**: *a glass of water*.
- **Plural:** *two glass**es** of water* (not *two glasses of waters*).
- **Café note:** In restaurants people sometimes say *A water, please* (= a bottle/glass). It's informal and common, but *a glass of water* is always correct.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I'd like a water glass.* (that means an empty glass for water!) · ❌ *I'd like two waters of glass.*
- **Pattern:** **a/two + container(s) + of + uncountable**
- **Quick check:** Order two cups of tea.

---

### EX-026-002 — "Can you give me some advice?"

- **Meaning:** Can you tell me what you think I should do? 🤔
- **Situation:** You have a problem and ask a friend.
- **Main lesson:** *advice* is **uncountable** → **some**, not *an*.
- **Parts:** **Can you give me** · **some advice** (object)
- **Word by word:**
  - **some** — an amount (works with U and plural C).
  - **advice** — helpful ideas from someone. U → no *an*, no -s.
- **One piece:** *Let me give you **a piece of advice**.* / *a **tip*** (countable).
- **Advice vs advise:** *advice* (noun, "ad-VICE" /s/) · *advise* (verb, "ad-VISE" /z/): *I advise you to rest.*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Can you give me an advice?* · ❌ *some advices*
- **Pattern:** **some + uncountable**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *She gave me many advices.*

---

### EX-026-003 — "The information on this website is correct."

- **Meaning:** The facts on this website are true and right. ✅
- **Situation:** You check facts for school.
- **Main lesson:** U noun as **subject** → **singular verb**.
- **Parts:** **The information on this website** (subject; head = *information*, U) · **is** · **correct**
- **Why *is*?** U nouns always take a singular verb (even if the "amount" is big).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The informations are correct.* · ❌ *The information are correct.*
- **One fact:** *a piece of information* / *a fact* (C).
- **Pattern:** **The + U noun + is…**
- **Quick check:** *The furniture in this room (is / are) old.*

---

### EX-026-004 — "We need some new furniture for the living room."

- **Meaning:** We must buy new things like sofas, chairs, tables for the living room. 🛋️
- **Situation:** You moved into a new flat.
- **Main lesson:** A **"group" uncountable noun**.
- **Parts:** **We need** · **some new furniture** (object) · **for the living room**
- **Word by word:**
  - **furniture** — U: all the movable things (chairs, tables, beds…) together. ❌ *furnitures*.
  - **new** — adjective, unchanged.
- **Count it:** *a piece of furniture*, or name the items: *a sofa, two chairs* (C).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *a new furniture* · ❌ *new furnitures*
- **Pattern:** **some + adjective + U noun**
- **Quick check:** Name three pieces of furniture in your home.

---

### EX-026-005 — "How much money do you have?" — "Only ten dollars."

- **Meaning:** What amount of money do you have? — Just $10. 💵
- **Situation:** You and a friend want to buy lunch.
- **Main lesson:** **How much** + U noun; *money* (U) vs *dollars* (C).
- **Parts:** **How much money** (object) · **do you have**
- **Why *much*?** *money* is U → *How much*. ❌ *How many money?*
- **Why *ten dollars*?** *dollar* is C (one dollar, ten dollars). We count **units** of money.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *How many money…?* · ❌ *I have ten moneys.*
- **Pattern:** **How much + U noun…? / How many + plural C…?**
- **Quick check:** *How (much / many) coins do you have?*

---

### EX-026-006 — "I have a lot of homework tonight."

- **Meaning:** Tonight I must do many school exercises. 📚😩
- **Situation:** A friend invites you out; you say no.
- **Main lesson:** *homework* is U; **a lot of** works with U and C.
- **Parts:** **I have** · **a lot of homework** · **tonight**
- **Why not *many homeworks*?** *homework* is U → no -s, no *many*. Use *a lot of* (natural in positive sentences).
- **Count it:** *three **exercises*** / *a piece of homework* / *a homework **task***.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I have many homeworks.* · ❌ *I have a homework.*
- **Pattern:** **a lot of + U** / **a lot of + plural C** (*a lot of friends*)
- **Quick check:** Correct: *The teacher gave us two homeworks.*

---

### EX-026-007 — "There is some cheese in the fridge, but there aren't any eggs."

- **Meaning:** The fridge has a little cheese, but zero eggs. 🧀🥚❌
- **Situation:** Deciding what to cook.
- **Main lesson:** U noun with **There is** (singular); C plural with **There are**.
- **Parts:** **There is some cheese** (U → *is*) · **but** · **there aren't any eggs** (C plural → *are*)
- **some / any:** *some* in positive; *any* in negative and questions ([L-069](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-069-some-and-any.md)).
- **Count cheese:** *a slice of cheese*, *a piece of cheese*.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *There are some cheese.* · ❌ *There isn't any eggs.*
- **Pattern:** **There is some + U / There are some + plural C**
- **Quick check:** *There (is / are) some bread on the table.*

---

### EX-026-008 — "Two coffees and a tea, please."

- **Meaning:** I'd like two cups of coffee and one cup of tea. ☕☕🍵
- **Situation:** Ordering at a café counter.
- **Main lesson:** U nouns used as **C** for **one portion/cup** ([§7.4](#74-nouns-that-can-be-both--with-different-meanings)).
- **Why is this correct?** In cafés and restaurants, *a coffee* = *a cup of coffee*. It's normal and natural.
- **Compare:** *I like **coffee**.* (U — the drink in general) · *Two **coffees**, please.* (C — two cups)
- **Same for:** *a beer, two juices, an ice cream* (in ordering).
- **Pattern:** **number + drink(s), please** (ordering only)
- **Quick check:** Is *I drink three coffees a day* correct? (Yes — it means three cups.)

---

### EX-026-009 — "We eat chicken on Sundays. My grandmother keeps chickens."

- **Meaning:** (1) Each Sunday we eat chicken meat. 🍗 (2) My grandmother has live chickens (birds) at home. 🐔
- **Situation:** Talking about family food traditions.
- **Main lesson:** The same noun as **U** (food/meat) and **C** (animal).
- **Sentence 1:** *chicken* — U (the meat) → no *a*, no -s.
- **Sentence 2:** *chickens* — C (the birds) → plural -s.
- **Same for:** *fish* (food U / animals C), *lamb* (meat U / baby sheep C), *turkey* ([L-022](lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *We eat a chicken on Sundays.* — possible only if you eat a **whole** chicken; for "chicken meat" → no *a*.
- **Quick check:** U or C? *I don't eat **meat**.* / *There are two **lambs** in the field.*

---

### EX-026-010 — "I'm sorry, I don't have time today. I called you three times!"

- **Meaning:** (1) I'm too busy today. (2) I phoned you on three occasions.
- **Situation:** A friend wants to meet, but you are busy — and they didn't answer your calls.
- **Main lesson:** *time* — U (minutes and hours in general) vs **C** *times* (occasions).
- **Sentence 1:** *time* — U → no *a*: ❌ *I don't have a time.*
- **Sentence 2:** *three times* — C (number of occasions).
- **Also:** *What **time** is it?* (U — the hour) · *We had a great **time**!* (C — an experience; fixed phrase).
- **Pattern:** **have time** (U) / **number + times** (C)
- **Quick check:** *How many (time / times) did you read the book?*

---

### EX-026-011 — "How was your trip? Did you have a lot of luggage?"

- **Meaning:** Was your journey good? Did you carry many bags? 🧳
- **Situation:** Meeting a friend at the airport.
- **Main lesson:** U/C pairs: *trip* (C), *luggage* (U) ([§7.5](#75-countable--uncountable-pairs-with-similar-meanings)).
- **Word by word:**
  - **trip** — C (*a trip, two trips*). ⚠️ *travel* is mostly U: *I love travel* / verb *I travel a lot*. ❌ *How was your travel?*
  - **luggage** — U (all the bags). ❌ *luggages*. Count: *two **bags** / suitcases*, *a piece of luggage*.
- **Did you have…?** — past question ([L-065](../verbs/lesson-065-past-simple-negative-and-questions.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *How was your travel?* · ❌ *Did you have many luggages?*
- **Quick check:** Correct: *I have three luggages.*

---

### EX-026-012 — "There's a lot of traffic today, so the buses are late."

- **Meaning:** Many cars are on the roads today, so the buses are not on time. 🚗🚗🚌
- **Situation:** You explain why you are late.
- **Main lesson:** U noun (*traffic*, singular verb) + C plural (*buses*, plural verb) in one sentence.
- **Parts:** **There's** (= there is) **a lot of traffic** (U → *is*) · **, so** · **the buses are late** (C plural → *are*)
- **traffic** — the cars, buses etc. on the road, seen as a mass → U. ❌ *traffics*.
- **so** — shows the result ([L-081](../conjunctions/lesson-081-because-and-so.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *There are a lot of traffics.*
- **Pattern:** **There's a lot of + U** / **There are a lot of + plural C**
- **Quick check:** *There (is / are) a lot of cars.*

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** Let's make pancakes! What do we need?
> **B:** Some **flour**, two **eggs**, a cup of **milk** and a little **sugar**.
> **A:** We have **flour** and **sugar**. How many **eggs** do we have?
> **B:** Only one! And there isn't any **milk**.
> **A:** OK, I'll go to the shop.

- U: flour, milk, sugar → *some, a cup of, a little, isn't any*.
- C: eggs, pancakes → *two, How many, one*.
- *I'll* = I will.

## 15. Story-based example

> *Nadia opened her new café on Monday. She bought **furniture**, **plates** and **cups**, and a big machine for **coffee**. On the first day, she sold forty **coffees**, twenty **slices of cake** and lots of **bread**. Her friends gave her good **advice**: "Smile, and the **customers** will come back!"*

- U: furniture, coffee (the drink), bread, advice.
- C: plates, cups, coffees (= cups), slices, customers.

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

- *a lot of* is said fast: "a-LOT-uv" / "a-LOD-uv" (US).
- *a cup of tea* → "a CUP-uh TEA" — *of* becomes a weak "uh" /ə/.
- *advice* "ad-VYS" (/s/) vs *advise* "ad-VYZ" (/z/).
- **Listening tip:** you often can't hear if a noun has -s. Listen for *is/are*, *much/many*, *a/some* — they tell you if it's U or C plural.

## 20. Quick revision sheet

- **C**: *a/an* ✅, plural ✅, numbers ✅, *many / a few / How many*.
- **U**: no *a/an*, no plural, **singular verb**, *much / a little / How much*.
- Both: *some, any, a lot of, the*.
- **Tricky U**: information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework, equipment, bread, money, work, traffic, research.
- Count U with **a + unit + of**: *a glass of water, a piece of advice, a slice of bread*.
- Some nouns are both: *coffee / a coffee, chicken / a chicken, time / three times, paper / a paper, hair / a hair*.

## 21. Memory aid

> **"If you can't put a number before it, put a CONTAINER before it."** 🥛 *two milks ❌ → two glasses of milk ✅*
