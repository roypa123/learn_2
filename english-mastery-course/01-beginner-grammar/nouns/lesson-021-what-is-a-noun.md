## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Say what a **noun** is, in simple words.
2. Name the **5 groups** of nouns: people, animals, places, things, ideas.
3. Use **4 tests** to find a noun in any sentence.
4. Find nouns in **4 positions**: subject, object, after *be*, after a small place/time word.
5. Know that a **noun phrase** is a noun with its "helper" words (*the big red bus*).
6. Know that some words can be a **noun** or a **verb**, and how to tell.

## 2. Why this topic matters

Nouns are the **most common** kind of word in English. Almost every sentence has at least one. The subject of a sentence is usually a noun; the object is usually a noun. If you can find the nouns, you can quickly understand **who** and **what** a sentence is about.

Also, many grammar rules are **about nouns**: plurals (*cats*), articles (*a, an, the*), possessives (*Tom's*), countable/uncountable (*an apple / some water*). You will study all these next. This lesson is the base for all of them.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- A **thing word** = a **noun** ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)). Test: *a / the / my* before it.
- **Subject** = who/what the sentence is about, before the verb ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Object** = who/what receives the action, after the verb ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- One word can have two jobs: *I drink **water*** (noun) / *I **water** the plants* (verb) ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).

## 4. Simple introduction

Look around you. Everything you can **name** has a noun:

> 🪑 chair · 🪟 window · 📱 phone · 👩 woman · 🐈 cat · 🏙️ city · ☕ coffee

Now think of things you **cannot** touch or see, but you can still name:

> ❤️ love · ⏰ time · 💡 idea · 😊 happiness · 🎵 music · 🧠 knowledge

These are **nouns** too.

## 5. Definition in easy English

> **A noun is a word that names a person, an animal, a place, a thing, or an idea.**

Short version: **A noun is a naming word.** 🏷️

## 6. Key words (terminology)

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **noun** | a naming word | *teacher, dog, park, book, love* |
| **concrete noun** | a noun you can see, touch, hear, smell or taste | *apple, music, rain* |
| **abstract noun** | a noun for an idea or feeling — you cannot touch it | *love, time, freedom* |
| **noun phrase** | a noun + the words that go with it | *the old man, my new phone* |
| **head noun** (main noun) | the most important noun in a noun phrase | *the old **man** with the dog* |
| **determiner** | a small word before a noun: *a, an, the, my, this, some…* | ***the** dog* |

You will learn **concrete/abstract** in detail later in the noun unit, and **determiners** in [L-032](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md)–[L-036](../pronouns/lesson-036-possessive-determiners.md). For now, just know the words.

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 The 5 groups of nouns

| Group | What it names | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 👩 **People** | persons, jobs, family | *woman, baby, doctor, mother, friend, Ana* |
| 🐾 **Animals** | living creatures | *dog, bird, fish, horse, elephant* |
| 📍 **Places** | buildings, areas, countries | *school, kitchen, park, city, Japan* |
| 📦 **Things** | objects, food, materials | *table, car, bread, water, phone, money* |
| 💭 **Ideas** | feelings, qualities, events, times | *love, fear, help, party, week, music* |

> 💡 Group 5 is the hardest to recognise because you cannot point at it. Use the tests below.

### 7.2 Four tests to find a noun

**Test 1 — The "a / the / my" test** (from [L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md))
Can you put *a*, *an*, *the* or *my* before it?
> *the **kitchen*** ✅ · *my **idea*** ✅ · *the **happy*** ❌ · *a **run*** — sometimes ✅ (*I went for a run*)

**Test 2 — The plural test**
Can you make it "more than one" (usually with **-s**)?
> *book → book**s*** ✅ · *idea → idea**s*** ✅ · *quick → ~~quicks~~* ❌
> ⚠️ Some nouns have no plural (*water, music, information*) — they are **uncountable** ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). So this test doesn't work for every noun.

**Test 3 — The describing-word test**
Can you put a describing word (adjective) before it?
> *a **big** house* ✅ · *a **good** idea* ✅ · *a **beautiful** music* ❌ — but *beautiful **music*** ✅ (no *a* with *music*, but the adjective still works).

**Test 4 — The position test**
Is it in a "noun place" in the sentence? (See 7.3.)

**If a word passes 2 or more tests, it is almost always a noun.**

### 7.3 Where do nouns go? Four positions

| Position | Example | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Subject** (before the verb) | ***Dogs** bark.* | Who/what does the action? |
| **B. Object** (after the verb) | *I love **music**.* | Receiver of the action. |
| **C. After *be*** (complement) | *My mother is a **teacher**.* | Says what the subject is ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)). |
| **D. After a preposition** | *The cat is under the **table**.* | After small words like *in, on, under, at, with, for, to* ([L-049](../prepositions/lesson-049-prepositions-of-place-in-on-under.md)). |

A sentence can have nouns in **several positions** at once:
> ***Maria** cooks **dinner** for her **family** in the **kitchen**.*
> → subject (Maria) · object (dinner) · after *for* (family) · after *in* (kitchen)

### 7.4 Noun phrases

A noun often comes with **helper words**. The noun + its helpers = a **noun phrase**. The noun is the **head** (the boss) of the phrase.

```
   the       big       red       bus
determiner adjective adjective  HEAD NOUN
```

| Noun phrase | Head noun |
|---|---|
| *a cup* | cup |
| *my best friend* | friend |
| *the old man with the black dog* | man |
| *three small children* | children |

**Why the head matters:** the verb agrees with the head noun ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md), [L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)):
> *The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table.* (head = *box*, singular → *is*)

### 7.5 Same word, noun or verb?

Many English words can be a noun **or** a verb. **Look at the position and the words around it.**

| Word | Noun | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| work | *I have a lot of **work**.* | *I **work** in a bank.* |
| help | *Thanks for your **help**.* | *Can you **help** me?* |
| call | *I got a **call** from Tom.* | *I **call** my mum every day.* |
| drink | *Would you like a **drink**?* | *I **drink** tea.* |
| rain | *The **rain** is heavy.* | *It **rains** a lot here.* |

**Quick rule:** after *a / the / my / your* → **noun**. After *I / you / they* (or *can, will*) → **verb**.

### 7.6 Nouns don't change for "doer" or "receiver"

In some languages, a noun changes its ending when it is the subject or the object. **English nouns don't.**
> ***The dog** sees the cat.* / *The cat sees **the dog**.* — *dog* looks the same in both.

That's why **word order** is so important in English ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)). (Only **pronouns** change: *I → me, he → him* — [L-028](../pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md).)

Nouns **do** change for:
- **number**: *book → books* ([L-023](lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md))
- **possession**: *Tom → Tom's* ([L-037](lesson-037-possessive-s.md))

## 8. Basic sentence patterns with nouns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Noun + verb | *Babies cry.* |
| Noun + verb + noun | *Cats eat fish.* |
| Noun + *be* + a/an + noun | *My father is a pilot.* |
| Noun + *be* + preposition + noun | *The keys are on the table.* |
| Noun + verb + noun + preposition + noun | *Lisa reads books in the library.* |

## 9. Rules and exceptions

| Rule | Exception / note |
|---|---|
| Most nouns can take *a/the/my*. | Names usually don't take *the*: ❌ *the Ana* ([L-022](lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md)). |
| Most nouns have a plural with -s. | Irregular plurals (*child → children*) ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)); uncountable nouns (*water*) have no plural ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). |
| One singular countable noun usually needs *a/the/my…* | ❌ *I have car.* → ✅ *I have **a** car.* ([L-032](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md)) |
| Nouns don't change for subject/object. | Pronouns do (*he/him*). |

## 10. Positive examples (fully explained)

### EX-021-001 — "Babies cry."

- **Meaning:** Very young children make loud sounds with tears when they are hungry, tired or unhappy. 👶😭
- **Situation:** A new parent says this to explain why they are tired.
- **Main lesson:** A noun as the **subject**.
- **Parts:** **Babies** (subject, noun) · **cry** (verb)
- **Find the noun:** Test 1 — *a baby* ✅. Test 2 — *baby/babies* ✅. Position — before the verb (subject) ✅.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Babies** — plural of *baby*. The **y** changes to **ies** (spelling rules: [L-024](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)). Group: **people**.
  - **cry** — verb, no -s because the subject is plural.
- **Remember 🔁:** Two words can be a complete sentence: subject + verb ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Other versions:** *The baby cries.* (one baby → *cries*) · *Children laugh.*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Babys cry.* (spelling) · ❌ *Babies cries.* (plural subject → no -s)
- **Pattern:** **Plural noun + verb.**
- **Quick check:** Which word is the noun in *Birds sing*?

### EX-021-002 — "Cats eat fish."

- **Meaning:** Fish is a food that cats eat. 🐈🐟
- **Situation:** A child asks what to give a cat.
- **Main lesson:** Nouns as **subject** and **object**.
- **Parts:** **Cats** (subject noun) · **eat** (verb) · **fish** (object noun)
- **Find the nouns:** *Who eats?* → cats. *Cats eat what?* → fish. Both pass Test 1: *a cat, a fish* ✅.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Cats** — animal noun, plural.
  - **eat** — verb.
  - **fish** — animal/food noun. ⚠️ The plural of *fish* is usually **fish** (no -s): *one fish, two fish* ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).
- **Remember 🔁:** S – V – O ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)). Swap them → *Fish eat cats* 😄 — different meaning!
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Cats fish eat.* (object before verb) · ❌ *Cats eat fishes.* (*fishes* exists, but for food we say *fish*)
- **Pattern:** **Noun + verb + noun.**
- **Quick check:** Find the subject noun and the object noun in *Cows eat grass.*

---

### EX-021-003 — "My father is a pilot."

- **Meaning:** My father's job is flying planes. ✈️👨‍✈️
- **Situation:** A classmate asks about your parents' jobs.
- **Main lesson:** A noun **after *be***.
- **Parts:** **My father** (subject noun phrase) · **is** (verb *be*) · **a pilot** (noun after *be*)
- **Nouns:** *father* (people), *pilot* (people — a job).
- **Word by word:**
  - **My** — determiner (belongs to me).
  - **father** — head noun of the subject.
  - **is** — *be*, singular ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
  - **a pilot** — a job noun needs **a/an** in English.
- **Is *a pilot* an object?** No. *Is* is not an action; *a pilot* tells what the father **is** ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My father is pilot.* (missing *a*) · ❌ *My father pilot.* (missing *is*)
- **Pattern:** **Noun + is + a/an + job noun.**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *She is engineer.*

### EX-021-004 — "The keys are on the table."

- **Meaning:** The keys are lying on top of the table. 🔑
- **Situation:** Your partner asks: "Where are my keys?"
- **Main lesson:** A noun **after a preposition** (*on*).
- **Parts:** **The keys** (subject) · **are** · **on the table** (preposition + noun)
- **Nouns:** *keys* (thing, plural), *table* (thing).
- **Word by word:**
  - **The keys** — plural head noun → **are**.
  - **on** — preposition of place: touching the top.
  - **the table** — noun after *on*. This is position D.
- **Remember 🔁:** Plural subject → **are** ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The keys is on the table.* · ❌ *The keys are on table.* (a singular countable noun needs *the/a*)
- **Pattern:** **Noun + is/are + on/in/under + the + noun.**
- **Quick check:** How many nouns are in *The phone is in the bag*?

### EX-021-005 — "Maria cooks dinner for her family in the kitchen."

- **Meaning:** Maria prepares the evening meal for the people in her family. She does this in the kitchen. 👩‍🍳
- **Situation:** Describing Maria's evening routine.
- **Main lesson:** Nouns in **four** positions in one sentence.
- **Parts and nouns:**

  | Maria | cooks | dinner | for her family | in the kitchen. |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | **noun** (subject) | verb | **noun** (object) | prep + **noun** | prep + **noun** |

- **Groups:** Maria (person), dinner (thing/food), family (people — a group), kitchen (place).
- **Word by word:**
  - **cooks** — verb + -s (one person).
  - **for** — preposition: who receives the benefit.
  - **her** — determiner (belongs to Maria).
  - **in** — preposition: inside a place.
- **Remember 🔁:** S – V – O – (extra) – Place ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Maria cooks in the kitchen dinner for her family.* (place between verb and object)
- **Pattern:** **Noun + verb + noun + for + noun + in + noun.**
- **Quick check:** Find all the nouns in *Tom buys flowers for his wife at the market.*

---

### EX-021-006 — "Love is important."

- **Meaning:** Caring deeply about people is a very big and necessary thing in life. ❤️
- **Situation:** A grandmother gives advice to her grandchild.
- **Main lesson:** An **abstract noun** (an idea) as the subject.
- **Parts:** **Love** (subject, abstract noun) · **is** · **important** (adjective)
- **Is *love* a noun here?** Position: before the verb, as the subject ✅. Test 3: *true love* ✅. You can't touch love, but it's still a noun.
- **Word by word:**
  - **Love** — an idea/feeling noun. No *a* or *the* here, because we mean love **in general** ([L-034](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-034-zero-article.md)).
  - **important** — adjective: very necessary, it matters a lot.
- **Love as a verb:** *I **love** you.* — after *I* → verb ([§7.5](#75-same-word-noun-or-verb)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The love is important.* (for general ideas, usually no *the*) · ❌ *Love important.* (missing *is*)
- **Pattern:** **Idea noun + is + adjective.** → *Health is important. Time is money.*
- **Quick check:** Is *happiness* a noun? Which test proves it?

### EX-021-007 — "The old man with the black dog lives next door."

- **Meaning:** An old man who has a black dog lives in the house beside mine. (Also in [L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md).)
- **Situation:** Describing your neighbours.
- **Main lesson:** A long **noun phrase** and its **head noun**.
- **The noun phrase:** *The old **man** with the black dog* → head noun = **man**.
- **Nouns:** *man* (head), *dog* (inside the extra part *with the black dog*), *door* (inside *next door*).
- **Why *lives*, not *live*?** The verb agrees with the head noun **man** (singular).
- **Remember 🔁:** Adjective before noun: *old man, black dog* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The old man with the black dogs live next door.* — head noun is still *man* → **lives**.
- **Pattern:** **The + adj + HEAD NOUN + with + the + adj + noun + verb-s…**
- **Quick check:** Head noun of *a cup of hot tea*?

### EX-021-008 — "Thanks for your help." / "Can you help me?"

- **Meaning:** (1) Thank you for helping me. (2) Are you able to help me?
- **Situation:** (1) After a friend carries your bag. (2) Before asking for help.
- **Main lesson:** The same word as a **noun** and a **verb**.
- **Sentence 1:** *your **help*** — after *your* (determiner) → **noun**. Position D (after *for*).
- **Sentence 2:** *can you **help*** — after *can you* → **verb**.
- **Remember 🔁:** *I drink **water*** / *I **water** the plants* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Thanks for your helping.* — use the noun *help*. ❌ *Can you to help me?* — no *to* after *can* ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **Pattern:** **Thanks for your + noun.** → *Thanks for your time / advice / email.*
- **Quick check:** Noun or verb? *I have a lot of **work** today.*

### EX-021-009 — "Is the bank open?" — "No, it isn't."

- **Meaning:** I want to know if the bank is working now. — No, it is closed.
- **Situation:** You need money. You ask someone near the bank.
- **Main lesson:** A noun as the subject **in a question**.
- **Parts:** **Is** · **the bank** (subject noun) · **open** (adjective)
- **Where is the noun?** In a question with *be*, the subject noun comes **after** *is* ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)). It is still the subject.
- **Short answer:** *No, **it** isn't.* — the noun *the bank* becomes the pronoun **it** ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Is open the bank?* (subject must come right after *is*) · ❌ *No, the bank isn't.* (possible, but the pronoun *it* is more natural)
- **Pattern:** **Is + the + noun + adjective?**
- **Quick check:** Make a question: *The museum is free.*

### EX-021-010 — "My sister doesn't like coffee."

- **Meaning:** My sister does not enjoy the drink coffee. ☕❌
- **Situation:** You are ordering drinks for your family.
- **Main lesson:** Nouns in a **negative** sentence.
- **Parts:** **My sister** (subject noun phrase) · **doesn't like** (negative verb) · **coffee** (object noun)
- **Word by word:**
  - **doesn't** — *does not*: helper for negative with he/she/it (full lesson: [L-044](../verbs/lesson-044-present-simple-negative.md)). After *doesn't*, the verb has **no -s**: *like*.
  - **coffee** — a noun for a drink. No *a*: we mean coffee **in general** (coffee is uncountable — [L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
- **Remember 🔁:** *I don't understand* ([L-017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)) — same negative idea.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My sister doesn't likes coffee.* · ❌ *My sister no like coffee.*
- **Pattern:** **Noun + doesn't/don't + verb + noun.**
- **Quick check:** Find the two nouns in *My brother doesn't eat meat.*

---

### EX-021-011 — "Where is the nearest hospital?"

- **Meaning:** Which hospital is closest to here, and where is it? 🏥
- **Situation:** Someone is hurt. You ask a stranger quickly.
- **Main lesson:** A noun phrase with an adjective, in a wh-question.
- **Parts:** **Where** · **is** · **the nearest hospital** (subject noun phrase)
- **Noun phrase:** *the* (determiner) + *nearest* (adjective = most near) + **hospital** (head noun, place).
- **Remember 🔁:** *Where is the station?* ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Where is nearest hospital?* — *nearest* (a "most" adjective) needs **the** ([L-073](../adjectives/lesson-073-superlative-adjectives.md)).
- **Pattern:** **Where is the + adjective + place noun?**
- **Quick check:** Ask for the nearest bus stop.

---

### EX-021-012 — "Time is money."

- **Meaning:** A famous saying: your time has value, like money. Don't waste it. ⏰💰
- **Situation:** A manager says this to explain why meetings must be short.
- **Main lesson:** Two **abstract/general** nouns joined by *be*.
- **Parts:** **Time** (subject noun) · **is** · **money** (noun after *be*)
- **Word by word:** **Time** and **money** are both used **in general**, so no *a/the*. Both are uncountable here.
- **Interesting:** You can't touch *time*. You can touch coins, but *money* here means value in general.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The time is the money.* (in general sayings, no *the*) · ❌ *Time are money.* (singular → is)
- **Pattern:** **Noun + is + noun** (for definitions and sayings). → *Knowledge is power.*
- **Quick check:** How many nouns are in *Knowledge is power*?

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I have car. | I have **a** car. | One countable noun needs *a/the/my…* ([L-032](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md)) |
| She is doctor. | She is **a** doctor. | Jobs need *a/an*. |
| The life is beautiful. | Life is beautiful. | General ideas: no *the* ([L-034](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-034-zero-article.md)). |
| Thanks for your helping. | Thanks for your **help**. | Use the noun form. |
| The box of chocolates are here. | The box of chocolates **is** here. | Verb agrees with the head noun *box*. |
| I like very much music. | I like music very much. | Don't split verb and object noun. |
| a informations | information | uncountable; no *a*, no -s ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). |

> **B:** My **phone**, my **keys**, a **book** and some **money**.
> **A:** No **water**?
> **B:** Oh no! I forgot my **bottle**.

- Every bold word is a noun. Notice: *a book* (one countable thing) but *some money*, *water* (uncountable — no *a*).
- *I forgot* = past of *forget* (not remember). (Past: [L-064](../verbs/lesson-064-past-simple-irregular-verbs.md).)

## 15. Story-based example

> *Every **morning**, **Leo** walks his **dog** in the **park**. The **dog** loves the **ducks** in the **lake**. Leo loves the quiet **time** before **work**.*

**Nouns:** morning (time/idea), Leo (person), dog (animal), park (place), ducks (animals), lake (place), time (idea), work (idea/activity).
- *walks his dog* — here **walk** is a verb (take for a walk).
- *before **work*** — *work* is a noun (after the preposition *before*).

## 16. Pronunciation and listening notes 🗣️👂

- **Nouns are usually stressed** in a sentence: *Maria cooks **DIN**ner for her **FAM**ily.*
- Many two-syllable nouns are stressed on the **first** part: ***TA**ble, **KIT**chen, **DOC**tor, **PI**lot, **MO**ney.*
- Some words change stress for noun vs verb: a ***RE**cord* (noun) / to *re**CORD*** (verb); a ***PRE**sent* (gift) / to *pre**SENT***. (More in pronunciation lessons.)

## 18. Guided practice

**Step 1.** Read: *My brother drives a taxi in London.*
**Step 2.** Find the verb → *drives*.
**Step 3.** Who drives? → *My brother* → noun = **brother**.
**Step 4.** Drives what? → *a taxi* → noun = **taxi**.
**Step 5.** In where? → *London* → noun = **London**.
**Result:** 3 nouns — brother, taxi, London.

Now do the same with: *The children play football in the garden after school.*
*(Answer: children, football, garden, school.)*

## 20. Quick revision sheet

- **Noun** = naming word: people, animals, places, things, ideas.
- **Tests:** *a/the/my* + word · plural -s · adjective before it · noun position.
- **Positions:** subject · object · after *be* · after a preposition.
- **Noun phrase** = determiner + adjectives + **head noun** (+ extra). Verb agrees with the head.
- Same word can be noun or verb: *my **help*** / *I **help***.
- English nouns don't change for subject/object — **order** shows the job.

## 21. Memory aid

> **"If you can put THE before it, and it's not a describing word — it's probably a noun."** 🏷️
