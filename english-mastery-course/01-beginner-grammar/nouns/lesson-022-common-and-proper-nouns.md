## 1. Learning objectives

1. Tell the difference between a **common noun** (*city*) and a **proper noun** (*Paris*).
2. Always use a **capital letter** for proper nouns.
3. Know which kinds of words are proper nouns: people, places, days, months, languages, nationalities, organisations, brands, titles, holidays.
4. Know when proper nouns take **the** (*the Nile, the USA*) and when they **don't** (*London, Japan*).
5. See how a **common noun becomes part of a name** (*a school* → *Green Park School*).

## 2. Why this topic matters

- **Writing:** a missing capital on a name looks like a mistake to every English reader. *i live in london* ❌.
- **Meaning:** capitals tell the reader "this is a special name". *I bought an **apple*** (fruit) vs *I bought an **Apple*** (a phone/computer from the company Apple).
- **Articles:** proper nouns follow special rules with *the* — a very common learner problem.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- Capital letters for: first word, **I**, names of people, places, languages, nationalities, days, months, important words in titles. Seasons: small ([L-004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- A **noun** names a person, animal, place, thing or idea ([L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md)).
- In [L-004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md) we called proper nouns "special names" and said: "You will learn this word in Lesson 022." Here it is!

## 4. Simple introduction

Think about the word **girl**. There are millions of girls in the world. *Girl* is a **general** word for all of them.

Now think about **Sofia** ([S-001](../../07-reading-and-stories/beginner-stories/story-001-a-new-student.md)). *Sofia* is the name of **one particular** girl.

| General word (common noun) | Special name (proper noun) |
|---|---|
| girl | **S**ofia |
| city | **P**aris |
| country | **J**apan |
| river | the **N**ile |
| day | **M**onday |
| school | **G**reen **P**ark **S**chool |

## 5. Definitions in easy English

> **A common noun** is a general name for any person, animal, place or thing of one type. It starts with a **small letter** (unless it is the first word of a sentence).
>
> **A proper noun** is the special name of **one particular** person, place, day, organisation, etc. It **always** starts with a **capital letter**.

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **common noun** | a general name | *teacher, river, dog* |
| **proper noun** | a special name of one particular thing | *Mrs Clark, the Amazon, Rex* |
| **capital letter** | big letter | *P, L, M* |
| **article** | the words *a, an, the* | *the Nile* |
| **brand** | the name of a company's product | *Nike, Toyota, Apple* |
| **title** | (1) Mr, Mrs, Dr… (2) the name of a book/film/song | *Dr Lee*, *Harry Potter* |

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 Kinds of proper nouns

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| 👩 People (first and family names) | *Ana, Kofi, Maria Lopez, Mr Smith, Dr Chen* |
| 🐾 Pets | *Luna, Rex, Max* |
| 📍 Cities, towns | *Tokyo, Cairo, Rome* |
| 📍 Countries, continents | *Brazil, India, Africa, Europe* |
| 📍 Streets, buildings, parks | *Park Street, the White House, Central Park* |
| 📍 Rivers, seas, mountains | *the Nile, the Red Sea, Mount Everest* |
| 💭 Days, months, holidays | *Monday, July, Christmas, Ramadan, Diwali* |
| 💭 Languages, nationalities | *English, Arabic, Japanese, Brazilian* |
| 🏢 Organisations, companies, brands | *the United Nations, Google, Toyota* |
| 📚 Titles (books, films, songs) | *The Lion King, Romeo and Juliet* |
| 🏫 Schools, universities | *Green Park School, Oxford University* |

> ⚠️ **Not proper nouns** (so small letters): seasons (*summer, winter*), school subjects except languages (*maths, history, science* — but *English, French* ✅ capital), directions (*north, south*) — unless part of a name (*North America*).

### 7.2 Capital letters for every important word in a name

When a proper noun has several words, each important word gets a capital:

> **N**ew **Y**ork · **S**outh **A**frica · **G**reen **P**ark **S**chool · the **U**nited **N**ations

Small words in the middle (*of, the, and*) usually stay small:

> the **U**niversity **o**f **T**oronto · the **G**ulf **o**f **M**exico

### 7.3 A common noun can become part of a name

| Common noun (small) | Proper noun (capital) |
|---|---|
| *I go to a big **school**.* | *I go to **Green Park School**.* |
| *There is a **river** near my town.* | *The **Thames River** / The **River Thames** is in London.* |
| *My **mum** is a teacher.* | *Can I go out, **Mum**?* (used as her name) |
| *The **doctor** is busy.* | *Is **Doctor Lee** here?* (with a name) |
| *She lives on a quiet **street**.* | *She lives on **Oak Street**.* |

**Rule:** used alone, as a general word → **small**. Used **as a name** (or part of one) → **Capital**.

### 7.4 Proper nouns and "the"

Most proper nouns take **no article**:

> ❌ *the Ana · the London · the Japan · the Monday · the English* (language)
> ✅ *Ana · London · Japan · Monday · English*

But some kinds **usually take *the***:

| Takes *the* | Examples |
|---|---|
| Rivers, seas, oceans | *the Nile, the Amazon, the Mediterranean, the Pacific* |
| Mountain **ranges** (groups of mountains) | *the Alps, the Andes, the Himalayas* |
| Country names with *States, Kingdom, Republic*, or plural | *the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, the Philippines* |
| Some famous buildings, hotels, organisations | *the Eiffel Tower, the Hilton, the United Nations* |
| A whole family (plural) | *the Smiths* = Mr and Mrs Smith and their children |

No *the*: single mountains (*Mount Everest*), lakes (*Lake Victoria*), most countries, cities, streets, people, days.

> 💡 You don't need to memorise every case now. **Learn each name with its *the* (or without it)**, like a new word. Full article rules: [L-033](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-033-the-article-the.md), [L-034](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-034-zero-article.md), and geographical names in Level 3 ([CURRICULUM_MAP](../../CURRICULUM_MAP.md)).

### 7.5 Proper nouns usually have no plural

A proper noun names **one** particular thing, so it doesn't usually take -s.
Exceptions: *the Smiths* (the Smith family), *There are two Annas in my class* (two people called Anna).

## 8. Basic sentence patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Proper noun + *be* + a/an + common noun | ***Rome** is a **city**.* |
| Common noun + *be* + proper noun | *My **teacher** is **Mrs Clark**.* |
| Proper noun + verb + preposition + proper noun | ***Ali** lives in **Dubai**.* |
| Proper noun + *be* + from + proper noun | ***Kofi** is from **Ghana**.* |
| on + day / in + month | *on **Monday** · in **July*** |

## 9. Rules and exceptions (summary)

| Rule | Exception |
|---|---|
| Proper nouns always have capitals. | Some brands use small letters on purpose (*iPhone, eBay*). Copy the official spelling. |
| Common nouns have small letters. | First word of a sentence; part of a name (*Oak **S**treet*). |
| Proper nouns usually have no *the*. | Rivers, seas, ranges, *the USA/UK*, plural names. |
| Proper nouns usually have no plural. | *the Smiths*, *two Annas*. |

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-022-001 — "Rome is a beautiful city."

- **Meaning:** Rome is a city, and it is very nice to look at. 🏛️
- **Situation:** A friend asks about your trip to Italy.
- **Main lesson:** A proper noun (*Rome*) and a common noun (*city*) for the same place.
- **Parts:** **Rome** (subject, proper noun) · **is** · **a beautiful city** (noun phrase, common noun *city*)
- **Why capital R?** *Rome* is the name of one particular city (and the first word).
- **Why small c?** *city* is the general word; there are thousands of cities.
- **Why "a" before city but not before Rome?** *city* is a countable common noun → needs *a* ([L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md) §9). *Rome* is a proper noun → no article.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The Rome is a beautiful city.* · ❌ *Rome is a beautiful City.*
- **Pattern:** **City name + is a/an + adjective + city.**
- **Quick check:** Write a sentence about your city.

---

### EX-022-002 — "My teacher is Mrs Clark."

- **Meaning:** The name of my teacher is Mrs Clark.
- **Situation:** Your parent asks who your new teacher is ([S-001](../../07-reading-and-stories/beginner-stories/story-001-a-new-student.md)).
- **Main lesson:** Common noun (*teacher*) + proper noun (*Mrs Clark*).
- **Parts:** **My teacher** (subject) · **is** · **Mrs Clark** (proper noun after *be*)
- **Word by word:**
  - **teacher** — small *t*: a general job word.
  - **Mrs Clark** — title + family name. Both capitals ([L-017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My Teacher is mrs clark.* — capitals in the wrong places.
- **Pattern:** **My + common noun + is + Proper Noun.** → *My doctor is Dr Patel.*
- **Quick check:** Why does *Mrs* have a capital?

---

### EX-022-003 — "Kofi is from Ghana. He speaks English and Twi."

- **Meaning:** Kofi's country is Ghana. He can speak two languages: English and Twi (a language of Ghana).
- **Situation:** Introducing a classmate.
- **Main lesson:** Proper nouns for a **person**, a **country**, and **languages**.
- **Proper nouns:** **Kofi** (person), **Ghana** (country), **English**, **Twi** (languages).
- **Remember 🔁:** Languages always have capitals ([L-002](../../00-foundations/01-what-is-language/lesson-002-what-is-english-used-for.md)). *He* = Kofi ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Kofi is from the Ghana.* (no *the* with most countries) · ❌ *He speaks english.*
- **Pattern:** **Name + is from + country. He/She speaks + language(s).**
- **Quick check:** Write this about yourself.

---

### EX-022-004 — "The Nile is the longest river in Africa."

- **Meaning:** Of all the rivers in Africa, the Nile is the longest. 🌊
- **Situation:** A geography lesson.
- **Main lesson:** A proper noun that takes **the** (a river).
- **Proper nouns:** **the Nile** (river), **Africa** (continent). **Common noun:** *river*.
- **Why "the Nile"?** River names usually take *the* (§7.4). But **Africa** (a continent) takes **no** *the*.
- **the longest** — "most long"; superlative adjectives need *the* ([L-073](../adjectives/lesson-073-superlative-adjectives.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Nile is the longest river in Africa.* · ❌ *The Nile is the longest river in the Africa.*
- **Note:** Some say the Amazon is longer — scientists still discuss it! The grammar is the same.
- **Pattern:** **The + river name + is …**
- **Quick check:** *the* or no *the*? ___ Amazon · ___ Brazil

---

### EX-022-005 — "My birthday is in May."

- **Meaning:** The day I was born each year is in the month of May. 🎂
- **Situation:** A friend asks: "When's your birthday?"
- **Main lesson:** Months are proper nouns → capital.
- **Nouns:** **birthday** (common, small) · **May** (proper, capital)
- **in May** — use *in* + month ([L-050](../prepositions/lesson-050-prepositions-of-time-at-on-in.md)).
- **Careful:** *may* (small) is also a different word — a helping verb: *May I come in?* The capital tells you which: **May** = the month.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My birthday is in may.* · ❌ *My Birthday is in May.*
- **Pattern:** **My birthday is in + Month.**
- **Quick check:** When is your birthday? Write it.

---

### EX-022-006 — "We have a holiday in summer, but not in winter."

- **Meaning:** We get free time (no school/work) in the summer season, but not in the winter season.
- **Situation:** Explaining school breaks.
- **Main lesson:** **Seasons** are common nouns → small letters.
- **Nouns:** *holiday*, *summer*, *winter* — all **common** (small).
- **Remember 🔁:** Days and months → capital; seasons → small ([L-004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *We have a holiday in Summer.*
- **Compare:** *the **Summer** Olympics* — here *Summer* is part of a **name** → capital.
- **Pattern:** **in + season** (small).
- **Quick check:** Capital or small? ___pring, ___uesday, ___ugust.

---

### EX-022-007 — "I go to a big school. Its name is Riverside School."

- **Meaning:** My school is big. The school is called Riverside School.
- **Situation:** Describing your school to a pen pal.
- **Main lesson:** A **common noun becomes part of a proper noun**.
- **First *school*** — common noun (*a big school*): small *s*, with *a*.
- **Riverside School** — the special name of **one** school: both words capital, no *a*.
- **Its** — belongs to it (the school). ⚠️ *its* (no apostrophe) ≠ *it's* (= it is) ([L-088](../punctuation/lesson-088-apostrophes.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Its name is Riverside school.* · ❌ *I go to a Riverside School.*
- **Pattern:** **I go to + School Name.** (no article)
- **Quick check:** Write the name of a school, shop or park near you.

---

### EX-022-008 — "Can I watch TV, Mum?" / "My mum is a nurse."

- **Meaning:** (1) A child asks their mother for permission. (2) A child tells someone what their mother's job is.
- **Situation:** At home (1) and at school (2).
- **Main lesson:** *Mum* as a **name** (capital) vs *mum* as a **common noun** (small).
- **Sentence 1:** *Mum* = what you **call** her, like her name → **M**um.
- **Sentence 2:** *my mum* — with *my*, it is a common noun → **m**um.
- **Same for:** *Dad / my dad*, *Grandma / my grandma*.
- **Tip:** if you can put **my** before it, use a small letter.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My Mum is a nurse.* (in careful writing, small *m* after *my*) — but you will see both in real texts.
- **Quick check:** Capital or small? *Thank you, ___ad!* · *My ___ad is tall.*

---

### EX-022-009 — "Is the Eiffel Tower in Paris?" — "Yes, it is."

- **Meaning:** Is the famous tall metal tower in the city of Paris? — Yes.
- **Situation:** A quiz question.
- **Main lesson:** A proper noun with **the** (a famous building) in a question.
- **Proper nouns:** **the Eiffel Tower**, **Paris**.
- **Why *the*?** Many famous buildings and monuments take *the* (§7.4). Both **E**iffel and **T**ower have capitals — *Tower* is part of the name.
- **Short answer:** *it* = the Eiffel Tower ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Is Eiffel Tower in Paris?* · ❌ *Is the eiffel tower in Paris?*
- **Quick check:** Write a question about a famous building in your country.

---

### EX-022-010 — "My brother doesn't work for Google. He works for a small company."

- **Meaning:** My brother's employer is not Google. His employer is a small business.
- **Situation:** Someone thinks your brother works at a famous company.
- **Main lesson:** A company name (proper) vs *company* (common); in a negative sentence.
- **Nouns:** *brother* (common), **Google** (proper), *company* (common).
- **Articles:** **Google** — no article. **a small company** — common countable noun → *a*.
- **doesn't work** — negative with *doesn't* + verb with no -s ([L-044](../verbs/lesson-044-present-simple-negative.md)).
- **work for** = be employed by.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *He works for the Google.* · ❌ *He works for a Small Company.*
- **Pattern:** **work for + Company Name / a + adjective + company**
- **Quick check:** Name a company you know, and use *work for*.

---

### EX-022-011 — "The Smiths live on Oak Street, next to the park."

- **Meaning:** The Smith family has a house on the street called Oak Street. It is beside the park.
- **Situation:** Giving directions to a neighbour's house.
- **Main lesson:** A **plural family name** with *the*; a street name; a common noun (*park*).
- **Proper nouns:** **the Smiths** (= Mr and Mrs Smith and their family — plural → **live**), **Oak Street**.
- **Common nouns:** *park* (no name given → small).
- **on + street** — we live **on** a street (UK also: *in Oak Street*).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The Smith live on Oak Street.* (family = plural → *Smiths*) · ❌ *…on Oak street.*
- **Pattern:** **The + family name + s + live on + Street Name.**
- **Quick check:** How would you write the family of Mr and Mrs Brown?

---

### EX-022-012 — "Have you read Harry Potter? It's my favourite book."

- **Meaning:** Did you ever read the book *Harry Potter*? I like it more than any other book. 📖⚡
- **Situation:** Talking about books with a classmate.
- **Main lesson:** A **title** is a proper noun.
- **Proper noun:** **Harry Potter** (title — and also the name of a character).
- **Common noun:** *book* (small).
- **Writing titles:** In typed text, titles are often in *italics* (*Harry Potter*) or "quotation marks". Important words get capitals: *The Lord **of** the Rings* (small *of*) ([L-004](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-004-capital-and-small-letters.md) rule 7).
- **Have you read…?** — present perfect question (you will learn it in Level 3 — [L-101](../../02-intermediate-grammar/tenses/lesson-101-present-perfect-form.md)). For now, learn it as a phrase: "*Did you ever read…?*"
- **Wrong:** ❌ *It's my favourite Book.*
- **Quick check:** Write the title of your favourite film with correct capitals.

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| i live in cairo. | I live in Cairo. | Capital for *I* and city names. |
| I study English and Maths. | I study English and **m**aths. | Only languages get capitals among school subjects. |
| The London is big. | London is big. | Cities: no *the*. |
| I swam in Nile. | I swam in **the** Nile. | Rivers take *the*. |
| We visited the Japan. | We visited Japan. | Most countries: no *the*. |
| I live in USA. | I live in **the** USA. | *the* with *States*. |
| My Friend Ana. | My **f**riend Ana. | *friend* is a common noun. |
| See you on monday! | See you on **M**onday! | Days → capital. |
| in Winter | in **w**inter | Seasons → small. |

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** Where do you work?
> **B:** At **Sunrise Hotel**, on **King Street**.
> **A:** Oh, the big **hotel** near the **river**?
> **B:** Yes! Near **the Thames**.

- *Sunrise Hotel*, *King Street*, *the Thames* — proper nouns (capitals). *hotel*, *river* — common nouns (small).
- Notice: B first uses the **name** (*Sunrise Hotel*); A uses the **general word** (*the big hotel*) for the same place.

## 15. Story-based example

> *In **July**, **Lena** and her family flew from **Berlin** to **Lisbon**. They stayed in a small **hotel** near the **sea**. Every **morning**, they ate fresh **bread** at a **café** called **Sol**.*

- Proper: July, Lena, Berlin, Lisbon, Sol.
- Common: family, hotel, sea, morning, bread, café.
- *flew* = past of *fly*.

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

- Capitals are **invisible** in speech! When listening, use **context**: *I bought an apple* (fruit) vs *an Apple* (computer) — usually the situation tells you.
- In names with two words, the **second** word is often stressed: *New **YORK**, Central **PARK***. But *-Street* names stress the first word: ***OAK** Street*.
- Many place names don't sound like their spelling: *Leicester* = "LES-ter", *Edinburgh* = "ED-in-bruh". Always check the sound of a new name.

## 18. Guided practice

Sentence: *last summer, my cousin sara visited the united kingdom and saw big ben.*

1. First word → **L**ast.
2. *summer* — season → small.
3. *cousin* — common noun → small.
4. *sara* → **S**ara (person).
5. *the united kingdom* → **the U**nited **K**ingdom (*the* stays small in the middle of a sentence).
6. *big ben* → **B**ig **B**en (a famous clock tower).

✅ ***Last summer, my cousin Sara visited the United Kingdom and saw Big Ben.***

## 20. Quick revision sheet

- **Common noun** = general word, small letter: *city, girl, river*.
- **Proper noun** = special name, **Capital**: *Paris, Sofia, the Nile*.
- Proper: people, pets, places, days, months, holidays, languages, nationalities, companies, titles.
- Not proper: seasons, school subjects (except languages), directions.
- Usually **no *the***: *London, Japan, Ana, Monday*.
- **With *the***: rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, *the USA/UK*, famous buildings, *the Smiths*.
- A common noun used as a name → capital: *Mum, Oak Street, Riverside School*.
