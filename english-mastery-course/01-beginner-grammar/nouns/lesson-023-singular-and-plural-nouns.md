## 1. Learning objectives

1. Know what **singular** (one) and **plural** (more than one) mean.
2. Make regular plurals with **-s**.
3. Make **everything around the noun match**: *a/an*, *this/these*, *is/are*, *it/they*, the verb **-s**.
4. Use plurals with **numbers**, and for things **in general** (*Dogs are friendly*).
5. Know special nouns: **always plural** (*jeans, glasses*) and **never plural** (*water, information*) — first look.

## 2. Why this topic matters

In English, being "one" or "many" changes **several words** in the sentence, not only the noun:

> ***This** **cat** **is** hungry. **It** want**s** food.* → ***These** **cats** **are** hungry. **They** want food.*

Five words changed! If you learn to make them all match, your English will sound much more correct. This "matching" is called **agreement**. It's one of the most common areas of learner mistakes.

## 3. Remember from before 🔁

- A **-s** on a noun means **more than one**: *friends, books* ([L-007](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
- **One → is**, **more than one → are** ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- **this/that** (one) · **these/those** (more than one) ([L-019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)).
- After *one* → singular; after *two, three…* → plural ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).
- The verb agrees with the **head noun** ([L-021 §7.4](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

## 4. Simple introduction

> 🍎 **one apple** — singular
> 🍎🍎🍎 **three apples** — plural

That's the basic idea. The rest of this lesson is about how the **other words** in the sentence change too.

## 5. Definitions in easy English

> **Singular** = one. *a book, the girl, my car*
> **Plural** = more than one. *books, the girls, my cars*

## 6. Key words

| Word | Simple meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **singular** | one | *cat* |
| **plural** | more than one | *cats* |
| **regular plural** | plural made with **-s** (or -es) | *dog → dogs* |
| **irregular plural** | plural made in a different way | *man → men* ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)) |
| **agreement** | when words in a sentence match in number (one/many) | *The **dogs are**…* |
| **countable noun** | a noun you can count: *one cup, two cups* | |
| **uncountable noun** | a noun you can't count with numbers: *water, music* ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)) | |

## 7. Detailed explanation

### 7.1 The basic rule: add -s

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| book | book**s** |
| car | car**s** |
| friend | friend**s** |
| idea | idea**s** |
| house | house**s** |

Some nouns add **-es**, or change their ending: *box → boxes, city → cities, knife → knives.* These **spelling rules** are in [L-024](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md). Some are completely different: *child → children*, *man → men* ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).

### 7.2 Agreement: make everything match

When the noun becomes plural, check these **5 partners**:

| Partner | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **1. a / an** | **a** book | ~~a~~ books (no *a*!) |
| **2. this / that** | **this** book | **these** books |
| **3. be** | the book **is** | the books **are** |
| **4. pronoun** | **it** is new | **they** are new |
| **5. verb -s** (present) | the dog bark**s** | the dogs bark |

> 💡 **The "one -s" rule** (present tense): in a simple subject + verb sentence, the **-s** usually appears **once** — on the noun **or** on the verb, not both.
> *The dog bark**s**.* (one dog → -s on the verb) · *The dog**s** bark.* (many dogs → -s on the noun)
> This is a helpful pattern, not a rule for every sentence — but it catches many mistakes.

### 7.3 "a/an" only with singular

**a** and **an** mean **one**, so they **never** go with plural nouns:

> ❌ *a books* · ❌ *an apples*
> ✅ *a book* · ✅ *books* · ✅ *some books* · ✅ *the books* · ✅ *two books*

### 7.4 Plurals for things "in general"

To talk about a type of thing **in general** (all of them, not specific ones), English often uses the **plural with no article**:

> ***Dogs** are friendly.* (dogs in general)
> *I like **cats**.* (all cats, cats as animals)
> ***Children** need sleep.*

Compare:
> *The dogs are friendly.* → specific dogs (for example, the dogs in this house).
> ❌ *I like cat.* / ❌ *I like the cats.* (when you mean cats in general)

(Full article rules: [L-033](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-033-the-article-the.md), [L-034](../articles-and-determiners/lesson-034-zero-article.md).)

### 7.5 Plurals with numbers and quantity words

> *one **book** · two **books** · 100 **books***
> *many **books** · a lot of **books** · some **books** · a few **books***
> *How many **books**…?* ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md))

⚠️ Number words **before** a noun don't take -s: ✅ *two hundred people* (not *two hundreds people*).

### 7.6 Singular with every / each / one of

> ***every** student* (singular!) → *Every student **has** a book.*
> ***each** day* (singular)
> ***one of** the student**s*** — plural noun after *one of the*, but the verb is **singular**: *One of the students **is** late.*

(Full lesson on *each/every*: Level 3; agreement: [L-193](../../02-intermediate-grammar/subject-verb-agreement/lesson-193-subject-verb-agreement-basics.md).)

### 7.7 Special nouns (first look)

**Always plural** — things with two parts, and a few others. They use **are/they** even for one item:

> *jeans, trousers (US: pants), shorts, glasses (for eyes 👓), scissors ✂️, clothes*
> *My **jeans are** blue.* ❌ *My jeans is blue.*
> To count them: *a **pair of** jeans, two **pairs of** glasses.*

**Usually no plural** — uncountable nouns:

> *water, milk, rice, money, music, information, advice, furniture, homework*
> ❌ *informations* · ❌ *advices* · ❌ *a homework*
> Full lesson: [L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md).

**Looks plural but is singular:** *news* (*The news **is** good.*), *maths/mathematics*, *physics*.

### 7.8 Describing words don't change

> *a **big** dog → two **big** dogs* (not *bigs*) ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md))

## 8. Basic sentence patterns

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| *This is a pen.* | *These are pens.* |
| *The cat is black.* | *The cats are black.* |
| *My brother works here.* | *My brothers work here.* |
| *Is the shop open? — Yes, it is.* | *Are the shops open? — Yes, they are.* |
| *There is a car outside.* | *There are two cars outside.* |

## 9. Rules and exceptions (summary)

| Rule | Exception |
|---|---|
| Plural = noun + -s. | -es / -ies / -ves spellings ([L-024](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)); irregular ([L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)). |
| No *a/an* with plurals. | — (none) |
| Plural subject → *are*, verb with no -s. | *every/each + singular*; *one of the + plural* → singular verb. |
| Uncountable nouns have no plural. | Some can be countable with another meaning (*a coffee* = a cup of coffee) ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). |
| Two-part things are always plural. | Count with *a pair of*. |
| Adjectives don't change. | — |

## 10. Examples (fully explained)

### EX-023-001 — "I have a brother and two sisters."

- **Meaning:** In my family there is one boy (my brother) and two girls (my sisters), not counting me.
- **Situation:** Someone asks about your family.
- **Main lesson:** Singular with **a**; plural with a **number** + **-s**.
- **Parts:** **I** · **have** · **a brother and two sisters** (object: two noun phrases joined by *and*)
- **Word by word:**
  - **a brother** — singular → **a** + no -s.
  - **two sisters** — plural → number + **-s**. No *a*.
- **Remember 🔁:** *have* = own/hold, also for family ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)). Numbers ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *I have a brother and two sister.* · ❌ *I have a brothers.*
- **Other versions:** *I have **three** brother**s** and **a** sister.* · *I have **no** brothers or sisters.* (*no* + plural)
- **Pattern:** **I have a + singular / number + plural.**
- **Quick check:** Write about your family.

---

### EX-023-002 — "This cat is hungry. These cats are hungry."

- **Meaning:** One cat (near me) wants food. / More than one cat (near me) wants food. 🐈
- **Situation:** You are feeding cats at an animal shelter.
- **Main lesson:** Three partners change together: **this → these**, **cat → cats**, **is → are**.
- **Compare:**

  | This | cat | is | hungry. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **These** | **cats** | **are** | hungry. |

- **What doesn't change:** *hungry* (adjective) ([§7.8](#78-describing-words-dont-change)).
- **Remember 🔁:** *this/these* ([L-019](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-019-this-is-and-that-is.md)), *is/are* ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *This cats are hungry.* · ❌ *These cats is hungry.* · ❌ *These cat are hungry.*
- **Pronunciation:** *this* (short i, /s/) vs *these* (long ee, /z/).
- **Pattern:** **These + plural noun + are + adjective.**
- **Quick check:** Make plural: *That dog is big.*

---

### EX-023-003 — "The dog barks at night. The dogs bark at night."

- **Meaning:** One dog / more than one dog makes loud noises at night. 🐕🌙
- **Situation:** You are complaining to a neighbour about noise.
- **Main lesson:** The **verb -s** changes too (the "one -s" pattern, [§7.2](#72-agreement-make-everything-match)).
- **Compare:**
  - *The dog bark**s***. — singular subject → **-s on the verb**.
  - *The dog**s** bark.* — plural subject → **-s on the noun**, none on the verb.
- **Remember 🔁:** One person/thing (he/she/it) + verb-s ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)). Full lesson: [L-043](../verbs/lesson-043-present-simple-he-she-it-s.md).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The dogs barks at night.* (two -s) · ❌ *The dog bark at night.* (no -s at all)
- **Pattern:** **The + singular + verb-s** / **The + plural + verb**.
- **Quick check:** *My friends (live / lives) in Rome.*

---

### EX-023-004 — "Is the window open?" — "No, it isn't." / "Are the windows open?" — "No, they aren't."

- **Meaning:** Asking if one / several windows are not closed.
- **Situation:** It's raining. You're leaving the house.
- **Main lesson:** Singular/plural in **questions** and **short answers** (*it* ↔ *they*).
- **Partners:** *Is ↔ Are* · *window ↔ windows* · *it ↔ they* · *isn't ↔ aren't*.
- **Remember 🔁:** Questions with *be* — move it to the front ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Are the windows open? — No, it isn't.* (plural question → *they*)
- **Pattern:** **Is the + singular…? — it** / **Are the + plurals…? — they**
- **Quick check:** Answer: *Are the shops closed?* (Yes)

---

### EX-023-005 — "Dogs are loyal animals."

- **Meaning:** In general, dogs stay with and protect their owners. 🐕❤️
- **Situation:** Explaining why you want a dog.
- **Main lesson:** **Plural + no article** = things in general ([§7.4](#74-plurals-for-things-in-general)).
- **Parts:** **Dogs** (subject, general) · **are** · **loyal animals** (plural noun phrase after *be*)
- **Why *animals* is plural:** the subject is plural, so the noun after *be* is plural too: *Dogs are animal**s***.
- **Word by word:** **loyal** = always supporting someone, faithful.
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The dogs are loyal animals.* (sounds like specific dogs) · ❌ *Dog is loyal animal.* · ❌ *Dogs are loyal animal.*
- **Also correct (more formal):** *The dog is a loyal animal.* (*the* + singular for a whole type — less common; Level 6)
- **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective + plural noun.**
- **Quick check:** Write a general sentence about cats.

---

### EX-023-006 — "We need three tickets, please."

- **Meaning:** I'm asking to buy three tickets. 🎟️
- **Situation:** At a cinema or train station.
- **Main lesson:** Number + plural; in a polite request.
- **Parts:** **We** · **need** · **three tickets** (object) · **please**
- **Word by word:** **ticket** — a paper or digital pass for a train, film, concert. **three** — a number; adjectives and numbers stay the same.
- **Natural speech:** *Three tickets, please.* (short form — very common at a ticket desk)
- **Wrong:** ❌ *We need three ticket.* · ❌ *We need a three tickets.*
- **Pattern:** **Number + plural noun, please.**
- **Quick check:** Ask for two coffees.

---

### EX-023-007 — "How many languages do you speak?" — "Two: Hindi and English."

- **Meaning:** What is the number of languages you can speak? — Two.
- **Situation:** Getting to know a classmate.
- **Main lesson:** **How many** + plural noun.
- **Parts:** **How many languages** (object, moved to the front) · **do** · **you** · **speak**
- **Why plural?** *How many* asks for a number → always plural noun ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).
- **Remember 🔁:** Wh-question with *do* ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)). Language names → capital ([L-022](lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *How many language do you speak?* · ❌ *How much languages…?*
- **Answer with one:** *Just one: Spanish.*
- **Pattern:** **How many + plural + do/does + subject + verb?**
- **Quick check:** Ask how many brothers someone has.

---

### EX-023-008 — "Every student has a laptop."

- **Meaning:** All the students have a laptop — each one has their own. 💻
- **Situation:** Describing a modern school.
- **Main lesson:** **every + singular** noun + singular verb ([§7.6](#76-singular-with-every--each--one-of)).
- **Parts:** **Every student** (subject, singular!) · **has** · **a laptop**
- **Why singular?** *Every* looks at the students **one by one**: student 1 has a laptop, student 2 has a laptop… So: *every student **has***.
- **has** — the he/she/it form of *have* ([L-040](../verbs/lesson-040-have-and-have-got.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Every students have a laptop.* · ❌ *Every student have a laptop.*
- **Same meaning, plural:** ***All** the students **have** laptops.*
- **Pattern:** **Every + singular noun + verb-s.**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *Every rooms have a TV.*

---

### EX-023-009 — "One of my friends is a chef."

- **Meaning:** I have many friends. One person among them works as a chef (a professional cook). 👨‍🍳
- **Situation:** Recommending a restaurant.
- **Main lesson:** **one of + plural noun**, but the verb is **singular**.
- **Parts:** **One of my friends** (subject; head = **One**) · **is** · **a chef**
- **Why *friends* is plural:** you choose one **from a group** of friends → *of my friend**s***.
- **Why *is*:** the head of the subject is **one** → singular verb. (Remember: the verb agrees with the head — [L-021](lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases).)
- **Wrong:** ❌ *One of my friend is a chef.* · ❌ *One of my friends are a chef.*
- **Pattern:** **One of + my/the + plural noun + is…**
- **Quick check:** *One of the windows (is / are) broken.*

---

### EX-023-010 — "My new jeans are too long."

- **Meaning:** The new jeans I bought are longer than my legs. 👖
- **Situation:** You're trying on clothes at home.
- **Main lesson:** A noun that is **always plural** ([§7.7](#77-special-nouns-first-look)).
- **Parts:** **My new jeans** (subject, plural) · **are** · **too long**
- **Why *are*?** *jeans* is always plural (it has two legs). Even for **one** item, we say *jeans are*.
- **too long** — more long than you want (*too* = more than is good) ([L-094](../adjectives/lesson-094-too-enough-very.md)).
- **Counting:** *I bought **a pair of** jeans.* / *I have three **pairs of** jeans.*
- **Wrong:** ❌ *My new jeans is too long.* · ❌ *I bought a jean.* · ❌ *I bought a jeans.*
- **Same for:** *trousers, shorts, glasses, scissors, pyjamas.*
- **Pattern:** **My + always-plural noun + are…** / **a pair of + always-plural noun**
- **Quick check:** Correct: *Where is my glasses?*

---

### EX-023-011 — "Can you give me some information about the trains?"

- **Meaning:** Please tell me facts about the trains (times, prices, platforms). 🚆
- **Situation:** At a station information desk.
- **Main lesson:** An **uncountable** noun (*information*, no plural) next to a **plural** noun (*trains*).
- **Parts:** **Can you give me** · **some information** (uncountable) · **about the trains** (plural, after preposition)
- **Why no -s on *information*?** It's uncountable: ❌ *informations*, ❌ *an information*. Use **some information** or **a piece of information** ([L-026](lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
- **Why *trains*?** There are many trains; you want information about all of them → plural.
- **Remember 🔁:** *Can you…?* polite request ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *Can you give me some informations…?* · ❌ *…about the train?* (if you mean all trains)
- **Pattern:** **some + uncountable** / **the + plural**.
- **Quick check:** Correct: *She gave me many advices.*

---

### EX-023-012 — "The news is good: the tickets are cheap!"

- **Meaning:** I have good information: the tickets don't cost a lot.
- **Situation:** You found cheap tickets for a concert and tell your friend.
- **Main lesson:** A noun that **looks plural but is singular** (*news*) and a real plural (*tickets*).
- **Parts:** **The news** (subject, singular!) · **is** · **good** · **:** · **the tickets** (plural) · **are** · **cheap**
- **Why *the news is*?** *News* ends in -s but it's an uncountable singular noun. ❌ *The news are good.* ❌ *a news* → ✅ *a piece of news* / *some news*.
- **The colon ( : )** — introduces the explanation of what the news is (punctuation: [L-256](../../02-intermediate-grammar/punctuation/lesson-256-quotation-marks-and-direct-speech.md) and Level 5).
- **Wrong:** ❌ *The news are good.* · ❌ *The tickets is cheap.*
- **Pattern:** **The news is + adjective.**
- **Quick check:** *Maths (is / are) my favourite subject.*

## 11. Common mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I have two cat. | I have two cat**s**. | number 2+ → plural |
| a books | a book / books | no *a* with plural |
| This books are mine. | **These** books are mine. | this → these |
| The children is happy. | The children **are** happy. | plural → are |
| My friends lives in Rome. | My friends **live** in Rome. | plural subject → no -s on verb |
| Every students have… | Every **student has**… | every + singular |
| One of my friend is… | One of my friend**s** is… | one of + plural |
| My glasses is broken. | My glasses **are** broken. | always plural |
| informations / advices | information / advice | uncountable |
| The news are bad. | The news **is** bad. | *news* is singular |
| two bigs houses | two big houses | adjectives don't change |
| I like the dogs. (in general) | I like dogs. | general → plural, no *the* |

## 14. Everyday conversation

> **A:** How many **people** are coming to the party?
> **B:** About twenty. Can you buy some **drinks** and two big **pizzas**?
> **A:** Sure. And **cups**?
> **B:** We have **cups**. But we need **plates**. Oh — and some **ice**!

- *people* = plural of *person* (irregular — [L-025](lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)) → *are coming*.
- *ice* — uncountable (no -s) → *some ice*.

## 15. Story-based example

> *Grandpa has a small **farm**. He has two **cows**, ten **chickens** and a **horse**. Every **morning**, the **chickens** wake him up. The **horse** is old, but it still works.*

- Singular: farm, horse, morning (*Every morning* — singular after *every*).
- Plural: cows, chickens (→ *wake*, no -s).
- *the horse … it* (singular pronoun) ✅.

## 16. Pronunciation and listening 🗣️👂

The plural **-s** has **three sounds** — full lesson with rules in [L-024 §7.6](lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md#76-how-to-pronounce-plural--s-and--es):

| Sound | Examples |
|---|---|
| /s/ | cat**s**, book**s**, cup**s** |
| /z/ | dog**s**, car**s**, friend**s** |
| /ɪz/ (an extra syllable "iz") | box**es**, bus**es**, hous**es** |

**Listening tip:** the plural -s is small and quick. Listen for the **other partners** too: *these, are, they* — they tell you it's plural!

## 18. Guided practice

Make the sentence plural, step by step: ***This** **phone** **is** expensive. **It** **has** a big screen.*

1. *This → These*
2. *phone → phones*
3. *is → are*
4. *It → They*
5. *has → have*
6. *a big screen → big screens* (each phone has a screen → plural; no *a*)

✅ ***These phones are expensive. They have big screens.***

## 20. Quick revision sheet

- **Singular** = one; **plural** = more than one (usually **+s**).
- When the noun is plural, change its partners: **a → (none)** · **this → these** · **is → are** · **it → they** · **verb-s → verb**.
- **General** statements: plural, no article — *Dogs are loyal.*
- **every / each + singular**; **one of + plural** (+ singular verb).
- **Always plural**: jeans, glasses, scissors, trousers, clothes → *are*, *a pair of*.
- **No plural**: information, advice, water, money… → *some*.
- **news** = singular.
- Adjectives **never** change.

## 21. Memory aid

> **"Plural is a team: when the noun changes, the whole team changes its shirt."** 👕👕👕 (*these – cats – are – they*)
