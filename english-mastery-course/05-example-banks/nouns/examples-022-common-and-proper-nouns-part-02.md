**Short reminders:** **P** = proper noun — the name of **one particular** person, place, day, month, holiday, language, brand… → **Capital letter**. **C** = common noun → small letter (unless it starts the sentence). Adjectives made from names (*Japanese, Canadian, French*) also take a capital. Most names have **no *the*** (*Paris, Italy, Ali*). Some take *the*: rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, groups of islands, *the Netherlands / the UK / the USA*, plural family names, many famous buildings and organisations ([L-022 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md#74-proper-nouns-and-the)).

> 🔁 **From Part 01:** names of people, places, days, months, languages and brands have capitals ([EX-022-013 – 050](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-01.md)). In this bank, **P** in step 10 means *proper noun*.

**New words in this part:** Christmas · grandparents · Scotland · writer · Louvre · silent · Mediterranean · Alps · United Nations · Colosseum · Canary Islands · Thanksgiving · china (plates) · turkey (bird).

## Step C (continued) — Questions and negatives

### EX-022-051 — "They don't live in London. They live in Manchester."

1. **Meaning:** Their home is not in London; their home is in Manchester. 🏠🇬🇧
2. **Situation:** Correcting a friend who thinks your cousins live in the capital.
3. **Main lesson:** City names keep their capital letter in **negative** and **positive** sentences.
4. **Grammar used:** present simple negative (*don't live*) + positive (*live*) + *in* + city.
5. **Sentence structure:** **They** (S) → **don't live** (V) → **in London** (place). **They** (S) → **live** (V) → **in Manchester** (place).
6. **Subject:** *They* (twice).
7. **Verb:** *live*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *do* + *not* → *don't* (first sentence only).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in London*, *in Manchester* — places.
10. **Word by word:** *They* · *don't* · *live* · *in* · **London** — P: one city → capital, no *the* · *They* · *live* · *in* · **Manchester** — P: one city.
11. **How the rules work together:** *don't* changes the meaning, not the name — a city is always capital, after *in* with no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We don't have school on Saturday* (EX-022-046) — the same *don't* + proper noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** *don't* + base verb; city names with capitals and no *the*.
14. **Other versions:** *They live in Manchester, not London.* · *Do they live in London? No, in Manchester.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They don't live in london.* → ✅ *London* · ❌ *They live in the Manchester.* → ✅ *in Manchester*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *No, not London — Manchester.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two short sentences, each with a full stop; *don't* has an apostrophe.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LUN-dun" · "MAN-chess-ter"; stress the corrected city: *They live in MANchester.*
19. **Pattern:** **S + don't + verb + in + City. S + verb + in + City.**
20. **More examples:** *We don't live in Lima. We live in Cusco.* · *I don't work in Delhi. I work in Pune.*
21. **Quick check:** Find the two proper nouns. → **Answer:** *London, Manchester*.

### EX-022-052 — "Which language do people speak in Brazil?"

1. **Meaning:** A question: what language do Brazilians use? 🇧🇷❓
2. **Situation:** Planning a holiday and asking a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** A **common noun** (*language, people*) and a **proper noun** (*Brazil*) in one question.
4. **Grammar used:** *Which* + noun + *do* + subject + verb + place?
5. **Sentence structure:** **Which language** (O) → **do** → **people** (S) → **speak** (V) → **in Brazil** (place)?
6. **Subject:** *people*.
7. **Verb:** *speak*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *do* (makes the question).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *which language* (the thing we ask about) · *in Brazil*.
10. **Word by word:** *Which* · *language* — C: any language → small · *do* · *people* — C · *speak* · *in* · **Brazil** — P: one country → capital, no *the*.
11. **How the rules work together:** the word *language* is common (small), but the **name** of a language or country is proper (capital).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Spanish is a beautiful language* (EX-022-042) — *language* small, *Spanish* capital.
13. **Why this is correct:** *Which* + noun + *do* + subject + base verb; *Brazil* with a capital.
14. **Other versions:** *What language do they speak in Brazil?* · *Do people speak Spanish in Brazil?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Which Language do people speak in brazil?* → ✅ *language … Brazil* · ❌ *Which language people speak in Brazil?* → ✅ *do people speak*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *What do they speak in Brazil?* → *Portuguese.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** question mark at the end; capital *B*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "bruh-ZIL"; *language* = "LANG-gwij".
19. **Pattern:** **Which + common noun + do + S + verb + in + Country?**
20. **More examples:** *Which language do people speak in Egypt?* (Arabic) · *Which city do you like in Japan?*
21. **Quick check:** Common or proper: *language*? *Brazil*? → **Answer:** *language* = common · *Brazil* = proper.

## Step D — Names in different times

### EX-022-053 — "Last Christmas, we visited my grandparents in Scotland."

1. **Meaning:** At Christmas last year, we went to see my grandparents in Scotland. 🎄🏴
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend about a winter holiday.
3. **Main lesson:** A **holiday** name and a **country** name in the **past simple**.
4. **Grammar used:** time phrase + past simple (*visited*) + object + place.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Last Christmas** (time), **we** (S) → **visited** (V) → **my grandparents** (O) → **in Scotland** (place).
6. **Subject:** *we*.
7. **Verb:** *visited* (past of *visit*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my grandparents* (O) · *in Scotland* · *Last Christmas*.
10. **Word by word:** *Last* · **Christmas** — P: a holiday → capital · *we* · *visited* · *my* · *grandparents* — C → small · *in* · **Scotland** — P: a country.
11. **How the rules work together:** *last* + holiday name = no *the*, no *on*; family words (*grandparents*) are common.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We celebrate Eid with our family* (EX-022-024) — holidays have capitals.
13. **Why this is correct:** past verb for last year; capitals for the holiday and country only.
14. **Other versions:** *Next Christmas, we will visit them again.* · *We visited my grandparents in Scotland at Christmas.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Last christmas* → ✅ *Christmas* · ❌ *my Grandparents* → ✅ *grandparents* · ❌ *on last Christmas* → ✅ *last Christmas*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We went to Scotland for Christmas to see my grandparents.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the opening time phrase *Last Christmas,*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KRISS-muss" (the *t* is silent) · "SKOT-lund".
19. **Pattern:** **Last + Holiday, S + past verb + O + in + Country.**
20. **More examples:** *Last Easter, we stayed in Wales.* · *Last Diwali, they visited Jaipur.*
21. **Quick check:** Which letter is silent in *Christmas*? → **Answer:** *t*.

### EX-022-054 — "Next Monday, Mr Lee will start his new job."

1. **Meaning:** On the coming Monday, Mr Lee begins a new job. 💼📅
2. **Situation:** Office news.
3. **Main lesson:** A **title + family name** (*Mr Lee*) and a **day** in the **future**.
4. **Grammar used:** *Next* + day, subject + *will* + base verb + object.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Next Monday** (time), **Mr Lee** (S) → **will start** (V) → **his new job** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Mr Lee*.
7. **Verb:** *start*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *will* (future).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *his new job* · *Next Monday*.
10. **Word by word:** *Next* · **Monday** — P: day → capital · **Mr** — title, part of the name → capital · **Lee** — P: family name · *will* · *start* · *his* · *new* · *job* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** a title before a name is part of the name, so both words take capitals; *next* + day has no *on*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Do you know Dr Brown?* (EX-022-045) — title + name.
13. **Why this is correct:** *will* + base verb; capitals for *Monday, Mr, Lee*.
14. **Other versions:** *Mr Lee starts his new job next Monday.* · *Mr Lee is going to start a new job on Monday.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *mr Lee* → ✅ *Mr Lee* · ❌ *On next Monday* → ✅ *Next Monday* · ❌ *will starts* → ✅ *will start*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Mr Lee starts on Monday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** British *Mr* (no dot); American *Mr.* (with a dot) — both OK; comma after *Next Monday*.
18. **Pronunciation:** *Mr* = "MISS-ter".
19. **Pattern:** **Next + Day, Title + Name + will + verb + O.**
20. **More examples:** *Next Friday, Ms Khan will give a talk.* · *Next June, Dr Silva will retire.*
21. **Quick check:** How do we say *Mr*? → **Answer:** "mister".

### EX-022-055 — "Shakespeare was a famous English writer."

1. **Meaning:** Shakespeare, a writer from England, was very well known. 📜✍️
2. **Situation:** A literature class.
3. **Main lesson:** A famous person's name + a **nationality adjective** (capital) + a common noun.
4. **Grammar used:** past simple *was* + *a* + adjective + adjective + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Shakespeare** (S) → **was** → **a famous English writer** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Shakespeare*.
7. **Verb:** *was* (past of *is*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a famous English writer*.
10. **Word by word:** **Shakespeare** — P: one person · *was* · *a* · *famous* — normal adjective, small · **English** — adjective from a place name → capital · *writer* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *famous* is small, *English* is capital — the capital comes from the country name *England*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My friend is Brazilian* (EX-022-021) — nationality words take capitals.
13. **Why this is correct:** past *was* (he lived long ago); capital for the name and for *English*.
14. **Other versions:** *Shakespeare was from England.* · *Was Shakespeare a writer? Yes, he was.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *a famous english writer* → ✅ *English* · ❌ *a English famous writer* → ✅ *a famous English writer* (opinion before origin)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Shakespeare? The famous English writer.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one sentence, full stop; no comma between *famous* and *English*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHAYK-speer" · "ING-glish".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + was + a + adjective + Nationality + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Frida Kahlo was a famous Mexican painter.* · *Mozart was a great Austrian composer.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *famous*? *english*? → **Answer:** *famous* small · *English* capital.

### EX-022-056 — "In 2010, my family moved to Toronto."

1. **Meaning:** In the year 2010 my family went to live in Toronto. 📦🇨🇦
2. **Situation:** Telling your life story.
3. **Main lesson:** **Years** are numbers (no capital needed); **cities** are proper nouns.
4. **Grammar used:** *In* + year, past simple *moved* + *to* + city.
5. **Sentence structure:** **In 2010** (time), **my family** (S) → **moved** (V) → **to Toronto** (place).
6. **Subject:** *my family*.
7. **Verb:** *moved*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *to Toronto* · *In 2010*.
10. **Word by word:** *In* · *2010* — a year, written in numbers · *my* · *family* — C · *moved* · *to* · **Toronto** — P: one city.
11. **How the rules work together:** *in* + year; *move to* + place; the city takes a capital, *family* doesn't.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Lisbon is in Portugal* (EX-022-034) — city names with no *the*.
13. **Why this is correct:** past verb with a past year; *to* shows the new place.
14. **Other versions:** *My family moved to Toronto in 2010.* · *We've lived in Toronto since 2010.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *moved in Toronto* → ✅ *moved to Toronto* · ❌ *My Family* → ✅ *my family* · ❌ *On 2010* → ✅ *In 2010*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We moved to Toronto back in 2010.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *In 2010*; year in numbers.
18. **Pronunciation:** *2010* = "twenty ten" · "tuh-RON-toh".
19. **Pattern:** **In + year, S + moved + to + City.**
20. **More examples:** *In 2015, she moved to Berlin.* · *In 1999, they moved to Sydney.*
21. **Quick check:** *moved ___ Toronto* — *in* or *to*? → **Answer:** *to*.

### EX-022-057 — "The meeting will be on Thursday at Green Hotel."

1. **Meaning:** The meeting happens on Thursday, in a hotel called Green Hotel. 🏨🗓️
2. **Situation:** An email about a work meeting.
3. **Main lesson:** A common noun (*hotel*) becomes **part of a name** (*Green Hotel*) → capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *will be* + *on* + day + *at* + place name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The meeting** (S) → **will be** (V) → **on Thursday** (time) → **at Green Hotel** (place).
6. **Subject:** *The meeting*.
7. **Verb:** *be*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *will*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *on Thursday*, *at Green Hotel*.
10. **Word by word:** *The* · *meeting* — C · *will* · *be* · *on* · **Thursday** — P · *at* · **Green Hotel** — P: the name of one hotel → both words capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a hotel* (any hotel) = small; *Green Hotel* (its name) = capital *H*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Dr Patel works at City Hospital* (EX-022-023) — the same idea with *hospital*.
13. **Why this is correct:** *on* + day; *at* + building name; capitals for the full name.
14. **Other versions:** *The meeting is at Green Hotel on Thursday.* · *We'll meet at the Green Hotel on Thursday.* (many hotel names take *the*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *at Green hotel* → ✅ *Green Hotel* · ❌ *in Thursday* → ✅ *on Thursday*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Thursday, Green Hotel — don't forget!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** both words of the hotel name start with a capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** *Thursday* = "THURZ-day" (*th* as in *think*); *hotel* = "hoh-TEL".
19. **Pattern:** **S + will be + on + Day + at + Building Name.**
20. **More examples:** *The party will be on Saturday at Rose Café.* · *The exam will be on Monday at Hall B.*
21. **Quick check:** *a green hotel* vs *Green Hotel* — which is a name? → **Answer:** *Green Hotel*.

### EX-022-058 — "My grandmother was born in India in May 1950."

1. **Meaning:** My grandmother's birth was in India, in May of the year 1950. 👵🇮🇳
2. **Situation:** A family-history project.
3. **Main lesson:** **Country + month + year** together.
4. **Grammar used:** *was born* + *in* + country + *in* + month + year.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandmother** (S) → **was born** (V) → **in India** (place) → **in May 1950** (time).
6. **Subject:** *My grandmother*.
7. **Verb:** *born*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *was* (*was born* = passive, past).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in India*, *in May 1950*.
10. **Word by word:** *My* · *grandmother* — C · *was born* · *in* · **India** — P · *in* · **May** — P: a month → capital · *1950*.
11. **How the rules work together:** place first, then time; *in* for countries, months and years.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My birthday isn't in June. It's in July* (EX-022-049) — months with *in*.
13. **Why this is correct:** *was born* (we never say *is born* for the past); capitals for *India* and *May*.
14. **Other versions:** *She was born in May 1950, in India.* · *Where was your grandmother born?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My grandmother born in India.* → ✅ *was born* · ❌ *in may* → ✅ *in May* · ❌ *My Grandmother* → ✅ *grandmother*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandma was born in India, in 1950.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma needed between *May* and *1950*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "IN-dee-uh" · *1950* = "nineteen fifty".
19. **Pattern:** **S + was born + in + Country + in + Month + year.**
20. **More examples:** *My father was born in Ghana in March 1972.* · *I was born in Chile in 2001.*
21. **Quick check:** Why does *May* have a capital here? → **Answer:** it is the name of a month.

### EX-022-059 — "We went to the Louvre in Paris last summer."

1. **Meaning:** Last summer we visited the Louvre, a famous museum in Paris. 🖼️🇫🇷
2. **Situation:** Sharing holiday photos.
3. **Main lesson:** Some **famous buildings** take *the* (*the Louvre*); cities don't (*Paris*). **Seasons** are common nouns.
4. **Grammar used:** past simple *went* + *to* + *the* + name + *in* + city + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **went** (V) → **to the Louvre** (place) → **in Paris** (place) → **last summer** (time).
6. **Subject:** *We*.
7. **Verb:** *went* (past of *go*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *to the Louvre*, *in Paris*, *last summer*.
10. **Word by word:** *We* · *went* · *to* · *the* · **Louvre** — P: one museum → capital · *in* · **Paris** — P · *last* · *summer* — C: seasons are **small** in English.
11. **How the rules work together:** months and days have capitals, but seasons (*spring, summer, autumn, winter*) don't.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *October is cold* (EX-022-020) — months take capitals; seasons don't.
13. **Why this is correct:** *the* + *Louvre* (fixed name); *Paris* with no article; *summer* small.
14. **Other versions:** *Last summer we visited the Louvre.* · *Have you been to the Louvre?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *last Summer* → ✅ *summer* · ❌ *to Louvre* → ✅ *to the Louvre* · ❌ *the Paris* → ✅ *Paris*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We did the Louvre last summer — amazing!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *the* small (not part of the name); *Louvre* capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LOO-vruh" (French) or "LOOV".
19. **Pattern:** **S + went to + the + Famous Building + in + City + last + season.**
20. **More examples:** *We went to the Prado in Madrid last spring.* · *They saw the Taj Mahal last winter.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *summer*? → **Answer:** small (seasons are common nouns).

### EX-022-060 — "Tomorrow is New Year's Day."

1. **Meaning:** The day after today is 1 January. 🎆
2. **Situation:** 31 December, at a party.
3. **Main lesson:** A **holiday name with several words** — every main word takes a capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *Tomorrow* + *is* + holiday name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Tomorrow** (S) → **is** → **New Year's Day** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Tomorrow*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *New Year's Day*.
10. **Word by word:** *Tomorrow* · *is* · **New Year's Day** — P: one holiday → **N**, **Y**, **D** all capitals; *'s* = possessive.
11. **How the rules work together:** in a long name every important word is capital; no article before the holiday.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Today is Friday* (EX-022-016) — *Today/Tomorrow is* + name.
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals for all three words; *is* (tomorrow is a fixed date).
14. **Other versions:** *Happy New Year!* · *New Year's Day is on 1 January.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *new year's day* → ✅ *New Year's Day* · ❌ *the New Year's Day* → ✅ *New Year's Day*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's New Year's tomorrow!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe before *s*: *Year's*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "new yeerz DAY".
19. **Pattern:** **Tomorrow is + Holiday Name.**
20. **More examples:** *Tomorrow is Independence Day.* · *Today is Mother's Day.*
21. **Quick check:** How many capitals in *New Year's Day*? → **Answer:** three.

## Step E — Names with adjectives

### EX-022-061 — "Venice is a small, beautiful city in Italy."

1. **Meaning:** Venice is a city in Italy; it is small and beautiful. 🛶🇮🇹
2. **Situation:** A travel blog.
3. **Main lesson:** Two **adjectives** describe the common noun *city*, not the name.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is a* + adjective, adjective + noun + *in* + country.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Venice** (S) → **is** → **a small, beautiful city** (B) → **in Italy** (place).
6. **Subject:** *Venice*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a small, beautiful city in Italy*.
10. **Word by word:** **Venice** — P · *is* · *a* · *small* · *beautiful* · *city* — C · *in* · **Italy** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** adjectives go before the common noun; names stay capital and article-free.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Tokyo is a big city* (EX-022-014).
13. **Why this is correct:** *a* + adjectives + *city*; capital *V* and *I*.
14. **Other versions:** *Venice is small and beautiful.* · *Italy has many beautiful cities.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Venice is a city small.* → ✅ *a small city* · ❌ *a small, beautiful City* → ✅ *city*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Venice is tiny but gorgeous.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** a comma between two adjectives of the same kind (*small, beautiful*) is common.
18. **Pronunciation:** "VEN-iss".
19. **Pattern:** **City + is a + adj, adj + city + in + Country.**
20. **More examples:** *Bruges is a quiet, pretty town in Belgium.* · *Hoi An is an old, colourful town in Vietnam.*
21. **Quick check:** Which noun do *small* and *beautiful* describe? → **Answer:** *city*.

### EX-022-062 — "I love Japanese films and Korean music."

1. **Meaning:** I really like films from Japan and music from Korea. 🎬🎵
2. **Situation:** Talking about hobbies.
3. **Main lesson:** **Nationality adjectives** take capitals; the nouns after them don't.
4. **Grammar used:** *I love* + adjective + noun + *and* + adjective + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **love** (V) → **Japanese films and Korean music** (O).
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *love*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** two objects joined by *and*.
10. **Word by word:** *I* · *love* · **Japanese** — from *Japan* → capital · *films* — C · *and* · **Korean** — from *Korea* → capital · *music* — C (uncountable).
11. **How the rules work together:** capital on the adjective, small on the noun; no *the* for things in general.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I love Italian food* (EX-022-032).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals only where a place name is inside the word.
14. **Other versions:** *I love films from Japan.* · *Do you like Korean music?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *japanese films* → ✅ *Japanese* · ❌ *Korean Music* → ✅ *music* · ❌ *the Korean music* (in general) → ✅ *Korean music*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm into Japanese films and K-pop.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma before *and* with only two items.
18. **Pronunciation:** "jap-uh-NEEZ" · "kuh-REE-un".
19. **Pattern:** **I love + Nationality + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *We like Mexican food and Brazilian music.* · *She reads Russian books.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *films*? → **Answer:** small.

### EX-022-063 — "Ali has an old Toyota."

1. **Meaning:** Ali owns a car made by Toyota, and it is old. 🚗
2. **Situation:** Talking about cars.
3. **Main lesson:** A **brand name** can be used like a common noun for one product (*a Toyota*) — but it keeps its capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *has* + *an* + adjective + brand.

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *biscuit!* (as the name) → ✅ *Biscuit!* · ❌ *What's your dogs name?* → ✅ *dog's*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *What's he called?* → *Biscuit!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *What's* = *What is*; *dog's* = possessive.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BISS-kit" (the *u* is silent).
19. **Pattern:** **What's your + animal's + name? → Name!**
20. **More examples:** *My cat is called Pepper.* · *Our fish is called Bubbles.*
21. **Quick check:** *biscuit* (food) or *Biscuit* (dog) — which is proper? → **Answer:** *Biscuit* (dog).

### EX-022-090 — "A: Excuse me, is this Park Lane? B: No, this is Hill Road. Park Lane is the next street on the left."

1. **Meaning:** A asks if this street is Park Lane; B says no, it's Hill Road, and Park Lane is the next street on the left. 🗺️🚶
2. **Situation:** Asking for directions.
3. **Main lesson:** **Street names** (proper) vs **street** (common).
4. **Grammar used:** *is this …?* · *No, this is …* · *the next street on the left*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **this** (S) **is** → **Park Lane**? · **Park Lane** (S) **is** → **the next street on the left** (B).
6. **Subject:** *this*; *Park Lane*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Hill Road*, *the next street on the left*.
10. **Word by word:** *Excuse me* · **Park Lane** — P · **Hill Road** — P · *the* · *next* · *street* — C · *on* · *the* · *left*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *Lane, Road* in names = capital; *street* alone = small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She lives on Elm Road* (EX-022-026).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals for full street names; *the* + common noun *street*.
14. **Other versions:** *Is this Park Lane?* — *No, it's the next one on the left.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *park lane* → ✅ *Park Lane* · ❌ *the next Street* → ✅ *street* · ❌ *is this the Park Lane?* → ✅ *Park Lane*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Sorry, no — Park Lane's the next left.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *Excuse me*, after *No*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PARK LAYN" · "HILL ROHD".
19. **Pattern:** **Is this + Street Name? → No, this is + Street Name.**
20. **More examples:** *A: Is this Green Street? B: Yes, it is.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *street* in *the next street*? → **Answer:** small.

## Step I — Names in a short story

> 📖 **The story:** (1) Ravi is a cook from Mumbai. (2) Every Friday, he cooks Indian food at Spice House, a small restaurant on Market Street. (3) In December, people come from London and Bristol to eat his famous curry.

### EX-022-091 — "Ravi is a cook from Mumbai."

1. **Meaning:** Ravi's job is cooking, and he comes from Mumbai. 👨‍🍳🇮🇳
2. **Situation:** The first line of a story — introducing the main person.
3. **Main lesson:** A story starts with a **name** + a **job** (common) + a **place** (proper).
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is a* + noun + *from* + city.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Ravi** (S) → **is** → **a cook from Mumbai** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Ravi*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a cook from Mumbai*.
10. **Word by word:** **Ravi** — P · *is* · *a* · *cook* — C: a job (here a noun!) · *from* · **Mumbai** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *cook* is a noun here (a person); *Mumbai* has no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Ana is a student* (EX-022-013).
13. **Why this is correct:** *a* + job; capital for the person and the city.
14. **Other versions:** *Ravi comes from Mumbai. He is a cook.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Ravi is cook* → ✅ *a cook* · ❌ *a Cook from mumbai* → ✅ *a cook from Mumbai*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Ravi's a cook from Mumbai.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one simple sentence to start a story.
18. **Pronunciation:** "RAH-vee" · "mum-BY".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + is a + job + from + City.**
20. **More examples:** *Lina is a nurse from Beirut.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *cook* a noun or a verb here? → **Answer:** a noun (a person).

---

### EX-022-092 — "Every Friday, he cooks Indian food at Spice House, a small restaurant on Market Street."

1. **Meaning:** On every Friday, Ravi cooks Indian dishes at a little restaurant called Spice House, on Market Street. 🍛
2. **Situation:** The second line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** A restaurant **name** (proper) followed by an explanation with a **common noun** (*a small restaurant*).
4. **Grammar used:** *Every* + day, present simple + object + *at* + name, explanation.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Every Friday** (time), **he** (S) → **cooks** (V) → **Indian food** (O) → **at Spice House, a small restaurant on Market Street**.
6. **Subject:** *he* (= Ravi).
7. **Verb:** *cooks* (now a verb!).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Indian food*; the place.
10. **Word by word:** *Every* · **Friday** — P · *he* · *cooks* · **Indian** — capital · *food* — C · *at* · **Spice House** — P · *a small restaurant* — C · *on* · **Market Street** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *cook* (noun) in sentence 1, *cooks* (verb) here; the pronoun *he* replaces *Ravi*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The Thai restaurant on Hill Street is very good* (EX-022-066).
13. **Why this is correct:** *cooks* for *he*; comma before the explanation; capitals on all names.
14. **Other versions:** *On Fridays, he cooks at Spice House.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *spice house* → ✅ *Spice House* · ❌ *a small Restaurant* → ✅ *restaurant* · ❌ *he cook* → ✅ *he cooks*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *He cooks at Spice House every Friday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *Every Friday* and after *Spice House*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SPYSS HOWSS".
19. **Pattern:** **Every + Day, S + verb + O + at + Name, a + adj + common noun + on + Street.**
20. **More examples:** *Every Sunday, she sings at Blue Note, a small club on Bay Street.*
21. **Quick check:** Who is *he*? → **Answer:** Ravi.

---

### EX-022-093 — "In December, people come from London and Bristol to eat his famous curry."

1. **Meaning:** In December, people travel from London and Bristol just to eat Ravi's well-known curry. 🍛🚆
2. **Situation:** The last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** A **month** + two **cities** + a common noun (*curry*).
4. **Grammar used:** present simple + *to* + verb (purpose).
5. **Sentence structure:** **In December** (time), **people** (S) → **come** (V) → **from London and Bristol** → **to eat his famous curry** (purpose).
6. **Subject:** *people*.
7. **Verb:** *come*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *from London and Bristol*; *to eat his famous curry*.
10. **Word by word:** *In* · **December** — P · *people* — C, plural · *come* · *from* · **London** · *and* · **Bristol** — P · *to eat* · *his* · *famous* · *curry* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *his* = Ravi's; *to* + verb gives the reason they come.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *They don't live in London. They live in Manchester* (EX-022-051).
13. **Why this is correct:** *people* is plural → *come*; *in* + month; capitals on all names.
14. **Other versions:** *In December, people from London and Bristol come to eat his curry.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *people comes* → ✅ *come* · ❌ *in december* → ✅ *December* · ❌ *for eat* → ✅ *to eat*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *People come all the way from London for his curry!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *In December*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "dih-SEM-ber" · "BRISS-tul" · "KUH-ree".
19. **Pattern:** **In + Month, people come from + City and City + to + verb.**
20. **More examples:** *In July, people come from Paris and Lyon to see the festival.*
21. **Quick check:** How many proper nouns are in the whole story (3 sentences)? → **Answer:** 8 — *Ravi, Mumbai, Friday, Spice House, Market Street, December, London, Bristol* (plus the proper adjective *Indian*).

## Step J — Names in real writing: a short letter

> ✉️ **The letter starts:** *Dear Mr Evans, / I am a student at Westfield College. / I would like to visit your office on Monday, 3 March.*

### EX-022-094 — "Dear Mr Evans,"

1. **Meaning:** The polite opening of a letter or email to Mr Evans. ✉️
2. **Situation:** A formal email to someone you don't know well.
3. **Main lesson:** **Greeting + title + family name** — all capitals.
4. **Grammar used:** *Dear* + title + surname + comma.
5. **Sentence structure:** not a full sentence — a fixed greeting: **Dear** + **Mr Evans** + **,**
6. **Subject:** none (a greeting).
7. **Verb:** none.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Mr Evans* — the person you write to.
10. **Word by word:** **Dear** — first word → capital · **Mr** — title · **Evans** — P: family name.
11. **How the rules work together:** formal letters use title + family name, not the first name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Next Monday, Mr Lee will start his new job* (EX-022-054) — *Mr* + surname.
13. **Why this is correct:** formal greeting; capitals; comma at the end (British style).
14. **Other versions:** *Dear Ms Evans,* · *Dear Sir or Madam,* (name unknown) · *Hi Tom,* (informal)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Dear Mr John,* (first name) → ✅ *Dear Mr Evans,* · ❌ *dear mr evans* → ✅ *Dear Mr Evans,*
16. **Natural spoken English:** (spoken) *Hello, Mr Evans.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** British: comma after the name; American: often a colon in business letters (*Dear Mr. Evans:*).
18. **Pronunciation:** "DEER MISS-ter EV-unz".
19. **Pattern:** **Dear + Title + Surname,**
20. **More examples:** *Dear Dr Ahmed,* · *Dear Mrs Clark,*
21. **Quick check:** *Dear Mr Evans* or *Dear Mr Tom*? → **Answer:** *Dear Mr Evans* (title + family name).

---

### EX-022-095 — "I am a student at Westfield College."

1. **Meaning:** I study at a college called Westfield College. 🏫
2. **Situation:** The first line of the letter — saying who you are.
3. **Main lesson:** *college* is common; in a **school's name** it becomes *College* (capital).
4. **Grammar used:** *I am a* + noun + *at* + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **am** → **a student at Westfield College** (B).
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *am*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a student at Westfield College*.
10. **Word by word:** **I** · *am* · *a* · *student* — C · *at* · **Westfield College** — P: both words capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** full forms (*I am*) sound more polite in formal letters than *I'm*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My sister studies at Cairo University* (EX-022-029).
13. **Why this is correct:** *a student*; capitals on the college's name.
14. **Other versions:** *I study at Westfield College.* · *I'm a student at a college in Leeds.* (no name → small)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I am student* → ✅ *a student* · ❌ *Westfield college* → ✅ *College* · ❌ *a Student* → ✅ *student*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm at Westfield College.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** formal letter → *I am*, not *I'm*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WEST-feeld KOL-ij".
19. **Pattern:** **I am a + role + at + Institution Name.**
20. **More examples:** *I am a nurse at St Mary's Hospital.*
21. **Quick check:** *I'm* or *I am* in a formal letter? → **Answer:** *I am*.

---

### EX-022-096 — "I would like to visit your office on Monday, 3 March."

1. **Meaning:** I politely ask to come to your office on Monday 3 March. 🗓️🏢
2. **Situation:** The second line of the letter — the request.
3. **Main lesson:** A **date** in a letter: day + number + month; capitals on the day and month.
4. **Grammar used:** *would like to* + verb (polite) + *on* + date.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **would like to visit** (V) → **your office** (O) → **on Monday, 3 March** (time).
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *like* (+ *to visit*).
8. **Helping verbs:** *would* (polite).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *your office*; *on Monday, 3 March*.
10. **Word by word:** *I* · *would* · *like* · *to* · *visit* · *your* · *office* — C · *on* · **Monday** — P · *3* · **March** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *would like* is polite; *on* + day/date.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: When is your exam? B: On Wednesday, the 5th of June* (EX-022-088).
13. **Why this is correct:** *would like to* + base verb; capitals for *Monday* and *March*.
14. **Other versions:** *Could I visit your office on Monday 3 March?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I would like visit* → ✅ *to visit* · ❌ *on monday, 3 march* → ✅ *Monday, 3 March* · ❌ *I want visit* → ✅ *I would like to visit*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Could I come by on Monday the third?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** British date: *3 March*; American: *March 3*.
18. **Pronunciation:** *3 March* = "the third of March".
19. **Pattern:** **I would like to + verb + O + on + Day, date + Month.**
20. **More examples:** *I would like to book a room on Friday, 10 May.*
21. **Quick check:** How do we say *3 March*? → **Answer:** "the third of March".

## Step K — Same word: common or proper?

### EX-022-097 — "I eat an apple every day. / My laptop is an Apple."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I eat a piece of fruit every day. (2) My laptop is made by the company Apple. 🍎💻
2. **Situation:** A funny double meaning.
3. **Main lesson:** The **same word** is common (fruit) or proper (brand).
4. **Grammar used:** present simple · *an* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **eat** (V) → **an apple** (O) → **every day**. · **My laptop** (S) → **is** → **an Apple** (B).
6. **Subject:** *I*; *My laptop*.
7. **Verb:** *eat*; *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *an apple* (fruit); *an Apple* (computer).
10. **Word by word:** *apple* — C: any apple → small · **Apple** — P: the brand → capital (here = one product).
11. **How the rules work together:** meaning decides the capital: fruit = small, company = capital.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Ali has an old Toyota* (EX-022-063) — brand = one product.
13. **Why this is correct:** small *a* for the fruit, capital *A* for the brand; *an* before the vowel sound.
14. **Other versions:** *My laptop is made by Apple.* · *I eat apples every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I eat an Apple every day.* → ✅ *apple* · ❌ *My laptop is an apple.* → ✅ *Apple*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's a Mac — an Apple.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** the capital is the only difference in writing.
18. **Pronunciation:** both "AP-ul".
19. **Pattern:** **common: an apple (fruit) · proper: an Apple (brand)**.
20. **More examples:** *I drink from a shell. / I buy petrol at Shell.*
21. **Quick check:** *apple* or *Apple*: *I bought a new ___ phone.* → **Answer:** *Apple*.

---

### EX-022-098 — "May I come in? / My exam is in May."

1. **Meaning:** (1) A polite question: can I enter? (2) My exam is in the month of May. 🚪📅
2. **Situation:** At a teacher's door; talking about exams.
3. **Main lesson:** *may* = helping verb (small inside a sentence) vs *May* = month (always capital).
4. **Grammar used:** *May I + verb?* · *in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **May** (helping verb) **I** (S) **come in** (V)? · **My exam** (S) → **is** → **in May**.
6. **Subject:** *I*; *My exam*.
7. **Verb:** *come in*; *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *May* (sentence 1 — asks for permission).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in May* (time).
10. **Word by word:** **May** (1) — helping verb, capital only because it is the first word · **May** (2) — P: a month → always capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** in the middle of a sentence: *you may go* (small) vs *in May* (capital).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My grandmother was born in India in May 1950* (EX-022-058).
13. **Why this is correct:** the month always has a capital; the verb only at the start.
14. **Other versions:** *You may sit down.* · *My birthday is in May.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My exam is in may.* → ✅ *May* · ❌ *You May sit down.* → ✅ *may*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Can I come in?* (more common than *May I*, but *May I* is more polite)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** question mark after *come in*.
18. **Pronunciation:** both "MAY".
19. **Pattern:** **may (verb) = small inside a sentence · May (month) = always capital.**
20. **More examples:** *We may visit in May.* (both in one sentence!)
21. **Quick check:** Correct the capitals: *we may travel in may.* → **Answer:** *We may travel in May.*

---

### EX-022-099 — "We bought new china for the kitchen. / My friend is from China."

1. **Meaning:** (1) We bought new plates and cups. (2) My friend comes from the country China. 🍽️🇨🇳
2. **Situation:** Shopping; introducing a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** *china* (common, uncountable) = fine plates and cups · *China* (proper) = a country.
4. **Grammar used:** past simple; *be from* + country.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **bought** (V) → **new china** (O) → **for the kitchen**. · **My friend** (S) → **is** → **from China**.
6. **Subject:** *We*; *My friend*.
7. **Verb:** *bought*; *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *new china* (O); *from China*.
10. **Word by word:** *china* — C: plates/cups, uncountable, no *a* · **China** — P: the country, no *the*.
11. **How the rules work together:** the capital changes the meaning completely.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I eat an apple every day. / My laptop is an Apple* (EX-022-097).
13. **Why this is correct:** *china* small for dishes; *China* capital for the country.
14. **Other versions:** *We bought some new plates.* · *My friend is Chinese.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *a new china* → ✅ *new china* (uncountable) · ❌ *from china* → ✅ *from China* · ❌ *from the China* → ✅ *from China*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We got some nice new china.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** only the capital shows the difference.
18. **Pronunciation:** both "CHY-nuh".
19. **Pattern:** **china (dishes) · China (country).**
20. **More examples:** *Grandma's best china came from China!*
21. **Quick check:** *She is from ___.* — *china* or *China*? → **Answer:** *China*.

### EX-022-100 — "We eat turkey at Thanksgiving. / Turkey is between Europe and Asia."

1. **Meaning:** (1) At the Thanksgiving holiday we eat turkey meat. (2) The country Turkey lies between Europe and Asia. 🦃🌍
2. **Situation:** A holiday meal; a geography lesson.
3. **Main lesson:** *turkey* (bird/meat, common) vs *Turkey* (country, proper); **holiday** names are proper.
4. **Grammar used:** present simple; *at* + holiday; *between … and …*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **eat** (V) → **turkey** (O) → **at Thanksgiving**. · **Turkey** (S) → **is** → **between Europe and Asia**.
6. **Subject:** *We*; *Turkey*.
7. **Verb:** *eat*; *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *turkey* (O, uncountable as meat); *between Europe and Asia*.
10. **Word by word:** *turkey* — C: the meat → small · *at* · **Thanksgiving** — P: holiday · **Turkey** — P: country (also called *Türkiye*) · *between* · **Europe** · *and* · **Asia** — P: continents.
11. **How the rules work together:** meat = uncountable, no *a*; country, holiday and continents = capitals, no *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Australia is a continent and a country* (EX-022-027).
13. **Why this is correct:** small *turkey* for food, capital *Turkey* for the country; *at* + holiday.
14. **Other versions:** *We have turkey for Thanksgiving dinner.* · *Istanbul is in Turkey.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *We eat a turkey at thanksgiving.* (as meat) → ✅ *turkey … Thanksgiving* · ❌ *turkey is between europe and asia* → ✅ *Turkey … Europe … Asia*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Turkey for Thanksgiving, of course!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** only the capital shows which *turkey* you mean.
18. **Pronunciation:** both "TUR-kee" · "THANKS-giv-ing".
19. **Pattern:** **turkey (food/bird) · Turkey (country).**
20. **More examples:** *We ate turkey in Turkey!* (both in one sentence)
21. **Quick check:** Correct the capitals: *we ate turkey in turkey.* → **Answer:** *We ate turkey in Turkey.*
