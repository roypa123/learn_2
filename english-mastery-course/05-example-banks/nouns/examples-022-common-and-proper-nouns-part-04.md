**Short reminders:** **P** = proper noun → capital · **C** = common noun → small. The big rules for **places** in this part:
- **Countries, cities, towns** → capital, usually **no *the*** (*Kenya, Lima, Oxford*). Exceptions: *the UK, the USA, the Czech Republic, the Netherlands, the Philippines*.
- **Street, Road, Park, Square, Station, Tower** are **common** alone (*a park, the station*) but **capital inside a name** (*Central Park, Union Station*) — and street/park/station names have **no *the***.
- Many **famous buildings** and names with ***of*** take *the*: *the White House, the British Museum, the Grand Canyon, the University of Toronto*.
- **North / south / east / west**: small for a direction (*north of the city*), capital in a region's name (*North Africa*).

> 🔁 **From Parts 01–03:** names take capitals and usually no article; *the* goes with seas, rivers, mountain ranges and some countries ([EX-022-071 – 078](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-02.md)). In this bank, **P** in step 10 means *proper noun*.

**New words in this part:** capital (city) · region · coast · square · stadium · temple · skyscraper · avenue · canyon · flat · floor · opposite · postcode · parcel.

## Step A — Places: short sentences

### EX-022-151 — "Nairobi is the capital of Kenya."

1. **Meaning:** The main city of Kenya, where the government is, is Nairobi. 🏛️🇰🇪
2. **Situation:** A geography quiz.
3. **Main lesson:** *capital* (the main city) is a **common noun** — small; the city and country are proper.
4. **Grammar used:** city + *is the capital of* + country.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Nairobi** (S) → **is** → **the capital of Kenya** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Nairobi*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the capital of Kenya*.
10. **Word by word:** **Nairobi** — P · *is* · *the* · *capital* — C: any country's main city → small · *of* · **Kenya** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** each country has only one capital → *the capital of …*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Paris isn't the capital of Italy. Rome is* (EX-022-044).
13. **Why this is correct:** *the* + common noun; names with capitals and no article.
14. **Other versions:** *Kenya's capital is Nairobi.* · *What is the capital of Kenya?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *the Capital of Kenya* → ✅ *capital* · ❌ *a capital of Kenya* → ✅ *the capital*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Nairobi's the capital.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** careful: *capital* (city) and *capital letter* are both common nouns.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ny-ROH-bee" · "KAP-ih-tul".
19. **Pattern:** **City + is the capital of + Country.**
20. **More examples:** *Accra is the capital of Ghana.* · *Hanoi is the capital of Vietnam.*
21. **Quick check:** *the capital* or *a capital* of one country? → **Answer:** *the capital*.

### EX-022-152 — "Egypt is in North Africa."

1. **Meaning:** The country Egypt is in the northern part of Africa. 🇪🇬🌍
2. **Situation:** Looking at a map.
3. **Main lesson:** **North** is capital when it is part of a **region's name** (*North Africa*).
4. **Grammar used:** country + *is in* + region.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Egypt** (S) → **is** → **in North Africa** (place).
6. **Subject:** *Egypt*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in North Africa*.
10. **Word by word:** **Egypt** — P · *is* · *in* · **North Africa** — P: a region → both capitals.
11. **How the rules work together:** *in the north of Africa* (direction → small) means the same, but *North Africa* is a name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *In winter, many people from Northern Europe travel to the Canary Islands* (EX-022-085).
13. **Why this is correct:** region name with capitals; no *the*.
14. **Other versions:** *Egypt is in the north of Africa.* · *Egypt is a North African country.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *in north Africa* → ✅ *North Africa* · ❌ *in the North Africa* → ✅ *in North Africa*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Egypt's up in North Africa.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *North Africa* (name) · *the north* (direction).
18. **Pronunciation:** "EE-jipt" · "NORTH AF-rih-kuh".
19. **Pattern:** **Country + is in + Region.**
20. **More examples:** *Chile is in South America.* · *Thailand is in Southeast Asia.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *We drove ___ to the hills.* (north) → **Answer:** small (*north*).

### EX-022-153 — "Our school is on Station Road."

1. **Meaning:** Our school's address is a road called Station Road. 🏫🛣️
2. **Situation:** Giving directions to a new parent.
3. **Main lesson:** *station* and *road* are common words, but **in a name** both are capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *is on* + street name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our school** (S) → **is** → **on Station Road** (place).
6. **Subject:** *Our school*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *on Station Road*.
10. **Word by word:** *Our* · *school* — C · *is* · *on* · **Station Road** — P: one road's name.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the station* (a building) = common; *Station Road* (a name) = proper.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She lives on Elm Road* (EX-022-026).
13. **Why this is correct:** *on* + road name; no *the*.
14. **Other versions:** *Our school is in Station Road.* (UK also says *in*) · *The school is near the station.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *on station road* → ✅ *Station Road* · ❌ *on the Station Road* → ✅ *on Station Road*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's on Station Road.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** short forms: *Rd* (Road), *St* (Street), *Ave* (Avenue).
18. **Pronunciation:** "STAY-shun ROHD".
19. **Pattern:** **S + is on + Street Name.**
20. **More examples:** *The bakery is on Church Street.* · *My office is on Bridge Road.*
21. **Quick check:** Short form of *Road*? → **Answer:** *Rd*.

### EX-022-155 — "Brook Park is beautiful in spring."

3. **Main lesson:** **Park names** → capitals, **no *the***.

### EX-022-157 — "Sri Lanka is an island."

1. **Meaning:** The country Sri Lanka is land with sea all around it. 🏝️🇱🇰
2. **Situation:** A geography class.
3. **Main lesson:** A two-word **country name** + the common noun *island*.

19. **Pattern:** **Country + is an island.**
20. **More examples:** *Cuba is an island.* · *Iceland is an island.*

## Step B — Places and pronouns

### EX-022-161 — "Kyoto is an old city. It has many temples."

1. **Meaning:** Kyoto is a historic city with lots of temples. ⛩️
2. **Situation:** Planning a trip to Japan.
3. **Main lesson:** A city name → replaced by **it**.
4. **Grammar used:** *is an* + adjective + noun; *It has* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Kyoto** (S) → **is** → **an old city** (B). **It** (S) → **has** (V) → **many temples** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Kyoto*, *It*.
7. **Verb:** *is*, *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *an old city*; *many temples*.
10. **Word by word:** **Kyoto** — P · *is* · *an* · *old* · *city* — C · *It* — = Kyoto · *has* · *many* · *temples* — C, plural.
11. **How the rules work together:** places → *it* (not *he/she*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Lisbon is in Portugal. It is near the sea* (EX-022-034).
13. **Why this is correct:** *an old*; *It has* (singular).
14. **Other versions:** *Kyoto, an old city, has many temples.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *a old city* → ✅ *an old city* · ❌ *She has many temples* → ✅ *It*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Kyoto's full of temples.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "kee-OH-toh" · "TEM-pulz".
19. **Pattern:** **City + is a/an + adj + city. It + has + …**
20. **More examples:** *Cusco is a high city. It has many old buildings.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for a city? → **Answer:** *it*.

21. **Quick check:** *go there* or *go to there*? → **Answer:** *go there*.

### EX-022-164 — "The Burj Khalifa is in Dubai. It is the tallest building in the world."

1. **Meaning:** The Burj Khalifa, in Dubai, is the highest building on Earth. 🏙️📏
2. **Situation:** A fun fact about skyscrapers.
3. **Main lesson:** Some **famous buildings take *the*** (*the Burj Khalifa*); cities don't.
4. **Grammar used:** *is in* + city; superlative *the tallest … in the world*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The Burj Khalifa** (S) → **is** → **in Dubai** (place). **It** (S) → **is** → **the tallest building in the world** (B).
6. **Subject:** *The Burj Khalifa*, *It*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in Dubai*; *the tallest building in the world*.
10. **Word by word:** *The* · **Burj Khalifa** — P · *is* · *in* · **Dubai** — P · *It* · *is* · *the tallest* · *building* — C · *in the world*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the* + famous building; *it* for a building; *in the world* after a superlative.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We took photos of the Eiffel Tower from the River Seine* (EX-022-077).
13. **Why this is correct:** *the Burj Khalifa*; *Dubai* no article; *in the world* (not *of the world*).
14. **Other versions:** *The world's tallest building is the Burj Khalifa in Dubai.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Burj Khalifa is in the Dubai* → ✅ *The Burj Khalifa is in Dubai* · ❌ *the tallest building of the world* → ✅ *in the world*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The Burj Khalifa's the tallest building ever!*

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in a small flat in Istanbul*.
10. **Word by word:** **Nadia** — P · *lives* · *in* · *a small flat* — C · *in* · **Istanbul** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** small place first, big place last.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Ravi is a cook from Mumbai* (EX-022-091) — another story opening.
13. **Why this is correct:** *lives* for she; capitals for names.
14. **Other versions:** *Nadia has a small flat in Istanbul.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *in a small Flat* → ✅ *flat* · ❌ *in the Istanbul* → ✅ *in Istanbul*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Nadia's got a little flat in Istanbul.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one simple sentence.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NAH-dee-uh" · "iss-tan-BOOL".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + lives in a + adj + home + in + City.**
20. **More examples:** *Leo lives in a big house in Lyon.*
21. **Quick check:** Order: *small place → big place* or the other way? → **Answer:** small place first.

### EX-022-195 — "From her window, she can see the Bosphorus and the Blue Mosque."

1. **Meaning:** Looking out of her window, Nadia can see the Bosphorus (a strait of water) and the Blue Mosque. 🪟🕌🌊
2. **Situation:** The second line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** A **water name** and a **famous building** — both with *the*.
4. **Grammar used:** *can see* + *the* + name + *and the* + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **From her window** (place), **she** (S) → **can see** (V) → **the Bosphorus and the Blue Mosque** (O).
6. **Subject:** *she* (= Nadia).
7. **Verb:** *see*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the Bosphorus and the Blue Mosque*.
10. **Word by word:** *From* · *her window* — C · *she* · *can see* · *the* · **Bosphorus** — P: a strait (sea passage) → *the* · *and* · *the* · **Blue Mosque** — P: here *Blue* is part of the name.
11. **How the rules work together:** water names and many famous buildings → *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We swam in the Mediterranean Sea near Nice* (EX-022-071).
13. **Why this is correct:** *can* + base verb; *the* twice.
14. **Other versions:** *She can see the Bosphorus and the Blue Mosque from her window.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *see Bosphorus* → ✅ *the Bosphorus* · ❌ *the blue mosque* → ✅ *the Blue Mosque*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's got a view of the Bosphorus and the Blue Mosque!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *From her window*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BOSS-fuh-rus" · "BLOO MOSK".
19. **Pattern:** **From + place, S + can see the + Name and the + Name.**
20. **More examples:** *From our hotel, we could see the Thames and the London Eye.*
21. **Quick check:** *a blue mosque* or *the Blue Mosque* — which is the famous building? → **Answer:** *the Blue Mosque*.

### EX-022-196 — "On Saturdays, she buys fish at the market in Karaköy."

1. **Meaning:** Every Saturday Nadia buys fish at the market in the Karaköy area. 🐟🛒
2. **Situation:** The last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** *the market* (common, no name) + district name (proper).
4. **Grammar used:** *On* + plural day, present simple + *at the* + noun + *in* + district.
5. **Sentence structure:** **On Saturdays** (time), **she** (S) → **buys** (V) → **fish** (O) → **at the market in Karaköy**.
6. **Subject:** *she*.
7. **Verb:** *buys*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *fish*; *at the market in Karaköy*.
10. **Word by word:** *On* · **Saturdays** — P, plural (every Saturday) · *she* · *buys* · *fish* — C · *at* · *the market* — C · *in* · **Karaköy** — P: a district.
11. **How the rules work together:** *on Saturdays* = every Saturday; the market has no name here → small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Chinatown is very busy at night* (EX-022-160) — district names.
13. **Why this is correct:** *buys* for she; capital *S* even in plural *Saturdays*.
14. **Other versions:** *Every Saturday, she buys fish at Karaköy market.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *on saturdays* → ✅ *Saturdays* · ❌ *at the Market* → ✅ *market*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Saturdays, she gets her fish in Karaköy.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** keep the special letter *ö* if you can; *Karakoy* is OK in plain English.
18. **Pronunciation:** "kah-rah-KOY".
19. **Pattern:** **On + Days (plural), S + verb + O + at the + place + in + District.**
20. **More examples:** *On Sundays, he buys bread at the bakery in Soho.*
21. **Quick check:** How many different names are in the whole story? → **Answer:** 6 — *Nadia, Istanbul, the Bosphorus, the Blue Mosque, Saturday(s), Karaköy*.

## Step J — Places in real writing: an address

> ✉️ **On an envelope:** *Ms Amina Yusuf / 14 Mill Lane / Oxford / OX1 2AB / United Kingdom*

### EX-022-197 — "14 Mill Lane, Oxford OX1 2AB"

1. **Meaning:** An address: house number 14, on Mill Lane, in Oxford, with the postcode OX1 2AB. ✉️🏠
2. **Situation:** Writing your address on a form or envelope.
3. **Main lesson:** In addresses, **street, town and country names** take capitals; **postcodes** are all capitals.
4. **Grammar used:** not a sentence — a fixed address format: number + street, town + postcode.
5. **Sentence structure:** **14** (house number) + **Mill Lane** (street) + **Oxford** (town) + **OX1 2AB** (postcode).
6. **Subject:** none (an address).
7. **Verb:** none.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** all parts give location.
10. **Word by word:** *14* · **Mill Lane** — P · **Oxford** — P · **OX1 2AB** — postcode, all capitals.
11. **How the rules work together:** biggest place last; on an envelope each part often goes on its own line.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I have lived on Rose Avenue since 2019* (EX-022-177).
13. **Why this is correct:** number before the street (UK/US style); capitals on names.
14. **Other versions:** on separate lines: *14 Mill Lane / Oxford / OX1 2AB*.
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Mill Lane 14* (UK/US) → ✅ *14 Mill Lane* · ❌ *ox1 2ab* → ✅ *OX1 2AB*
16. **Natural spoken English:** "fourteen Mill Lane, Oxford, O-X-one two-A-B".
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *postcode*; US *ZIP code*.
18. **Pronunciation:** say postcodes letter by letter: "oh-ex-one, two-ay-bee".
19. **Pattern:** **Number + Street, Town + Postcode**.
20. **More examples:** *221B Baker Street, London NW1 6XE*.
21. **Quick check:** Where does the house number go in a UK address? → **Answer:** before the street name.

### EX-022-198 — "Please send the parcel to my office in Glasgow."

1. **Meaning:** A request: send the package to my workplace in Glasgow. 📦🏢
2. **Situation:** An email to an online shop.
3. **Main lesson:** *my office* (common) + city name (proper) in a polite request.
4. **Grammar used:** *Please* + imperative + object + *to* + place.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Please** → **(you)** → **send** (V) → **the parcel** (O) → **to my office in Glasgow**.
6. **Subject:** *(you)*.
7. **Verb:** *send*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the parcel*; *to my office in Glasgow*.
10. **Word by word:** *Please* · *send* · *the* · *parcel* — C · *to* · *my* · *office* — C · *in* · **Glasgow** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *Please* makes the order polite.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I would like to visit your office on Monday, 3 March* (EX-022-096).
13. **Why this is correct:** imperative + *to* + place; capital only on *Glasgow*.
14. **Other versions:** *Could you send the parcel to my office in Glasgow?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *my Office* → ✅ *office* · ❌ *send the parcel at my office* → ✅ *to my office*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Can you send it to my office in Glasgow?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *parcel*, US *package*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GLAZ-goh" · "PAR-sul".
19. **Pattern:** **Please send + O + to my + place + in + City.**
20. **More examples:** *Please deliver the books to my flat in Leeds.*
21. **Quick check:** *send … to* or *send … at*? → **Answer:** *to*.

## Step K — Same word: common or proper?

### EX-022-199 — "Reading is my hobby. / My friend lives in Reading."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I like reading books. (2) My friend lives in a town called Reading. 📚🏘️
2. **Situation:** A funny double meaning.
3. **Main lesson:** *reading* (an activity, common) vs *Reading* (a town in England, proper).
4. **Grammar used:** *-ing* noun as subject; *lives in* + town.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Reading** (S) → **is** → **my hobby** (B). · **My friend** (S) → **lives** (V) → **in Reading**.
6. **Subject:** *Reading* (activity); *My friend*.
7. **Verb:** *is*; *lives*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my hobby*; *in Reading*.
10. **Word by word:** *Reading* (1) — C: the activity (capital only as first word!) · **Reading** (2) — P: a town → capital everywhere.
11. **How the rules work together:** in sentence 1 the capital comes from the start of the sentence; in sentence 2 it comes from the name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Bill paid the bill* (EX-022-150).
13. **Why this is correct:** the town keeps its capital in the middle of a sentence.
14. **Other versions:** *I love reading.* (small) · *Reading is near London.* (the town)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My friend lives in reading.* → ✅ *Reading* · ❌ *I love Reading* (the hobby) → ✅ *reading*
16. **Natural spoken English:** the town is "RED-ing" — the hobby is "REED-ing"!
17. **Writing & punctuation:** only the capital (and the sound) shows the difference.
18. **Pronunciation:** hobby: "REE-ding" · town: "RED-ing".
19. **Pattern:** **reading (activity) · Reading (town).**
20. **More examples:** *I like reading in Reading!*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *She enjoys ___.* (reading) → **Answer:** small.

### EX-022-200 — "I live in a new town. / My aunt lives in Newtown."

1. **Meaning:** (1) My town was built recently. (2) My aunt lives in a place named Newtown. 🏘️
2. **Situation:** Explaining where family members live.
3. **Main lesson:** *a new town* (adjective + common noun) vs *Newtown* (one-word proper name).
4. **Grammar used:** *live(s) in* + *a* + adj + noun / + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **live** (V) → **in a new town**. · **My aunt** (S) → **lives** (V) → **in Newtown**.
6. **Subject:** *I*; *My aunt*.
7. **Verb:** *live*, *lives*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in a new town*; *in Newtown*.
10. **Word by word:** *a* · *new* · *town* — C: a description · **Newtown** — P: the name of a town (one word, capital).
11. **How the rules work together:** description → *a* + small letters + two words; name → no article + capital + one word.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Reading is my hobby. / My friend lives in Reading* (EX-022-199).
13. **Why this is correct:** *a* + common noun vs proper noun with no article.
14. **Other versions:** *Newtown is not a new town — it's very old!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I live in a New Town.* → ✅ *a new town* · ❌ *My aunt lives in newtown.* → ✅ *Newtown* · ❌ *in the Newtown* → ✅ *in Newtown*
16. **Natural spoken English:** stress changes: *a new TOWN* vs *NEWtown*.
17. **Writing & punctuation:** spaces and capitals show the difference.
18. **Pronunciation:** "a nyoo TOWN" · "NYOO-town".
19. **Pattern:** **a + adj + common noun** (description) · **Name** (proper).
20. **More examples:** *We visited a new castle in Newcastle.*
21. **Quick check:** Correct it: *my aunt lives in newtown.* → **Answer:** *My aunt lives in Newtown.*
