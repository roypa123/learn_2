**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

**New words in this part:** lecture · professor · notebook · uniform · timetable · grade · essay · printer · tutor · course · revision · result · prize · substitute · semester · handwriting · medal · pencil case · website · project · staff room · engineering.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-251 — "The lecture is boring."

1. **Meaning:** The university talk we are listening to is not interesting. 🥱
2. **Situation:** A student whispers to a friend during a long university class.
3. **Main lesson:** An **event** noun (*lecture*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The lecture** (S) → **is** → **boring**.
6. **Subject:** *The lecture*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *boring* — adjective (not interesting).
10. **Word by word:** *lecture* 💭 — a talk given to students at a university · *boring* — makes you feel tired and uninterested.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The concert was amazing!* (EX-021-057) — event nouns.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The lectures are boring.* · *Is the lecture interesting?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The lecture is bored.* → ✅ *boring* (*bored* = how **you** feel; *boring* = what the lecture is) · ❌ *Lecture is boring.* → ✅ *The lecture*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This lecture's so boring!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LEK-cher".
19. **Pattern:** **The + event + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The film is exciting. · The meeting is long.*
21. **Quick check:** *I am ___. The lecture is ___.* (bored / boring) → **Answer:** *bored · boring*.

### EX-021-252 — "My notebook is full."

1. **Meaning:** There are no empty pages left in my notebook. 📓
2. **Situation:** You need to buy a new one before class.
3. **Main lesson:** A compound thing noun (one word).
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My notebook** (S) → **is** → **full**.
6. **Subject:** *My notebook*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *full*.
10. **Word by word:** *notebook* 📦 — *note* + *book*: a book of empty pages for writing (also a small laptop) · *full*.
11. **How the rules work together:** S + *be* + adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The fridge is empty* (EX-021-109) — opposite.
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *My notebooks are full.* · *Is your notebook full?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My note book is full.* → ✅ *notebook* · ❌ *My notebook full.* → ✅ *is full*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My notebook's full — I need a new one.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NOHT-buk".
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is + full/empty.**
20. **More examples:** *My bag is full. · The class is full.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite of *full*? → **Answer:** *empty*.

### EX-021-253 — "Art is fun."

1. **Meaning:** Doing art (painting, drawing) is enjoyable. 🎨
2. **Situation:** A child describes their favourite lesson.
3. **Main lesson:** A **school subject** noun (U) as the subject — and *fun* as an uncountable noun used like an adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + *fun*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Art** (S) → **is** → **fun**.
6. **Subject:** *Art*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *fun* — enjoyable.
10. **Word by word:** *Art* 💭 — U subject, no article · *fun* — actually a U **noun** (*We had fun*), but often used like an adjective after *be*.
11. **How the rules work together:** subjects → no article; *fun* → no *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The children had fun* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** natural English.
14. **Other versions:** *Art is a lot of fun.* (*fun* as a noun — *a lot of*) · *Is art fun?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Art is a fun.* → ✅ *Art is fun.* · ❌ *Art is very fun.* — very common in speech (especially US); careful English: *great fun / a lot of fun*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Art's so much fun!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital only at the start.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ART iz FUN".
19. **Pattern:** **Subject + is + fun.**
20. **More examples:** *Music is fun. · Swimming is fun.*
21. **Quick check:** *a fun* or *fun*? → **Answer:** *fun* (no *a*).

### EX-021-254 — "The professor speaks slowly."

1. **Meaning:** The university teacher talks at a slow speed. 👨‍🏫
2. **Situation:** A foreign student is happy because they can understand.
3. **Main lesson:** A **job** noun as subject + an adverb (not a noun).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + adverb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The professor** (S) → **speaks** (V) → **slowly** (how).
6. **Subject:** *The professor*.
7. **Verb:** *speaks*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *slowly* — adverb (how he speaks).
10. **Word by word:** *professor* 👩 — a senior university teacher · *speaks* · *slowly* — *slow* + *-ly*.
11. **How the rules work together:** adverb after the verb tells *how*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Can you speak more slowly?* ([L-017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement; adverb form.
14. **Other versions:** *The professors speak fast.* · *Does the professor speak slowly?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The professor speaks slow.* (informal) → ✅ *slowly* · ❌ *The professor speak…* → ✅ *speaks*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Our professor talks really slowly.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Professor* takes a capital as a title: *Professor Lee*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "pruh-FES-er".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person + verb-s + adverb.**
20. **More examples:** *The doctor writes quickly. · The baby sleeps quietly.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *slowly* a noun? → **Answer:** No — an adverb.

### EX-021-255 — "Our school has a uniform."

1. **Meaning:** At our school, all students must wear the same special clothes. 👔
2. **Situation:** A new student from another country asks about clothes.
3. **Main lesson:** Place noun (S) + thing noun (O) — *uniform* with *a* (vowel letter, consonant sound!).
4. **Grammar used:** *our* + noun + *has a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our school** (S) → **has** → **a uniform** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Our school*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a uniform*.
10. **Word by word:** *school* · *a* — ***u**niform* begins with a "you" sound (consonant /j/) → *a* · *uniform* 📦 — special clothes for a school/job.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a/an* depends on sound.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *a university* ([L-005](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article by sound.
14. **Other versions:** *Do you wear a uniform? · Our school doesn't have a uniform.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…has an uniform.* → ✅ *a uniform* · ❌ *…has uniform.* → ✅ *a uniform*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We have to wear a uniform.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "YOO-ni-form".
19. **Pattern:** **Our + place + has a + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Our school has a band. · The hotel has a gym.*
21. **Quick check:** *a* or *an*: ___ uniform, ___ umbrella? → **Answer:** *a uniform, an umbrella*.

### EX-021-257 — "Grades are important for university."

1. **Meaning:** Your marks matter a lot if you want to go to university. 📊
2. **Situation:** A parent talks to a teenager.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural noun (general) + noun after *for* (no article).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adj + *for* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Grades** (S) → **are** → **important** → **for university**.
6. **Subject:** *Grades* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *important for university*.
10. **Word by word:** *grades* 💭 — marks (A, B, C… or numbers) · *university* — here the idea of studying there → no article.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural; institution as activity → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *start university* (EX-021-058).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct general form.
14. **Other versions:** *Good grades are important.* · *Are grades important?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The grades are important for the university.* (sounds specific) · ❌ *Grades is…* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *You need good grades to get into uni.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK also *marks*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GRAYDZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are important for + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Vegetables are important for health. · Friends are important for happiness.*
21. **Quick check:** UK word for *grades*? → **Answer:** *marks*.

### EX-021-258 — "Her essay is excellent."

1. **Meaning:** The piece of writing she did is very, very good. 📝⭐
2. **Situation:** A teacher returns homework.
3. **Main lesson:** Possessive + noun (S).
4. **Grammar used:** *her* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Her essay** (S) → **is** → **excellent**.
6. **Subject:** *Her essay*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *excellent*.
10. **Word by word:** *Her* · *essay* 📦 — a short piece of writing on one topic · *excellent* — extremely good.
11. **How the rules work together:** possessive determiner + noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *her* = possessive here (+ noun) ([L-028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md) bank EX-028-097).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct form.
14. **Other versions:** *Their essays are excellent.* · *Is her essay good?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Her essay is very excellent.* → ✅ *excellent* (already very strong) or *really excellent* · ❌ *She essay…* → ✅ *Her essay*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Her essay's brilliant!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ES-ay"; "EK-suh-lunt".
19. **Pattern:** **His/Her + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *His drawing is beautiful. · Their project is great.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *her* here a pronoun alone or before a noun? → **Answer:** before a noun (possessive).

## Step B — School nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-261 — "I lost my student card. I need a new one."

1. **Meaning:** I can't find my student ID card; I must get another. 🪪
2. **Situation:** At the university office.
3. **Main lesson:** Compound noun + *one* replacing it.
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + past verb + *my* + noun + noun · *I need a new one*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **lost** → **my student card** (O) · **I** → **need** → **a new one** (O).
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *lost* · *need*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my student card* · *a new one* (= a new student card).
10. **Word by word:** *student card* — card that shows you are a student (head **card**) · *one* — replaces *student card*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a new one* (singular) replaces a singular C noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I want new ones* (EX-021-037).
13. **Why this is correct:** *one* matches singular *card*.
14. **Other versions:** *I lost my keys. I need new ones.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I need a new.* → ✅ *a new one* · ❌ *I need new one.* → ✅ *a new one*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've lost my student card — I need a new one.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** ***STOO**dent card*.
19. **Pattern:** **I lost my + noun. I need a new one.**
20. **More examples:** *I broke my pencil. I need a new one.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *one* replace? → **Answer:** *student card*.

### EX-021-262 — "The new students are shy. Please help them."

1. **Meaning:** The students who just arrived feel nervous with others; please help them.
2. **Situation:** A teacher talks to the class on the first day.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural noun (S) → *them* (O).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + adj + plural + *are* + adj · instruction + *them*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The new students** (S) → **are** → **shy** · (*You*) **Please help** → **them** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The new students* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *help*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *shy* · *them*.
10. **Word by word:** *new students* · *shy* — nervous with new people · *them* — the new students.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Give them some bread* (EX-021-042).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *The new student is shy. Please help her.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please help they.* → ✅ *them* · ❌ *The new students is shy.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Be nice to the new kids — they're a bit shy.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHY".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + adj. Please help them.**
20. **More examples:** *The old people are tired. Please help them.*
21. **Quick check:** Singular version? → **Answer:** *The new student is shy. Please help him/her.*

### EX-021-263 — "My tutor is very patient. He explains everything twice."

1. **Meaning:** My private teacher is calm and doesn't get angry; he explains things two times.
2. **Situation:** Recommending a tutor to a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** Person noun → *He*; *everything* (pronoun) as object.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is very* + adj · *He* + verb-s + pronoun + adverb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My tutor** (S) → **is** → **very patient** · **He** (S) → **explains** (V) → **everything** (O) → **twice**.
6. **Subject:** *My tutor* · *He*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *explains*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very patient* · *everything* · *twice* (two times).
10. **Word by word:** *tutor* 👩 — a private teacher · *patient* — calm (also a **noun**: a person in hospital!) · *everything* · *twice*.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → pronoun; adverb *twice* at the end.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** words with two jobs ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement and order.
14. **Other versions:** *My tutors are very patient. They explain everything twice.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *He explains twice everything.* → ✅ *He explains everything twice.* (don't split verb and object) · ❌ *He explain…* → ✅ *explains*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My tutor's so patient — he goes over everything twice.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PAY-shunt".
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is very + adj. He/She + verb-s + everything + twice.**
20. **More examples:** *My coach is very strict. She checks everything twice.*
21. **Quick check:** *patient* — adjective or noun in *The patient is in Room 4*? → **Answer:** noun (a person).

### EX-021-264 — "The library closes at six. It opens again at eight in the morning."

1. **Meaning:** The library shuts at 6:00 and opens again at 8:00 the next morning.
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend when to study.
3. **Main lesson:** Place noun → *It*; time nouns.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + *at* + time · *It* + verb-s + *again* + *at* + time + *in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The library** (S) → **closes** → **at six** · **It** (S) → **opens** → **again** → **at eight** → **in the morning**.
6. **Subject:** *The library* · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *closes* · *opens*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** time phrases.
10. **Word by word:** *closes* (-s; sound /ɪz/) · *It* · *again* · *in the morning*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *at* + clock time; *in the* + part of the day.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *in the afternoon* ([L-013](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-013-the-object.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct prepositions.
14. **Other versions:** *The shops close at nine. They open again at ten.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…at eight in morning.* → ✅ *in the morning* · ❌ *…on the morning.* → ✅ *in the morning*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It shuts at six and opens at eight.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KLOH-ziz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + closes at + time. It opens again at + time + in the morning.**
20. **More examples:** *The café closes at five. It opens again at seven in the morning.*
21. **Quick check:** *in the morning* or *on the morning*? → **Answer:** *in the morning*.

### EX-021-265 — "Our class has a new student. Her name is Yuki."

1. **Meaning:** A new student joined our class; she is called Yuki.
2. **Situation:** Telling your parents about school.
3. **Main lesson:** First mention *a* + noun, then possessive *Her* + noun + name.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + *has a* + adj + noun · *Her* + noun + *is* + proper noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our class** (S) → **has** → **a new student** (O) · **Her name** (S) → **is** → **Yuki**.
6. **Subject:** *Our class* · *Her name*.
7. **Verb:** *has* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a new student* · *Yuki*.
10. **Word by word:** *class* (group) · *a new student* — first mention · *Her* — the student's · *name* · *Yuki* (proper).
11. **How the rules work together:** new information → *a*; known → pronoun/possessive.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I have a cat. Her name is Luna.* (EX-021-036).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *Our class has two new students. Their names are Yuki and Tom.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Our class has new student.* → ✅ *a new student* · ❌ *She name is Yuki.* → ✅ *Her name*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There's a new girl in our class — she's called Yuki.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **Y**.
18. **Pronunciation:** "YOO-kee".

13. **Why this is correct:** correct modal and preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *All books must be returned by Thursday.* (passive — formal)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Students must to return…* → ✅ *must return* · ❌ *…until Thursday.* (*until* = during the whole time up to) → for a deadline: *by*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Bring all your books back by Thursday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "THURZ-day".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + must + verb + all + plural noun + by + day.**
20. **More examples:** *Visitors must leave all bags at reception.*
21. **Quick check:** *by Thursday* or *until Thursday* for a deadline? → **Answer:** *by*.

### EX-021-297 — "Thank you. — The Librarian"

1. **Meaning:** The notice ends with thanks and the name of the person who wrote it (the librarian).
2. **Situation:** End of the notice.
3. **Main lesson:** A **job title** used as a signature — capital letters.
4. **Grammar used:** *Thank you* + *the* + job noun (as a title).
5. **Sentence structure:** **Thank you.** · **— The Librarian** (signature).
6. **Subject:** none (fixed phrase *Thank you*).
7. **Verb:** none written.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** signature.
10. **Word by word:** *Librarian* 👩 — the person who works in a library (*library* + *-ian*); capital **L** here because it's used as a title/signature.
11. **How the rules work together:** job nouns get capitals when used as titles or signatures.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Mum* (name use) vs *my mum* ([L-022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md#73-a-common-noun-can-become-part-of-a-name)).
13. **Why this is correct:** standard notice format.
14. **Other versions:** *— The Management* · *— The Headteacher*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Thanks you.* → ✅ *Thank you.* · ❌ *the librarian* (small, as a signature) → ✅ *The Librarian*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Thanks!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** a dash (—) before the signature.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ly-BRAIR-ee-un".
19. **Pattern:** **Thank you. — The + Job Title**
20. **More examples:** *Thank you. — The School Office*
21. **Quick check:** Person who works in a library? → **Answer:** *librarian*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-298 — "I study in the library. / Our house has a study."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I do my studying in the library. (2) Our house has a small room for working and reading.
2. **Situation:** Talking about where you work.
3. **Main lesson:** *study* as a verb (1) and a place noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + verb + *in the* + noun · noun + *has a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **study** (V) → **in the library** · (2) **Our house** (S) → **has** → **a study** (O).
6. **Subject:** *I* · *Our house*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *study* · (2) *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) place · (2) *a study*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *study* after *I* → verb · (2) *a study* after *a* → noun 📍 (a room).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides job **and** meaning.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *park* verb/noun (EX-021-200).
13. **Why this is correct:** each in its natural position.
14. **Other versions:** *She studies every evening. · My dad works in his study.* · *a study* can also mean a piece of research.
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I study at the library in.* → ✅ *I study in the library.* · ❌ *Our house has study.* → ✅ *a study*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Dad's in his study.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STUD-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **I + verb + in the + place / a + noun**
20. **More examples:** *I paint at weekends. / This is a beautiful painting.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a study*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-299 — "Please don't copy your friend's answers. / I need a copy of the timetable."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Don't take your friend's answers as your own. (2) I need one printed version of the timetable.
2. **Situation:** A teacher in an exam / a student at the office.
3. **Main lesson:** *copy* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** negative instruction + possessive + plural · *I need a* + noun + *of the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Please don't copy** (V) → **your friend's answers** (O) · (2) **I** → **need** → **a copy** (O) → **of the timetable**.
6. **Subject:** hidden *you* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *copy* · (2) *need*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *don't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *your friend's answers* · (2) *a copy of the timetable*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *copy* after *don't* → verb · (2) *a copy* after *a* → noun (plural *copies*).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** possessive *friend's* ([L-037](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-037-possessive-s.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct forms.
14. **Other versions:** *Can you make two copies?* · *She copies everything.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please not copy…* → ✅ *don't copy* · ❌ *…two copys.* → ✅ *copies*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *No copying!* (*copying* = noun, the activity)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe in *friend's*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KOP-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **Please don't + verb + noun / I need a + noun + of the + noun**
20. **More examples:** *Please don't change the plan. / There's a change in the plan.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *copy*? → **Answer:** *copies*.

### EX-021-300 — "The students line up outside. / Draw a line under the title."

1. **Meaning:** (1) The students stand one behind the other outside. (2) Make a line with your pen below the title.
2. **Situation:** At school — the playground / in a lesson.
3. **Main lesson:** *line* as a verb (*line up* — phrasal verb) and a noun.
4. **Grammar used:** plural + phrasal verb + adverb · instruction + *a* + noun + *under the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **The students** (S) → **line up** (V) → **outside** · (2) (*You*) **Draw** → **a line** (O) → **under the title**.
6. **Subject:** *The students* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *line up* · (2) *Draw*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *outside* · (2) *a line* · *under the title*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *line up* — form a queue · (2) *a line* — noun (a long thin mark) · *title*.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides; phrasal verbs act as one verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** phrasal verbs ([L-028 §7.7](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md#77-phrasal-verbs-turn-it-off-not-turn-off-it)).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Stand in a line.* (noun) · *Please line up by the door.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The students lines up…* → ✅ *line up* · ❌ *Draw line…* → ✅ *a line*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Line up, everyone!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LYN UP".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + line up + place / Draw a + noun + under the + noun**
20. **More examples:** *We queue at the bus stop. / There's a long queue.* (UK *queue* = line)
21. **Quick check:** UK word for a line of people? → **Answer:** *queue*.
