**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **From Part 19:** sports and games → no article (*play chess*); instruments → *the* (*play the piano*); an *-ing* activity as the subject → singular verb (*Watching films **is**…*). **New here:** group nouns like *band, orchestra, audience* (one group → usually singular verb; UK can also use plural).

**New words in this part:** festival · board game · orchestra · gardening · actor · camera · loud · novel · video game · gallery · band · plans · kind · horror film · turn · trampoline · pets · barbecue · talent show · dinosaur · stamps · record · paints · drawer · stage · series · rules · theme park · fireworks · superhero · ending · text.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-951 — "The party starts at eight."

1. **Meaning:** The party begins at 8 o'clock. 🎉🕗
2. **Situation:** Inviting a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** An event noun as the subject + verb-*s*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + *at* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The party** (S) → **starts** (V) → **at eight** (P).
6. **Subject:** *The party*.
7. **Verb:** *starts*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at eight*.
10. **Word by word:** *party* 💭 — event with friends, music, food (plural *parties*) · *starts* · *at eight*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → verb + *-s*; times → *at*; timetable/plan → present simple.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The party is on Saturday* (EX-021-031).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + time preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *The party finishes at midnight.* · *What time does the party start?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The party start at eight.* → ✅ *starts* · ❌ *…in eight.* → ✅ *at eight*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It kicks off at eight.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PAR-tee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + event + starts at + time.**
20. **More examples:** *The film starts at nine. · The match starts at three.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *party*? → **Answer:** *parties*.

### EX-021-952 — "Reading is relaxing."

1. **Meaning:** Reading books makes you calm. 📖😌
2. **Situation:** Recommending a hobby.
3. **Main lesson:** *-ing* activity as the subject + *-ing* adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** verb-*ing* noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Reading** (S) → **is** → **relaxing**.
6. **Subject:** *Reading*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *relaxing*.
10. **Word by word:** *Reading* — the activity (noun) · *relaxing* — adjective: makes you relaxed.
11. **How the rules work together:** activity → singular verb; *relaxing* (cause) vs *relaxed* (feeling).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Smoking is dangerous* (EX-021-856).
13. **Why this is correct:** *-ing* noun + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *I feel relaxed when I read.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Read is relaxing.* → ✅ *Reading* · ❌ *Reading is relaxed.* → ✅ *relaxing*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Reading really chills me out.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "REE-ding-iz-ri-LAK-sing".
19. **Pattern:** **Verb-ing + is + -ing adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Swimming is tiring. · Cooking is interesting.*
21. **Quick check:** What is the subject? → **Answer:** *Reading*.

### EX-021-953 — "The festival lasts three days."

1. **Meaning:** The festival goes on for three days. 🎪📅
2. **Situation:** Reading about a summer music festival.
3. **Main lesson:** event noun + *lasts* + number + plural noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + number + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The festival** (S) → **lasts** (V) → **three days**.
6. **Subject:** *The festival*.
7. **Verb:** *lasts* (= continues for).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *three days* (time).
10. **Word by word:** *festival* — a big public celebration/event · *lasts* · *three days*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *-s*; number + plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The bus from the airport … takes forty minutes* (EX-021-689).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + plural.
14. **Other versions:** *The festival lasts for three days.* (*for* optional)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The festival last three days.* → ✅ *lasts* · ❌ *…three day.* → ✅ *days*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's a three-day festival.* (compound adjective, no *-s*)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FES-ti-vul".
19. **Pattern:** **The + event + lasts + number + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The course lasts six weeks.*
21. **Quick check:** *a three-days festival* or *a three-day festival*? → **Answer:** *a three-day festival*.

### EX-021-954 — "Board games are fun."

1. **Meaning:** Games you play on a board (like chess or Monopoly) are enjoyable. 🎲😄
2. **Situation:** Planning a family evening.
3. **Main lesson:** noun + plural noun subject + U noun *fun* after *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + plural noun + *are* + *fun*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Board games** (S) → **are** → **fun**.
6. **Subject:** *Board games* (head **games**).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *fun* — enjoyment (U noun, used like an adjective).
10. **Word by word:** *board* — type · *games* — head · *fun* — U noun: no *a*, no *-s*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural head → *are*; *fun* is U (*a lot of fun*, not *a fun*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Chess is difficult* (EX-021-905).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + U noun.
14. **Other versions:** *Board games are a lot of fun.* · *We had fun.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Board games are a fun.* → ✅ *fun* · ❌ *We had a lot of funs.* → ✅ *fun*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Board games are great fun.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *fun* (enjoyable) ≠ *funny* (makes you laugh).
18. **Pronunciation:** "BORD-GAYMZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + plural noun + are fun.**
20. **More examples:** *Card games are fun. · Parties are fun.*
21. **Quick check:** *a fun* or *fun*? → **Answer:** *fun*.

### EX-021-955 — "The orchestra is excellent."

1. **Meaning:** The big group of musicians plays very well. 🎻🎺🥁
2. **Situation:** After a concert.
3. **Main lesson:** group noun (*orchestra*) → singular verb.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + group noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The orchestra** (S) → **is** → **excellent**.
6. **Subject:** *The orchestra*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *excellent*.
10. **Word by word:** *orchestra* — large group of musicians (silent *h*; "OR-kis-truh") · *excellent*.
11. **How the rules work together:** one group → usually *is* (UK sometimes *are* when thinking of the people).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our team has a new coach* (EX-021-030) — another group noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** group as one unit → singular.
14. **Other versions:** *The orchestras are excellent.* (two or more)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The orchestra are excellent.* (possible UK, but beginners: *is*) · ❌ *The orkestra* → ✅ *orchestra*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The orchestra was brilliant tonight.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *ch* = /k/ in *orchestra*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "OR-kis-truh".
19. **Pattern:** **The + group noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The choir is wonderful. · The audience is quiet.*
21. **Quick check:** How is *ch* pronounced in *orchestra*? → **Answer:** /k/.

### EX-021-956 — "Gardening is good exercise."

1. **Meaning:** Working in the garden is a good way to move your body. 🌱💪
2. **Situation:** An older person explains their hobby.
3. **Main lesson:** *-ing* noun subject + U noun after *is*.
4. **Grammar used:** verb-*ing* noun + *is* + adj + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Gardening** (S) → **is** → **good exercise** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Gardening*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *good exercise* — U noun, no *a*.
10. **Word by word:** *Gardening* — the activity · *good* · *exercise* — U (activity).
11. **How the rules work together:** activity subject → *is*; U complement → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Exercise is good for you* (EX-021-807) · *We garden every weekend* (EX-021-198).
13. **Why this is correct:** U nouns without *a*.
14. **Other versions:** *Swimming is great exercise.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Gardening is a good exercise.* (for activity → no *a*) · ❌ *Garden is good exercise.* → ✅ *Gardening*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Gardening keeps you fit.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GAR-dn-ing".
19. **Pattern:** **Verb-ing + is + adj + U noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Walking is good exercise.*
21. **Quick check:** *a good exercise* or *good exercise* (activity)? → **Answer:** *good exercise*.

### EX-021-958 — "The music is too loud."

1. **Meaning:** The music has too much volume. 🔊😖
2. **Situation:** At a party, asking someone to turn it down.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun *music* + *too* + adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *is too* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The music** (S) → **is** → **too loud**.
6. **Subject:** *The music*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *too loud*.
10. **Word by word:** *music* — U (never *musics*) · *too* · *loud* — high volume.
11. **How the rules work together:** U → *is*; specific music → *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Music helps* (EX-021-019) — general → no *the*.
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Can you turn the music down?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The musics are too loud.* → ✅ *The music is* · ❌ *The music is too much loud.* → ✅ *too loud*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Turn it down — it's way too loud!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MYOO-zik" · "LOWD".
19. **Pattern:** **The + U noun + is too + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The noise is too loud. · The water is too cold.*
21. **Quick check:** *musics* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *music* (U).

## Step B — Free-time nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-961 — "My friends are coming tonight. I'm cooking dinner for them."

1. **Meaning:** My friends will visit this evening, and I'm making dinner for them. 👫🍝
2. **Situation:** Telling a neighbour your plans.
3. **Main lesson:** *my friends* → *them* after *for*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + plural noun + *are coming tonight* · *I'm cooking* + meal + *for them*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My friends** (S) → **are coming** (V) → **tonight** · **I** (S) → **'m cooking** (V) → **dinner** (O) → **for them** (P).
6. **Subject:** *My friends* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *coming* · *cooking*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *are* · *'m* (plans).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *dinner* · *for them*.
10. **Word by word:** *friends* · *are coming* — arranged plan · *dinner* — no article · *for them*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural people → *them* after a preposition.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We met two backpackers from Chile. We had dinner with them* (EX-021-664).
13. **Why this is correct:** plan form + object pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *My sister is coming tonight. I'm cooking dinner for her.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…for they.* → ✅ *for them* · ❌ *I'm cooking a dinner…* (simple meal → *dinner*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've got friends coming round — I'm making dinner.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FRENDZ" (silent *i*).
19. **Pattern:** **My + plural noun + are coming tonight. I'm cooking + meal + for them.**
20. **More examples:** *My parents are visiting. I'm making a cake for them.*
21. **Quick check:** *for they* or *for them*? → **Answer:** *for them*.

### EX-021-962 — "This novel is great. I can't put it down."

1. **Meaning:** This book is so good that I can't stop reading it. 📕😍
2. **Situation:** Reading late at night.
3. **Main lesson:** *this novel* → *it* inside a phrasal verb (*put it down*).
4. **Grammar used:** *This* + noun + *is* + adj · *I can't put it down.*
5. **Sentence structure:** **This novel** (S) → **is** → **great** · **I** (S) → **can't put** (V) → **it** (O) → **down**.
6. **Subject:** *This novel* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *put … down*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *great* · *it*.
10. **Word by word:** *novel* — long story book · *put down* — phrasal verb; pronoun goes **in the middle** · *it* — the novel.
11. **How the rules work together:** pronoun object goes between the verb and *down* ([L-143](../../02-intermediate-grammar/phrasal-verbs/lesson-143-what-are-phrasal-verbs.md) later).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Last term, I read five novels* (EX-021-225).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun position.
14. **Other versions:** *I can't put this book down.* (noun can go before *down*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I can't put down it.* → ✅ *put it down* · ❌ *This novel is great. I can't put him down.* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's a real page-turner!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NOV-ul".
19. **Pattern:** **This + noun + is + adj. I can't put it down.**
20. **More examples:** *This jacket is warm. I never take it off.*
21. **Quick check:** *put down it* or *put it down*? → **Answer:** *put it down*.

### EX-021-963 — "The kids love the new video game. They play it every evening."

1. **Meaning:** The children really like the new computer game; they play it each evening. 🎮
2. **Situation:** A parent talks about screen time.
3. **Main lesson:** *the kids* → *they*; *the new video game* → *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *love the new* + noun + noun · *They play it* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The kids** (S) → **love** (V) → **the new video game** (O) · **They** (S) → **play** (V) → **it** (O) → **every evening**.
6. **Subject:** *The kids* · *They*.
7. **Verb:** *love* · *play*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the new video game* · *it* · *every evening*.
10. **Word by word:** *kids* · *video game* — noun + noun (head *game*) · *They* · *it*.
11. **How the rules work together:** two pronouns in one sentence: *they* (people), *it* (game).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I love this song. I listen to it every day* (EX-021-911).
13. **Why this is correct:** both pronouns match.
14. **Other versions:** *My son loves the new video game. He plays it every evening.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They play them every evening.* (one game) → ✅ *it* · ❌ *The kids loves…* → ✅ *love*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids are obsessed with that new game.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "VID-ee-oh-GAYM".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + love the + noun. They play it + time.**
20. **More examples:** *My parents love the new series. They watch it every night.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *the new video game*? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-964 — "Our neighbour paints. His pictures are in a gallery."

12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Painting relaxes me* (EX-021-960).

14. **Other versions:** *Our neighbour sings. Her voice is beautiful.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *He pictures are…* → ✅ *His pictures* · ❌ *His pictures is…* → ✅ *are*

17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GAL-uh-ree".

### EX-021-965 — "The band is from Ireland. Their songs are famous."

1. **Meaning:** The music group comes from Ireland; people everywhere know their songs. 🎸🇮🇪
2. **Situation:** A music magazine.
3. **Main lesson:** group noun *band* → *is*, but possessive *their* (the members).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + group noun + *is from* + country · *Their* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The band** (S) → **is** → **from Ireland** (P) · **Their songs** (S) → **are** → **famous**.
6. **Subject:** *The band* · *Their songs*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *from Ireland* · *famous*.
10. **Word by word:** *band* — group of musicians · *Their* — belongs to the band members · *songs*.
11. **How the rules work together:** group noun → *is*, but we say *their* (not *its*) because we think of the people.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The orchestra is excellent* (EX-021-955).
13. **Why this is correct:** natural mix: *is* + *their*.
14. **Other versions:** *The band are from Ireland.* (UK style, also correct)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Its songs are famous.* (possible, but sounds odd for people) → ✅ *Their songs* · ❌ *There songs* → ✅ *Their*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They're an Irish band — everyone knows their songs.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *their* (possessive) ≠ *there* (place) ≠ *they're* (they are).
18. **Pronunciation:** "THAIR" (all three sound the same!).
19. **Pattern:** **The + group noun + is from + country. Their + plural noun + are + adj.**

4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adj, *and the* + plural noun + *are* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The story** (S) → **is** → **exciting** · **and** · **the actors** (S) → **are** → **excellent**.
6. **Subject:** *The story* · *the actors*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *exciting* · *excellent*.
10. **Word by word:** *story* · *exciting* · *and* · *actors* · *excellent*.
11. **How the rules work together:** each clause has its own agreement.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The hotel is lovely and the beach is beautiful* (EX-021-647) · *The actors are brilliant* (EX-021-957).
13. **Why this is correct:** two correct clauses.
14. **Other versions:** *The music is beautiful, and the songs are catchy.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The story is exciting, and the actors is excellent.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The story are…* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Great story, great actors.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *and* joining two longer clauses (optional).
18. **Pronunciation:** "STOR-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adj, and the + plural noun + are + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *The food is tasty, and the waiters are friendly.*
21. **Quick check:** *the actors is* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *are*.

### EX-021-998 — "The ending is a bit sad, but I recommend it."

1. **Meaning:** The end of the film is slightly sad, but I still say people should watch it. 😢👍
2. **Situation:** The review continues.
3. **Main lesson:** *-ing* noun (*ending*) as subject; *it* = the film.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is a bit* + adj, *but I recommend it*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The ending** (S) → **is** → **a bit sad** · **but** · **I** (S) → **recommend** (V) → **it** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The ending* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *recommend*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a bit sad* · *it*.
10. **Word by word:** *ending* — the last part of a story (noun from *end*) · *a bit* — a little · *recommend* · *it* — the film.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a bit* + adjective (it's not *a* + noun here!).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: What do you recommend?* (EX-021-593).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear contrast + pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *The ending is happy, and I loved it.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The end is a bit sad.* (OK too; *ending* is more common for films/books) · ❌ *…but I recommend.* → ✅ *recommend it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The ending's a bit sad, but it's worth watching.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *but*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "EN-ding".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is a bit + adj, but I recommend it.**
20. **More examples:** *The beginning is a bit slow, but I recommend it.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *it* refer to? → **Answer:** the film.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-999 — "I'll text you later. / Thanks for your text."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I'll send you a phone message later. (2) Thank you for your message. 📱💬
2. **Situation:** Arranging to meet.
3. **Main lesson:** *text* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I'll* + verb + *you later* · *Thanks for your* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** (S) → **'ll text** (V) → **you** (O) → **later** · (2) **Thanks** → **for your text** (P).
6. **Subject:** (1) *I* · (2) none (fixed phrase *Thanks for…*).
7. **Verb:** (1) *text*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *'ll* (*will*).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *you* · *later* · *your text*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *text* after *'ll* → verb · (2) *your text* — after a possessive → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** helping verb → verb; possessive → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Please book a room for us. / I'm reading a good book* (EX-021-650).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *She texted me.* (verb, past) · *I got three texts.* (noun, plural)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I'll text to you.* → ✅ *text you* · ❌ *Thanks for your texts message.* → ✅ *text message*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'll drop you a text.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TEKST".
19. **Pattern:** **I'll + verb + you later / Thanks for your + noun**
20. **More examples:** *I'll email you. / Thanks for your email.*
21. **Quick check:** *text to you* or *text you*? → **Answer:** *text you*.

### EX-021-1000 — "Can you film the concert? / We watched a great film."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Can you make a video of the concert? (2) We saw a very good film. 📹 / 🎬
2. **Situation:** (1) At a concert; (2) the next day.
3. **Main lesson:** *film* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *Can you* + verb + *the* + noun? · *We* + past verb + *a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Can** → **you** (S) → **film** (V) → **the concert** (O)? · (2) **We** (S) → **watched** (V) → **a great film** (O).
6. **Subject:** *you* · *We*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *film* · (2) *watched*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *Can*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the concert* · *a great film*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *film* after *Can you* → verb (record on video) · (2) *a great film* — after *a* + adjective → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** this is the core skill of L-021: **look at the position** to know if a word is a noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Thanks for your help. / Can you help me?* (EX-021-008) — the very first noun-or-verb pair in this lesson.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *They filmed the wedding.* (verb) · *Let's see a film.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Can you films the concert?* → ✅ *film* · ❌ *We watched great film.* → ✅ *a great film*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Can you video the concert?* · *That was a brilliant film!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FILM" (noun and verb the same).
19. **Pattern:** **Can you + verb + the + noun? / We watched a + adj + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Can you photograph the cake? / Look at this photograph.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a great film*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.
