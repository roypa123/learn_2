**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **From Part 17:** body parts take *my/your/his* · *a cold, a headache* but *(the) flu* · *exercise, sleep, health, hair* are **U**. **New here:** a verb + *-ing* can be a **U noun subject** (*Smoking is dangerous*), and *stress, energy, water, food* are U.

**New words in this part:** stress · vitamin · ambulance · muscle · smoking · bruise · nose · trainer · kilometre · surgeon · swallow · fit · sweets · blood pressure · lifestyle · runny · accident · first-aid kit · dumbbell · corner · blood test · loose · address · secret · energy · laughter · workout · smoke.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-851 — "The gym is crowded in January."

1. **Meaning:** In January, many people go to the gym. 🏋️🧍🧍
2. **Situation:** New Year: everyone wants to get fit.
3. **Main lesson:** place noun + month after *in*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective + *in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The gym** (S) → **is** → **crowded** → **in January** (P).
6. **Subject:** *The gym*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *crowded* · *in January*.
10. **Word by word:** *gym* 📍 — place for exercise (short for *gymnasium*) · *crowded* · *January* — capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*; *in* + month.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The beach is crowded in August* (EX-021-659) · *The canteen is next to the gym* (EX-021-238).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective + time.
14. **Other versions:** *The gym is empty in summer.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…on January.* → ✅ *in January* · ❌ *The gym is crowd.* → ✅ *crowded*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The gym's packed every January.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *J*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "JIM".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + adjective + in + month.**
20. **More examples:** *The pool is busy in July.*
21. **Quick check:** Long form of *gym*? → **Answer:** *gymnasium*.

### EX-021-852 — "Stress is bad for your heart."

1. **Meaning:** Feeling worried and under pressure hurts your heart. 😰❤️
2. **Situation:** A doctor's warning.
3. **Main lesson:** U idea noun + *bad for your* + body part.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is bad for your* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Stress** (S) → **is** → **bad** → **for your heart** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Stress*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *bad* · *for your heart*.
10. **Word by word:** *stress* 💭 — worry and pressure (U) · *bad for* · *heart*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U in general → no article, *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *It's a stressful job* (EX-021-378) — adjective *stressful* from the noun *stress*.
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Too much stress is bad for you.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A stress is bad…* → ✅ *Stress* · ❌ *Stresses are bad…* → ✅ *Stress is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Stress isn't good for your heart.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STRES" · "HART".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is bad for your + body part.**
20. **More examples:** *Salt is bad for your heart.*
21. **Quick check:** Adjective from *stress*? → **Answer:** *stressful*.

### EX-021-853 — "Vitamins are important for children."

1. **Meaning:** Children need vitamins to grow well. 🍊💊🧒
2. **Situation:** A school health talk.
3. **Main lesson:** plural C noun in general → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective + *for* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Vitamins** (S) → **are** → **important** → **for children** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Vitamins*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *important* · *for children*.
10. **Word by word:** *vitamins* — things in food that the body needs (C plural) · *children*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plurals → no *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Parks are important for children* (EX-021-705).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *Vitamin C is good for colds.* (name of one vitamin → no *the*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Vitamins is important.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The vitamin C…* → ✅ *Vitamin C*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Kids need their vitamins.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital letter in *Vitamin C, D*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "VIT-uh-minz" (UK) / "VY-tuh-minz" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + important for + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Vegetables are important for everyone.*
21. **Quick check:** *is* or *are*? → **Answer:** *are*.

### EX-021-855 — "My muscles are tired."

1. **Meaning:** The parts of my body that move me feel weak after exercise. 💪😩
2. **Situation:** After a long run.
3. **Main lesson:** plural body noun + adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My muscles** (S) → **are** → **tired**.
6. **Subject:** *My muscles*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *tired*.
10. **Word by word:** *muscles* — silent *c* · *tired* — how you feel (*-ed* adjective).
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Life in a big city is exciting but tiring* (EX-021-740) — *tiring* (cause) vs *tired* (feeling).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + correct *-ed* adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *My legs are tired.* · *My muscles hurt.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My muscles are tiring.* (= they make others tired) → ✅ *tired* · ❌ *My muscles is…* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My muscles are aching.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MUS-ulz" (silent *c*).
19. **Pattern:** **My + plural noun + are + -ed adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *My eyes are tired. · My feet are sore.*
21. **Quick check:** Is the *c* in *muscle* pronounced? → **Answer:** No.

### EX-021-856 — "Smoking is dangerous."

1. **Meaning:** Using cigarettes can seriously hurt you. 🚬☠️
2. **Situation:** A sign in a hospital.
3. **Main lesson:** verb + *-ing* used as a **U noun subject** (gerund).
4. **Grammar used:** verb-*ing* noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Smoking** (S) → **is** → **dangerous**.
6. **Subject:** *Smoking* (the activity).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *dangerous*.
10. **Word by word:** *Smoking* — *smoke* + *-ing* = the activity, used like a noun · *dangerous*.
11. **How the rules work together:** an activity as the subject → singular verb *is* ([L-109](../../02-intermediate-grammar/gerunds-and-infinitives/lesson-109-verb-plus-ing.md) later).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Exercise is good for you* (EX-021-807) — another U activity noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** *-ing* noun + singular verb.
14. **Other versions:** *Smoking is bad for your lungs.* · *Swimming is good exercise.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Smoke is dangerous.* (= the grey cloud) → ✅ *Smoking* (the habit) · ❌ *Smoking are dangerous.* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Smoking kills.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SMOH-king" · "DAYN-jer-us".
19. **Pattern:** **Verb-ing + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Walking is healthy. · Running is hard.*
21. **Quick check:** In *Smoking is dangerous*, what is the subject? → **Answer:** *Smoking*.

### EX-021-857 — "The bruise on my arm is purple."

1. **Meaning:** The dark mark on my arm (from a hit) is purple. 🟣💪
2. **Situation:** After bumping into a door.
3. **Main lesson:** subject noun + place phrase → *is*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *on my* + noun + *is* + colour.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The bruise on my arm** (S) → **is** → **purple**.
6. **Subject:** head **bruise**.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *purple*.
10. **Word by word:** *bruise* — mark on skin after a hit ("BROOZ") · *on my arm* · *purple*.
11. **How the rules work together:** head singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *There's a cut on my finger* (EX-021-833).
13. **Why this is correct:** head agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *I have a big bruise on my leg.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The bruise in my arm…* → ✅ *on my arm* · ❌ *The bruise on my arm are purple.* → ✅ *is*

17. **Writing & punctuation:** spelling: *b-r-u-i-s-e*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BROOZ".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + on my + body part + is + colour.**
20. **More examples:** *The scar on his face is small.*
21. **Quick check:** *on* or *in* for a mark on the skin? → **Answer:** *on*.

### EX-021-858 — "Water is the best drink."

1. **Meaning:** No drink is better for you than water. 💧🥇
2. **Situation:** A nutrition class.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun subject + superlative + C noun after *be*.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is the best* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Water** (S) → **is** → **the best drink** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Water*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the best drink*.
10. **Word by word:** *Water* — U, general · *the best* — superlative of *good* · *drink* — C noun here (a type of drink).
11. **How the rules work together:** superlative → *the*; *drink* can be a noun or a verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Breakfast is the most important meal* (EX-021-506).
13. **Why this is correct:** U subject + superlative complement.
14. **Other versions:** *Water is better than juice.* (comparative)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The water is the best drink.* (general → *Water*) · ❌ *Water is best drink.* → ✅ *the best drink*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Nothing beats water.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WAW-ter".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is the best + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Sleep is the best medicine.*
21. **Quick check:** Superlative of *good*? → **Answer:** *the best*.

## Step B — Health nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-861 — "My trainer is very strict. She makes me run five kilometres."

1. **Meaning:** My fitness coach is tough; she forces me to run 5 km. 🏃‍♀️📏
2. **Situation:** Talking about your gym sessions.
3. **Main lesson:** *my trainer* → *she*; number + plural noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is very* + adj · *She makes me* + verb + number + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My trainer** (S) → **is** → **very strict** · **She** (S) → **makes** (V) → **me** (O) → **run five kilometres**.
6. **Subject:** *My trainer* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *makes* (+ *run*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very strict* · *me* · *five kilometres*.
10. **Word by word:** *trainer* 👩 — person who trains you (also = sports shoe in UK!) · *strict* · *makes me run* — *make* + person + base verb · *kilometres*.
11. **How the rules work together:** woman → *she*; *five* + plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We have a strict teacher* (EX-021-229).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + verb pattern.
14. **Other versions:** *My coach is very strict. He makes me swim every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She makes me to run…* → ✅ *makes me run* · ❌ *…five kilometre.* → ✅ *kilometres*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My trainer's tough — she has me running 5K.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *kilometre* / US *kilometer*; short *km*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KIL-uh-mee-terz" or "ki-LOM-i-terz".
19. **Pattern:** **My + person + is very + adj. She makes me + verb + number + nouns.**
20. **More examples:** *My teacher is very kind. She lets us leave early.*
21. **Quick check:** *makes me to run* or *makes me run*? → **Answer:** *makes me run*.

### EX-021-862 — "I bought new running shoes. They are very comfortable."

8. **Helping verbs:** none.

11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun → *they* + *are*.

17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "RUN-ing-SHOOZ".
19. **Pattern:** **I bought new + -ing noun + plural noun. They are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *I bought new swimming goggles. They are blue.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *shoes*? → **Answer:** *they*.

11. **How the rules work together:** *people* plural; man → *him* as object.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The statue is famous* (EX-021-755).

14. **Other versions:** *The surgeon is famous. Patients come from abroad to see her.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *People comes…* → ✅ *come* · ❌ *…to see he.* → ✅ *him*

17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SUR-jun".

20. **More examples:** *The chef is famous. People come from all over the city to eat his food.*
21. **Quick check:** *People comes* or *People come*? → **Answer:** *People come*.

### EX-021-864 — "This vitamin pill is huge. I can't swallow it."

17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SWOL-oh".

### EX-021-865 — "My grandparents walk every day. It keeps them fit."

1. **Meaning:** My grandparents go for a walk daily, and this makes them stay healthy. 👵👴🚶
2. **Situation:** Talking about family habits.
3. **Main lesson:** *it* = the whole idea (walking); *them* = grandparents.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + plural noun + verb + *every day* · *It keeps them* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandparents** (S) → **walk** (V) → **every day** · **It** (S) → **keeps** (V) → **them** (O) → **fit**.
6. **Subject:** *My grandparents* · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *walk* · *keeps*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *every day* · *them* · *fit*.
10. **Word by word:** *grandparents* — plural · *walk* — no *-s* · *It* — walking every day · *keeps them fit* — *keep* + object + adjective.
11. **How the rules work together:** *it* can refer to a whole action/idea; plural people → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Regular exercise and a good diet keep your heart healthy* (EX-021-840).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *Walking every day keeps my grandparents fit.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My grandparents walks…* → ✅ *walk* · ❌ *It keeps they fit.* → ✅ *them*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My grandparents walk every day — that's what keeps them fit.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
