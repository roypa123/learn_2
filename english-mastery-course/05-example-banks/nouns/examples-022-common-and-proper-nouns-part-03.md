# Example Bank 022 · Part 03 — Common and Proper Nouns (EX-022-101 to EX-022-150)

**Lesson:** [L-022 — Common Nouns and Proper Nouns](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md) · **All parts:** [index](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md) · **Previous:** [Part 02](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-02.md) · **Next:** [Part 04](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-04.md)
**Theme of this part:** 👨‍👩‍👧 **people** — titles (*Mr, Dr, Professor, Captain, King*), family words used as names (*Mum, Grandpa, Uncle Ben*), nicknames and pets' names.

## How every example is explained (21 steps, always in this order)

**Short reminders:** **P** = proper noun → capital · **C** = common noun → small. The big rules for **people** in this part:
- **Family word used as a name** → capital: *Mum is at work.* · *Hi, Grandpa!* But with *my / your / his / the* → small: *my mum*, *your grandpa*.
- **Title + name** → both capital: *Uncle Ben, Dr Ali, Professor Lin, Captain Reyes, King Charles*. A title **without** a name → small: *the professor, our captain, the king*.
- **Nicknames and pets' names** are names → capital: *Bunny, Big Joe, Kiwi*.
- *sir / madam* used to talk to someone → usually small: *Excuse me, sir.*

> 🔁 **From Parts 01–02:** names of people take a capital and no article (*Ana*, *Mr Lee*); a title before a name is part of the name ([EX-022-054](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-02.md)). In this bank, **P** in step 10 means *proper noun*.

**New words in this part:** nickname · title · professor · captain · hamster · parrot · retired · coach · saint · nephew · niece · head teacher · pope.

## Step A — Family words, titles and nicknames: short sentences

### EX-022-101 — "Mum is at work today."

1. **Meaning:** My mother is at her job today. 👩‍💼
2. **Situation:** A child answers the phone at home.
3. **Main lesson:** A **family word used as a name** takes a **capital** (*Mum*).
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is at* + place + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Mum** (S) → **is** → **at work** (place) → **today** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Mum*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at work*, *today*.
10. **Word by word:** **Mum** — P: used instead of her name → capital, no *my* · *is* · *at* · *work* — C · *today*.
11. **How the rules work together:** no *my* before it → it works like a name → capital.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Mum is in the garden. She is planting flowers* (EX-021-111).
13. **Why this is correct:** *Mum* = a name here; *at work* (no article).
14. **Other versions:** *My mum is at work today.* (small *m* after *my*) · *Mom is at work.* (US)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Mum is at the work.* → ✅ *at work* · ❌ *My Mum is at work.* → ✅ *My mum*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Mum's at work today.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** British *Mum*, American *Mom*.
18. **Pronunciation:** UK "MUM" · US *Mom* = "MOM".
19. **Pattern:** **Family word (as a name) + is …**
20. **More examples:** *Dad is asleep.* · *Grandma is here!*
21. **Quick check:** *Mum* or *mum*: ___ *is at work.* (no *my*) → **Answer:** *Mum*.

### EX-022-102 — "My mum is a nurse."

1. **Meaning:** My mother's job is nursing. 👩‍⚕️
2. **Situation:** A class talk about parents' jobs.
3. **Main lesson:** With **my**, the family word is **common** → small letter.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is a* + job.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My mum** (S) → **is** → **a nurse** (B).
6. **Subject:** *My mum*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a nurse*.
10. **Word by word:** *My* · *mum* — C: after *my* → small · *is* · *a* · *nurse* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** compare EX-022-101: *Mum* (name) vs *my mum* (common noun).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Mum is at work today* (EX-022-101).
13. **Why this is correct:** *my* makes *mum* a normal noun → small.
14. **Other versions:** *My mother is a nurse.* · *Mum is a nurse.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My Mum is a nurse.* → ✅ *mum* · ❌ *My mum is nurse.* → ✅ *a nurse*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My mum's a nurse.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** small *m* in the middle of the sentence.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NURSS".
19. **Pattern:** **my + family word (small) + is a + job.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad is a driver.* · *Her grandma is a teacher.*
21. **Quick check:** Correct it: *My Dad is a driver.* → **Answer:** *My dad is a driver.*

### EX-022-103 — "Grandpa tells great stories."

1. **Meaning:** My grandfather is good at telling stories. 👴📖
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend about your family.
3. **Main lesson:** *Grandpa* used as a name → capital.
4. **Grammar used:** present simple (*tells*) for a habit.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Grandpa** (S) → **tells** (V) → **great stories** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Grandpa*.
7. **Verb:** *tells*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *great stories*.
10. **Word by word:** **Grandpa** — P (used as his name) · *tells* — *-s* for he · *great* · *stories* — C, plural.
11. **How the rules work together:** *Grandpa* = he → verb + *s*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Grandpa reads the newspaper every morning* (EX-021-153).
13. **Why this is correct:** name-like family word → capital; *tells* for one person.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandpa tells great stories.* · *Grandpa always tells stories.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Grandpa tell* → ✅ *tells* · ❌ *Grandpa says great stories* → ✅ *tells*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandpa's stories are the best!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** also *Grandad*, *Granddad*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GRAN-pah" (the *d* is usually silent).
19. **Pattern:** **Family word (name) + verb-s + O.**
20. **More examples:** *Grandma makes great soup.* · *Dad tells bad jokes.*
21. **Quick check:** *tell* or *tells* after *Grandpa*? → **Answer:** *tells*.

### EX-022-104 — "Uncle Ben lives near us."

1. **Meaning:** My uncle, called Ben, has his home close to ours. 🏡
2. **Situation:** Talking about family.
3. **Main lesson:** **Family title + first name** → both capitals.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *lives near* + object pronoun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Uncle Ben** (S) → **lives** (V) → **near us** (place).
6. **Subject:** *Uncle Ben*.
7. **Verb:** *lives*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *near us*.
10. **Word by word:** **Uncle** — title before a name → capital · **Ben** — P · *lives* · *near* · *us* — object pronoun.
11. **How the rules work together:** *my uncle* (small) vs *Uncle Ben* (title → capital).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My uncle is a farmer* (EX-021-023) — no name → small.
13. **Why this is correct:** *Uncle* is part of the name; *lives* for one person.
14. **Other versions:** *My uncle Ben lives near us.* (small *u* — also correct)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *uncle Ben lives near us.* → ✅ *Uncle Ben* · ❌ *near we* → ✅ *near us*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Uncle Ben's just round the corner.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Uncle Ben* (title) — *my uncle, Ben* (explanation with a comma) — both fine.
18. **Pronunciation:** "UNG-kul BEN".
19. **Pattern:** **Uncle / Aunt + Name + verb …**
20. **More examples:** *Aunt Mary lives in Leeds.* · *Cousin Jo is coming.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *my ___ is a farmer* (uncle)? → **Answer:** small *uncle*.

### EX-022-105 — "Professor Lin teaches maths."

1. **Meaning:** A university teacher called Professor Lin teaches mathematics. 👨‍🏫➗
2. **Situation:** A university timetable.
3. **Main lesson:** **Professor** before a name = a title → capital.
4. **Grammar used:** title + name + present simple + school subject.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Professor Lin** (S) → **teaches** (V) → **maths** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Professor Lin*.
7. **Verb:** *teaches*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *maths*.
10. **Word by word:** **Professor** — title → capital · **Lin** — P · *teaches* — *-es* after *ch* · *maths* — C: school subjects are small (but languages are capital).
11. **How the rules work together:** *maths, history, science* are small; *English, French* are capital.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Do you know Dr Brown?* (EX-022-045) — title + name.
13. **Why this is correct:** title + name capitals; subject small.
14. **Other versions:** *Lin is a professor.* (no name after it → small) · *Prof. Lin teaches maths.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *professor Lin* → ✅ *Professor Lin* · ❌ *teaches Maths* → ✅ *maths*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Professor Lin does the maths classes.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** short form *Prof.*; US *math*, UK *maths*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "pruh-FESS-er LIN".
19. **Pattern:** **Title + Surname + verb + subject.**
20. **More examples:** *Professor Ali teaches history.* · *Ms Kim teaches English.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *maths*? *English*? → **Answer:** *maths* small · *English* capital.

### EX-022-106 — "Her nickname is Bunny."

1. **Meaning:** People call her by a friendly short name: Bunny. 🐰
2. **Situation:** Explaining a friend's funny name.
3. **Main lesson:** A **nickname** is a name → capital, even if it is a normal word.
4. **Grammar used:** *Her nickname is* + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Her nickname** (S) → **is** → **Bunny** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Her nickname*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Bunny*.
10. **Word by word:** *Her* · *nickname* — C: the word for a type of name · *is* · **Bunny** — P: her nickname → capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a bunny* (a young rabbit) = common; *Bunny* (her) = proper.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: What's your dog's name? B: Biscuit!* (EX-022-089) — a normal word becomes a name.
13. **Why this is correct:** capital for the nickname; *nickname* itself is small.
14. **Other versions:** *Everyone calls her Bunny.* · *Her real name is Bernadette.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Her nickname is bunny.* → ✅ *Bunny* · ❌ *Her Nickname* → ✅ *nickname*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We all call her Bunny.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** you may put nicknames in quotation marks: *"Bunny"*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BUN-ee" · "NIK-naym".
19. **Pattern:** **Possessive + nickname + is + Nickname.**
20. **More examples:** *His nickname is Spike.* · *My nickname is Lulu.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *nickname* proper or common? → **Answer:** common.

### EX-022-107 — "Kiwi is our parrot."

1. **Meaning:** Our parrot is called Kiwi. 🦜
2. **Situation:** Showing a visitor your pet bird.
3. **Main lesson:** A pet named after a **food or thing** still takes a capital.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is our* + animal.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Kiwi** (S) → **is** → **our parrot** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Kiwi*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *our parrot*.
10. **Word by word:** **Kiwi** — P: the bird's name · *is* · *our* · *parrot* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a kiwi* (fruit/bird type) = common; *Kiwi* (this parrot) = proper.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Rex is my dog* (EX-022-015).
13. **Why this is correct:** pet's name → capital; *our* + common noun.
14. **Other versions:** *Our parrot is called Kiwi.* · *This is Kiwi, our parrot.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *kiwi is our parrot.* → ✅ *Kiwi* · ❌ *Kiwi is our Parrot.* → ✅ *parrot*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Meet Kiwi — our parrot!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *K* as the name.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KEE-wee" · "PA-rut".
19. **Pattern:** **Pet's name + is our + animal.**
20. **More examples:** *Mango is our cat.* · *Peanut is my hamster.*
21. **Quick check:** Proper or common: *parrot*? → **Answer:** common.

### EX-022-108 — "King Charles is the king of the United Kingdom."

1. **Meaning:** Charles is the king who rules the UK. 👑🇬🇧
2. **Situation:** A history or citizenship class.
3. **Main lesson:** **King + name** = title → capital; **the king** (no name) → small.
4. **Grammar used:** title + name + *is the* + noun + *of the* + country.
5. **Sentence structure:** **King Charles** (S) → **is** → **the king of the United Kingdom** (B).
6. **Subject:** *King Charles*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the king of the United Kingdom*.
10. **Word by word:** **King** — title → capital · **Charles** — P · *is* · *the* · *king* — C: no name after it → small · *of* · *the* · **United Kingdom** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** the same word *king* is capital before a name, small as a normal noun; *Kingdom* is capital inside the country name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She studied at Harvard and then moved to the UK* (EX-022-076).
13. **Why this is correct:** capital *King* (title) and small *king* (job/role).
14. **Other versions:** *Charles is the British king.* · *The King spoke on TV.* (some writers use a capital for *the King* of their own country)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *king Charles* → ✅ *King Charles* · ❌ *the King of the united kingdom* → ✅ *the king of the United Kingdom*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *King Charles is the UK's king.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** numbers after names: *King Charles III* = "Charles the Third".
18. **Pronunciation:** "KING CHARLZ" · *III* = "the third".
19. **Pattern:** **Title + Name** (capital) · **the + title** (small).
20. **More examples:** *Queen Rania is the queen of Jordan.* · *Prince William is a prince.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *the ___ of Spain* (king)? → **Answer:** small (*the king of Spain*).

### EX-022-110 — "Auntie May makes the best cake."

1. **Meaning:** My aunt, May, bakes better cake than anyone. 🎂
2. **Situation:** Planning a birthday party.
3. **Main lesson:** A **friendly family title** (*Auntie*) + name → capitals.
4. **Grammar used:** name + present simple + *the best* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Auntie May** (S) → **makes** (V) → **the best cake** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Auntie May*.
7. **Verb:** *makes*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the best cake*.
10. **Word by word:** **Auntie** — friendly title → capital · **May** — P: her name (not the month here!) · *makes* · *the best* · *cake* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *May* can be a month or a person's name — both are proper → capital.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *May I come in? / My exam is in May* (EX-022-098).

17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Auntie* (UK) / *Aunty* (also used).
18. **Pronunciation:** UK "AHN-tee"; US *aunt* often "ANT".

20. **More examples:** *Uncle Raj makes the best tea.* · *Nana Joy tells the best jokes.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *May* here a month or a person? → **Answer:** a person.

## Step B — People's names and pronouns

### EX-022-111 — "Dad is tired. He works at night."

1. **Meaning:** My father is tired because he works during the night. 😴🌙
2. **Situation:** Telling friends to be quiet at home.
3. **Main lesson:** *Dad* (name-like) → replaced by **he**.
4. **Grammar used:** *is* + adjective; pronoun + present simple.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Dad** (S) → **is** → **tired** (B). **He** (S) → **works** (V) → **at night** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Dad*, *He*.
7. **Verb:** *is*, *works*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *tired*, *at night*.
10. **Word by word:** **Dad** — P (used as a name) · *is* · *tired* · *He* — = Dad · *works* · *at* · *night* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** first the name, then the pronoun so we don't repeat it.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Omar is my cousin. He lives in Jordan* (EX-022-033).
13. **Why this is correct:** *He* for a man; *works* for he.
14. **Other versions:** *My dad is tired because he works at night.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Dad is tired. Dad works at night.* (repetitive) → ✅ *He works* · ❌ *at the night* → ✅ *at night*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Shh — Dad's sleeping. He works nights.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Dad* capital at the start anyway; *my dad* small.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DAD".
19. **Pattern:** **Family name + is + adj. He/She + verb-s …**
20. **More examples:** *Mum is busy. She has a meeting.*
21. **Quick check:** Which pronoun replaces *Dad*? → **Answer:** *he*.

### EX-022-112 — "Princess Diana was very popular. People loved her."

1. **Meaning:** Many people liked Princess Diana a lot. 👸❤️
2. **Situation:** A history lesson about the 1990s.
3. **Main lesson:** Title + name (*Princess Diana*) → object pronoun **her**.
4. **Grammar used:** past simple *was*, *loved* + object pronoun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Princess Diana** (S) → **was** → **very popular** (B). **People** (S) → **loved** (V) → **her** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Princess Diana*, *People*.
7. **Verb:** *was*, *loved*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very popular*; *her*.
10. **Word by word:** **Princess** — title → capital · **Diana** — P · *was* · *very* · *popular* · *People* — C · *loved* · *her* — object pronoun for a woman.
11. **How the rules work together:** after a verb we use *her*, not *she*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My best friend is Yuki. I call her every Sunday* (EX-022-041).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals on the title + name; *her* as object.
14. **Other versions:** *Everyone loved Princess Diana.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *princess Diana* → ✅ *Princess Diana* · ❌ *People loved she.* → ✅ *her*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Everyone loved Diana.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *a princess* (no name) → small.
18. **Pronunciation:** "prin-SESS dy-AN-uh".
19. **Pattern:** **Title + Name + was … People + verb + her/him.**
20. **More examples:** *King Arthur was brave. People followed him.*
21. **Quick check:** *People loved ___* (Diana): *she* or *her*? → **Answer:** *her*.

### EX-022-113 — "Coco is my hamster. She is tiny."

1. **Meaning:** My hamster's name is Coco, and she is very small. 🐹
2. **Situation:** Showing a pet at school.
3. **Main lesson:** For a pet **with a name** we often use **he / she**, not *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is my* + animal; *She is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Coco** (S) → **is** → **my hamster** (B). **She** (S) → **is** → **tiny** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Coco*, *She*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my hamster*, *tiny*.
10. **Word by word:** **Coco** — P · *is* · *my* · *hamster* — C · *She* — = Coco · *is* · *tiny*.
11. **How the rules work together:** named pets feel like family → *he/she*; unknown animals → *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Luna is our cat. She sleeps all day* (EX-022-037).
13. **Why this is correct:** pet's name capital; *she* for a female pet.
14. **Other versions:** *My hamster, Coco, is tiny.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *coco is my hamster* → ✅ *Coco* · ❌ *my Hamster* → ✅ *hamster*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This is Coco — isn't she tiny?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two short sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KOH-koh" · "HAM-ster".
19. **Pattern:** **Pet name + is my + animal. He/She + is + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *Max is my rabbit. He is fluffy.*
21. **Quick check:** For a named pet: *it* or *she/he*? → **Answer:** usually *he/she*.

### EX-022-114 — "Mr and Mrs Ito are retired. They live by the sea."

1. **Meaning:** The married couple Mr and Mrs Ito don't work any more; their home is near the sea. 👫🌊
2. **Situation:** Describing neighbours.
3. **Main lesson:** **Two titles + one family name** → plural → **they**.
4. **Grammar used:** *are* + adjective; *They live* + place.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Mr and Mrs Ito** (S) → **are** → **retired** (B). **They** (S) → **live** (V) → **by the sea**.
6. **Subject:** *Mr and Mrs Ito*, *They*.
7. **Verb:** *are*, *live*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *retired*, *by the sea*.
10. **Word by word:** **Mr** · *and* · **Mrs** — titles → capitals · **Ito** — P · *are* · *retired* · *They* · *live* · *by* · *the* · *sea* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** two people → *are*, *they*, *live* (no *-s*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The Garcias are our neighbours. They have three dogs* (EX-022-035).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural verb; one family name for both people.
14. **Other versions:** *The Itos are retired.* · *Mr Ito and his wife are retired.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Mr and Mrs Ito is retired* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *They lives* → ✅ *live*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The Itos are retired now — they live by the sea.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Mrs* = married woman · *Ms* = any woman · *Miss* = young/unmarried.

## Step I — People in a short story

> 📖 **The story:** (1) Granny Rose has a parrot called Captain. (2) Every morning, Captain shouts, "Hello, Rose!" (3) The children on her street call him the Talking Bird.

### EX-022-144 — "Granny Rose has a parrot called Captain."

1. **Meaning:** Granny Rose owns a parrot, and its name is Captain. 👵🦜
2. **Situation:** The first line of a story.
3. **Main lesson:** A **job word used as a pet's name** (*Captain*) → capital.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *has a* + noun + *called* + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Granny Rose** (S) → **has** (V) → **a parrot called Captain** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Granny Rose*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a parrot called Captain*.
10. **Word by word:** **Granny Rose** — P · *has* · *a* · *parrot* — C · *called* · **Captain** — P: the parrot's name.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a captain* (job) = common; *Captain* (this bird) = proper.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Captain Reyes flies the plane* (EX-022-109) — *Captain* as a title.
13. **Why this is correct:** *called* + name; capitals on all names.
14. **Other versions:** *Granny Rose's parrot is called Captain.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *a parrot called captain* → ✅ *Captain* · ❌ *a parrot calls Captain* → ✅ *called*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Granny Rose has this parrot called Captain.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma before *called*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GRAN-ee ROHZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + has a + animal + called + Name.**
20. **More examples:** *Tom has a dog called Chief.*
21. **Quick check:** *captain* or *Captain* (the parrot)? → **Answer:** *Captain*.

---

### EX-022-145 — "Every morning, Captain shouts, "Hello, Rose!""

1. **Meaning:** Each morning the parrot shouts the words "Hello, Rose!" 🦜📢
2. **Situation:** The second line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** Names inside **direct speech** (quotation marks).
4. **Grammar used:** present simple + direct speech.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Every morning** (time), **Captain** (S) → **shouts** (V) → **"Hello, Rose!"** (what he says).
6. **Subject:** *Captain*.
7. **Verb:** *shouts*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** the words in quotation marks.
10. **Word by word:** *Every* · *morning* — C · **Captain** — P · *shouts* · *Hello* · **Rose** — P: addressed → comma before it.
11. **How the rules work together:** speech starts with a capital; the addressed name has a comma.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: Hi, I'm Alexander. B: Nice to meet you, Alexander* (EX-022-141).
13. **Why this is correct:** comma before the quote; ! inside the quotation marks.
14. **Other versions:** *"Hello, Rose!" Captain shouts every morning.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *"hello rose!"* → ✅ *"Hello, Rose!"* · ❌ *Captain shout* → ✅ *shouts*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Every morning the parrot yells "Hello, Rose!"*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *shouts*; quotation marks around the speech.
18. **Pronunciation:** "huh-LOH, ROHZ!"
19. **Pattern:** **Name + shouts/says, "Greeting, Name!"**
20. **More examples:** *Every evening, Dad says, "Goodnight, Sam!"*
21. **Quick check:** Where does *!* go — inside or outside the quotation marks? → **Answer:** inside.

---

### EX-022-146 — "The children on her street call him the Talking Bird."

1. **Meaning:** The local children have a nickname for Captain: the Talking Bird. 🧒🦜
2. **Situation:** The last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** A **nickname made from common words** becomes a name → capitals (*the Talking Bird*).
4. **Grammar used:** *call* + object + nickname.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The children on her street** (S) → **call** (V) → **him** (O) → **the Talking Bird** (name).
6. **Subject:** *The children on her street*.
7. **Verb:** *call*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *him* + *the Talking Bird*.
10. **Word by word:** *The* · *children* — C, plural · *on* · *her* · *street* — C · *call* · *him* — = Captain · *the* · **Talking Bird** — P: a nickname.
11. **How the rules work together:** *a talking bird* (description, small) vs *the Talking Bird* (his nickname, capitals).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Why do people call him Big Joe?* (EX-022-121).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural *children* → *call*; nickname capitals.
14. **Other versions:** *To the children on her street, Captain is the Talking Bird.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The children calls him* → ✅ *call* · ❌ *the talking bird* (as a nickname) → ✅ *the Talking Bird*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids call him the Talking Bird.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *the* small; *Talking Bird* capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TAW-king BURD" (*l* silent).
19. **Pattern:** **S + call + him/her + the + Nickname.**
20. **More examples:** *People call the city the Big Apple.*
21. **Quick check:** How many names (people/animals) are in the whole story? → **Answer:** *Granny Rose, Captain, Rose, the Talking Bird* — 4 (Rose twice).

## Step J — People's names in real writing: a card

> 💌 **A birthday card:** *Dear Aunt Lucy, … Love from your nephew, Sam*

### EX-022-147 — "Dear Aunt Lucy,"

1. **Meaning:** The opening of a friendly letter or card to my aunt Lucy. 💌
2. **Situation:** Writing a birthday card.
3. **Main lesson:** *Dear* + **family title + first name** → all capitals.
4. **Grammar used:** greeting + title + name + comma.
5. **Sentence structure:** a fixed greeting: **Dear** + **Aunt Lucy** + **,**
6. **Subject:** none.
7. **Verb:** none.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Aunt Lucy* — the reader.
10. **Word by word:** **Dear** · **Aunt** — title · **Lucy** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** family letters use the first name; formal ones use title + family name (EX-022-094).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Dear Mr Evans,* (EX-022-094).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals on all three words; comma at the end.
14. **Other versions:** *Hi Aunt Lucy,* · *Dearest Aunt Lucy,*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Dear aunt Lucy* → ✅ *Dear Aunt Lucy,* · ❌ *Dear Aunt Brown* (family letter) → ✅ *Dear Aunt Lucy*
16. **Natural spoken English:** (spoken) *Hi, Aunt Lucy!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the name; start the next line with a capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DEER AHNT LOO-see".
19. **Pattern:** **Dear + Family title + First name,**
20. **More examples:** *Dear Grandpa,* · *Dear Uncle Raj,*
21. **Quick check:** Correct it: *dear aunt lucy* → **Answer:** *Dear Aunt Lucy,*

---

### EX-022-148 — "Love from your nephew, Sam"

1. **Meaning:** The friendly ending of the card: it's from Sam, your nephew. ❤️
2. **Situation:** Signing the birthday card.
3. **Main lesson:** *nephew* (common) + the writer's **name** (proper) in a sign-off.
4. **Grammar used:** *Love from* + noun phrase + , + name.
5. **Sentence structure:** fixed ending: **Love from** + **your nephew** + **, Sam**.
6. **Subject:** none (a closing phrase).
7. **Verb:** none.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *your nephew, Sam*.
10. **Word by word:** **Love** — first word → capital · *from* · *your* · *nephew* — C: son of your brother/sister · **Sam** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** only the first word and the name take capitals.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: Is that your sister? B: No, that's my cousin, Mia* (EX-022-143) — family word + comma + name.
13. **Why this is correct:** small *nephew*; capital *Sam*; no full stop needed.
14. **Other versions:** *Lots of love, Sam* · *With love from Sam*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Love from your Nephew, Sam* → ✅ *nephew* · ❌ *love from sam* → ✅ *Love from Sam*
16. **Natural spoken English:** — (written only)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** often on two lines: *Love from your nephew,* / *Sam*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NEF-yoo" (UK also "NEV-yoo").
19. **Pattern:** **Love from + your + family word, + Name**
20. **More examples:** *Love from your niece, Ella* · *Hugs from your grandson, Leo*
21. **Quick check:** A *nephew* is a boy or a girl? → **Answer:** a boy (a girl = *niece*).

## Step K — Same word: common or proper?

### EX-022-149 — "My mother is very kind. / Thank you, Mother!"

1. **Meaning:** (1) I say my mother is kind. (2) I thank her, using *Mother* as her name. 👩❤️
2. **Situation:** Talking about her; talking to her.
3. **Main lesson:** *my mother* (common, small) vs *Mother* (used as a name, capital).
4. **Grammar used:** *is* + adjective; *Thank you, + name*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My mother** (S) → **is** → **very kind** (B). · **Thank you**, **Mother** (person addressed)!
6. **Subject:** *My mother*; *(I)* hidden in *Thank you*.
7. **Verb:** *is*; *thank*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very kind*; *you*.
10. **Word by word:** *mother* — C after *my* → small · **Mother** — P: used to call her → capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** test: can you put her real name in its place? *Thank you, Maria!* ✓ → capital.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: Mum, can I go out? B: Ask your dad* (EX-022-142).
13. **Why this is correct:** small with *my*; capital when it replaces her name.
14. **Other versions:** *My mum is very kind. / Thanks, Mum!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My Mother is very kind.* → ✅ *mother* · ❌ *Thank you, mother!* → ✅ *Mother*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Thanks, Mum!* (*Mother* sounds formal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before the addressed name.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MUTH-er" (*th* as in *this*).
19. **Pattern:** **my mother (common) · Mother (as a name)**.
20. **More examples:** *My father is tall. / Goodnight, Father.*
21. **Quick check:** Name test: *Is ___ home?* (mother, no *my*) → **Answer:** *Is Mother home?* (capital).

### EX-022-150 — "Bill paid the bill."

1. **Meaning:** A man called Bill paid the money for the meal or service. 💵🧾
2. **Situation:** After dinner at a restaurant.
3. **Main lesson:** The **same word** as a person's **name** (*Bill*) and a **common noun** (*bill*).
4. **Grammar used:** past simple *paid* + *the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Bill** (S) → **paid** (V) → **the bill** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Bill*.
7. **Verb:** *paid* (past of *pay*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the bill*.
10. **Word by word:** **Bill** — P: a man's name (short for *William*) · *paid* · *the* · *bill* — C: paper showing the money to pay.
11. **How the rules work together:** a name → capital, no article; a common noun → small, with *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The bill is wrong* (EX-021-408) — *bill* as a common noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** capital for the man, small + *the* for the paper.
14. **Other versions:** *Bill paid for everyone.* · *Who paid the bill? Bill did!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *bill paid the Bill.* → ✅ *Bill paid the bill.* · ❌ *Bill payed* → ✅ *paid*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Bill got the bill — nice!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** in speech you hear no difference; in writing the capital shows it.
18. **Pronunciation:** both "BIL".
19. **Pattern:** **Name (capital) + verb + the + common noun (small)** — same word!
20. **More examples:** *Rose picked a rose.* · *Jack used a jack to lift the car.*
21. **Quick check:** Correct the capitals: *rose gave me a Rose.* → **Answer:** *Rose gave me a rose.*
