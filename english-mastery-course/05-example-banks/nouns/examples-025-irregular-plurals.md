# Example Bank 025 — Irregular Plurals (EX-025-013 to EX-025-100)

**For lesson:** [L-025 — Irregular Plurals](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md) · EX-025-001 to 012 are in the lesson.

## How to use this bank

- **Cover the notes.** For each **bold** word: (1) singular or plural? (2) which **type** (1–5)? (3) are the partners correct?
- **Types:** **T1** vowel change · **T2** -en/-ren · **T3** same form · **T4** different word · **T5** Latin/Greek

> 🔁 Never add -s to an irregular plural. Partners agree as usual: *these, are, they*, verb without -s ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).

## Step 1 — Short sentences (EX-025-013 to 022)

**EX-025-013** — *The **children** are happy.* — T2 · *are*
**EX-025-014** — *The **men** are tall.* — T1 · *are*
**EX-025-015** — *Two **women** are here.* — T1 · say "WIM-in"
**EX-025-016** — ***People** are kind.* — T4 · *are*
**EX-025-017** — *My **feet** are cold.* — T1
**EX-025-018** — *Her **teeth** are white.* — T1
**EX-025-019** — *The **mice** are small.* — T1
**EX-025-020** — *Three **sheep** are in the field.* — T3 (no -s)
**EX-025-021** — *Two **fish** are in the bowl.* — T3
**EX-025-022** — *The **geese** are noisy.* — T1 · *goose → geese*

## Step 2 — Different nouns and verbs (EX-025-023 to 032)

**EX-025-023** — *Our **children** go to the same school.*
- T2 · plural → *go* (no -s).

**EX-025-024** — *These **women** work at the hospital.*
- T1 · *These* + plural · *work*.

**EX-025-025** — *Most **people** like music.*
- T4 · *Most* (= almost all) + plural.

**EX-025-026** — *Some **men** wear hats in summer.*
- T1 · *wear* (no -s).

**EX-025-027** — *Cats catch **mice**.*
- T1 · object; general statement.

**EX-025-028** — *We saw two **deer** in the forest.*
- T3 · ❌ *deers*.

**EX-025-029** — *The **aircraft** land on this runway.*
- T3 · plural here (many aircraft) → *land*. *runway* = the road where planes land.

**EX-025-030** — *I have two **children** and one grandchild.*
- T2 plural vs singular *grandchild* (→ plural *grandchildren*).

**EX-025-031** — *Elephants have big **feet**.*
- T1 · general statement.

**EX-025-032** — *Ten **people** came to the meeting.*
- T4 · *came* = past of *come*.

## Step 3 — With pronouns (EX-025-033 to 042)

**EX-025-033** — *The **children** are tired. **They** want to sleep.*
- *children* → **they**.

**EX-025-034** — *Those two **women** are my aunts. I visit **them** every week.*
- *women* → **them** (object).

**EX-025-035** — *A **person** called you. **He** left a message.*
- singular *person* → **he** (or *she*, or *they* if unknown — see note).
- Note: when we don't know if the person is a man or a woman, English often uses **they** for one person: *Someone called. **They** left a message.* This is normal and correct.

**EX-025-036** — *The **people** next door are friendly. **They** invited us to dinner.*
- *people* → **they**.

**EX-025-037** — *My **teeth** hurt. I think **they** need a check.*
- *teeth* → **they**.

**EX-025-038** — *The **sheep** are hungry. Give **them** some grass.*
- *sheep* (plural here) → **them**.

**EX-025-039** — *This **fish** is delicious. Where did you buy **it**?*
- *fish* (singular here — one fish / as food) → **it**.

**EX-025-040** — *The **men** in the photo are my brothers. **They** live in Lima.*
- head *men* → *are*, **they**.

**EX-025-041** — *I love my **feet**! **They** are small and cute.* 😄
- *feet* → **they**.

**EX-025-042** — *We have **mice** in the kitchen. I don't like **them**!*
- *mice* → **them**.

## Step 4 — Questions and negatives (EX-025-043 to 052)

**EX-025-043** — *How many **children** do you have?*
- *How many* + plural (T2).

**EX-025-044** — *Are the **women** in the team ready?*
- head *women* → *Are*.

**EX-025-045** — *How many **people** live in your city?*
- T4 · *How many people* ✅ (❌ *How much people*).

**EX-025-046** — *The **children** don't like vegetables.*
- plural → *don't*.

**EX-025-047** — *Do **men** and **women** have the same rights here?*
- *rights* = what the law allows everyone.

**EX-025-048** — *My **feet** aren't big.*
- *aren't* with plural.

**EX-025-049** — *Are there any **fish** in this river?*
- T3 · *any fish*.

**EX-025-050** — *Why are the **geese** so loud?*
- T1.

**EX-025-051** — ***People** don't usually walk here at night.*
- T4 · *don't*.

**EX-025-052** — *Does the farmer have any **sheep**?*
- T3 · *any sheep*. (*Does* — because the subject *the farmer* is singular.)

## Step 5 — Different times (EX-025-053 to 060)

**EX-025-053** — *The **children** were very quiet yesterday.*
- *were* (past plural).

**EX-025-054** — *Twenty **people** will come to the wedding.*
- *will come* (future — same for S and P).

**EX-025-055** — *Two **men** helped me with my car.*
- *helped* (past, -ed).

**EX-025-056** — *My grandmother had very strong **teeth**.*
- *had* = past of *have*.

**EX-025-057** — *Last year, we saw hundreds of **sheep** in New Zealand.*
- T3 · *hundreds of* = very many.

**EX-025-058** — *The **women** won the match!*
- *won* = past of *win*. *match* = a sports game.

**EX-025-059** — *In the past, **people** didn't have phones.*
- *didn't have* = past negative ([L-065](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-065-past-simple-negative-and-questions.md)).

**EX-025-060** — *Next year, the **children** will start a new school.*
- future.

**EX-025-069** — *Wild **geese** fly south in winter.* — T1
**EX-025-070** — *These are my favourite **people**!* — T4

## Step 7 — Irregular plurals + adjectives + prepositions/places (EX-025-071 to 078)

**EX-025-071** — *The **children** in my class are from five countries.*
- head *children* → *are*.

**EX-025-072** — *There are two **men** at the door.*
- *There are* + plural.

**EX-025-073** — *The **women** in this photo are my grandmother's sisters.*
- head *women*.

**EX-025-074** — *Most **people** in my town work in factories.*
- *factories* — regular (y → ies).

**EX-025-075** — *The **fish** in this lake are very big.*
- T3 · plural here → *are*.

**EX-025-076** — *I have pain in both **feet**.*
- *both* + plural.

**EX-025-077** — *The **mice** behind the fridge come out at night.*
- head *mice* → *come*.

**EX-025-078** — *The **people** on the bus were very friendly.*
- head *people* → *were*.

## Step 8 — Longer sentences (EX-025-079 to 086)

**EX-025-079** — *In my office, there are twelve **women** and eight **men**, and all of them speak at least two languages.*
- *all of them* + *speak* (plural).

**EX-025-080** — *The dentist checked the **children's** **teeth** and gave them new toothbrushes.*
- *children's* (possessive) · *teeth* T1 · *toothbrush* — regular plural (-sh + es).

**EX-025-081** — *Some **people** think that **mice** like cheese, but in fact they prefer seeds and fruit.*
- *they* = mice. *prefer* = like more.

**EX-025-082** — *On the farm, there were sheep, cows, geese and three very lazy dogs.*
- **sheep** (T3), **cows** (regular), **geese** (T1), **dogs** (regular).

**EX-025-083** — *The **firefighters** saved two **children** and a woman from the burning house.*
- *firefighters* (neutral word, regular) · *children* (T2) · *a woman* (singular).

**EX-025-084** — *Each **person** in the group has a map, but only a few **people** know the way.*
- *each* + singular *person* → *has*; *a few people* → *know*.

**EX-025-085** — *These **species** of **fish** live only in the warm seas around Indonesia.*
- *species* T3 (*this species / these species*) · *fish* T3.

**EX-025-086** — *The news media often report on economic **crises** in different countries.*
- *media* T5 (plural of *medium*) · *crises* T5 (plural of *crisis*, "CRY-seez").

## Step 9 — Conversations (EX-025-087 to 090)

**EX-025-087**
> **A:** Do you have any **children**?
> **B:** Yes, two. A boy and a girl.
- *two* = two children (we don't repeat the noun).

**EX-025-088**
> **A:** How many **people** are coming?
> **B:** About fifteen. Ten **adults** and five **kids**.
- *kids* = informal word for children (regular plural). *adults* = grown-up people.

**EX-025-089**
> **A:** Ow! My **feet**!
> **B:** New shoes?
> **A:** Yes. They're too small.
- *They* = the shoes.

**EX-025-090**
> **A:** Excuse me, where is the **women's** toilet?
> **B:** Down the corridor, next to the **men's**.
- *women's / men's* — possessive of irregular plurals.

## Step 10 — Short story (EX-025-091 to 093)

> *(1) Every spring, the **children** in our village help the farmers. (2) They count the new baby **sheep** and feed the **geese**. (3) The **women** cook a big lunch, and all the **people** eat together in the field.*

**EX-025-091** — (1) *children* (T2) → *help*.
**EX-025-092** — (2) *They* = the children · *sheep* T3 · *geese* T1.
**EX-025-093** — (3) *women* T1 → *cook*; *people* T4 → *eat*.

---

## Step 11 — Real writing: a notice (EX-025-094 to 096)

> ***NOTICE** — Swimming pool rules: Children under 8 must come with an adult. Maximum 30 **persons** in the pool. **People** with long hair must wear a swimming cap.*

**EX-025-094** — *Children under 8 must come with an adult.* — *children* T2 · *must* = it's a rule ([L-083](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-083-must-and-have-to.md)).
**EX-025-095** — *Maximum 30 persons in the pool.* — *persons* — the **formal** plural, used on official signs.
**EX-025-096** — *People with long hair must wear a swimming cap.* — everyday plural *people*.

---

## Step 12 — Telling similar forms apart (EX-025-097 to 100)

**EX-025-097** — *one **woman** / two **women***
- Spelling: one letter changes (a → e). Sound: "WUM-un" → "WIM-in". Listen to the **first** vowel.

**EX-025-098** — *a **person** / **people** / **persons** / **peoples***
- *person* = one · *people* = everyday plural · *persons* = formal (signs, laws) · *peoples* = nations/ethnic groups.

**EX-025-099** — *I caught a **fish**. / I caught three **fish**. / I like **fish**.*
- singular · plural (same form) · uncountable (food) ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).

**EX-025-100** — *two **mice** (animals) / two **mice** or **mouses** (computer)*
- For animals → always *mice*. For computers → *mice* is more common, *mouses* is sometimes heard.
