**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Food nouns:** many are **U** in general (*rice, soup, meat, bread, salt, food*) and **C** for items/portions/kinds (*a lemon, two eggs, a coffee, three cheeses*) ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).

**New words in this part:** menu · meal · steak · dish · nuts · counter · freezer · recipe · dessert · service · spicy · homemade · pepper · garlic · heat.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-501 — "The soup is hot."

1. **Meaning:** The soup has a high temperature — be careful! 🍲🔥
2. **Situation:** A parent serves soup to a child.
3. **Main lesson:** A **U food noun** with *the* (specific soup).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The soup** (S) → **is** → **hot**.
6. **Subject:** *The soup* — the soup in the bowl (specific).
7. **Verb:** *is* (U → singular).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *hot*.
10. **Word by word:** *soup* 📦 — liquid food (U) · *hot* — high temperature (also *spicy*!).
11. **How the rules work together:** specific U noun → *the* + singular verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The soup is hot. Don't drink it yet.* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Soup is good in winter.* (general → no *the*) · *Is the soup hot?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The soup are hot.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *A soup is hot.* → ✅ *The soup*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Careful — it's really hot!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SOOP".
19. **Pattern:** **The + U food noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The coffee is cold. · The rice is ready.*
21. **Quick check:** General version? → **Answer:** *Soup is hot.* → better: *Soup is good in winter.*

### EX-021-502 — "Rice is cheap."

1. **Meaning:** Rice doesn't cost much money. 🍚
2. **Situation:** Students talk about cheap food.
3. **Main lesson:** A U food noun in general (no article).
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Rice** (S) → **is** → **cheap**.
6. **Subject:** *Rice*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *cheap*.
10. **Word by word:** *Rice* (U — no -s, even though there are many grains) · *cheap*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general U → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Rice is cheap* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *A bag of rice is cheap.* · *Is rice expensive here?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Rices are cheap.* → ✅ *Rice is cheap.* · ❌ *The rice is cheap.* (means specific rice)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Rice is really cheap.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "RYS".
19. **Pattern:** **U food noun + is + cheap/expensive.**
20. **More examples:** *Bread is cheap. · Meat is expensive.*
21. **Quick check:** Count rice: *two ___ of rice*. → **Answer:** *two bags / kilos / bowls of rice*.

### EX-021-503 — "The waiter is busy."

1. **Meaning:** The person who serves food has a lot to do right now. 🧑‍🍳🏃
2. **Situation:** You wait a long time for the menu.
3. **Main lesson:** A restaurant **job** noun as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The waiter** (S) → **is** → **busy**.
6. **Subject:** *The waiter*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *busy*.
10. **Word by word:** *waiter* 👩 — *wait* + *-er* (female *waitress*; neutral *server*) · *busy*.
11. **How the rules work together:** S + *be* + adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We are looking for a friendly waiter* (EX-021-349).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct form.
14. **Other versions:** *The waiters are busy.* · *Is the waiter free?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Waiter is busy.* → ✅ *The waiter* · ❌ *The waiter busy.* → ✅ *is busy*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The waiter's run off his feet.* (= very busy, idiom)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WAY-ter".
19. **Pattern:** **The + job + is + busy.**
20. **More examples:** *The chef is busy. · The cashier is free.*
21. **Quick check:** Neutral word for *waiter/waitress*? → **Answer:** *server*.

### EX-021-504 — "Vegetables are healthy."

1. **Meaning:** Eating vegetables is good for your body. 🥦🥕
2. **Situation:** A parent tells a child to eat vegetables.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural C food noun in general.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Vegetables** (S) → **are** → **healthy**.
6. **Subject:** *Vegetables*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *healthy*.
10. **Word by word:** *vegetables* 📦 (C plural) · *healthy* — good for health (from *health* + *-y*).
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural, no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Apples are good for you* ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural + *are*.
14. **Other versions:** *Fruit is healthy.* (U → *is*) · *Are vegetables healthy?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Vegetables is healthy.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The vegetables are healthy.* (sounds specific)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Veg is good for you!* (*veg* — informal, often U)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HEL-thee".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural food noun + are + healthy.**
20. **More examples:** *Nuts are healthy. · Sweets are bad for your teeth.*
21. **Quick check:** *Fruit is* or *Fruit are*? → **Answer:** *Fruit is* (U).

### EX-021-505 — "The menu is in English."

1. **Meaning:** The list of food in the restaurant is written in English. 📋🇬🇧
2. **Situation:** A tourist is happy they can read it.
3. **Main lesson:** A restaurant thing noun + *in* + language (proper noun).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is in* + language.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The menu** (S) → **is** → **in English**.
6. **Subject:** *The menu*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in English*.
10. **Word by word:** *menu* 📦 — list of food and drinks · *in English* — written/spoken in the English language (capital **E**).
11. **How the rules work together:** *in* + language.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *How do you say "gato" in English?* ([L-017](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-017-greetings-and-classroom-english.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *Do you have a menu in English?* · *The menus are in French.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The menu is on English.* → ✅ *in English* · ❌ *…in english.* → ✅ *English*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Do you have an English menu?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **E**.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MEN-yoo".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is in + language.**
20. **More examples:** *The sign is in Spanish. · The book is in Arabic.*
21. **Quick check:** *in* or *on* English? → **Answer:** *in*.

### EX-021-506 — "Breakfast is the most important meal."

1. **Meaning:** Of all meals, breakfast is the most necessary. 🍳
2. **Situation:** A nutritionist's advice.
3. **Main lesson:** Meal noun (no article) + superlative + *meal*.
4. **Grammar used:** U meal noun + *is the most* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Breakfast** (S) → **is** → **the most important meal**.
6. **Subject:** *Breakfast*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the most important meal* — head **meal**.
10. **Word by word:** *Breakfast* (no article) · *the most important* — superlative (more than all others — [L-073](../../01-beginner-grammar/adjectives/lesson-073-superlative-adjectives.md)) · *meal* (C).
11. **How the rules work together:** superlatives need *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *the nearest hospital* (EX-021-011).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article use.
14. **Other versions:** *Dinner is my favourite meal.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Breakfast is most important meal.* → ✅ *the most important meal* · ❌ *The breakfast is…* (general) → ✅ *Breakfast*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They say breakfast is the most important meal of the day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "the MOHST im-POR-tunt MEEL".
19. **Pattern:** **Meal + is the most + adjective + meal.**
20. **More examples:** *Sleep is the most important thing. · Rome is the most beautiful city.*
21. **Quick check:** Article needed before *most important*? → **Answer:** yes: *the*.

### EX-021-507 — "The steak is delicious."

1. **Meaning:** The thick piece of meat tastes very good. 🥩😋
2. **Situation:** At a restaurant, eating.
3. **Main lesson:** A C food noun (one piece of meat).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The steak** (S) → **is** → **delicious**.
6. **Subject:** *The steak*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *delicious*.
10. **Word by word:** *steak* 📦 — a thick slice of meat (C: *a steak*) · ⚠️ *steak* sounds like *stake* ("STAYK").
11. **How the rules work together:** C singular with *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *meat* (U) vs *a steak* (C).

16. **Natural spoken English:** *This steak's amazing!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** ⚠️ "STAYK" (like *break*).
19. **Pattern:** **The + food noun + is + delicious.**
20. **More examples:** *The salad is fresh. · The burger is huge.*
21. **Quick check:** How do you say *steak*? → **Answer:** "STAYK".

### EX-021-510 — "The chef is French."

1. **Meaning:** The head cook comes from France. 👨‍🍳🇫🇷
2. **Situation:** Describing a restaurant.
3. **Main lesson:** Job noun + nationality adjective (capital).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + nationality.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The chef** (S) → **is** → **French**.
6. **Subject:** *The chef*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *French* — nationality adjective.
10. **Word by word:** *chef* — "SHEF" (the *ch* sounds like *sh* — a French word!) · *French* — capital **F**.
11. **How the rules work together:** nationality adjectives always take capitals.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My friend is Brazilian* ([L-022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md) bank).

14. **Other versions:** *The chef is from France.* · *The chefs are Italian.*

16. **Natural spoken English:** *The chef's French.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **F**.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHEF iz FRENCH".
19. **Pattern:** **The + job + is + nationality.**
20. **More examples:** *The waiter is Spanish. · The owner is Japanese.*
21. **Quick check:** *a French* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *French* (adjective) or *a French person*.

## Step B — Food nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-511 — "This cake is amazing. Did you make it?"

1. **Meaning:** This cake is fantastic; did you bake it yourself? 🎂
2. **Situation:** At a friend's party.
3. **Main lesson:** *this cake* → *it* in a past question.
4. **Grammar used:** *This* + noun + *is* + adj · *Did you make it?*
5. **Sentence structure:** **This cake** (S) → **is** → **amazing** · **Did** → **you** → **make** → **it** (O)?
6. **Subject:** *This cake* · *you*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *make*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Did*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *amazing* · *it* (= the cake).
10. **Word by word:** *cake* (C here — one whole cake) · *amazing* · *make* · *it*.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → *it*; past question with *did*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *These oranges are sweet. Where did you buy them?* (EX-021-465).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *These biscuits are amazing. Did you make them?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Did you made it?* → ✅ *make* · ❌ *Did you make him?* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This cake's incredible — did you bake it?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "did-yuh-MAYK-it" ↗.
19. **Pattern:** **This + noun + is + adj. Did you make it?**
20. **More examples:** *This soup is lovely. Did you cook it?*
21. **Quick check:** *make a cake* or *do a cake*? → **Answer:** *make* (or *bake*).

8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *friendly* · *three languages*.

12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our new receptionist… speaks four languages* (EX-021-361).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement.

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They speaks…* → ✅ *speak* · ❌ *…three language.* → ✅ *languages*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The staff are lovely — they speak three languages.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FREND-lee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural job + are + adj. They speak + number + languages.**
20. **More examples:** *The guides are helpful. They know the city well.*
21. **Quick check:** Singular version? → **Answer:** *The waiter is friendly. He/She speaks three languages.*

### EX-021-513 — "I love strawberries. I eat them every day in summer."

1. **Meaning:** I like strawberries very much; in summer I eat them daily. 🍓
2. **Situation:** Talking about favourite foods.
3. **Main lesson:** General plural (O) → *them*.
4. **Grammar used:** *I love* + plural · *I eat them* + time + *in* + season.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **love** → **strawberries** · **I** → **eat** → **them** → **every day** → **in summer**.
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *love* · *eat*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *strawberries* · *them* · time phrases.
10. **Word by word:** *strawberries* (y → ies) · *them* · *in summer* (small **s**).
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** consonant + y → ies ([L-024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct plural and pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *I love chocolate. I eat it every day.* (U → *it*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I love strawberry.* → ✅ *strawberries* (general) · ❌ *I eat it every day.* (for strawberries) → ✅ *them*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm addicted to strawberries!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** small **s** in *summer*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STRAW-buh-reez".
19. **Pattern:** **I love + plural. I eat them + time.**
20. **More examples:** *I love grapes. I eat them at lunch.*
21. **Quick check:** U food → which pronoun? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-514 — "My grandmother makes soup on Sundays. It's delicious."

1. **Meaning:** Every Sunday my grandmother cooks soup; it tastes very good.
2. **Situation:** Family traditions.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun (O) → *It*.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + verb-s + U noun + *on* + plural day · *It's* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandmother** → **makes** → **soup** (O) → **on Sundays** · **It** (S) → **'s** → **delicious**.
6. **Subject:** *My grandmother* · *It* (= the soup).
7. **Verb:** *makes* · *'s*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *soup* · *delicious*.
10. **Word by word:** *soup* (U) · *It* = the soup.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → *it* (singular).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Thank you for the advice. It was very useful.* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** U → *it*.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandmother makes cookies on Sundays. They're delicious.* (C → *they*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…They're delicious.* (for soup) → ✅ *It's* · ❌ *…makes a soup…* — possible (one pot/kind), but *soup* is more usual
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandma's Sunday soup is the best.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "its-di-LISH-us".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + makes + U food + on + days. It's + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad makes bread on Saturdays. It's wonderful.*
21. **Quick check:** *soup* → *it* or *they*? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-515 — "The children don't like carrots. They never eat them."

1. **Meaning:** The children don't enjoy carrots; they don't eat them at all. 🥕❌
2. **Situation:** A babysitter tells a parent.
3. **Main lesson:** Two plural nouns → *They* and *them* (different references!).
4. **Grammar used:** plural + *don't like* + plural · *They never eat them*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The children** → **don't like** → **carrots** · **They** → **never** → **eat** → **them**.
6. **Subject:** *The children* · *They* (= the children).
7. **Verb:** *like* · *eat*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *don't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *carrots* · *them* (= carrots).
10. **Word by word:** *children* · *carrots* · *never* — at no time (before the verb) · *They* = children; *them* = carrots.
11. **How the rules work together:** subject pronoun vs object pronoun show which plural noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *never* before the main verb ([L-051](../../01-beginner-grammar/adverbs/lesson-051-adverbs-of-frequency.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronouns.
14. **Other versions:** *My son doesn't like carrots. He never eats them.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They don't never eat them.* → ✅ *They never eat them.* (no double negative) · ❌ *They eat never them.* → ✅ *They never eat them.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids won't touch carrots!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KA-ruts".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural + don't like + plural. They never eat them.**
20. **More examples:** *The students don't like tests. They never enjoy them.*
21. **Quick check:** *They* = ? *them* = ? → **Answer:** *They* = the children; *them* = carrots.

## Step C — Questions and negatives

### EX-021-516 — "Can we have the menu, please?"

1. **Meaning:** Please bring us the list of food.

7. **Verb:** *Try* · *'s*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the fish* · *fantastic*.
10. **Word by word:** *Try* — taste for the first time · *the fish* — the fish dish here · *fantastic*.
11. **How the rules work together:** instruction + specific noun; then *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I'll have the chicken* (EX-021-541).
13. **Why this is correct:** natural recommendation.
14. **Other versions:** *Try the soup — it's delicious!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Try the fish — they're fantastic!* (one dish) → ✅ *it's* · ❌ *Try fish…* → ✅ *the fish* (this restaurant's)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *You've got to try the fish!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** a dash (—) for a pause; **!**
18. **Pronunciation:** "fan-TAS-tik".
19. **Pattern:** **Try the + dish — it's + adjective!**
20. **More examples:** *Try the cake — it's amazing!*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *the fish* (one dish)? → **Answer:** *it*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-549 — "Please salt the water before you add the pasta. / Pass the salt, please."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Put salt in the water before the pasta. (2) Please give me the salt.
2. **Situation:** Cooking together / eating dinner.
3. **Main lesson:** *salt* as a **verb** (1) and a **U noun** (2).
4. **Grammar used:** instruction + verb + *the* + noun + *before* + clause · instruction + *the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Please salt** (V) → **the water** (O) → **before you add the pasta** · (2) **Pass** → **the salt** (O) → **please**.
6. **Subject:** hidden *you* (both).
7. **Verb:** (1) *salt* · (2) *Pass*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the water* · *the salt*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *salt* after *Please* → verb (= put salt in) · (2) *salt* after *the* → noun · *before you add…* — a time part.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *water* verb/noun (EX-021-148).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Don't salt the soup too much.* · *There's too much salt in this.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please put salt to the water.* → ✅ *salt the water* / *put salt in the water* · ❌ *Pass me salt.* → ✅ *Pass (me) the salt*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Could you pass the salt?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SAWLT".
19. **Pattern:** **Please + verb + the + noun / Pass the + noun, please**
20. **More examples:** *Please butter the bread. / Where's the butter?*
21. **Quick check:** In *Pass the salt*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-550 — "I'll heat the soup. / I can't stand the heat in the kitchen."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I'll make the soup hot. (2) The high temperature in the kitchen is too much for me. 🔥
2. **Situation:** Cooking on a hot summer day.
3. **Main lesson:** *heat* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I'll* + verb + *the* + noun · *I can't stand the* + U noun + *in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **'ll heat** (V) → **the soup** (O) · (2) **I** → **can't stand** (V) → **the heat** (O) → **in the kitchen**.
6. **Subject:** *I* (both).
7. **Verb:** (1) *'ll heat* · (2) *can't stand* (= can't accept, really dislike).
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *'ll* (will) · (2) *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the soup* · *the heat* · *in the kitchen*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *heat* after *'ll* → verb · (2) *the heat* → noun (U: high temperature) · *can't stand* — strongly dislike.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The heating doesn't work* (EX-021-159) — related noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** each in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Heat the oven to 200°C.* · *The heat is terrible today.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I'll hot the soup.* → ✅ *heat* (*hot* is an adjective) · ❌ *I can't stand the heats.* → ✅ *the heat*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'll warm the soup up.* · *If you can't stand the heat, get out of the kitchen!* (famous saying)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HEET".
19. **Pattern:** **I'll + verb + the + noun / I can't stand the + U noun**
20. **More examples:** *I'll cool the drinks. / I love the cool of the evening.*
21. **Quick check:** *I'll hot the soup* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *heat* (verb).
