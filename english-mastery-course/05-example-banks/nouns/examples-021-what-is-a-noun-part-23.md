**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Animal nouns to watch:** irregular plurals *mouse → mice, goose → geese, sheep → sheep, fish → fish, deer → deer* ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)). Pronouns: unknown/wild animal → **it**; your own pet → often **he / she**. General statements → plural, no article (*Elephants are huge*). Baby animals: *puppy, kitten, calf, lamb, chick*.

**New words in this part:** elephant · puppy · mouse/mice · sheep · lion · jungle · cow · parrot · spider · hamster · goose/geese · rabbit · zookeeper · duck · giraffe · insect · snake · bear · chicken · dolphin · vet · farm · fur · stripe · kitten · owl · stable · barn · tank · peacock · tail · pack · whale · stroke · sheepdog · reward · collar · scratch.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-1101 — "Elephants are huge."

1. **Meaning:** Elephants are very, very big animals. 🐘
2. **Situation:** A child at the zoo.
3. **Main lesson:** A general plural animal noun → no article.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Elephants** (S) → **are** → **huge**.
6. **Subject:** *Elephants* (all elephants).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *huge*.
10. **Word by word:** *elephants* 🐾 — *elephant* + *s* · *huge* — very big.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → no *the*; plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Dogs bark* (EX-021-013) — the first general animal plural.
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *An elephant is huge.* · *The elephants at this zoo are huge.* (specific)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The elephants are huge.* (for all elephants) → ✅ *Elephants* · ❌ *Elephants is huge.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Elephants are massive!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "EL-uh-funts" (*ph* = /f/).
19. **Pattern:** **Plural animal + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Giraffes are tall. · Mice are small.*
21. **Quick check:** How is *ph* pronounced in *elephant*? → **Answer:** /f/.

3. **Main lesson:** a baby-animal noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is very* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The puppy** (S) → **is** → **very playful**.

10. **Word by word:** *puppy* — baby dog (plural *puppies*) · *playful* — *play* + *-ful*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The baby is asleep* (EX-021-020).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.

17. **Writing & punctuation:** *-ful* has one *l*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PUP-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + baby animal + is very + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The kitten is very curious.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *puppy*? → **Answer:** *puppies*.

### EX-021-1104 — "Sheep eat grass."

1. **Meaning:** Sheep feed on grass. 🐑🌱
2. **Situation:** On a farm visit.
3. **Main lesson:** *sheep* — same form in singular and plural; U object.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun (no *-s*) + verb + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Sheep** (S) → **eat** (V) → **grass** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Sheep* (plural here).
7. **Verb:** *eat* (no *-s* — plural).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *grass*.
10. **Word by word:** *sheep* — one sheep, two sheep · *eat* · *grass* — U.
11. **How the rules work together:** we know *sheep* is plural here because the verb is *eat*, not *eats*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *In winter, fish live under the ice* (EX-021-1036) — *fish* works the same way.
13. **Why this is correct:** general plural + U object.
14. **Other versions:** *A sheep eats grass.* (one → *eats*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Sheeps eat grass.* → ✅ *Sheep* · ❌ *Sheep eat grasses.* → ✅ *grass*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Sheep just eat grass all day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHEEP".
19. **Pattern:** **Same-form plural + verb + U noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Deer eat leaves. · Fish eat insects.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *sheep*? → **Answer:** *sheep*.

### EX-021-1105 — "The lion is the king of the jungle."

1. **Meaning:** People call the lion the strongest, most important wild animal. 🦁👑
2. **Situation:** A children's story.
3. **Main lesson:** *the* + singular animal = the whole species; noun phrase after *is*.
4. **Grammar used:** *The* + noun + *is the* + noun + *of the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The lion** (S) → **is** → **the king of the jungle** (B).
6. **Subject:** *The lion* (= lions as a type).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the king of the jungle*.
10. **Word by word:** *the lion* — the species · *king* · *of the jungle* — thick tropical forest.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the* + singular can mean "this type of animal" (formal/scientific).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Elephants are huge* (EX-021-1101) — the everyday way: plural, no article.
13. **Why this is correct:** species use of *the*.
14. **Other versions:** *Lions are the kings of the jungle.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Lion is the king…* → ✅ *The lion* · ❌ *…the king of jungle.* → ✅ *of the jungle*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They call the lion the king of the jungle.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LY-un" · "JUNG-gul".
19. **Pattern:** **The + animal + is the + noun + of the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The eagle is the king of the birds.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *the lion* mean here? → **Answer:** lions as a type (the species).

### EX-021-1106 — "Cows give us milk."

1. **Meaning:** We get milk from cows. 🐄🥛
2. **Situation:** A farm lesson for children.
3. **Main lesson:** general plural subject + two objects (*us* + *milk*).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + verb + *us* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Cows** (S) → **give** (V) → **us** (O1) → **milk** (O2).
6. **Subject:** *Cows*.
7. **Verb:** *give*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *us* (who receives) · *milk* (what).
10. **Word by word:** *cows* · *give* · *us* · *milk* — U.
11. **How the rules work together:** *give* + person + thing.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Bees make honey* (EX-021-029).
13. **Why this is correct:** two-object pattern.
14. **Other versions:** *Chickens give us eggs.* · *Sheep give us wool.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Cows give to us milk.* → ✅ *give us milk* / *give milk to us* · ❌ *Cows gives…* → ✅ *give*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Milk comes from cows.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KOWZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural animal + give us + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Bees give us honey.*
21. **Quick check:** *give to us milk* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *give us milk*.

## Step B — Animal nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-1111 — "We have a rabbit. We feed it carrots every day."

1. **Meaning:** We have a pet rabbit, and we give it carrots daily. 🐰🥕
2. **Situation:** Talking about pets.
3. **Main lesson:** *a rabbit* → *it*; *feed* + animal + food.
4. **Grammar used:** *We have a* + noun · *We feed it* + plural noun + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** → **have** (V) → **a rabbit** (O) · **We** → **feed** (V) → **it** (O1) → **carrots** (O2) → **every day**.
6. **Subject:** *We* (both).
7. **Verb:** *have* · *feed*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a rabbit* · *it* · *carrots* · *every day*.
10. **Word by word:** *rabbit* · *feed* — give food to · *it* — the rabbit · *carrots*.
11. **How the rules work together:** animal (not named) → *it*; *feed* + who + what.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The children … feed the goldfish* (EX-021-194).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + two objects.
14. **Other versions:** *We have a rabbit called Snowy. We feed her carrots.* (named pet → *her*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *We feed carrots it.* → ✅ *feed it carrots* · ❌ *We feed it carrot.* → ✅ *carrots*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We've got a rabbit — it eats carrots every day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "RAB-it".
19. **Pattern:** **We have a + animal. We feed it + food + time.**
20. **More examples:** *We have a dog. We walk him twice a day.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for an unnamed animal? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-1112 — "The zookeeper is very brave. She feeds the lions."

1. **Meaning:** The woman who looks after the zoo animals is brave; she gives food to the lions. 👩‍🌾🦁
2. **Situation:** A school trip to the zoo.
3. **Main lesson:** compound person noun → *she*; plural animal object.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is very* + adj · *She* + verb-s + *the* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The zookeeper** (S) → **is** → **very brave** · **She** (S) → **feeds** (V) → **the lions** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The zookeeper* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *feeds*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very brave* · *the lions*.
10. **Word by word:** *zookeeper* — *zoo* + *keeper* · *brave* · *feeds* · *the lions* — the specific lions at this zoo.
11. **How the rules work together:** woman → *she*; specific animals → *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Firefighters are brave* (EX-021-751).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *The zookeepers are very brave. They feed the tigers.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She feed the lions.* → ✅ *feeds* · ❌ *…feeds lions.* (specific → *the lions*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's the one who feeds the lions!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *zookeeper* — one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ZOO-kee-per".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person + is very + adj. She + verb-s + the + plural animals.**
20. **More examples:** *The farmer is very busy. He milks the cows.*
21. **Quick check:** *She feed* or *She feeds*? → **Answer:** *She feeds*.

### EX-021-1113 — "My dog is old, but he still loves walks."

1. **Meaning:** My dog isn't young, but he still enjoys going for walks. 🐕🦮
2. **Situation:** Talking to a neighbour in the park.
3. **Main lesson:** pet → *he/she* (not only *it*); *walks* as a plural noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is* + adj, *but he still* + verb-s + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My dog** (S) → **is** → **old** · **but** · **he** (S) → **still** → **loves** (V) → **walks** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My dog* · *he*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *loves*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *old* · *walks*.
10. **Word by word:** *he* — owners often use *he/she* for pets · *still* · *walks* — noun (plural).
11. **How the rules work together:** your own pet → *he/she* is natural; *it* is also correct.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Let's walk to the square. / It's a short walk* (EX-021-749).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + contrast.
14. **Other versions:** *My cat is old, but she still loves playing.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…but he still love walks.* → ✅ *loves* · ❌ *…but he still loves walk.* → ✅ *walks*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *He's an old boy, but he still loves his walks.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *but*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WAWKS" (silent *l*).
19. **Pattern:** **My + pet + is + adj, but he/she still loves + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My horse is old, but she still loves apples.*
21. **Quick check:** Can you use *he/she* for your pet? → **Answer:** Yes.

### EX-021-1115 — "I found a little bird. It can't fly."

1. **Meaning:** I found a small bird, and it isn't able to fly. 🐦🩹
2. **Situation:** A child brings a bird home.
3. **Main lesson:** *a little bird* (first mention) → *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + past verb + *a* + adj + noun · *It can't* + verb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **found** (V) → **a little bird** (O) · **It** (S) → **can't fly** (V).
6. **Subject:** *I* · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *found* · *fly*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a little bird*.
10. **Word by word:** *found* — past of *find* · *a little bird* · *It* — unknown animal → *it* · *can't fly*.
11. **How the rules work together:** wild/unknown animal → *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My dog is old, but he still loves walks* (EX-021-1113) — own pet → *he*.

14. **Other versions:** *I found two little birds. They can't fly.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I finded…* → ✅ *found* · ❌ *It can't flies.* → ✅ *fly*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I found this little bird — I think its wing is hurt.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FOWND".
19. **Pattern:** **I found a + adj + animal. It can't + verb.**
20. **More examples:** *I saw a big spider. It was black.*
21. **Quick check:** Past of *find*? → **Answer:** *found*.

## Step C — Questions and negatives

### EX-021-1116 — "Do you have any pets?"

1. **Meaning:** Do you keep any animals at home? 🐶🐱❓
2. **Situation:** Getting to know someone.
3. **Main lesson:** *any* + plural noun in a question.

5. **Sentence structure:** **If you see her,** (condition) → **(you)** → **please call** (V) → **this number** (O).
6. **Subject:** *you* (condition) · hidden *you* (main).
7. **Verb:** *see* · *call*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *her* · *this number*.
10. **Word by word:** *If* — condition · *her* — the cat (named pet) · *please call* · *this number*.
11. **How the rules work together:** named pet → *her*; *this* + singular.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: Is that your dog? B: Yes, his name is Max* (EX-021-1141).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + instruction.
14. **Other versions:** *If you find him, please contact us.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *If you see she…* → ✅ *her* · ❌ *…please call to this number.* → ✅ *call this number*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *If you spot her, give us a ring!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the *if* clause when it comes first.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NUM-ber".
19. **Pattern:** **If you see her, please call this + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *If you see him, please tell us.*
21. **Quick check:** *call to this number* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *call this number*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-1149 — "I can't bear the noise. / We saw a bear in the forest."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I can't stand the noise (it's too much). (2) We saw a big wild animal — a bear — in the forest. 😣 / 🐻
2. **Situation:** (1) Noisy neighbours; (2) a camping trip.
3. **Main lesson:** *bear* as a verb (1: tolerate) and a C noun (2: animal).
4. **Grammar used:** *I can't* + verb + *the* + noun · *We saw a* + noun + *in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **can't bear** (V) → **the noise** (O) · (2) **We** → **saw** (V) → **a bear** (O) → **in the forest** (P).
6. **Subject:** *I* · *We*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *bear* · (2) *saw*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the noise* · *a bear* · *in the forest*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *bear* after *can't* → verb (tolerate) · (2) *a bear* — after *a* → noun (animal).
11. **How the rules work together:** after a modal → verb; after *a* → noun — even with a totally different meaning.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We fly to Rome tomorrow. / There's a fly in my soup!* (EX-021-700).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *She can't bear spiders.* (verb) · *Bears sleep in winter.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I can't bearing the noise.* → ✅ *bear* · ❌ *We saw bear…* → ✅ *a bear*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I can't stand that noise!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BAIR" (noun and verb the same; also same as *bare*!).
19. **Pattern:** **I can't + verb + the + noun / We saw a + noun + in the + place**
20. **More examples:** *I can't stand the heat. / Please take a stand.*
21. **Quick check:** In *I can't bear the noise*, is *bear* a noun or a verb? → **Answer:** verb.

### EX-021-1150 — "The cat scratched me. / I have a scratch on my arm."

1. **Meaning:** (1) The cat cut my skin with its claws. (2) There's a thin cut on my arm. 🐈💢 / 💪🩹
2. **Situation:** Playing with a cat.
3. **Main lesson:** *scratch* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *The* + noun + past verb + *me* · *I have a* + noun + *on my* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **The cat** (S) → **scratched** (V) → **me** (O) · (2) **I** (S) → **have** (V) → **a scratch** (O) → **on my arm** (P).
6. **Subject:** *The cat* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *scratched* · (2) *have*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *me* · *a scratch* · *on my arm*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *scratched* — past verb · (2) *a scratch* — after *a* → noun (plural *scratches*).
11. **How the rules work together:** after a subject → verb; after *a* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *There's a cut on my finger* (EX-021-833).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Don't scratch the car!* (verb) · *There are scratches on the table.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The cat scratch me yesterday.* → ✅ *scratched* · ❌ *I have scratch on my arm.* → ✅ *a scratch*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Ouch, the cat got me!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences; plural *scratches* (*-ch* → *-es*).
18. **Pronunciation:** "SKRACH" · "SKRACHT".
19. **Pattern:** **The + animal + past verb + me / I have a + noun + on my + body part**
20. **More examples:** *The dog bit me. / I have a bite on my leg.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a scratch on my arm*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.
