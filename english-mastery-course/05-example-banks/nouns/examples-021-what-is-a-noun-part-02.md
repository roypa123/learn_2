**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** = the main noun in a group of words; the verb agrees with it ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

## Step D (end) — Questions and negatives

### EX-021-051 — "What is your favourite food?"

1. **Meaning:** Which food do you like more than all other foods?
2. **Situation:** Getting to know a new classmate.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun as the subject of a wh-question, with an adjective before it.
4. **Grammar used:** wh-question with *be* · possessive *your* · adjective + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **What** (question word) → **is** (verb) → **your favourite food** (subject) → **?**
6. **Subject:** *your favourite food* — head noun **food**. (In this question, *What* asks for the information; *your favourite food* is what we talk about.)
7. **Verb:** *is* — *be*, present, singular.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *What* stands for the answer (e.g., *pizza*).
10. **Word by word:** *What* — question word · *your* — belongs to you · *favourite* — adjective: the one you like most (US: *favorite*) · *food* — noun 📦, here uncountable/general.
11. **How the rules work together:** wh-word + *is* + noun phrase; adjective before the head noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *What's your name?* ([L-003](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-003-the-english-alphabet.md)); adjectives go before nouns ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** normal wh-question order with *be*.
14. **Other versions:** *What are your favourite foods?* (plural) · *My favourite food is rice.* · *What's your favourite colour?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *What your favourite food is?* → ✅ *What is your favourite food?* · ❌ *Which is your food favourite?* → ✅ *your favourite food*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *What's your favourite food?* — Answer: *Pizza!* / *I love sushi.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?** at the end; UK *favourite* / US *favorite* — both correct.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FAY-vrit" (2 syllables in fast speech). Voice goes **down** ↘ (wh-question).
19. **Pattern:** **What is your favourite + noun?**
20. **More examples:** *What is your favourite film? · What is your favourite city? · What is your favourite sport?*
21. **Quick check:** Answer the question about yourself. → **Answer (example):** *My favourite food is pasta.*

### EX-021-052 — "My phone doesn't have a camera."

1. **Meaning:** There is no camera in my phone. 📱❌📷
2. **Situation:** A friend asks you to take a photo; your old phone can't.
3. **Main lesson:** Nouns as subject and object in a negative sentence.
4. **Grammar used:** noun subject + *doesn't have* + *a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My phone** (S) → **doesn't have** (V) → **a camera** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My phone* (head noun *phone*).
7. **Verb:** *have* — base form after *doesn't*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *doesn't* = does not (for he/she/it — *my phone* = it).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a camera* = object.
10. **Word by word:** *phone* — noun 📦 · *doesn't* — negative helper · *have* — own/contain · *a* — one · *camera* — noun 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *doesn't*; after it, *have* (not *has*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My sister doesn't like coffee* (EX-021-010).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct negative helper and verb form.
14. **Other versions:** *My phone has a camera.* · *Does your phone have a camera?* · *Old phones don't have cameras.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My phone doesn't has a camera.* → ✅ *have* · ❌ *My phone don't have…* → ✅ *doesn't* · ❌ *…have camera.* → ✅ *a camera*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My phone hasn't got a camera.* (UK)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe in *doesn't*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CAM-ruh" (often 2 syllables).
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + doesn't have + a/an + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My car doesn't have a radio. · The room doesn't have a window.*
21. **Quick check:** Make it positive. → **Answer:** *My phone has a camera.*

## Step E — Nouns with different times

> 🔁 Nouns **don't change** for time — only the verb does ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)). Past *was/were* and *-ed*, future *will* are first looks here.

### EX-021-053 — "The weather was bad yesterday."

1. **Meaning:** Yesterday it rained or was cold — the weather was not nice. 🌧️
2. **Situation:** Explaining why you stayed at home yesterday.
3. **Main lesson:** An uncountable noun subject in the past.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *was* + adjective + time word.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The weather** (S) → **was** (V) → **bad** (adjective) → **yesterday** (time).
6. **Subject:** *The weather*.
7. **Verb:** *was* — past of *is* ([L-061](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-061-was-were.md)).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *bad* = complement · *yesterday* = time.
10. **Word by word:** *weather* — noun 💭, uncountable (no *a*, no -s) · *was* · *bad* · *yesterday* — the day before today.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → singular verb (*was*, not *were*); time word at the end.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *It is cold* (L-011); U nouns ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** singular past verb for an uncountable noun.
14. **Other versions:** *The weather is bad today.* · *The weather will be good tomorrow.* · *Was the weather good?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A bad weather* → ✅ *bad weather* · ❌ *The weather were bad.* → ✅ *was* · ❌ *The weathers…* → ✅ *weather*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The weather was awful yesterday!* (*awful* = very bad)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *weather* ≠ *whether* (= if) — same sound!
18. **Pronunciation:** "WETH-er" (/ð/ — tongue between teeth).
19. **Pattern:** **The weather + was + adjective + time.**
20. **More examples:** *The food was great last night. · The traffic was terrible this morning.*
21. **Quick check:** *weather* or *whether*: "The ___ is nice." → **Answer:** *weather*.

### EX-021-054 — "The students will have a test tomorrow."

1. **Meaning:** Tomorrow, the students must do an exam. 📝
2. **Situation:** A teacher reminds the class.
3. **Main lesson:** Nouns don't change in the **future**.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *will* + verb + *a* + noun + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The students** (S) → **will have** (V) → **a test** (O) → **tomorrow**.
6. **Subject:** *The students*.
7. **Verb:** *will have* — future.
8. **Helping verbs:** *will* — shows the future; the same for all subjects ([L-077](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-077-future-will.md)).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a test* = object · *tomorrow* = time.
10. **Word by word:** *students* — noun 👩, plural · *will* · *have* (base form) · *test* — noun 💭 (event) · *tomorrow*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *will* + base verb; nouns stay the same.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** S-V-O-Time ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct future form and order.
14. **Other versions:** *The students have a test today.* · *Will the students have a test?* · *The students won't have a test.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The students will has a test.* → ✅ *have* · ❌ *…will have test.* → ✅ *a test*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We've got a test tomorrow!* (student speaking)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** *will* often → *'ll*: "the STOO-dents'll have a TEST".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + will + verb + noun + time.**
20. **More examples:** *The workers will finish the job tomorrow. · My parents will visit us next week.*
21. **Quick check:** Which word shows the future? → **Answer:** *will*.

### EX-021-055 — "My grandmother cooked rice and fish."

1. **Meaning:** My grandmother made rice and fish (in the past). 🍚🐟
2. **Situation:** Describing a family dinner last weekend.
3. **Main lesson:** Two object nouns joined by *and*, past tense.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + past verb (-ed) + U noun + *and* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandmother** (S) → **cooked** (V) → **rice and fish** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My grandmother*.
7. **Verb:** *cooked* — past simple (*cook* + -ed) ([L-062](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-062-past-simple-regular-verbs.md)).
8. **Helping verbs:** none (positive past simple).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *rice and fish* = two objects.
10. **Word by word:** *grandmother* 👩 · *cooked* · *rice* 📦 (U) · *and* · *fish* 📦 (U as food).
11. **How the rules work together:** food nouns used as U → no articles.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *fish* as food is U ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md), [L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct past form, no articles with U food.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandmother cooks rice every day.* · *Did your grandmother cook fish?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…cooked a rice and a fish.* → ✅ *rice and fish* · ❌ *My grandmother cook rice yesterday.* → ✅ *cooked*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandma made rice and fish.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma for only two items with *and*.
18. **Pronunciation:** *cooked* = "kukt" (-ed = /t/).
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + verb-ed + noun and noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My father painted the door and the window. · We visited Rome and Venice.*
21. **Quick check:** How many object nouns? → **Answer:** two (*rice, fish*).

### EX-021-056 — "Every day, the train is full."

1. **Meaning:** Each day, the train has no free seats. 🚆
2. **Situation:** A commuter complains.
3. **Main lesson:** A time noun phrase at the front; a noun subject.
4. **Grammar used:** time phrase + comma + *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Every day,** (time) → **the train** (S) → **is** → **full**.
6. **Subject:** *the train*.
7. **Verb:** *is* — present (a habit/fact).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *full* = adjective · *Every day* = time.
10. **Word by word:** *Every* + singular noun *day* 💭 · *train* 📦 · *full* — no space left.
11. **How the rules work together:** time can move to the front; *every* + singular.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** time at the front + comma ([L-014](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-014-why-word-order-matters.md)); *every* + singular ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#76-singular-with-every--each--one-of)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct structure and agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *The train is full every day.* · *The trains are full every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Every days, the train…* → ✅ *Every day* · ❌ *Everyday, the train…* → ✅ *Every day* (two words = each day)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The train's always packed.* (*packed* = very full)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the time phrase at the front.
18. **Pronunciation:** small pause at the comma.
19. **Pattern:** **Every + time noun, + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Every morning, the café is busy. · Every summer, the beach is crowded.*
21. **Quick check:** *every day* or *everyday*? "I walk ___." → **Answer:** *every day*.

### EX-021-057 — "The concert was amazing!"

1. **Meaning:** The music show was very, very good! 🎤🎸
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend about last night.
3. **Main lesson:** An **event** noun as the subject, past tense.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *was* + adjective + **!**
5. **Sentence structure:** **The concert** (S) → **was** → **amazing** → **!**
6. **Subject:** *The concert*.
7. **Verb:** *was*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *amazing* = adjective.
10. **Word by word:** *concert* — noun 💭 (event: live music) · *amazing* — extremely good.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *was*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** events are nouns: *party, match, meeting* ([L-021 §7.1](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#71-the-5-groups-of-nouns)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct past *be*.
14. **Other versions:** *The concerts were amazing.* · *Was the concert good?* · *The concert wasn't very good.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The concert were amazing.* → ✅ *was* · ❌ *The concert amazing!* → ✅ *was amazing*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The concert was awesome!* (US informal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **!** for strong feeling.
18. **Pronunciation:** "a-MAY-zing" — stress the middle.
19. **Pattern:** **The + event + was + adjective!**
20. **More examples:** *The party was fun! · The match was exciting! · The film was boring.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *concert* a thing you can touch? → **Answer:** No — it's an event (idea noun).

### EX-021-058 — "Next year, my sister will start university."

1. **Meaning:** In the coming year, my sister will begin studying at university. 🎓
2. **Situation:** Family news.
3. **Main lesson:** A place/institution noun used as an object **without an article**.
4. **Grammar used:** time phrase + noun + *will* + verb + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Next year,** (time) → **my sister** (S) → **will start** (V) → **university** (O).
6. **Subject:** *my sister*.
7. **Verb:** *will start* — future.
8. **Helping verbs:** *will*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *university* = object.
10. **Word by word:** *year* 💭 · *sister* 👩 · *start* · *university* 📍 — used for the **activity** (studying there) → no article, like *go to school*, *start school*.
11. **How the rules work together:** institution as activity → no article ([L-034](../../01-beginner-grammar/articles-and-determiners/lesson-034-zero-article.md)).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *a university* — "y" sound → *a* not *an* ([L-005](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-005-vowels-and-consonants.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** *start university* = begin studies (no article).
14. **Other versions:** *She will go to university in London.* · *She is at university now.* · *She studies at **a** big university.* (a specific building/place → article)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…will start the university.* → ✅ *start university* · ❌ *…will starts…* → ✅ *will start*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's starting uni next year.* (UK *uni* = university)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *Next year*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "yoo-ni-VUR-si-tee".
19. **Pattern:** **Next year, + person + will start + school/university/work.**
20. **More examples:** *Next month, my brother will start work. · Next week, the children will start school.*
21. **Quick check:** Article or not: "She goes to ___ university." → **Answer:** no article.

### EX-021-059 — "The children played in the snow."

1. **Meaning:** The children had fun outside in the snow (in the past). ⛄
2. **Situation:** A parent describes a winter day.
3. **Main lesson:** An uncountable noun after a preposition, past tense.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + past verb + *in the* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The children** (S) → **played** (V) → **in the snow** (place).
6. **Subject:** *The children*.
7. **Verb:** *played* — past (*play* + -ed).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in the snow* — place; *snow* = position P.
10. **Word by word:** *children* 👩 (irregular plural) · *played* · *in* · *the snow* 📦 (U; *the* = the snow on that day).
11. **How the rules work together:** past -ed is the same for all subjects; U noun → no -s.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Children play* (EX-021-016); *-ed* past ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct past form and preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *The children play in the snow every winter.* · *Did the children play in the snow?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…in the snows.* → ✅ *snow* · ❌ *…on the snow.* → usually *in the snow*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids played in the snow all day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** *played* = "playd" (-ed = /d/).
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + verb-ed + in the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The dog ran in the rain. · We walked in the park.*
21. **Quick check:** Countable or uncountable: *snow*? → **Answer:** uncountable.

### EX-021-060 — "Our flight is at seven, but the taxi is late."

1. **Meaning:** Our plane leaves at 7:00, and the taxi to the airport hasn't arrived yet. ✈️🚕
2. **Situation:** Waiting outside your house with suitcases, worried.
3. **Main lesson:** Two subject nouns in two parts joined by *but*.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + *is* + time · *but* · noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our flight** (S1) → **is** → **at seven** · **, but** · **the taxi** (S2) → **is** → **late**.
6. **Subject:** *Our flight* · *the taxi*.
7. **Verb:** *is* (×2) — present for a timetable and a situation now.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at seven* (time) · *late* (adjective).
10. **Word by word:** *flight* — noun 💭 (a plane journey) · *at* + clock time · *but* — contrast · *taxi* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** each part has its own subject + verb; *but* joins them.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *but* ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)); *at* + time ([L-050](../../01-beginner-grammar/prepositions/lesson-050-prepositions-of-time-at-on-in.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** two complete clauses joined correctly.
14. **Other versions:** *Our flight is at seven, and the taxi is here.* · *Is our flight at seven?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Our flight is at seven, the taxi is late.* → ✅ *…, but the taxi…* (comma alone can't join — [L-010](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)) · ❌ *…in seven* → ✅ *at seven*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Our flight's at seven and the taxi's late!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *but*.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress the contrast: *…but the TAXi is LATE.*
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + is + …, but + noun + is + …**
20. **More examples:** *The film is at eight, but the cinema is closed. · The food is ready, but the guests are late.*
21. **Quick check:** What joins the two parts? → **Answer:** *but*.

## Step F — Nouns with adjectives

> 🔁 Adjective **before** the noun; adjectives never take -s ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).

### EX-021-061 — "She has a small house with a big garden."

1. **Meaning:** Her house is not big, but the garden is large. 🏡🌳
2. **Situation:** Describing a friend's home.
3. **Main lesson:** Two adjective + noun pairs.
4. **Grammar used:** *She has a* + adj + noun + *with a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **She** (S) → **has** (V) → **a small house** (O) → **with a big garden** (extra).
6. **Subject:** *She*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a small house* = object; *with a big garden* = describes the house.
10. **Word by word:** *small* + *house* 📍 · *with* — having · *big* + *garden* 📍.
11. **How the rules work together:** each noun gets its own article and adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The old man with the black dog* (EX-021-007).
13. **Why this is correct:** adjectives before nouns, correct articles.
14. **Other versions:** *They have small houses with big gardens.* · *Does she have a big garden?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *a house small* → ✅ *a small house* · ❌ *with big garden* → ✅ *with a big garden*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's got a little house with a huge garden.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no commas needed.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress the adjectives: *a SMALL house with a BIG garden*.
19. **Pattern:** **a + adjective + noun + with a + adjective + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *a big room with a small window · a quiet street with old trees*
21. **Quick check:** Find the two adjectives. → **Answer:** *small, big*.

### EX-021-062 — "Fresh bread smells wonderful."

1. **Meaning:** Bread that was just made has a very nice smell. 🍞
2. **Situation:** Walking past a bakery in the morning.
3. **Main lesson:** Adjective + uncountable noun as the subject (no article).
4. **Grammar used:** adjective + U noun + verb-s + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Fresh bread** (S) → **smells** (V) → **wonderful** (adjective).
6. **Subject:** *Fresh bread* (head noun *bread*).
7. **Verb:** *smells* — a linking verb here (like *is*): it joins the subject and the adjective ([L-095](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-095-linking-verbs.md)).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *wonderful* = adjective complement (not an adverb: ❌ *wonderfully*).
10. **Word by word:** *Fresh* — just made · *bread* 📦 (U) · *smells* · *wonderful* — very good.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun = singular → *smells*; general → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Water is life* (EX-021-014) — U noun as subject.
13. **Why this is correct:** linking verb + adjective; correct agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *The bread smells wonderful.* (specific bread) · *Does the bread smell good?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A fresh bread smells…* → ✅ *Fresh bread* · ❌ *…smells wonderfully.* → ✅ *wonderful*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Mmm, that bread smells amazing!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FRESH BRED smelz WUN-der-ful".
19. **Pattern:** **Adjective + U noun + smells/tastes/looks + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Hot coffee smells good. · Fresh fruit tastes sweet.*
21. **Quick check:** Why no *a* before *fresh bread*? → **Answer:** *bread* is uncountable.

### EX-021-063 — "I want a quiet room, please."

1. **Meaning:** I'd like a room with no noise.
2. **Situation:** At a hotel reception desk.
3. **Main lesson:** Adjective + countable noun as the object.
4. **Grammar used:** *I want a* + adjective + noun + *please*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **want** (V) → **a quiet room** (O) → **, please**.
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *want*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a quiet room* = object.
10. **Word by word:** *quiet* — not noisy · *room* 📍.

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a warm coat* (O) · *for the winter* — purpose/time.

19. **Pattern:** **I bought a/an + adjective + noun + for the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *I bought new boots for the trip. · She bought a big bag for the holiday.*
21. **Quick check:** Past of *buy*? → **Answer:** *bought*.

### EX-021-080 — "A cup of tea and a piece of cake cost five pounds."

1. **Meaning:** Together, one cup of tea and one slice of cake cost £5. ☕🍰
2. **Situation:** Reading a café menu.
3. **Main lesson:** A subject with **two** head nouns joined by *and*.
4. **Grammar used:** unit + *of* + U noun (×2) + *and* + verb + price.
5. **Sentence structure:** **A cup of tea and a piece of cake** (S) → **cost** (V) → **five pounds** (O).
6. **Subject:** two heads: **cup** + **piece** → plural.
7. **Verb:** *cost* — plural (no -s). (Some people say *costs*, thinking of "one deal" — both are heard.)
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *five pounds* — the price.
10. **Word by word:** *cup of tea* · *piece of cake* — units for U nouns ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md#73-how-to-count-uncountable-things-containers-and-pieces)) · *pounds* — £.
11. **How the rules work together:** X + Y = plural → *cost*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Tom and I are…* — two subjects → plural ([L-011](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-011-the-subject.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** two heads → plural verb.
14. **Other versions:** *A cup of tea costs two pounds.* (one head → *costs*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A tea of cup…* → ✅ *A cup of tea* · ❌ *…cost five pound.* → ✅ *pounds*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's a fiver for tea and cake.* (*a fiver* = £5, UK informal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** £5 (sign first, said last).
18. **Pronunciation:** *cup of tea* → "CUP-uh-TEE".
19. **Pattern:** **A + unit + of + U noun and a + unit + of + U noun + cost + price.**
20. **More examples:** *A bowl of soup and a glass of water cost six euros.*
21. **Quick check:** Why *cost* and not *costs*? → **Answer:** two things (two heads) → plural.

14. **Other versions (full sentence):** *It's a present for my sister.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Present for my sister.* (missing *a* — sounds like a label) → ✅ *A present for my sister.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Oh, just a present for my sister — it's her birthday tomorrow.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** in formal writing, use full sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** noun ***PRES**ent* (gift) vs verb *pre**SENT*** (show).
19. **Pattern:** **A + noun + for + my + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *A card for my teacher. · Some flowers for my mum.*
21. **Quick check:** Another word for *present* (gift)? → **Answer:** *gift*.

## Step J — Nouns in a short story

> *(1) Sam is a **baker**. (2) Every **morning**, he makes **bread**, **cakes** and **cookies** in his small **shop**. (3) The **smell** of fresh **bread** brings many **customers** to his **door**.*

### EX-021-091 — "Sam is a baker."

1. **Meaning:** Sam's job is making bread. 🧑‍🍳🍞
2. **Situation:** The first line of a short story.
3. **Main lesson:** Proper noun subject + job noun after *be*.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is a* + job.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Sam** (S) → **is** → **a baker**.
6. **Subject:** *Sam*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a baker* — complement.
10. **Word by word:** *Sam* — proper noun · *baker* 👩 — *bake* + *-er* (person who bakes).
11. **How the rules work together:** jobs need *a/an*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *-er* for people: *teacher, farmer* (EX-021-021, 023).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + *a* + job.
14. **Other versions:** *Sam and Ali are bakers.* · *Is Sam a baker?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Sam is baker.* → ✅ *a baker*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Sam's a baker.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **S**.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BAY-ker".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + is a/an + job.**
20. **More examples:** *Lea is a dentist. · Omar is an artist.*
21. **Quick check:** What verb is *baker* made from? → **Answer:** *bake*.

---

### EX-021-092 — "Every morning, he makes bread, cakes and cookies in his small shop."

1. **Meaning:** Each morning, Sam makes bread, cakes and cookies in his little shop.
2. **Situation:** The story describes his routine.
3. **Main lesson:** A list of object nouns — U and C together.
4. **Grammar used:** time + *he* + verb-s + list + *in his* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Every morning,** → **he** (S) → **makes** (V) → **bread, cakes and cookies** (O) → **in his small shop**.
6. **Subject:** *he* (= Sam).
7. **Verb:** *makes* (-s for *he*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** a list of three objects · place phrase.
10. **Word by word:** *bread* (U → no -s) · *cakes, cookies* (C plural) · *shop* 📍.
11. **How the rules work together:** U and C nouns can be in one list; each keeps its own form.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *bread* U ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)); *every* + singular (EX-021-056).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct forms for each noun.
14. **Other versions:** *Every morning, he makes three loaves of bread.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…makes breads, cakes…* → ✅ *bread* · ❌ *…in his shop small.* → ✅ *small shop*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *He bakes bread, cakes and cookies every morning.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the time phrase; commas in the list.
18. **Pronunciation:** list intonation ↗ ↗ ↘.
19. **Pattern:** **Every + time, he/she + verb-s + list + in his/her + place.**
20. **More examples:** *Every evening, she reads books, magazines and news online.*
21. **Quick check:** Which object noun is uncountable? → **Answer:** *bread*.

---

### EX-021-093 — "The smell of fresh bread brings many customers to his door."

1. **Meaning:** People smell the bread and come to his shop. 👃🍞➡️🚪
2. **Situation:** The story explains why the shop is successful.
3. **Main lesson:** Head noun *smell* (singular) → *brings*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *of* + adj + U noun + verb-s + *many* + plural + *to his* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The smell of fresh bread** (S) → **brings** (V) → **many customers** (O) → **to his door** (direction).
6. **Subject:** head **smell**.
7. **Verb:** *brings* (-s).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *many customers* (O) · *to his door* (where).
10. **Word by word:** *smell* 💭 (noun here — also a verb: *It smells good*) · *customers* 👩 — people who buy · *door* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** head noun decides the verb; *many* + plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** EX-021-084 (*The noise from the street keeps…*).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement with *smell*.
14. **Other versions:** *The smells from the kitchen bring…* (plural head).
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…bring many customers…* → ✅ *brings* · ❌ *…many customer…* → ✅ *customers*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The smell of the bread pulls people in.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KUS-tuh-merz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + of + noun + verb-s + many + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The sound of the music brings people to the square.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *smell* a noun or a verb here? → **Answer:** a noun (after *The*).

## Step K — Nouns in real writing: a short message

> *Hi Jo! Thanks for the **flowers**. They are beautiful! Can you come for **dinner** on **Friday**? Bring your **sister** too. See you, **Mia***

### EX-021-094 — "Thanks for the flowers."

1. **Meaning:** Thank you for giving me the flowers. 💐
2. **Situation:** A text message after receiving a gift.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun after *Thanks for*.
4. **Grammar used:** *Thanks for the* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Thanks** → **for the flowers**.
6. **Subject:** none — *Thanks* is a fixed expression (= I thank you).
7. **Verb:** none written (understood: *I thank you*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *for the flowers* — the reason; *flowers* = P.
10. **Word by word:** *Thanks* — informal *thank you* · *for* · *the flowers* 📦 — specific flowers (the ones Jo gave).
11. **How the rules work together:** *Thanks for* + noun (or + -ing: *Thanks for coming* — later).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Thanks for your help* (EX-021-008).
13. **Why this is correct:** standard expression.
14. **Other versions:** *Thank you for the flowers.* (more formal) · *Thanks for the present!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Thanks you for the flowers.* → ✅ *Thanks* / *Thank you* · ❌ *Thanks to the flowers.* (= because of the flowers — different meaning)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Thanks so much for the flowers!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop or **!**
18. **Pronunciation:** "THANKS" — /θ/ tongue between teeth.
19. **Pattern:** **Thanks for the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Thanks for the card. · Thanks for the lovely dinner.*
21. **Quick check:** More formal version? → **Answer:** *Thank you for the flowers.*

---

### EX-021-095 — "Can you come for dinner on Friday?"

1. **Meaning:** Are you free to come to my home and eat dinner with us on Friday? 🍽️
2. **Situation:** An invitation in the same message.
3. **Main lesson:** A meal noun and a day noun after prepositions.
4. **Grammar used:** *Can you* + verb + *for* + meal + *on* + day?
5. **Sentence structure:** **Can** → **you** (S) → **come** (V) → **for dinner** → **on Friday** → **?**
6. **Subject:** *you*.
7. **Verb:** *come*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Can* — asking about possibility (and inviting).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *for dinner* (purpose) · *on Friday* (time).
10. **Word by word:** *dinner* 📦 (meal → no article) · *Friday* 💭 proper noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** *come for* + meal; *on* + day.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *on Saturday* (EX-021-031).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct prepositions and no article with the meal.
14. **Other versions:** *Would you like to come for dinner on Friday?* (more polite)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…for a dinner…* → ✅ *for dinner* · ❌ *…in Friday?* → ✅ *on Friday*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Wanna come over for dinner Friday?* (very informal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **F**; **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "FRY-day".
19. **Pattern:** **Can you come for + meal + on + day?**
20. **More examples:** *Can you come for lunch on Sunday? · Can you come for coffee tomorrow?*
21. **Quick check:** Preposition with days? → **Answer:** *on*.

---

### EX-021-096 — "Bring your sister too."

1. **Meaning:** Please bring your sister with you as well.
2. **Situation:** End of the invitation message.
3. **Main lesson:** A person noun as the object of an instruction.
4. **Grammar used:** instruction + *your* + noun + *too*.
5. **Sentence structure:** (*You*) **Bring** (V) → **your sister** (O) → **too**.
6. **Subject:** hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *Bring* — instruction (an invitation, friendly).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *your sister* (O) · *too* = also.
10. **Word by word:** *Bring* — come with someone/something · *sister* 👩 · *too* — at the end.
11. **How the rules work together:** instruction + object; *too* at the end.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** instructions ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct instruction form.
14. **Other versions:** *Bring her too.* (pronoun) · *Don't forget to bring your sister!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Bring too your sister.* → ✅ *Bring your sister too.* · ❌ *Take your sister too.* (*take* = go away with; *bring* = come here with)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Feel free to bring your sister!* (polite, friendly)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma needed before *too* in short sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BRING yor SIS-ter TOO".
19. **Pattern:** **Bring + your + noun + too.**
20. **More examples:** *Bring your camera too. · Bring a friend too.*
21. **Quick check:** *bring* or *take*: "___ your umbrella when you go out." → **Answer:** *take*.

## Step L — Noun or verb? Telling them apart

### EX-021-097 — "I email my boss every Monday. / I got an email from my boss."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Each Monday I send my boss an email. (2) My boss sent me an email.
2. **Situation:** Talking about office routines.
3. **Main lesson:** *email* as a **verb** (1) and as a **noun** (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + verb + noun + time · *I got an* + noun + *from my* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **email** (V) → **my boss** (O) → **every Monday** · (2) **I** → **got** (V) → **an email** (O) → **from my boss**.
6. **Subject:** *I* (both).
7. **Verb:** (1) *email* · (2) *got* (past of *get*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *my boss* · (2) *an email*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *email* after *I* → **verb** · (2) *email* after *an* → **noun** · *boss* 👩 — the person in charge of you.
11. **How the rules work together:** the word **before** decides: *I* → verb; *an* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** [L-021 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#75-same-word-noun-or-verb).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its correct position.
14. **Other versions:** *She emails me every day. · I have ten new emails.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I got email from my boss.* → ✅ *an email* · ❌ *I email to my boss.* → ✅ *I email my boss.* (no *to*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'll email you.* · *Did you get my email?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *email* (also *e-mail*, older).
18. **Pronunciation:** "EE-mayl" (same for noun and verb).
19. **Pattern:** **I + email + person** / **an email + from + person**
20. **More examples:** *I text my mum. / I got a text from my mum.*
21. **Quick check:** *I sent you an ___.* — noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun (*email*).

---

### EX-021-098 — "We walk to work. / Let's go for a walk."

1. **Meaning:** (1) We go to our jobs on foot. (2) I suggest we take a walk (for fun).
2. **Situation:** A couple talking about their day.
3. **Main lesson:** *walk* as a verb (1) and a noun (2); *work* as a noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *We* + verb + *to* + noun · *Let's go for a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **We** → **walk** (V) → **to work** · (2) **Let's** → **go** → **for a walk**.
6. **Subject:** *We* · *Let's* (= let us — we).
7. **Verb:** (1) *walk* · (2) *go*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none (*Let's* is a suggestion form — [L-028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md#78-let-me--lets)).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *to work* (direction; *work* = noun, the workplace) · (2) *for a walk* (purpose).
10. **Word by word:** *walk* after *We* → verb · *a walk* after *a* → noun · *work* after *to* → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the job.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *go to work / at work* — no article ([L-018](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-018-first-sentences-with-am-is-are.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** each form in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *I take a walk every evening. · She walks her dog.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *We walk to the work.* → ✅ *to work* · ❌ *Let's go for walk.* → ✅ *for a walk*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Fancy a walk?* (UK, informal invitation)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two separate sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** *walk* "wawk" — silent **l**.
19. **Pattern:** **go for a + noun** (walk, swim, run, drive).
20. **More examples:** *Let's go for a swim. · We went for a drive.*
21. **Quick check:** In *I walk to work*, is *work* a noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

---

### EX-021-099 — "Please answer the question. / I don't know the answer."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Please reply to the question. (2) I can't say the correct reply.
2. **Situation:** A teacher and a student in class.
3. **Main lesson:** *answer* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** instruction + *the* + noun · negative + *the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) (*You*) **answer** (V) → **the question** (O) · (2) **I** → **don't know** (V) → **the answer** (O).
6. **Subject:** hidden *you* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *answer* · (2) *know*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (2) *don't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *the question* · (2) *the answer*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *answer* after *Please* → verb · (2) *answer* after *the* → noun · *question* 💭.
11. **How the rules work together:** instruction → verb; *the* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I know the answer* ([L-006](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)) — silent **w** in *answer*.
13. **Why this is correct:** correct positions.
14. **Other versions:** *She answered all the questions. · The answers are on page 10.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please answer to the question.* → ✅ *answer the question* (no *to*) · ❌ *I don't know answer.* → ✅ *the answer*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *No idea!* (= I don't know the answer)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "AN-ser" / "AHN-ser" (UK).
19. **Pattern:** **answer + the question** / **know + the answer**
20. **More examples:** *Please call me. / I got your call.*
21. **Quick check:** *answer the question* — is there *to*? → **Answer:** No.

---

### EX-021-100 — "The dream was strange. / I dream about flying."

1. **Meaning:** (1) The pictures in my mind while I slept were unusual. (2) When I sleep, I often see myself flying. 🛌✈️
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend at breakfast.
3. **Main lesson:** *dream* as a noun (1) and a verb (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *The* + noun + *was* + adjective · *I* + verb + *about* + -ing.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **The dream** (S) → **was** → **strange** · (2) **I** → **dream** (V) → **about flying**.
6. **Subject:** *The dream* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *was* · (2) *dream*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *strange* · (2) *about flying* — *flying* is an **-ing form used like a noun** after a preposition (a gerund — later lesson).
10. **Word by word:** (1) *dream* after *The* → noun · (2) *dream* after *I* → verb · *flying* — the activity of flying.
11. **How the rules work together:** after a preposition, a verb takes **-ing** (it acts like a noun).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** words have two jobs ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct positions; *about* + -ing.
14. **Other versions:** *I had a strange dream last night.* · *Do you dream in English?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I dream about fly.* → ✅ *about flying* · ❌ *The dream were strange.* → ✅ *was*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I had the weirdest dream!* (*weird* = strange)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DREEM" (long ee).
19. **Pattern:** **I dream about + noun / verb-ing.**
20. **More examples:** *I dream about the sea. · She dreams about winning.*
21. **Quick check:** Why *flying* and not *fly*? → **Answer:** after a preposition (*about*), use -ing.
