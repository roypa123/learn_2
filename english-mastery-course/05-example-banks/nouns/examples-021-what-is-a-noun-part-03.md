**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

**Home words in this part:** kitchen · living room · bedroom · bathroom · garden · sofa · fridge · lamp · cupboard · shelf · stairs · towel · plate · dishwasher.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-101 — "My family is big."

1. **Meaning:** There are many people in my family. 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦👴👵
2. **Situation:** A new classmate asks about your family.
3. **Main lesson:** A **collective** noun (a group) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My family** (S) → **is** → **big**.
6. **Subject:** *My family* — one group.
7. **Verb:** *is* — singular (the family as **one** unit).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *big* — adjective.
10. **Word by word:** *My* — belongs to me · *family* 👩 — parents, children, grandparents… · *big* — large.
11. **How the rules work together:** one group → singular verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our team has a new coach* (EX-021-030) — group nouns are usually singular.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective; singular agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *My family is small.* · *Is your family big?* · *Our families are big.* (two families)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My family are big.* — possible in UK when thinking of the members, but *is* is safest · ❌ *My familys is big.* → ✅ *family*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I come from a big family.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **M**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FAM-lee" (often 2 syllables in fast speech).
19. **Pattern:** **My + group noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *My class is small. · Our group is noisy.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *family*? → **Answer:** *families* (y → ies).

### EX-021-102 — "The kitchen is clean."

1. **Meaning:** The room where we cook has no dirt. ✨🍳
2. **Situation:** After cleaning, you tell your partner: "The kitchen is clean now."
3. **Main lesson:** A **place** noun (room) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The kitchen** (S) → **is** → **clean**.
6. **Subject:** *The kitchen*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *clean* — adjective.
10. **Word by word:** *kitchen* 📍 — room for cooking · *clean* — not dirty (here an adjective).
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *the* = we both know which kitchen (in our home).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The kitchen isn't clean.* · *Is the kitchen clean?* · *The bathrooms are clean.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Kitchen is clean.* → ✅ *The kitchen* · ❌ *The kitchen clean.* → ✅ *is clean*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kitchen's spotless!* (*spotless* = completely clean)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KICH-en".
19. **Pattern:** **The + room + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The bathroom is small. · The living room is warm.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite of *clean*? → **Answer:** *dirty*.

### EX-021-103 — "Grandma knits scarves."

1. **Meaning:** My grandmother makes scarves with wool and needles. 🧶🧣
2. **Situation:** Explaining where your scarf came from.
3. **Main lesson:** A family noun (subject) + a thing noun with an irregular-spelling plural (object).
4. **Grammar used:** noun + verb-s + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Grandma** (S) → **knits** (V) → **scarves** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Grandma* — used like a name here.
7. **Verb:** *knits* — -s for one person.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *scarves* — object.
10. **Word by word:** *Grandma* — informal for grandmother; capital **G** when used as a name ([L-022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md#73-a-common-noun-can-become-part-of-a-name)) · *knits* — the **k** is silent ("nits") · *scarves* — plural of *scarf* (f → ves; *scarfs* also correct).
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *knits*; plural object → general (scarves in general).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** f → ves ([L-024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md#74-rule-4--many-nouns-in--f---fe-change-to--ves)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct verb form and plural spelling.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandma knits scarves.* (small **g** after *my*) · *Does Grandma knit hats?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Grandma knit scarves.* → ✅ *knits* · ❌ *…scarfes.* → ✅ *scarves / scarfs*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandma knits all our scarves.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Grandma* (name) vs *my grandma* (common noun).
18. **Pronunciation:** "NITS" (silent k) · "SKARVZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Family name + verb-s + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Grandpa grows tomatoes. · Mum bakes cakes.*
21. **Quick check:** Is the **k** in *knits* pronounced? → **Answer:** No — it's silent.

### EX-021-104 — "The sofa is very comfortable."

1. **Meaning:** The sofa feels soft and nice to sit on. 🛋️
2. **Situation:** A guest sits down in your living room.
3. **Main lesson:** A **furniture** noun (countable!) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is very* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The sofa** (S) → **is** → **very comfortable**.
6. **Subject:** *The sofa*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very comfortable* — complement.
10. **Word by word:** *sofa* 📦 — a long soft seat for 2–3 people (US also *couch*) · *comfortable* — nice to sit on/wear.
11. **How the rules work together:** *sofa* is countable (*a sofa, two sofas*), but *furniture* is uncountable.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *furniture* (U) vs *a sofa* (C) ([L-026 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md#75-countable--uncountable-pairs-with-similar-meanings)).
13. **Why this is correct:** singular C noun with *the* → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *The sofas are very comfortable.* · *This furniture is very comfortable.* (U)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The sofa is very comfortably.* → ✅ *comfortable* (adjective after *be*) · ❌ *The furnitures are comfortable.* → ✅ *The furniture is…*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This sofa's really comfy!* (*comfy* = informal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** ⚠️ "KUMF-tuh-bul" (3 syllables in normal speech — the *or* is almost silent).
19. **Pattern:** **The + furniture item + is very + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The bed is very soft. · The chair is very hard.*
21. **Quick check:** Countable or uncountable: *sofa*? *furniture*? → **Answer:** *sofa* = C, *furniture* = U.

### EX-021-105 — "Our garden has an apple tree."

1. **Meaning:** In our garden there is a tree that grows apples. 🌳🍎
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend why you always have apples in autumn.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun used **like an adjective** before another noun (*apple tree*).
4. **Grammar used:** *our* + noun + *has an* + noun + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our garden** (S) → **has** (V) → **an apple tree** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Our garden*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *an apple tree* — object; head noun **tree**.
10. **Word by word:** *garden* 📍 · *an* — because ***a**pple* starts with a vowel sound · *apple* — a noun used as a describer (what kind of tree?) · *tree* — head noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** in *apple tree*, the first noun describes the second; the first noun stays **singular** (❌ *apples tree*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** compound nouns *post office, bus stop* (EX-021-047).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article by sound; head noun *tree* singular.
14. **Other versions:** *Our garden has two apple trees.* (only *tree* becomes plural) · *Does your garden have a tree?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…has a apple tree.* → ✅ *an* · ❌ *…has two apples trees.* → ✅ *two apple trees*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We've got an apple tree in the garden.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two words: *apple tree*.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress on the first noun: ***AP**ple tree*.
19. **Pattern:** **Place + has + a/an + noun + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The house has a flower garden. · The room has a glass door.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *apple tree*? → **Answer:** *apple trees*.

### EX-021-106 — "My little sister loves dolls."

1. **Meaning:** My young sister really likes playing with dolls. 🪆
2. **Situation:** Choosing a birthday present.
3. **Main lesson:** An adjective + family noun (subject) + plural thing noun (object).
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + adj + noun + verb-s + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My little sister** (S) → **loves** (V) → **dolls** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My little sister* (head *sister*).
7. **Verb:** *loves*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *dolls* — object (dolls in general).
10. **Word by word:** *little* — young/small · *sister* 👩 · *loves* · *dolls* 📦 — toy people.
11. **How the rules work together:** general liking → plural, no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I like cats* (general) ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#74-plurals-for-things-in-general)).
13. **Why this is correct:** S-V-O; general plural.
14. **Other versions:** *My little sisters love dolls.* · *Does your sister like dolls?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…loves the dolls.* (in general) → ✅ *loves dolls* · ❌ *My sister little…* → ✅ *My little sister*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My baby sister is crazy about dolls.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LIT-ul SIS-ter LUVZ DOLLZ".
19. **Pattern:** **My + adjective + family noun + loves + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My big brother loves cars. · My old aunt loves cats.*
21. **Quick check:** *younger sister* or *little sister* — similar meaning? → **Answer:** Yes.

### EX-021-107 — "Dad washes the car on Sundays."

1. **Meaning:** Every Sunday, my father cleans the car with water. 🚗🧽
2. **Situation:** Describing family routines.
3. **Main lesson:** A family name-noun + verb-es + object + time noun.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + verb-es + *the* + noun + *on* + plural day.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Dad** (S) → **washes** (V) → **the car** (O) → **on Sundays** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Dad* (used as his name → capital).
7. **Verb:** *washes* — *wash* + **es** (after -sh).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the car* (O) · *on Sundays* = every Sunday.
10. **Word by word:** *Dad* · *washes* · *car* 📦 · *Sundays* — plural day = every Sunday.
11. **How the rules work together:** -sh → -es (same rule for nouns and verbs).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She watches videos* ([L-024 §7.7](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md#77-same-rules-for-verbs-he--she--it)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct spelling and order (S-V-O-Time).
14. **Other versions:** *My dad washes the car every Sunday.* · *Does Dad wash the car?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Dad washs the car.* → ✅ *washes* · ❌ *Dad washes on Sundays the car.* → ✅ object before time
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Dad always washes the car on a Sunday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Dad* capital (name use), *Sundays* capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WOSH-iz" (2 syllables).
19. **Pattern:** **Family name + verb-(e)s + the + noun + on + days.**
20. **More examples:** *Mum cleans the windows on Saturdays. · Grandpa reads the paper on Sundays.*
21. **Quick check:** Why *washes* and not *washs*? → **Answer:** after -sh, add -es.

### EX-021-108 — "The baby has a new toy."

1. **Meaning:** Someone gave the baby a new thing to play with. 👶🧸
2. **Situation:** A visitor notices the baby smiling.
3. **Main lesson:** Person noun (S) + thing noun (O) with an adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *has a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The baby** (S) → **has** → **a new toy** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The baby*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a new toy*.
10. **Word by word:** *baby* 👩 · *new* · *toy* 📦 — a thing for children to play with.
11. **How the rules work together:** article → adjective → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *have/has* = own ([L-040](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-040-have-and-have-got.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** S-V-O with correct article.
14. **Other versions:** *The babies have new toys.* · *Does the baby have a toy?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The baby have a new toy.* → ✅ *has* · ❌ *…a toy new.* → ✅ *a new toy*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Look, the baby's got a new toy!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TOY" (/ɔɪ/).
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + has a/an + adjective + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The dog has a new ball. · The teacher has a new car.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *toy*? → **Answer:** *toys* (vowel + y → +s).

### EX-021-109 — "The fridge is empty."

1. **Meaning:** There is no food in the fridge. 🧊❌
2. **Situation:** You open the fridge — time to go shopping!
3. **Main lesson:** A thing (appliance) noun as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The fridge** (S) → **is** → **empty**.
6. **Subject:** *The fridge*.

11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** short forms of nouns: *photo* (photograph), *fridge* (refrigerator).

17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FRIJ"; "EMP-tee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + appliance + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The oven is hot. · The washing machine is broken.*

### EX-021-110 — "My cousins visit us in August."

1. **Meaning:** Every August, the children of my aunt/uncle come to stay with us.
2. **Situation:** Explaining summer plans.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural family noun (S) + month noun (P).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + verb + object pronoun + *in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My cousins** (S) → **visit** (V) → **us** (O) → **in August** (time).
6. **Subject:** *My cousins*.
7. **Verb:** *visit* — plural → no -s.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *us* (O) · *in August* (time).
10. **Word by word:** *cousins* 👩 · *us* — object pronoun · *in* + month · *August* — proper noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** *in* + month; *on* + day.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** months take capitals ([L-022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md)); *us* ([L-028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement and prepositions.
14. **Other versions:** *My cousin visits us in August.* · *Do your cousins visit you?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…visit we…* → ✅ *us* · ❌ *…on August.* → ✅ *in August* · ❌ *…in august.* → ✅ *August*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My cousins come over every August.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **A**.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KUZ-inz"; "AW-gust".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural family noun + visit + us/me + in + month.**
20. **More examples:** *My grandparents visit us in December.*
21. **Quick check:** *in* or *on*: ___ Monday / ___ May? → **Answer:** *on* Monday, *in* May.

## Step B — Nouns and pronouns at home

### EX-021-111 — "Mum is in the garden. She is planting flowers."

1. **Meaning:** My mother is outside in the garden, putting flowers into the ground. 🌷
2. **Situation:** A visitor asks where your mother is.
3. **Main lesson:** Noun → pronoun (*She*); place noun after *in*; object noun.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + *is in the* + noun · *She is* + verb-ing + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Mum** (S) → **is** → **in the garden** · **She** (S) → **is planting** (V) → **flowers** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Mum* · *She* (= Mum).
7. **Verb:** *is* · *is planting* (action now).
8. **Helping verbs:** *is* (in *is planting*).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in the garden* (place) · *flowers* (object).
10. **Word by word:** *Mum* (name use) · *garden* 📍 · *She* — pronoun · *planting* — putting in the ground · *flowers* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun first, pronoun second.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** don't repeat: ❌ *Mum she is…* ([L-027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#75-three-big-mistakes)).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference; correct tenses.
14. **Other versions:** *Dad is in the garage. He is fixing the bike.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She is plant flowers.* → ✅ *planting* · ❌ *Mum is in garden.* → ✅ *in the garden*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Mum's in the garden — she's planting flowers.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PLAN-ting" / UK "PLAHN-ting".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + is in the + place. He/She is + verb-ing + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Grandpa is in the kitchen. He is making tea.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *She* refer to? → **Answer:** Mum.

### EX-021-112 — "Our neighbours have a dog. It barks at the postman."

1. **Meaning:** The people next door own a dog, and the dog barks at the person who brings letters. 🐕📬
2. **Situation:** Explaining a noise every morning.
3. **Main lesson:** *a dog* (first mention) → *It*; a job noun after a preposition.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *have a* + noun · *It* + verb-s + *at the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our neighbours** (S) → **have** → **a dog** · **It** (S) → **barks** → **at the postman**.
6. **Subject:** *Our neighbours* · *It* (= the dog).
7. **Verb:** *have* · *barks*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a dog* (O) · *at the postman* (P).
10. **Word by word:** *neighbours* 👩 · *dog* 🐾 · *It* — (we don't know the dog well → *it*) · *postman* 👩 — person who delivers post (neutral: *mail carrier*, US).
11. **How the rules work together:** first mention *a*, then the pronoun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** unknown animals → *it* ([L-027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#72-choosing-the-right-pronoun)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement and reference.
14. **Other versions:** *Our neighbour has two dogs. They bark at night.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *It bark at the postman.* → ✅ *barks* · ❌ *…barks to the postman.* → ✅ *at*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The neighbours' dog barks at the postman every day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "POHST-mun".
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + have a + animal. It + verb-s + at the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My aunt has a parrot. It talks to the cat!*
21. **Quick check:** *bark at* or *bark to*? → **Answer:** *bark at*.

### EX-021-113 — "My grandparents live with us. They help with the cooking."

1. **Meaning:** My grandmother and grandfather share our home; they help make the meals.
2. **Situation:** Describing your household.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural family noun → *They*; *the cooking* as a noun (an activity).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + verb + *with* + pronoun · *They help with the* + -ing noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandparents** (S) → **live** → **with us** · **They** (S) → **help** → **with the cooking**.
6. **Subject:** *My grandparents* · *They*.
7. **Verb:** *live* · *help* (no -s).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *with us* · *with the cooking*.
10. **Word by word:** *grandparents* 👩 · *us* — object pronoun after *with* · *cooking* — here a **noun** (the activity of cooking), after *the*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the* + verb-ing = the activity as a noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** -ing after a preposition ([EX-021-100](examples-021-what-is-a-noun-part-02.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement and correct pronoun forms.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandmother lives with us. She helps with the cleaning.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They help with the cook.* → ✅ *the cooking* (*a cook* = a person!) · ❌ *…live with we.* → ✅ *with us*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My grandparents live with us — they do a lot of the cooking.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GRAN-pair-ents" (the **d** is often silent).
19. **Pattern:** **They help with the + activity-ing.**
20. **More examples:** *My brother helps with the shopping. · We all help with the cleaning.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *cooking* a noun here? → **Answer:** Yes (after *the*).

### EX-021-114 — "The towels are wet. Put them on the line."

1. **Meaning:** The towels have water in them; hang them outside on the washing line to dry.
2. **Situation:** After a day at the beach.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural noun → *them* (object); place noun after *on*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adj · instruction + *them* + *on the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The towels** (S) → **are** → **wet** · (*You*) **Put** → **them** (O) → **on the line** (place).
6. **Subject:** *The towels* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *Put*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *wet* · *them* (= the towels) · *on the line*.

10. **Word by word:** *homework* (U — no -s) · *their* — belongs to them · *news* (U, singular) — *the news* = the TV news programme.
11. **How the rules work together:** each clause has its own subject + verb; U nouns stay singular.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *do* as a main verb ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)); *homework, news* U ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement and U nouns correct.
14. **Other versions:** *After dinner, the boy does his homework, and the father watches the news.* (singular → -s/-es)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…do their homeworks…* → ✅ *homework* · ❌ *…make their homework…* → ✅ *do* · ❌ *…watch the new.* → ✅ *the news*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *After dinner the kids do homework and Mum and Dad watch the news.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *After dinner*; comma before *and* joining two clauses.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NYOOZ" / US "NOOZ".
19. **Pattern:** **After + meal, + noun + verb + their + noun, and + noun + verb + the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *After lunch, the students play football, and the teachers drink coffee.*
21. **Quick check:** *do homework* or *make homework*? → **Answer:** *do homework*.

## Step J — Home nouns in real writing: a note for the babysitter

> *Hi Emma! The **children's** **dinner** is in the **fridge**. **Bedtime** is at eight. Please call me if there is a **problem**. Thanks! — Sara*

### EX-021-144 — "The children's dinner is in the fridge."

1. **Meaning:** The food for the children's dinner is ready, in the fridge.
2. **Situation:** A parent leaves a note for the babysitter.
3. **Main lesson:** Possessive noun + meal noun (subject) + place.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + possessive + noun + *is in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The children's dinner** (S) → **is** → **in the fridge**.
6. **Subject:** head **dinner** → *is*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** place.
10. **Word by word:** *children's* — for/of the children · *dinner* — here the food itself · *fridge* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** with a possessive before it, *dinner* can mean the food → *the children's dinner*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *children's* ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** singular head → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Your dinner is on the table.* · *Is the children's dinner ready?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The childrens dinner…* → ✅ *children's* · ❌ *…are in the fridge.* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids' dinner's in the fridge.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe before **s** in *children's*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHIL-drenz DIN-er".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person's + noun + is in the + place.**
20. **More examples:** *The baby's milk is on the table.*
21. **Quick check:** Head noun? → **Answer:** *dinner*.

### EX-021-145 — "Bedtime is at eight."

1. **Meaning:** The children must go to bed at 8:00. 🛏️🕗
2. **Situation:** Same babysitter note.
3. **Main lesson:** A **compound noun** of time (*bedtime*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** compound noun + *is at* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Bedtime** (S) → **is** → **at eight**.
6. **Subject:** *Bedtime*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at eight* — time.
10. **Word by word:** *bedtime* 💭 — *bed* + *time*, one word, no article · *at* + time.
11. **How the rules work together:** routine time nouns usually have no article (*bedtime, lunchtime*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Breakfast is ready* (EX-021-022) — no article.
13. **Why this is correct:** no article; correct preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *Lunchtime is at twelve.* · *When is bedtime?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The bedtime is at eight.* → ✅ *Bedtime* · ❌ *Bed time is in eight.* → ✅ *Bedtime is at eight.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Bed at eight, OK?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BED-tym".
19. **Pattern:** **Routine time noun + is at + time.**
20. **More examples:** *Lunchtime is at one. · Playtime is after school.*
21. **Quick check:** One word or two: *bed time*? → **Answer:** one: *bedtime*.

### EX-021-146 — "Please call me if there is a problem."

1. **Meaning:** Phone me if something goes wrong. 📞
2. **Situation:** End of the babysitter note.
3. **Main lesson:** An idea noun (*problem*) after *there is a*.
4. **Grammar used:** instruction + object pronoun + *if there is a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Please call me** (main instruction) → **if there is a problem** (condition).
6. **Subject:** main part: hidden *you* · *if* part: *there* (+ *a problem*).
7. **Verb:** *call* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *me* (O) · *a problem* (what exists).
10. **Word by word:** *call* — phone · *if* — on the condition that ([L-138](../../02-intermediate-grammar/conditionals/lesson-138-zero-and-first-conditional.md)) · *problem* 💭 — something bad or difficult.
11. **How the rules work together:** instruction + *if* + *there is a* + noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *There is* + singular noun (EX-021-071).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article and structure.
14. **Other versions:** *Call me if there are any problems.* (plural) · *Don't call me unless there's a problem.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…if there is problem.* → ✅ *a problem* · ❌ *…if is a problem.* → ✅ *if there is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Ring me if anything happens.* (UK *ring* = call)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma needed when *if* comes second.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PROB-lum".
19. **Pattern:** **Please call me if there is a/an + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Tell me if there is a mistake. · Shout if there is a fire!*
21. **Quick check:** Plural version? → **Answer:** *if there are any problems*.

## Step K — Noun or verb? Home words

### EX-021-147 — "I iron my shirts on Sunday. / Be careful — the iron is hot."

1. **Meaning:** (1) On Sunday I make my shirts smooth with an iron. (2) The iron (the tool) is hot — don't touch it.
2. **Situation:** Doing housework.
3. **Main lesson:** *iron* as a **verb** (1) and a **noun** (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + verb + noun · *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **iron** (V) → **my shirts** (O) → **on Sunday** · (2) **the iron** (S) → **is** → **hot**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *the iron*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *iron* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my shirts* · *hot*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *iron* after *I* → verb · (2) *iron* after *the* → noun 📦 (also the metal *iron*, U) · *shirts* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the job.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** [L-021 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#75-same-word-noun-or-verb).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its natural position.
14. **Other versions:** *She irons the clothes. · We need a new iron.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I do iron my shirts.* (only for emphasis) → ✅ *I iron my shirts.* · ❌ *the iron are hot* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I do the ironing on Sundays.* (*the ironing* = noun)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** a dash (—) before the warning.
18. **Pronunciation:** ⚠️ *iron* = "EYE-un" (UK) / "EYE-ern" (US) — the **r** is not where you expect!
19. **Pattern:** **I + verb + my + noun / the + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *I brush my hair. / Where's my brush?*
21. **Quick check:** In *the iron is hot*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-148 — "Please water the plants. / Can I have some water?"

1. **Meaning:** (1) Please give water to the plants. (2) Can I have some water to drink?
2. **Situation:** Leaving a friend in charge of your home while you travel / at a friend's house.
3. **Main lesson:** *water* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** instruction + verb + *the* + noun · *Can I have some* + U noun?
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) (*You*) **water** (V) → **the plants** (O) · (2) **Can** → **I** → **have** → **some water** (O)?
6. **Subject:** hidden *you* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *water* · (2) *have*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (2) *Can*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the plants* · *some water*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *water* after *Please* → verb · (2) *water* after *some* → noun (U).
11. **How the rules work together:** instruction → verb; *some* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I water the plants* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct positions.
14. **Other versions:** *She waters the garden every evening.* · *The water is cold.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Can I have a water?* — possible in cafés (a bottle/glass), but at home → *some water / a glass of water* · ❌ *Please water to the plants.* → ✅ *water the plants*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Could you water my plants while I'm away?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** same sound for noun and verb.
19. **Pattern:** **water + the + plants / some + water**
20. **More examples:** *Please feed the cat. / Buy some cat food.*
21. **Quick check:** *some water* — noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

---

### EX-021-149 — "My mum cooks every day. / My mum is a good cook."

1. **Meaning:** (1) My mother makes food every day. (2) My mother makes food very well.
2. **Situation:** Talking about family.
3. **Main lesson:** *cook* as a verb (1) and a person noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** noun + verb-s + time · noun + *is a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **My mum** → **cooks** → **every day** · (2) **My mum** → **is** → **a good cook**.
6. **Subject:** *My mum* (both).
7. **Verb:** (1) *cooks* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (2) *a good cook* — complement.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *cooks* — verb with -s · (2) *cook* — noun 👩 after *a good*.
11. **How the rules work together:** after *a + adjective* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** EX-021-137 — *cook* (person) vs *cooker* (machine, UK).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct positions.
14. **Other versions:** *My dad doesn't cook. He's a terrible cook!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My mum is a good cooker.* → ✅ *cook* · ❌ *My mum cook every day.* → ✅ *cooks*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Mum's a brilliant cook.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KUKS" / "KUK".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + verb-s / Person + is a good + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *He drives every day. / He's a careful driver.* (here the noun adds *-er*)
21. **Quick check:** Is *cooker* a person? → **Answer:** No — a machine (UK).

### EX-021-150 — "We paint the fence every spring. / Don't touch — the paint is still wet!"

1. **Meaning:** (1) Every spring we put new colour on the fence. (2) The paint hasn't dried yet — don't touch it!
2. **Situation:** Spring housework in the garden.
3. **Main lesson:** *paint* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *We* + verb + *the* + noun + time · instruction · *the* + U noun + *is still* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **We** → **paint** (V) → **the fence** (O) → **every spring** · (2) **Don't touch** · **the paint** (S) → **is** → **still wet**.
6. **Subject:** *We* · hidden *you* · *the paint*.
7. **Verb:** *paint* · *Don't touch* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Don't* (negative instruction).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the fence* · *still wet*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *paint* after *We* → verb · (2) *paint* after *the* → noun (U: the liquid colour) · *fence* 📦 — a wall of wood/metal around a garden · *still* — continuing until now.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the job; U noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We painted the walls blue* (EX-021-123).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct positions and agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *We need two tins of paint.* (U → counted with a container)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *the paints is wet* → ✅ *the paint is wet* · ❌ *Not touch!* → ✅ *Don't touch!*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Wet paint!* (a common sign)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **!** for a warning.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PAYNT".
19. **Pattern:** **We + verb + the + noun / the + U noun + is still + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *I glue the pieces. / The glue is still wet.*
21. **Quick check:** Count *paint*: *two ___ of paint*. → **Answer:** *two tins/cans of paint*.
