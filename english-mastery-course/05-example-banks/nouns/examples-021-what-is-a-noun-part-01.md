## Step A — One noun, very short sentences

### EX-021-013 — "Dogs bark."

1. **Meaning:** Dogs make their short, loud sound — "woof!" 🐶
2. **Situation:** A child hears a noise at night and asks "What is that?" You say: "Dogs bark. It's the neighbour's dog."
3. **Main lesson:** A noun as the **subject** of a sentence.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun (subject) + present simple verb (no -s).
5. **Sentence structure:** **Dogs** (subject) → **bark** (verb) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Dogs*. Find it: the verb is *bark* → ask "Who/what barks?" → *dogs*.
7. **Verb:** *bark* — present simple (things that happen usually / in general). No -s because the subject is plural.
8. **Helping verbs:** none — present simple positive sentences don't need one.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none. *Bark* doesn't need an object (you can't "bark something").
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Dogs* — noun 🐾 (animal), plural (*dog + s*). Tests: *a dog* ✅, *two dogs* ✅, *big dogs* ✅.
    - *bark* — verb: the sound a dog makes.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun subject → verb without -s ("one -s" pattern: the -s is on *dogs*, not on *bark*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** Two words can be a full sentence: subject + verb ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)). General statements use a plural with no *the* ([L-023 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#74-plurals-for-things-in-general)).
13. **Why this is correct:** it has a subject (a noun) and a verb, in the right order, with the right verb form.
14. **Other versions:** *A dog barks.* (one) · *The dog is barking.* (now) · *Dogs don't bark at night.* (negative) · *Do dogs bark?* (question)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Dogs barks.* → ✅ *Dogs bark.* (plural → no -s on the verb)
    - ❌ *Bark dogs.* → ✅ *Dogs bark.* (subject before the verb)
    - ❌ *The dogs bark.* (for dogs in general) → ✅ *Dogs bark.* (*the* = specific dogs)
16. **Natural spoken English:** said just like that, or *Dogs bark, that's normal.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **D** (first word), full stop at the end.
18. **Pronunciation:** *dogs* = "dogz" (/z/ — g is voiced, [L-024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md#76-how-to-pronounce-plural--s-and--es)). Stress both words: **DOGS BARK**.
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + verb.**
20. **More examples:** *Cats sleep. · Birds sing. · Babies cry.*
21. **Quick check:** What is the noun in *Cows moo*? → **Answer:** *cows* (subject).

### EX-021-014 — "Water is life."

1. **Meaning:** We need water to live. Without water, there is no life. 💧
2. **Situation:** A teacher explains why we must not waste water.
3. **Main lesson:** Two nouns joined by *be* — one as subject, one after *be*.
4. **Grammar used:** uncountable noun subject + *be* (*is*) + uncountable noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Water** (subject) → **is** (verb *be*) → **life** (noun after *be*) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Water*. "What is life?" → *water*.
7. **Verb:** *is* — *be*, present, singular (works like "=").
8. **Helping verbs:** none — *is* is the main (only) verb here.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *life* = complement after *be* (it says what water **is**). Not an object.
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Water* — noun 📦 (thing/liquid), uncountable: no *a*, no -s.
    - *is* — singular *be*, because *water* is uncountable (always singular).
    - *life* — noun 💭 (idea), used in general → no *the*.
11. **How the rules work together:** uncountable subject → singular verb *is*; both nouns are general ideas → no articles.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *be* works like "=" ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)). Uncountable nouns take a singular verb ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). Like *Time is money* (EX-021-012).
13. **Why this is correct:** subject + verb + complement; correct agreement; no articles for general meanings.
14. **Other versions:** *Water is important.* · *Clean water is life.* · *Is water life? Yes!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *The water is the life.* → ✅ *Water is life.* (general ideas → no *the*)
    - ❌ *Water are life.* → ✅ *Water is life.* (uncountable → *is*)
    - ❌ *Waters is lifes.* → ✅ *Water is life.* (no plural for these meanings)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Water's life.* (*'s* = is) — but the full form sounds stronger in a saying.
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **W**, full stop. Often written on posters as a slogan.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WAW-ter iz LIFE" (UK) / "WAH-der iz LIFE" (US — the t sounds like a quick d).
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + is + noun** (for sayings/definitions).
20. **More examples:** *Health is wealth. · Knowledge is power. · Family is everything.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *life* an object here? → **Answer:** No. It comes after *be*, so it's a complement.

### EX-021-015 — "The phone is ringing."

1. **Meaning:** The phone is making its sound right now — someone is calling. 📱🔔
2. **Situation:** You are cooking, and you shout to your family: "The phone is ringing! Can someone answer it?"
3. **Main lesson:** A noun (with *the*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + singular noun (subject) + present continuous (*is* + verb-ing).
5. **Sentence structure:** **The phone** (subject) → **is ringing** (verb: helper + main verb) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *The phone*. "What is ringing?" → *the phone*.
7. **Verb:** *is ringing* — present continuous: an action happening **now**.
8. **Helping verbs:** *is* — helps *ringing* show "now". (Full lesson: [L-056](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-056-present-continuous-positive.md).)
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:**
    - *The* — we both know which phone (the one in this house).
    - *phone* — noun 📦 (thing). Tests: *a phone* ✅, *phones* ✅, *my old phone* ✅.
    - *is* — helping verb (singular, because *phone* is singular).
    - *ringing* — main verb *ring* + -ing.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun subject → *is*; *is* + -ing → action now.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** helping verb + main verb: *I am cooking* ([L-012](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-012-the-verb.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** subject noun + correct helper (*is*) + -ing form.
14. **Other versions:** *The phones are ringing.* · *My phone isn't ringing.* · *Is the phone ringing?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *The phone ringing.* → ✅ *The phone is ringing.* (helper missing)
    - ❌ *The phone is ring.* → ✅ *…is ringing.* (after *is*, use -ing)
    - ❌ *Phone is ringing.* → ✅ *The phone is ringing.* (one countable noun needs *the/a/my*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The phone's ringing!* or just *Phone!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **T**, full stop (or **!** if you shout).
18. **Pronunciation:** *phone* = "fone" (**ph** = /f/ — [L-006](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)). *The phone's* → "thuh FOHNZ".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + verb-ing.**
20. **More examples:** *The baby is crying. · The kettle is boiling. · The dog is barking.*
21. **Quick check:** Which word is the noun? → **Answer:** *phone*.

### EX-021-016 — "Children play."

1. **Meaning:** Young people play games — that's what children do. 🧒⚽
2. **Situation:** Someone complains about noise in the park. You say: "Children play. It's normal."
3. **Main lesson:** An **irregular plural** noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** irregular plural noun + present simple verb (no -s).
5. **Sentence structure:** **Children** (subject) → **play** (verb) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Children*. "Who plays?" → *children*.
7. **Verb:** *play* — present simple, general truth. No -s (plural subject).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none (*play* can stand alone).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Children* — noun 👩 (people), plural of *child* (irregular — no -s).
    - *play* — verb: have fun with games.
11. **How the rules work together:** irregular plural is still plural → verb without -s.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *child → children* ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)). Never *childrens*.
13. **Why this is correct:** plural noun + verb with no -s.
14. **Other versions:** *A child plays.* · *The children are playing.* · *Children don't play here.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Childs play.* → ✅ *Children play.*
    - ❌ *Children plays.* → ✅ *Children play.*
    - ❌ *Childrens play.* → ✅ *Children play.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Kids play.* (*kids* = informal word for children)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **C**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHIL-dren" — short **i** (not like *child* "chyld").
19. **Pattern:** **Irregular plural noun + verb.**
20. **More examples:** *People talk. · Men work. · Women laugh.*
21. **Quick check:** Singular of *children*? → **Answer:** *child*.

### EX-021-017 — "The sun is hot."

1. **Meaning:** The sun gives a lot of heat. ☀️
2. **Situation:** At the beach at midday, you tell a child to wear a hat.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun that names something **unique** (only one) — it takes *the*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + singular noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The sun** (subject) → **is** (verb) → **hot** (adjective) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *The sun*. "What is hot?" → *the sun*.
7. **Verb:** *is* — *be*, present, singular.
8. **Helping verbs:** none (*is* is the main verb).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *hot* = adjective complement (describes the sun).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *The* — there is only one sun, so we always say *the sun* (also *the moon, the sky, the sea*).
    - *sun* — noun 📦 (thing in nature).
    - *hot* — adjective (describing word), not a noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** unique noun → *the*; singular → *is*; adjective after *be*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** adjectives can go after *be*: *The bus is late* ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** a complete sentence with the correct article for a unique thing.
14. **Other versions:** *The sun is very hot today.* · *The sun isn't hot in winter.* · *Is the sun hot?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Sun is hot.* → ✅ *The sun is hot.* (unique things take *the*)
    - ❌ *The sun hot.* → ✅ *The sun is hot.* (verb missing)
    - ❌ *A sun is hot.* → ✅ *The sun is hot.* (there's only one)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The sun's really hot today!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** small **s** in *sun* (common noun), capital **T** at the start.
18. **Pronunciation:** *sun* sounds the same as *son* (/sʌn/) — a homophone ([L-006](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)).
19. **Pattern:** **The + unique noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The sky is blue. · The moon is bright. · The sea is cold.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *hot* a noun? → **Answer:** No — it's an adjective (test: *very hot* ✅).

### EX-021-018 — "Rain falls."

1. **Meaning:** Water drops come down from the sky. 🌧️
2. **Situation:** A child asks why the ground is wet. You explain in simple words.
3. **Main lesson:** A word that can be a noun **or** a verb — here it is a **noun**.
4. **Grammar used:** uncountable noun subject + present simple verb + -s.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Rain** (subject) → **falls** (verb) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Rain*. "What falls?" → *rain*.
7. **Verb:** *falls* — present simple; **-s** because *rain* is uncountable = singular (like *it*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Rain* — noun 📦 (weather), uncountable. Position: before the verb → noun.
    - *falls* — verb *fall* + -s.
11. **How the rules work together:** uncountable noun = singular → verb gets -s.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** same word, two jobs: *The rain is heavy* (noun) / *It rains* (verb) ([L-021 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#75-same-word-noun-or-verb)).
13. **Why this is correct:** noun in subject position + singular verb form.
14. **Other versions:** *The rain is falling.* (now) · *It rains a lot here.* (*rain* as a verb) · *Heavy rain falls in April.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Rain fall.* → ✅ *Rain falls.* (uncountable = singular → -s)
    - ❌ *Rains fall.* → ✅ *Rain falls.* (no plural for rain here)
16. **Natural spoken English:** people usually say *It's raining.* — *Rain falls* is a simple explanation for children or science.
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **R**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** *falls* = "fawlz" (/z/).
19. **Pattern:** **Uncountable noun + verb-s.**
20. **More examples:** *Snow melts. · Water flows. · Time flies.*
21. **Quick check:** In *It rains*, is *rains* a noun? → **Answer:** No, a verb (after *it*).

### EX-021-019 — "Music helps."

1. **Meaning:** Listening to music makes things better (for example, when you're sad or stressed). 🎵
2. **Situation:** A friend is stressed before an exam. You put on some calm music and say: "Music helps."
3. **Main lesson:** A noun you can **hear but not touch** is still a noun.
4. **Grammar used:** uncountable noun subject + present simple + -s.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Music** (subject) → **helps** (verb) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Music*. "What helps?" → *music*.
7. **Verb:** *helps* — present simple, -s (singular subject).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none here (*help* can be used alone; full version: *Music helps **me***).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Music* — noun 💭/📦, uncountable. Tests: *the music* ✅, *good music* ✅ (adjective test).
    - *helps* — verb.
11. **How the rules work together:** uncountable = singular → *helps*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** ideas and things you can't touch are nouns too (group 5 — [L-021 §7.1](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#71-the-5-groups-of-nouns)).
13. **Why this is correct:** subject noun + singular verb.
14. **Other versions:** *Music helps me relax.* · *Loud music doesn't help.* · *Does music help you study?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Music help.* → ✅ *Music helps.*
    - ❌ *The music helps.* (for music in general) → ✅ *Music helps.*
    - ❌ *A music helps.* → ✅ *Music helps.* (uncountable — no *a*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Music really helps.* / *Try some music — it helps.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **M**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MYOO-zik" — the **s** sounds like /z/.
19. **Pattern:** **Uncountable noun + verb-s.**
20. **More examples:** *Exercise helps. · Sleep helps. · Practice helps.*
21. **Quick check:** Can you say *two musics*? → **Answer:** No — *music* is uncountable (*two songs* ✅).

### EX-021-020 — "The baby is asleep."

1. **Meaning:** The baby is sleeping now. 👶💤
2. **Situation:** A parent whispers to a visitor: "Shh — the baby is asleep."
3. **Main lesson:** Find the noun when the sentence also has an adjective that looks like a verb.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *be* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The baby** (subject) → **is** (verb) → **asleep** (adjective) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *The baby*.
7. **Verb:** *is* — *be*, present, singular.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *asleep* = adjective (= sleeping). It describes the baby.
10. **Word by word:**
    - *baby* — noun 👩 (person). Tests: *a baby* ✅, *babies* ✅.
    - *asleep* — adjective (not a noun, not a verb). It only goes **after** *be*: ❌ *an asleep baby* → ✅ *a sleeping baby*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*; *be* + adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** describing words ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** the noun is the subject; *asleep* correctly follows *be*.
14. **Other versions:** *The baby is sleeping.* (same meaning) · *The babies are asleep.* · *Is the baby asleep?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *The baby asleep.* → ✅ *The baby is asleep.*
    - ❌ *The baby is sleep.* → ✅ *…is asleep / is sleeping.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The baby's asleep.* (*'s* = is)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **T**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "a-SLEEP" — stress on the second part.
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The cat is awake. · The children are tired. · The room is quiet.*
21. **Quick check:** Which word is the noun? → **Answer:** *baby*.

### EX-021-021 — "Teachers work hard."

1. **Meaning:** Teachers do a lot of work. Their job is difficult. 👩‍🏫
2. **Situation:** On Teachers' Day, a parent says thank you.
3. **Main lesson:** A **job noun** (person) in plural as the subject; telling it apart from an adverb.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + present simple verb + adverb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Teachers** (subject) → **work** (verb) → **hard** (adverb: how?) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Teachers*.
7. **Verb:** *work* — present simple, plural subject → no -s.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *hard* = adverb (tells **how** they work). Not a noun, not an object.
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Teachers* — noun 👩 (people/job). *teach* (verb) + *-er* = person who teaches.
    - *work* — verb here (after the subject). (*work* can also be a noun: *a lot of work*.)
    - *hard* — adverb here (*work hard* = work a lot). ⚠️ *hardly* means "almost not" — different!
11. **How the rules work together:** plural subject → *work*; adverb goes after the verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** word families: *teach → teacher* ([L-007](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-007-what-is-a-word.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + V + adverb, correct agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *My teacher works hard.* (one → -s) · *Teachers don't work on Sundays.* · *Do teachers work hard?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Teachers works hard.* → ✅ *Teachers work hard.*
    - ❌ *Teachers work hardly.* → ✅ *…work hard.* (*hardly* = almost not)
    - ❌ *Teachers hard work.* → ✅ *Teachers work hard.* (verb before the adverb)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Teachers work really hard.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **T**, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TEE-cherz WURK HARD".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural job noun + verb + adverb.**
20. **More examples:** *Nurses work long hours. · Farmers get up early. · Doctors work hard.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *work* a noun or verb here? → **Answer:** a verb (after the subject *Teachers*).

### EX-021-022 — "Breakfast is ready."

1. **Meaning:** The morning meal is prepared — come and eat! 🍳
2. **Situation:** A parent calls the family to the kitchen at 7 a.m.
3. **Main lesson:** **Meal** nouns usually have no article.
4. **Grammar used:** noun (no article) + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Breakfast** (subject) → **is** → **ready** (adjective) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *Breakfast*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *ready* = adjective (prepared).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *Breakfast* — noun 📦 (meal). Normally no *a/the*: *Breakfast is ready*, *I have breakfast at 7*.
    - *ready* — adjective.
11. **How the rules work together:** meal noun → no article; singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *ready* — *Are you ready?* ([L-010](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-010-capital-letter-and-full-stop.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** meals in general use no article.
14. **Other versions:** *Lunch is ready. · Dinner isn't ready yet. · Is breakfast ready?* · With an adjective: ***A big** breakfast is good for you.* (then *a* is possible)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *The breakfast is ready.* → usually ✅ *Breakfast is ready.* (*the* is possible only for a specific breakfast already mentioned)
    - ❌ *Breakfast ready.* → ✅ *Breakfast is ready.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Breakfast's ready!* / *Breakfast!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **B**; often **!** when calling.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BREK-fust" (not "break-fast") — the vowel changes.
19. **Pattern:** **Meal + is + ready.**
20. **More examples:** *Lunch is ready. · Dinner is at seven. · Tea is served.*
21. **Quick check:** Article or no article: *I have ___ lunch at 1.* → **Answer:** no article.

## Step B — Nouns from all 5 groups

### EX-021-023 — "My uncle is a farmer."

1. **Meaning:** My mother's or father's brother works on a farm. 🧑‍🌾
2. **Situation:** A classmate asks about your family's jobs.
3. **Main lesson:** Two **person** nouns — one as subject, one after *be*.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is* + *a* + job noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My uncle** (subject) → **is** → **a farmer** (noun after *be*) → **.**
6. **Subject:** *My uncle*. Head noun: *uncle*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a farmer* = complement (what he is).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *My* — determiner (belongs to me).
    - *uncle* — noun 👩 (family).
    - *a* — needed before a job noun.
    - *farmer* — noun 👩 (job): *farm + -er* = person who works on a farm.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *is*; job after *be* → *a/an*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My father is a pilot* (EX-021-003).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + *a* + job noun.
14. **Other versions:** *My uncles are farmers.* · *My uncle isn't a farmer.* · *Is your uncle a farmer?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *My uncle is farmer.* → ✅ *…is **a** farmer.*

19. **Pattern:** **My + family noun + is + a/an + job.**

21. **Quick check:** How many nouns? → **Answer:** two (*uncle, farmer*).

2. **Situation:** Telling a classmate about your pet.
3. **Main lesson:** An animal noun (object), then a noun phrase with a possessive.
4. **Grammar used:** *I have a* + noun · possessive *her* + noun + *is* + proper noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **have** → **a cat** (O) · **Her name** (S) → **is** → **Luna** (complement).
6. **Subject:** *I* · *Her name* (head noun *name*).
7. **Verb:** *have* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a cat* (object) · *Luna* (proper noun after *be*).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *cat* — noun 🐾.
    - *Her* — possessive determiner (= the cat's) ([L-036](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-036-possessive-determiners.md)).
    - *name* — noun 💭.
    - *Luna* — proper noun → capital **L**.
11. **How the rules work together:** first *a cat* (new information), then *her* (we know which cat now).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** pets often get *he/she* ([L-027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#72-choosing-the-right-pronoun)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article, clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *I have two cats. Their names are Luna and Leo.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *I have cat.* → ✅ *I have **a** cat.*
    - ❌ *Her name is luna.* → ✅ *Luna*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've got a cat called Luna.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital **L** for the name.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LOO-na".
19. **Pattern:** **I have a/an + animal. His/Her name is + Name.**
20. **More examples:** *I have a dog. His name is Max. · We have a parrot. Its name is Kiwi.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *her* a noun? → **Answer:** No, a possessive word (determiner).

### EX-021-037 — "The shoes are old. I want new ones."

1. **Meaning:** These shoes are not new; I want to buy new shoes. 👟
2. **Situation:** In a shoe shop with a parent.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural noun, and the word **ones** that replaces it.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective · *I want* + adjective + *ones*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The shoes** (S) → **are** → **old** · **I** → **want** → **new ones** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The shoes* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *want*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *new ones* = object (*ones* = shoes).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *shoes* — noun 📦, plural.
    - *ones* — a replacing word (= shoes), used after an adjective so we don't repeat the noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun → *are*; *ones* (plural) replaces a plural countable noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** don't repeat nouns unnecessarily ([L-027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** *ones* matches the plural *shoes*.
14. **Other versions:** *This shoe is old. I want a new one.* (singular → *a … one*)

    - ❌ *I want a new ones.* → ✅ *new ones* (no *a* with plural)
    - ❌ *I want new one.* → ✅ *new ones* (plural)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *These shoes are so old — I need new ones.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** *ones* = "wunz" (same sound as *one's*).
19. **Pattern:** **I want + adjective + one/ones.**
20. **More examples:** *This cup is dirty. I need a clean one. · My jeans are too small. I need bigger ones.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *ones* replace? → **Answer:** *shoes*.

### EX-021-039 — "The bus is late again. It is always late!"

1. **Meaning:** The bus didn't come on time — again. It never comes on time! 🚌😠
2. **Situation:** Waiting at the bus stop in the morning.
3. **Main lesson:** A thing noun replaced by *it*; an adverb of frequency.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + *is* + adjective + adverb · *it* + *is* + *always* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The bus** (S) → **is** → **late** → **again** · **It** (S) → **is** → **always** → **late**.
6. **Subject:** *The bus* · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *is* (×2).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *late* (adjective) · *again* (one more time) · *always* (every time).
10. **Word by word:**
    - *bus* — noun 📦.
    - *It* — pronoun (= the bus).
    - *always* — adverb of frequency; goes **after** *be* ([L-051](../../01-beginner-grammar/adverbs/lesson-051-adverbs-of-frequency.md)).
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → *it*; *always* after *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The big red bus is late* ([L-009](../../00-foundations/04-what-is-a-sentence/lesson-009-what-is-a-sentence.md)).

14. **Other versions:** *The buses are always late.* · *The bus isn't usually late.*

    - ❌ *The bus it is late.* → ✅ *The bus is late.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The bus is late again! It's always late!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **!** shows annoyance.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress shows feeling: *It's **AL**ways late!*
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adjective. It is always + adjective!**
20. **More examples:** *The train is full again. It's always full! · My boss is angry again. He's always angry!*
21. **Quick check:** Put *always* in: *The shop is busy.* → **Answer:** *The shop is always busy.*

7. **Verb:** *is* · *have*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can* — makes a polite request question.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *cold* · *a hot one* (= a hot coffee).

4. **Grammar used:** *Is* + subject noun + *a* + noun?
5. **Sentence structure:** **Is** (verb) → **your brother** (S) → **a teacher** (complement) → **?**
6. **Subject:** *your brother* — comes **after** *is* in the question.
7. **Verb:** *is* — moved to the front (inversion).

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a teacher* = complement.
10. **Word by word:** *brother*, *teacher* — person nouns 👩.

12. **Remember from before 🔁:** Way 1 questions ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct inversion; article before the job.

    - ❌ *Your brother is a teacher?* → ✅ *Is your brother a teacher?* (normal question form)
    - ❌ *Does your brother is a teacher?* → ✅ *Is your brother…?*
    - ❌ *Is your brother teacher?* → ✅ *a teacher*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Is your brother a teacher too?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** voice goes up at the end ↗.
19. **Pattern:** **Is + your + noun + a/an + job?**
20. **More examples:** *Is your mother a doctor? · Is your friend a student?*
21. **Quick check:** Answer "no" with a short answer. → **Answer:** *No, he isn't.*

1. **Meaning:** There is no milk in our house now. 🥛❌
2. **Situation:** Making coffee in the morning.
3. **Main lesson:** An **uncountable** noun as the object of a negative.
4. **Grammar used:** *We don't have* + uncountable noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **don't have** (V) → **milk** (O).
6. **Subject:** *We* (pronoun).
7. **Verb:** *have* (base form after *don't*).
8. **Helping verbs:** *don't* (do not).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *milk* = object.
10. **Word by word:** *milk* — noun 📦, uncountable: no *a*, no -s.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → no article; *don't* + base verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *any* in negatives ([L-026 §7.6](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md#76-quantity-words--first-look)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct negative and no article for U.
14. **Other versions:** *We don't have **any** milk.* (more natural) · *Do we have any milk?* · *We have no milk.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *We don't have a milk.* → ✅ *milk / any milk*
    - ❌ *We not have milk.* → ✅ *don't have*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We're out of milk.* (= we have none left)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe in *don't*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DOHNT HAV MILK".
19. **Pattern:** **We don't have (any) + uncountable noun.**
20. **More examples:** *We don't have bread. · I don't have time.*
21. **Quick check:** Make it more natural with one word. → **Answer:** *We don't have any milk.*

### EX-021-050 — "Does the hotel have a pool?"

1. **Meaning:** Is there a swimming pool at the hotel? 🏨🏊
2. **Situation:** Booking a holiday by phone.
3. **Main lesson:** Two **place** nouns in a question with *does*.
4. **Grammar used:** *Does* + singular noun + *have* + *a* + noun?
5. **Sentence structure:** **Does** (helper) → **the hotel** (S) → **have** (V) → **a pool** (O) → **?**
6. **Subject:** *the hotel*.
7. **Verb:** *have* (base form — not *has* — after *does*).
8. **Helping verbs:** *Does* — singular subject.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a pool* = object.
10. **Word by word:** *hotel* — noun 📍 · *pool* — noun 📍 (swimming pool).
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *does*; after *does* → *have*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *has → have* after *does* ([L-015](../../00-foundations/05-basic-word-order/lesson-015-what-makes-a-question.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** right helper, base verb, article.
14. **Other versions:** *Yes, it does. / No, it doesn't.* · *The hotel has a pool.* · *Do the hotels have pools?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:**
    - ❌ *Does the hotel has a pool?* → ✅ *have*
    - ❌ *Do the hotel have a pool?* → ✅ *Does*
    - ❌ *Has the hotel a pool?* → old-fashioned; ✅ *Does the hotel have…?*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Is there a pool at the hotel?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "duz-thuh-ho-TEL hav-uh-POOL" ↗.
19. **Pattern:** **Does + the + noun + have + a/an + noun?**
20. **More examples:** *Does the flat have a balcony? · Does the car have air conditioning?*
21. **Quick check:** Short answer "yes"? → **Answer:** *Yes, it does.*
