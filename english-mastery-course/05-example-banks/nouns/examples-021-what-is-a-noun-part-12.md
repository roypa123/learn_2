**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Food nouns:** many are **U** in general (*cheese, sugar, milk, honey, bread*) and **C** for items/portions/kinds (*a potato, two eggs, a glass of water*) ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). **U** nouns take a singular verb (*Cheese **is**…*) and no *a/an*.

**New words in this part:** kettle · oven · stale · knife/knives · pan · jar · jam · mushroom · fork · toast · bakery · picnic · cupboard · sink · shelf · cereal · cookbook · queue · flour · recipe · lamb · curry.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-551 — "The kettle is boiling."

1. **Meaning:** The water in the kettle is very hot now and making bubbles. 🫖💨
2. **Situation:** You are making tea in the morning.
3. **Main lesson:** A kitchen-tool noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + verb-*ing*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The kettle** (S) → **is boiling** (V).
6. **Subject:** *The kettle*.
7. **Verb:** *boiling* (main verb, *-ing* form).
8. **Helping verbs:** *is* (present continuous = happening now).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:** *the* — the one in our kitchen · *kettle* 📦 — a pot with a spout for heating water · *is boiling* — making bubbles now.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*; *is* + *-ing* → now.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The soup is hot* (EX-021-501) — same S + *is* shape.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *is* + *-ing* for an action happening now.
14. **Other versions:** *The water is boiling.* · *Is the kettle boiling?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Kettle is boiling.* → ✅ *The kettle* · ❌ *The kettle boiling.* → ✅ *is boiling*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kettle's boiling — tea or coffee?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *T*, full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KET-l" (the *e* in the 2nd syllable is almost silent).
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + verb-ing.**
20. **More examples:** *The rice is cooking. · The milk is boiling.*
21. **Quick check:** Which word is the noun? → **Answer:** *kettle*.

### EX-021-552 — "Cheese is expensive here."

1. **Meaning:** In this place, cheese costs a lot of money. 🧀💰
2. **Situation:** A visitor shops in a new country.
3. **Main lesson:** A **U** food noun as the subject, with no article.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective + place word.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Cheese** (S) → **is** → **expensive** → **here**.
6. **Subject:** *Cheese* (cheese in general).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *expensive* (complement) · *here* (place).
10. **Word by word:** *cheese* 📦 — U food made from milk · *expensive* — costs a lot · *here* — in this place.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → singular verb *is*; general meaning → no *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Rice is cheap* (EX-021-502) — the opposite idea, same pattern.
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun in general + *is* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *Cheese is cheap in France.* · *This cheese is expensive.* (one particular cheese → *this*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Cheese are expensive.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *A cheese is expensive here.* → ✅ *Cheese* (general)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Cheese is really pricey here.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHEEZ" (long *ee*, ends with a *z* sound).
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is + adjective (+ here).**
20. **More examples:** *Fruit is cheap here. · Coffee is expensive at the airport.*
21. **Quick check:** *Cheese is* or *Cheese are*? → **Answer:** *is* (U noun).

### EX-021-553 — "The plates are dirty."

1. **Meaning:** The plates have food on them; they are not clean. 🍽️
2. **Situation:** After dinner, looking at the table.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural noun as the subject → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The plates** (S) → **are** → **dirty**.
6. **Subject:** *The plates*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *dirty* (complement).
10. **Word by word:** *plates* 📦 — flat dishes for food (*plate* + *s*) · *dirty* — not clean.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun → plural verb *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Vegetables are healthy* (EX-021-504) — plural + *are*.
13. **Why this is correct:** subject and verb agree.
14. **Other versions:** *The plate is dirty.* (one) · *Are the plates clean?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The plates is dirty.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The plate are dirty.* → ✅ *plate is* / *plates are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The plates need washing.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PLAYTS" · "DUR-tee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The cups are clean. · The glasses are wet.*
21. **Quick check:** Singular of *plates*? → **Answer:** *plate*.

### EX-021-554 — "Sugar is bad for your teeth."

1. **Meaning:** Eating sugar hurts your teeth. 🍬🦷
2. **Situation:** A dentist talks to a child.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun subject + a noun after a preposition (*for your teeth*).
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective + *for* + possessive + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Sugar** (S) → **is** → **bad** → **for your teeth** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Sugar*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *bad* (complement) · *for your teeth* (preposition phrase).
10. **Word by word:** *sugar* 📦 — U, sweet white food · *bad for* — harmful to · *teeth* — irregular plural of *tooth*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → *is*; noun after *for* → position **P**.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *tooth → teeth* is an irregular plural ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** U subject + *is*; correct irregular plural.
14. **Other versions:** *Sweets are bad for your teeth.* (plural → *are*) · *Fruit is good for you.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Sugar is bad for your tooths.* → ✅ *teeth* · ❌ *The sugar is bad for your teeth.* → ✅ *Sugar* (general)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Too much sugar's bad for your teeth.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHUG-er" (starts with *sh*!) · "TEETH".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is + good/bad + for + your + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Water is good for your skin. · Smoking is bad for your lungs.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *tooth*? → **Answer:** *teeth*.

### EX-021-555 — "The oven is hot."

1. **Meaning:** The oven has a high temperature now. 🔥
2. **Situation:** Warning someone not to touch it.
3. **Main lesson:** A kitchen noun + *is* + adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The oven** (S) → **is** → **hot**.
6. **Subject:** *The oven*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *hot*.
10. **Word by word:** *oven* 📦 — a box in the kitchen that cooks with heat (for cakes, bread) · *hot* — adjective.
11. **How the rules work together:** one oven → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *hot* is an adjective; the noun is *heat* (EX-021-550).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The oven is ready.* · *Is the oven on?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The oven is heat.* → ✅ *hot* (adjective after *is*) · ❌ *An oven is hot.* → ✅ *The oven* (our oven)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Careful — the oven's hot!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop (or **!** as a warning).
18. **Pronunciation:** "UV-un" (not "OH-ven").
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The pan is hot. · The fridge is cold.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun or adjective: *hot*? → **Answer:** adjective.

### EX-021-556 — "Potatoes are cheap in autumn."

1. **Meaning:** In autumn, potatoes don't cost much. 🥔🍂
2. **Situation:** Talking about prices at the market.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural noun with *-es*, and a season noun after *in*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective + *in* + season.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Potatoes** (S) → **are** → **cheap** → **in autumn** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Potatoes* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *cheap* · *in autumn*.
10. **Word by word:** *potatoes* — *potato* + **-es** (consonant + *o*) · *cheap* · *autumn* 💭 — season (US: *fall*).
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*; seasons after *in* usually have no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *-o* nouns: *potato → potatoes, tomato → tomatoes* ([L-024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural spelling + plural verb.
14. **Other versions:** *Strawberries are expensive in winter.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Potatos are cheap.* → ✅ *potatoes* · ❌ *Potatoes is cheap.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Potatoes are really cheap this time of year.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** seasons have a **small** letter (*autumn*).
18. **Pronunciation:** "puh-TAY-tohz" · "AW-tum" (the *n* is silent).
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective + in + season.**
20. **More examples:** *Tomatoes are sweet in summer.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *potato*? → **Answer:** *potatoes*.

### EX-021-557 — "The bread is stale."

1. **Meaning:** The bread is old and hard; it is not fresh. 🍞
2. **Situation:** Opening the bread bag at breakfast.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun *bread* with *the* (our bread) + *is*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The bread** (S) → **is** → **stale**.
6. **Subject:** *The bread* (the bread in our kitchen).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *stale* — old, dry and hard.
10. **Word by word:** *the* — specific bread · *bread* — U · *stale* — opposite of *fresh*.
11. **How the rules work together:** specific U noun → *the* + *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Fresh bread smells wonderful* (EX-021-062) — general bread, no *the*.
13. **Why this is correct:** specific U noun → *the*; singular verb.
14. **Other versions:** *This bread is fresh.* · *The bread was stale.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The breads are stale.* → ✅ *The bread is* · ❌ *A bread is stale.* → ✅ *The bread* / *This loaf*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This bread's gone stale.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STAYL".
19. **Pattern:** **The + U noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The milk is sour. · The coffee is cold.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite of *stale*? → **Answer:** *fresh*.

## Step B — Food nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-561 — "I bought some apples. They're in the bag."

1. **Meaning:** I bought apples; you can find them in the bag. 🍎🛍️
2. **Situation:** Coming home from the shop.
3. **Main lesson:** plural noun *apples* → pronoun *they*.
4. **Grammar used:** *I bought some* + plural noun · *They're in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **bought** (V) → **some apples** (O) · **They** (S) → **'re** → **in the bag** (P).
6. **Subject:** *I* · *They* (= the apples).
7. **Verb:** *bought* (past of *buy*) · *'re* (*are*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *some apples* · *in the bag*.
10. **Word by word:** *some* — a number of · *apples* (C plural) · *They* — replaces *apples* · *bag* (C).
11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun → *they*; *they* + *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I love strawberries. I eat them every day* (EX-021-513) — *them* for objects, *they* for subjects.
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun, right verb.
14. **Other versions:** *I bought an apple. It's in the bag.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I buyed some apples.* → ✅ *bought* · ❌ *It's in the bag.* (for apples) → ✅ *They're*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Got some apples — they're in the bag.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *They're* = *They are* (apostrophe replaces *a*); not *their*!
18. **Pronunciation:** "BAWT" · "THAIR-in-thuh-BAG".
19. **Pattern:** **I bought some + plural noun. They're + place.**
20. **More examples:** *I made some sandwiches. They're in the fridge.*
21. **Quick check:** *They're* or *Their* here? → **Answer:** *They're* (= they are).

### EX-021-562 — "The new chef is very young. She is only twenty-two."

1. **Meaning:** The new head cook is young; she's just 22 years old. 👩‍🍳
2. **Situation:** Restaurant staff talking about a new colleague.
3. **Main lesson:** person noun *chef* → *she*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the new* + noun + *is very* + adjective · *She is only* + number.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The new chef** (S) → **is** → **very young** · **She** (S) → **is** → **only twenty-two**.
6. **Subject:** *The new chef* (head **chef**) · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very young* · *only twenty-two*.
10. **Word by word:** *chef* 👩 — main cook in a restaurant · *only* — not more than · *twenty-two* — age (no *years old* needed).
11. **How the rules work together:** person noun → *he/she*; age with *be*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The chef is French* (EX-021-510).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun matches the person; age uses *be*.
14. **Other versions:** *The new chef is very young. He is only twenty-two.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She has twenty-two years.* → ✅ *She is twenty-two.* · ❌ *It is only twenty-two.* → ✅ *She*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The new chef's so young — she's only twenty-two!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** hyphen in *twenty-two*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SHEF" (starts with *sh*).
19. **Pattern:** **The new + person noun + is + adj. She/He is + age.**
20. **More examples:** *The new waiter is very tall. He is from Spain.*
21. **Quick check:** *She has 22 years* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *She is 22.*

### EX-021-563 — "My brother loves spaghetti. He eats it every day."

1. **Meaning:** My brother really likes spaghetti and has it daily. 🍝
2. **Situation:** Talking about family food habits.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun *spaghetti* → *it*; person → *he*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + verb-s + U noun · *He eats it every day.*
5. **Sentence structure:** **My brother** (S) → **loves** (V) → **spaghetti** (O) · **He** (S) → **eats** (V) → **it** (O) → **every day**.
6. **Subject:** *My brother* · *He*.
7. **Verb:** *loves* · *eats*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *spaghetti* · *it* · *every day* (time).
10. **Word by word:** *spaghetti* — U in English (one dish, not *spaghettis*) · *it* — replaces *spaghetti* · *every day* — two words for "daily".
11. **How the rules work together:** he/she/it + verb-**s**; U noun → *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My grandmother makes soup on Sundays. It's delicious* (EX-021-514).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronouns match; verbs agree.
14. **Other versions:** *My sisters love spaghetti. They eat it every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *He eats them every day.* → ✅ *it* · ❌ *He eat it everyday.* → ✅ *eats … every day*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My brother's crazy about spaghetti — he has it every day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *every day* (two words) = daily; *everyday* (one word) is an adjective.
18. **Pronunciation:** "spuh-GET-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + loves + U food. He/She eats it + time.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad loves rice. He eats it with everything.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *spaghetti*? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-564 — "This pan is too heavy. I can't lift it."

1. **Meaning:** The pan weighs too much; I'm not strong enough to pick it up. 🍳💪
2. **Situation:** Cooking with a big iron pan.
3. **Main lesson:** *this pan* → *it* as an object.
4. **Grammar used:** *This* + noun + *is too* + adjective · *I can't* + verb + *it*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **This pan** (S) → **is** → **too heavy** · **I** (S) → **can't lift** (V) → **it** (O).
6. **Subject:** *This pan* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *lift*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't* (= cannot).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *too heavy* · *it* (= the pan).
10. **Word by word:** *pan* 📦 — flat metal pot for frying · *too heavy* — more than OK · *lift* — pick up.
11. **How the rules work together:** thing noun → *it*; *can't* + base verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *This cake is amazing. Did you make it?* (EX-021-511).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun; modal + base verb.
14. **Other versions:** *These pans are too heavy. I can't lift them.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I can't to lift it.* → ✅ *can't lift* · ❌ *This pan is very heavy. I can't lift it.* (OK, but *too* explains *why* you can't)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This pan weighs a ton — I can't lift it!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *can't* = *cannot*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KAHNT" (UK) / "KANT" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **This + noun + is too + adj. I can't + verb + it.**
20. **More examples:** *This box is too big. I can't carry it.*
21. **Quick check:** *can't to lift* or *can't lift*? → **Answer:** *can't lift*.

### EX-021-565 — "Our neighbours make jam. They give us a jar every year."

1. **Meaning:** The people next door make jam, and each year they give us one jar of it. 🍓🫙

8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a small bakery* · *last year*.
10. **Word by word:** *Nadia* 👩 · *opened* · *a* — first mention · *small* · *bakery* 📍 · *last year* 💭.
11. **How the rules work together:** first mention → *a*; past time → past verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Ali works as a cook in a small café* (EX-021-544) — another story opening.
13. **Why this is correct:** clear story start.
14. **Other versions:** *Nadia has a small bakery.* (now)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *nadia opened…* → ✅ *Nadia* (capital) · ❌ *…the last year.* → ✅ *last year*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Nadia opened her own little bakery last year.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *N*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NAH-dee-uh".
19. **Pattern:** **Name + opened + a + adj + noun + last year.**
20. **More examples:** *Tom opened a small café in 2020.*
21. **Quick check:** Which noun is proper? → **Answer:** *Nadia*.

---

### EX-021-595 — "Her bread and cakes are very popular."

1. **Meaning:** Lots of people like the bread and cakes she makes. 🍞🍰👍
2. **Situation:** Second line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** two nouns joined by *and* → plural verb *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *Her* + U noun + *and* + plural noun + *are very* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Her bread and cakes** (S) → **are** → **very popular**.
6. **Subject:** *Her bread and cakes* (two things).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very popular*.
10. **Word by word:** *Her* — Nadia's · *bread* U · *and* · *cakes* — C plural (different cakes) · *popular*.
11. **How the rules work together:** A + B → plural verb, even though *bread* is U.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The food was excellent, but the service was slow* (EX-021-547) — separate clauses, each *was*.
13. **Why this is correct:** two subjects → *are*.
14. **Other versions:** *Her bread is very popular.* (one U noun → *is*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Her bread and cakes is very popular.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *Her breads…* → ✅ *bread*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Everyone loves her bread and cakes.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "POP-yuh-ler".
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + and + noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *His coffee and sandwiches are cheap.*
21. **Quick check:** *is* or *are*? → **Answer:** *are* (two subjects).

---

### EX-021-596 — "On Saturdays, there is always a queue outside the shop."

1. **Meaning:** Every Saturday, people stand in a line outside the bakery. 📅🧍🧍
2. **Situation:** Last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** *there is* + C noun; plural day noun for a habit.
4. **Grammar used:** *On* + plural day, + *there is always a* + noun + *outside the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **On Saturdays,** → **there** → **is** → **always** → **a queue** (S) → **outside the shop** (P).
6. **Subject:** *a queue* (real subject).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *always* (frequency) · *outside the shop*.
10. **Word by word:** *On Saturdays* — every Saturday (plural = habit) · *there is* · *always* — goes after *be* · *queue* · *shop* 📍 (= the bakery; *the* = already mentioned).
11. **How the rules work together:** one queue → *is*; *the shop* because the reader knows which shop.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The queue outside the new burger restaurant is very long* (EX-021-590).
13. **Why this is correct:** *there is* + singular; *always* after *be*.
14. **Other versions:** *There are always lots of people outside the shop.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…there always is a queue.* → ✅ *there is always* · ❌ *On saturdays…* → ✅ *Saturdays* (capital)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There's always a queue on Saturdays.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** days have capital letters; comma after the time phrase.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SAT-er-dayz".
19. **Pattern:** **On + days, there is always a + noun + outside the + place.**
20. **More examples:** *On Mondays, there is always a lot of traffic.*
21. **Quick check:** Why *the shop* and not *a shop*? → **Answer:** the reader already knows which shop (Nadia's bakery).

## Step J — Real writing: a recipe

> 🥞 **Easy pancakes** — *Mix the **flour**, the **eggs** and the **milk** in a **bowl**. Cook the **pancakes** in a hot **pan** for two **minutes** on each **side**.*

### EX-021-597 — "Mix the flour, the eggs and the milk in a bowl."

1. **Meaning:** Put the flour, eggs and milk together in a bowl and stir them. 🥣
2. **Situation:** Step 1 of a recipe.
3. **Main lesson:** a list of nouns as the object of an imperative.
4. **Grammar used:** verb (imperative) + noun, noun *and* noun + *in a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **(You)** → **Mix** (V) → **the flour, the eggs and the milk** (O) → **in a bowl** (P).
6. **Subject:** *you* (hidden in imperatives).
7. **Verb:** *Mix*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** three nouns · *in a bowl*.
10. **Word by word:** *Mix* — stir together · *flour* — U: white powder for bread/cake · *eggs* C · *milk* U · *bowl* C.
11. **How the rules work together:** imperatives start with the base verb; *the* = the ingredients listed above.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My mother's recipe for chocolate cake is famous* (EX-021-540) — *recipe* as a noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** standard recipe instruction.
14. **Other versions:** *Mix flour, eggs and milk in a bowl.* (recipes often drop *the*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *You mixes the flour…* → ✅ *Mix the flour…* · ❌ *the flours* → ✅ *the flour* (U)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *First, mix everything together in a bowl.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** commas in a list; no comma needed before *and* (UK style).
18. **Pronunciation:** *flour* = "FLOW-er" — the same sound as *flower* 🌸!
19. **Pattern:** **Verb + the + noun, the + noun and the + noun + in a + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Put the butter, the sugar and the eggs in a large bowl.*
21. **Quick check:** Which word sounds the same as *flour*? → **Answer:** *flower*.

### EX-021-598 — "Cook the pancakes in a hot pan for two minutes on each side."

1. **Meaning:** Fry each pancake in a hot pan: 2 minutes, turn it, then 2 more minutes. 🥞⏲️
2. **Situation:** Step 2 of the recipe.
3. **Main lesson:** many nouns in one instruction: object, place, time, part.
4. **Grammar used:** imperative + *the* + plural noun + *in a* + adj + noun + *for* + number + plural noun + *on each* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Cook** (V) → **the pancakes** (O) → **in a hot pan** (P) → **for two minutes** (P) → **on each side** (P).
6. **Subject:** *you* (hidden).
7. **Verb:** *Cook*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the pancakes* · *in a hot pan* · *for two minutes* · *on each side*.
10. **Word by word:** *pancakes* C plural · *pan* C · *for* — length of time · *minutes* C plural · *each* + **singular** noun (*side*).
11. **How the rules work together:** *two* + plural (*minutes*); *each* + singular (*side*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *This pan is too heavy. I can't lift it* (EX-021-564).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct number words + nouns.
14. **Other versions:** *Fry the pancakes for two minutes on each side.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…for two minute…* → ✅ *minutes* · ❌ *…on each sides.* → ✅ *each side*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Give them two minutes each side.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** numbers in recipes are often digits: *2 minutes*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MIN-its".
19. **Pattern:** **Verb + the + noun + in a + noun + for + number + nouns + on each + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Bake the cake in the oven for forty minutes.*
21. **Quick check:** *each side* or *each sides*? → **Answer:** *each side* (singular).

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-599 — "I'll toast the bread. / I eat toast for breakfast."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I'll make the bread hot and brown. (2) Hot brown bread is my morning food.
2. **Situation:** Making breakfast.
3. **Main lesson:** *toast* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I'll* + verb + *the* + noun · *I eat* + U noun + *for* + meal.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **'ll toast** (V) → **the bread** (O) · (2) **I** → **eat** (V) → **toast** (O) → **for breakfast**.
6. **Subject:** *I* (both).
7. **Verb:** (1) *'ll toast* · (2) *eat*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *'ll* (*will*) · (2) none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the bread* · *toast* · *for breakfast*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *toast* after *'ll* → verb · (2) *toast* after *eat* (object position) → noun (U).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides: after a helping verb → verb; in the object slot → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Yesterday, I burned the toast* (EX-021-573) — noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word fits its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Can you toast two slices?* · *I'd like toast with butter.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I eat a toast.* → ✅ *toast* / *a slice of toast* · ❌ *I'll toasting the bread.* → ✅ *toast*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Want some toast?* (Also: *Let's make a toast!* = raise glasses and drink — a different meaning, C.)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TOHST" (noun and verb the same).
19. **Pattern:** **I'll + verb + the + noun / I eat + U noun + for + meal**
20. **More examples:** *I'll butter the toast. / Where's the butter?*
21. **Quick check:** In *I eat toast*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-600 — "Please taste the sauce. / This sauce has a strange taste."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Try a little of the sauce. (2) The sauce has an unusual flavour. 🥄🤔
2. **Situation:** Cooking together; something seems wrong.
3. **Main lesson:** *taste* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *Please* + verb + *the* + noun · *This* + noun + *has a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **(You)** → **taste** (V) → **the sauce** (O) · (2) **This sauce** (S) → **has** (V) → **a strange taste** (O).
6. **Subject:** (1) hidden *you* · (2) *This sauce*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *taste* · (2) *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the sauce* · *a strange taste*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *taste* at the start of a command → verb · (2) *a strange taste* — after *a* + adjective → noun · *sauce* 📦 — liquid you put on food.
11. **How the rules work together:** after *a* (+ adjective) → always a noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She has expensive taste* (EX-021-479) — *taste* = style (U); here *a taste* = flavour (C).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word fits its position.
14. **Other versions:** *It tastes strange.* (verb) · *The taste is strange.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *This sauce has a strange tasty.* → ✅ *taste* (*tasty* is an adjective = delicious) · ❌ *Please taste at the sauce.* → ✅ *taste the sauce*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Taste this — does it taste funny to you?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TAYST" · *sauce* "SAWS".
19. **Pattern:** **Please + verb + the + noun / This + noun + has a + adj + noun**
20. **More examples:** *Smell the flowers. / The flowers have a lovely smell.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a strange taste*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun (after *a* + adjective).
