**Short reminders:** **P** = proper noun → capital · **C** = common noun → small. The big rules for **time** in this part:
- **Days, months, holidays, festivals** → capital: *Sunday, April, Diwali, Halloween, Valentine's Day*.
- **Seasons** → small: *spring, summer, autumn (fall), winter*. Words like *day, week, month, year, weekend, holiday, festival* → small (unless part of a name: *Mother's Day*, *the Edinburgh Festival*).
- **Famous events and history periods** often take *the* + capitals: *the Olympics, the World Cup, the Middle Ages, the Second World War*.
- Prepositions: **on** + day/date (*on Sunday, on 31 October*), **in** + month/season/year (*in April, in spring*), **at** + some holidays (*at Christmas, at Easter*).

> 🔁 **From Parts 01–04:** days, months and holidays take capitals ([EX-022-016](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-01.md), [EX-022-060](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-02.md)); seasons are small ([EX-022-059](examples-022-common-and-proper-nouns-part-02.md)). In this bank, **P** in step 10 means *proper noun*.

**New words in this part:** festival · celebrate · fireworks · parade · candle · harvest · pumpkin · term · period · century · blossom · invitation.

## Step A — Time names: short sentences

### EX-022-201 — "My birthday is in April."

1. **Meaning:** The day I was born comes in the month of April. 🎂
2. **Situation:** A new friend asks about your birthday.
3. **Main lesson:** *birthday* (common) + **month** (proper).
4. **Grammar used:** *is in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My birthday** (S) → **is** → **in April** (time).
6. **Subject:** *My birthday*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in April*.
10. **Word by word:** *My* · *birthday* — C: everyone has one → small · *is* · *in* · **April** — P: month.
11. **How the rules work together:** *in* + month; *birthday* is not a holiday name, so small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My birthday isn't in June. It's in July* (EX-022-049).
13. **Why this is correct:** capital *A* for the month; *in*.
14. **Other versions:** *My birthday is on 12 April.* (*on* + date) · *I was born in April.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My Birthday is in april.* → ✅ *birthday … April* · ❌ *on April* → ✅ *in April*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My birthday's in April.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** short form *Apr*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "AY-prul".
19. **Pattern:** **My birthday is in + Month.**
20. **More examples:** *Her birthday is in November.*
21. **Quick check:** *in April* or *on April*? → **Answer:** *in April*.

### EX-022-202 — "Saturday is my favourite day."

1. **Meaning:** Of all the days of the week, I like Saturday most. 😄
2. **Situation:** Chatting about the week.
3. **Main lesson:** **Day name** (proper) vs the word **day** (common).
4. **Grammar used:** day + *is my favourite* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Saturday** (S) → **is** → **my favourite day** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Saturday*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my favourite day*.
10. **Word by word:** **Saturday** — P · *is* · *my* · *favourite* · *day* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** the name of the day = capital; the general word *day* = small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Today is Friday* (EX-022-016).
13. **Why this is correct:** capital *S*; small *day*.
14. **Other versions:** *My favourite day is Saturday.* · *I love Saturdays.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *my favourite Day* → ✅ *day* · ❌ *The Saturday is …* → ✅ *Saturday is …*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Saturdays are the best!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** short form *Sat*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SAT-er-day".
19. **Pattern:** **Day name + is my favourite day.**
20. **More examples:** *Friday is my favourite day.*
21. **Quick check:** Common or proper: *day*? → **Answer:** common.

### EX-022-203 — "Diwali is the festival of lights."

1. **Meaning:** Diwali is a festival known for its lights and lamps. 🪔✨
2. **Situation:** Explaining a Hindu festival to a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** **Festival name** (proper) + the word **festival** (common).
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is the* + noun + *of* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Diwali** (S) → **is** → **the festival of lights** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Diwali*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the festival of lights*.
10. **Word by word:** **Diwali** — P · *is* · *the* · *festival* — C · *of* · *lights* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** the description *the festival of lights* stays small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We celebrate Eid with our family* (EX-022-024).
13. **Why this is correct:** capital for the festival name; no *the* before *Diwali*.
14. **Other versions:** *Diwali is called the festival of lights.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *diwali* → ✅ *Diwali* · ❌ *the Diwali* → ✅ *Diwali* · ❌ *the Festival of Lights* (as a description) → ✅ small is safer
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Diwali — the festival of lights!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** also spelled *Deepavali*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "dih-WAH-lee".
19. **Pattern:** **Festival + is the festival of + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Holi is the festival of colours.*
21. **Quick check:** Common or proper: *festival*? → **Answer:** common.

### EX-022-204 — "Halloween is on 31 October."

1. **Meaning:** The holiday Halloween happens on the last day of October. 🎃
2. **Situation:** Planning a costume party.
3. **Main lesson:** **Holiday + date**: *on* + day number + month.
4. **Grammar used:** *is on* + date.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Halloween** (S) → **is** → **on 31 October** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Halloween*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *on 31 October*.
10. **Word by word:** **Halloween** — P · *is* · *on* · *31* · **October** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *on* + exact date; *in* + month only.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A: When is your exam? B: On Wednesday, the 5th of June* (EX-022-088).
13. **Why this is correct:** two proper nouns, both capital; *on* for a date.
14. **Other versions:** *Halloween is on October 31st.* (US)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *in 31 October* → ✅ *on 31 October* · ❌ *halloween* → ✅ *Halloween*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Halloween's on the thirty-first.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *31 October*, US *October 31*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "hal-oh-EEN"; *31 October* = "the thirty-first of October".
19. **Pattern:** **Holiday + is on + date + Month.**
20. **More examples:** *Christmas Day is on 25 December.*
21. **Quick check:** *on* or *in* + *31 October*? → **Answer:** *on*.

3. **Main lesson:** **Holiday** (capital) vs **season** (small).

### EX-022-206 — "The Olympics happen every four years."

1. **Meaning:** The Olympic Games take place once every four years. 🏅
2. **Situation:** Sports talk.
3. **Main lesson:** Big **events** often take *the* + capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *The* + event + *happen* + *every* + number + *years*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The Olympics** (S) → **happen** (V) → **every four years** (time).
6. **Subject:** *The Olympics*.
7. **Verb:** *happen* (plural form — *Olympics* looks plural).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *every four years*.
10. **Word by word:** *The* · **Olympics** — P: event · *happen* · *every* · *four* · *years* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the Olympics* usually takes a plural verb; *the Olympic Games* too.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The Philippines has more than 7,000 islands* (EX-022-078) — there one country = singular; *the Olympics* = many games → plural is usual.
13. **Why this is correct:** *the* + event; plural *happen*.
14. **Other versions:** *The Olympic Games take place every four years.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Olympics happen* → ✅ *The Olympics* · ❌ *every four year* → ✅ *years*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The Olympics come round every four years.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *Olympic* (adjective) also capital: *an Olympic medal*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "uh-LIM-piks".
19. **Pattern:** **The + Event + happen(s) every + number + years.**
20. **More examples:** *The World Cup happens every four years too.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *an ___ medal* (olympic)? → **Answer:** *Olympic*.

### EX-022-207 — "Valentine's Day is in February."

1. **Meaning:** The day for love, Valentine's Day, is in February. ❤️💌
2. **Situation:** Shopping for a card.
3. **Main lesson:** A holiday with **'s + Day** → both words capital.
4. **Grammar used:** *is in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Valentine's Day** (S) → **is** → **in February** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Valentine's Day*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in February*.
10. **Word by word:** **Valentine's Day** — P: *'s* belongs to Valentine · *is* · *in* · **February** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *Day* is capital when it's part of a holiday's name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Tomorrow is New Year's Day* (EX-022-060).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals on both words; *in* + month.
14. **Other versions:** *Valentine's Day is on 14 February.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Valentine's day* → ✅ *Day* · ❌ *Valentines Day* → ✅ *Valentine's Day*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Valentine's is in February.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe before *s*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "VAL-un-tynz day" · "FEB-roo-air-ee" (the first *r* is often not said: "FEB-yoo-air-ee").
19. **Pattern:** **Name's Day + is in + Month.**
20. **More examples:** *Father's Day is in June.*
21. **Quick check:** *Valentine's day* or *Valentine's Day*? → **Answer:** *Valentine's Day*.

### EX-022-208 — "Chinese New Year is a big holiday."

1. **Meaning:** Chinese New Year is an important celebration. 🧧🐉
2. **Situation:** A culture class.
3. **Main lesson:** A **holiday name** (three capitals) vs the common noun *holiday*.
4. **Grammar used:** name + *is a big* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Chinese New Year** (S) → **is** → **a big holiday** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Chinese New Year*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a big holiday*.
10. **Word by word:** **Chinese New Year** — P: all three capital · *is* · *a* · *big* · *holiday* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** the name has capitals; the type (*holiday*) is small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Diwali is the festival of lights* (EX-022-203).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals; no article before the name.
14. **Other versions:** *Chinese New Year is also called Lunar New Year or the Spring Festival.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Chinese new year* → ✅ *New Year* · ❌ *a big Holiday* → ✅ *holiday*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Chinese New Year's huge!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *the new year* (the start of any year) → small is OK; *New Year* (the holiday) → capital.
18. **Pronunciation:** "chy-NEEZ nyoo YEER".
19. **Pattern:** **Holiday Name + is a big holiday.**
20. **More examples:** *Eid al-Fitr is a big holiday.*
21. **Quick check:** How many capitals in *Chinese New Year*? → **Answer:** three.

1. **Meaning:** In this place, the month of August has very hot weather. ☀️🥵
2. **Situation:** Advising a tourist when to visit.
3. **Main lesson:** A **month as the subject** → capital, no article.
4. **Grammar used:** month + *is very* + adjective + *here*.

7. **Verb:** *is*.

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very hot*, *here*.
10. **Word by word:** **August** — P · *is* · *very* · *hot* · *here*.
11. **How the rules work together:** month names never take *the* when we talk about them in general.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *October is cold* (EX-022-020).
13. **Why this is correct:** capital *A*; no article.
14. **Other versions:** *It's very hot here in August.*

17. **Writing & punctuation:** short form *Aug*; *august* (small) is a rare adjective meaning "important".

19. **Pattern:** **Month + is + adj + here.**
20. **More examples:** *January is very cold here.*
21. **Quick check:** *The August* or *August*? → **Answer:** *August*.

## Step B — Time names and pronouns

### EX-022-211 — "We love Diwali. It is so colourful."

1. **Meaning:** We really enjoy Diwali because it is full of colour. 🎆
2. **Situation:** Telling a classmate about your festival.
3. **Main lesson:** A festival name → replaced by **it**.
4. **Grammar used:** *love* + name; *It is so* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **love** (V) → **Diwali** (O). **It** (S) → **is** → **so colourful** (B).
6. **Subject:** *We*, *It*.
7. **Verb:** *love*, *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *Diwali*; *so colourful*.
10. **Word by word:** *We* · *love* · **Diwali** — P · *It* — = Diwali · *is* · *so* · *colourful*.
11. **How the rules work together:** events and festivals → *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Diwali is the festival of lights* (EX-022-203).
13. **Why this is correct:** *it* for a thing/event; capital name.
14. **Other versions:** *We love Diwali because it is so colourful.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She is so colourful* → ✅ *It* · ❌ *the Diwali* → ✅ *Diwali*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We love Diwali — it's so colourful!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *colourful*, US *colorful*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KUL-er-ful".
19. **Pattern:** **We love + Festival. It is + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *We love Carnival. It is so loud and happy.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *Diwali*? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-022-212 — "Halloween is fun. Children love it."

1. **Meaning:** Halloween is an enjoyable holiday, and kids love it. 👻🍬
2. **Situation:** Talking about holidays.
3. **Main lesson:** Holiday name → **it** as an **object**.
4. **Grammar used:** *is* + adjective; *love* + *it*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Halloween** (S) → **is** → **fun** (B). **Children** (S) → **love** (V) → **it** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Halloween*, *Children*.
7. **Verb:** *is*, *love*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *fun*; *it*.
10. **Word by word:** **Halloween** — P · *is* · *fun* · *Children* — C, plural · *love* · *it* — = Halloween.
11. **How the rules work together:** *it* works as subject and object; *children* is plural → *love*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Spanish is a beautiful language. Millions of people speak it* (EX-022-042).
13. **Why this is correct:** *it* after the verb; plural verb.
14. **Other versions:** *Children love Halloween because it's fun.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Children loves it* → ✅ *love* · ❌ *Children love him* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Kids love Halloween!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two short sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHIL-drun".
19. **Pattern:** **Holiday + is + adj. People + love + it.**
20. **More examples:** *Christmas is magical. Everyone enjoys it.*
21. **Quick check:** *Children love ___* (Halloween)? → **Answer:** *it*.

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *tomorrow*; *it*.

11. **How the rules work together:** fixed events use the present simple for the future; *it* = the World Cup.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The Olympics happen every four years* (EX-022-206).
13. **Why this is correct:** *the* + event; *it* as object.
14. **Other versions:** *The World Cup begins tomorrow.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *World Cup starts* → ✅ *The World Cup* · ❌ *the world cup* → ✅ *World Cup* · ❌ *millions people* → ✅ *millions of people*

### EX-022-215 — "Hanukkah lasts eight days. Families light candles during it."

1. **Meaning:** The Jewish festival Hanukkah goes on for eight days, and families light candles in that time. 🕎🕯️
2. **Situation:** Learning about world festivals.
3. **Main lesson:** Festival name → **it** after a preposition (*during it*).
4. **Grammar used:** *lasts* + time; *during* + *it*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Hanukkah** (S) → **lasts** (V) → **eight days**. **Families** (S) → **light** (V) → **candles** (O) → **during it**.
6. **Subject:** *Hanukkah*, *Families*.
7. **Verb:** *lasts*, *light*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *eight days*; *candles*; *during it*.
10. **Word by word:** **Hanukkah** — P · *lasts* · *eight days* — C · *Families* — C · *light* · *candles* — C · *during* · *it* — = Hanukkah.

3. **Main lesson:** *the month* (common) + *Saturday* + a **square name**.

5. **Sentence structure:** **On the last Saturday of the month** (time), **farmers** (S) → **sell** (V) → **apples and pumpkins** (O) → **in Market Square**.
6. **Subject:** *farmers*.
7. **Verb:** *sell*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *apples and pumpkins*; *in Market Square*.
10. **Word by word:** *On the last* · **Saturday** · *of the month* — C · *farmers* — C · *sell* · *apples* · *and* · *pumpkins* — C · *in* · **Market Square** — P.
11. **How the rules work together:** *the market* (common) vs *Market Square* (name).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Times Square is always bright* (EX-022-158).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural *farmers* → *sell*; capitals on names.
14. **Other versions:** *Farmers sell apples and pumpkins in Market Square on the last Saturday.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *farmers sells* → ✅ *sell* · ❌ *in market square* → ✅ *Market Square* · ❌ *of the Month* → ✅ *month*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *On the last Saturday, the farmers come to Market Square.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the long time phrase.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PUMP-kinz".
19. **Pattern:** **On the last + Day + of the month, S + verb + O + in + Square.**
20. **More examples:** *On the first Sunday of the month, artists sell paintings in Church Square.*
21. **Quick check:** Common or proper: *month* here? → **Answer:** common.

### EX-022-246 — "In the evening, everyone dances until midnight."

1. **Meaning:** Later that day, all the people dance until 12 o'clock at night. 💃🕛
2. **Situation:** The last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** Parts of the day (*evening, midnight*) are **common nouns** → small.
4. **Grammar used:** *In the evening*, *everyone* + singular verb + *until* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **In the evening** (time), **everyone** (S) → **dances** (V) → **until midnight** (time).
6. **Subject:** *everyone*.
7. **Verb:** *dances*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *until midnight*.
10. **Word by word:** *In* · *the* · *evening* — C · *everyone* — takes a singular verb · *dances* · *until* · *midnight* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** *everyone* = singular → *dances*; times of day are small.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Chinatown is very busy at night* (EX-022-160) — *night* small.
13. **Why this is correct:** singular verb with *everyone*; no capitals except *In*.
14. **Other versions:** *Everyone dances until midnight in the evening.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *everyone dance* → ✅ *dances* · ❌ *until Midnight* → ✅ *midnight* · ❌ *In the Evening* → ✅ *evening*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Then everyone dances till midnight!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after *In the evening*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "EEV-ning" · "MID-nyt".
19. **Pattern:** **In the + part of day, everyone + verb-s + until + time.**
20. **More examples:** *In the morning, everyone has breakfast together.*
21. **Quick check:** How many proper nouns are in the whole story? → **Answer:** 4 — *October, Harvest Festival, Saturday, Market Square*.

## Step J — Time in real writing: an invitation and a diary

### EX-022-247 — "You are invited to our Spring Party on Saturday, 26 April, at 7 p.m."

1. **Meaning:** A written invitation to a party called the Spring Party, on Saturday 26 April at 7 in the evening. 💌🌷
2. **Situation:** An invitation card.
3. **Main lesson:** An **event's name** takes capitals (*Spring Party*), even with a season word in it.
4. **Grammar used:** passive *are invited to* + event + day, date + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **You** (S) → **are invited** (V) → **to our Spring Party** → **on Saturday, 26 April,** → **at 7 p.m.**
6. **Subject:** *You*.
7. **Verb:** *invited*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *are* (passive).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** the event, the date, the time.
10. **Word by word:** *You are invited* · *to* · *our* · **Spring Party** — P: name of the event · *on* · **Saturday** · *26* · **April** · *at* · *7 p.m.* — evening time.
11. **How the rules work together:** *spring* is small as a season, but capital inside an event's name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Every October, our town has a Harvest Festival* (EX-022-244).
13. **Why this is correct:** formal passive; capitals for names; commas around the date.
14. **Other versions:** *Please come to our Spring Party on Saturday 26 April at 7 p.m.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *on saturday, 26 april* → ✅ *Saturday, 26 April* · ❌ *at 7 p.m. o'clock* → ✅ *at 7 p.m.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Come to our Spring Party — Saturday the 26th, seven o'clock!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *p.m.* (after midday) / *a.m.* (before midday); also written *pm*, *PM*.
18. **Pronunciation:** *p.m.* = "pee-EM".
19. **Pattern:** **You are invited to our + Event Name + on + Day, date + Month, at + time.**
20. **More examples:** *You are invited to our Summer Barbecue on Sunday, 6 July, at 1 p.m.*
21. **Quick check:** Capital or small: *spring* in *our Spring Party*? → **Answer:** capital (part of the name).

### EX-022-248 — "Wednesday, 14 May — Today was the first warm day of the year."

1. **Meaning:** A diary entry: on Wednesday 14 May, the weather was warm for the first time this year. 📓☀️
2. **Situation:** Writing a diary.
3. **Main lesson:** A **diary date** at the top (capitals) + common time words in the sentence.
4. **Grammar used:** date heading + past simple *was*.
5. **Sentence structure:** heading **Wednesday, 14 May** — **Today** (S) → **was** → **the first warm day of the year** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Today*.
7. **Verb:** *was*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the first warm day of the year*.
10. **Word by word:** **Wednesday** · *14* · **May** · *Today* · *was* · *the first warm day* — C · *of the year* — C.
11. **How the rules work together:** in diaries we write about today in the **past**, because the day is finished.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *What day is it today?* (EX-022-216).
13. **Why this is correct:** capitals on the day and month; *day* and *year* small.
14. **Other versions:** *Wed 14 May — First warm day this year!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *wednesday, 14 may* → ✅ *Wednesday, 14 May* · ❌ *the first warm Day* → ✅ *day*
16. **Natural spoken English:** — (written diary style)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma after the day; a dash (—) can separate the date from the text.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WENZ-day, the fourteenth of May".
19. **Pattern:** **Day, date + Month — Today was …**
20. **More examples:** *Sunday, 2 March — Today we went to the beach.*
21. **Quick check:** Which letter is silent in *Wednesday*? → **Answer:** the first *d*.

## Step K — Same word: common or proper?

### EX-022-249 — "Soldiers march in the parade. / My birthday is in March."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Soldiers walk in step in the parade. (2) My birthday is in the month of March. 🪖📅
2. **Situation:** A parade on TV; talking about birthdays.
3. **Main lesson:** *march* (a verb: walk in step) vs *March* (a month, proper noun).
4. **Grammar used:** present simple *march*; *is in* + month.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Soldiers** (S) → **march** (V) → **in the parade**. · **My birthday** (S) → **is** → **in March**.
6. **Subject:** *Soldiers*; *My birthday*.
7. **Verb:** *march*; *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in the parade*; *in March*.
10. **Word by word:** *march* — verb → small · **March** — P: month → capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** verbs are never capital in the middle of a sentence; month names always are.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *May I come in? / My exam is in May* (EX-022-098).
13. **Why this is correct:** small verb; capital month.
14. **Other versions:** *They march every March!* (both in one sentence)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Soldiers March in the parade.* → ✅ *march* · ❌ *My birthday is in march.* → ✅ *March*
16. **Natural spoken English:** both sound the same: "MARCH".
17. **Writing & punctuation:** only the capital shows the difference.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MARCH".
19. **Pattern:** **march (verb) · March (month).**
20. **More examples:** *We march in March.*
21. **Quick check:** Correct the capitals: *the band will March in march.* → **Answer:** *The band will march in March.*

### EX-022-250 — "I love spring. / We live on Spring Street."

1. **Meaning:** (1) My favourite season is spring. (2) Our home is on a street called Spring Street. 🌷🏘️
2. **Situation:** Talking about seasons; giving an address.
3. **Main lesson:** *spring* (season, common → small) vs *Spring Street* (a street name → capital).
4. **Grammar used:** *love* + noun; *live on* + street.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** (S) → **love** (V) → **spring** (O). · **We** (S) → **live** (V) → **on Spring Street**.
6. **Subject:** *I*; *We*.
7. **Verb:** *love*; *live*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *spring*; *on Spring Street*.
10. **Word by word:** *spring* — C: a season → small · **Spring Street** — P: a street's name → capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** seasons are small — until they become part of a name.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *You are invited to our Spring Party* (EX-022-247).
13. **Why this is correct:** small season; capital name.
14. **Other versions:** *In spring, Spring Street is full of flowers!*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I love Spring.* → ✅ *spring* · ❌ *on spring street* → ✅ *Spring Street*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Spring's my favourite season.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** same with *summer*: *in summer* / *Summer Road*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SPRING".
19. **Pattern:** **season (small) · Season + Street/Party/Festival (capital, name).**
20. **More examples:** *We love winter. / The Winter Olympics are exciting.*
21. **Quick check:** Correct the capitals: *in Spring, we walk on spring street.* → **Answer:** *In spring, we walk on Spring Street.*
