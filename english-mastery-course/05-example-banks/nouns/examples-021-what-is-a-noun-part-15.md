**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **City nouns to watch:** *the police* is always **plural** (*The police **are**…*). *Pollution, rubbish, traffic, life* (in general) are **U**. Street and city names are **proper nouns** with capital letters and usually **no** *the* (*Green Street, Tokyo*) ([L-022](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-022-common-and-proper-nouns.md)).

**New words in this part:** pollution · church · fountain · police · ancient · neighbourhood · pavement · musician · mayor · village · zoo · stadium · worker · tradition · town hall · clock tower · bench · playground · population · villager · streetlight · council · candle.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-701 — "The streets are clean."

1. **Meaning:** There is no rubbish on the streets. 🛣️✨
2. **Situation:** A visitor is surprised by a tidy city.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural place noun as the subject → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The streets** (S) → **are** → **clean**.
6. **Subject:** *The streets*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *clean*.
10. **Word by word:** *the* — the streets here · *streets* 📍 — roads in a town with houses/shops · *clean*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *He knows every street in the city* (EX-021-396).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective; agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *The street is clean.* · *Are the streets clean?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The streets is clean.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The streets are cleans.* → ✅ *clean* (adjectives never take *-s*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The streets here are spotless!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STREETS" · "KLEEN".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The parks are green. · The buildings are tall.*
21. **Quick check:** Singular of *streets*? → **Answer:** *street*.

### EX-021-702 — "The cinema is new."

1. **Meaning:** The building where we watch films opened recently. 🎬🆕
2. **Situation:** Talking about changes in your town.
3. **Main lesson:** A public-place noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The cinema** (S) → **is** → **new**.
6. **Subject:** *The cinema*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *new*.
10. **Word by word:** *cinema* 📍 — UK (US *movie theater*) · *new*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The museum is free* (EX-021-654).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The cinema is old.* · *Is the cinema new?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Cinema is new.* → ✅ *The cinema* · ❌ *The cinema is a new.* → ✅ *new* (no *a* before an adjective alone)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They've just opened a new cinema.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SIN-uh-muh".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The hospital is modern. · The station is old.*
21. **Quick check:** US words for *cinema*? → **Answer:** *movie theater*.

### EX-021-703 — "Pollution is a big problem."

1. **Meaning:** Dirty air and water cause serious trouble. 🏭💨
2. **Situation:** A news story about a big city.
3. **Main lesson:** U idea noun as the subject + C noun after *be*.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is a* + adjective + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Pollution** (S) → **is** → **a big problem** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Pollution*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a big problem* (noun after *be*).
10. **Word by word:** *pollution* 💭 — dirt in air, water or land (U) · *a big problem* — C, needs *a*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U subject → *is*, no article; C complement → *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Love is important* (EX-021-006) — another U idea noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is* + *a* + C noun.
14. **Other versions:** *Air pollution is a big problem in this city.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The pollution is a big problem.* (general → no *the*) · ❌ *Pollutions are…* → ✅ *Pollution is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Pollution's a huge problem here.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "puh-LOO-shun".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is a + adjective + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Noise is a big problem. · Traffic is a serious problem.*
21. **Quick check:** C or U: *pollution*? → **Answer:** U.

### EX-021-704 — "The church is very old."

1. **Meaning:** The church was built a long time ago. ⛪
2. **Situation:** A walk around a historic town.
3. **Main lesson:** place noun + *very* + adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is very* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The church** (S) → **is** → **very old**.
6. **Subject:** *The church*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very old*.
10. **Word by word:** *church* 📍 — Christian building for prayer (plural *churches*) · *very* · *old*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*; *very* goes before the adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *-ch* plurals add **-es**: *church → churches* ([L-024](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-024-plural-spelling-rules.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adverb + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The churches are very old.* · *How old is the church?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The church is old very.* → ✅ *very old* · ❌ *the churchs* → ✅ *churches*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *That church is ancient.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHURCH" · plural "CHUR-chiz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is very + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The bridge is very long. · The castle is very big.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *church*? → **Answer:** *churches*.

### EX-021-705 — "Parks are important for children."

1. **Meaning:** Children need parks (to play and be outside). 🌳🧒
2. **Situation:** A town meeting about a new park.
3. **Main lesson:** general plural subject + plural person noun after *for*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective + *for* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Parks** (S) → **are** → **important** → **for children** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Parks* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *important* · *for children*.
10. **Word by word:** *parks* 📍 · *important* · *for* · *children* — irregular plural of *child*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plurals → no *the*; plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *child → children* ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)) · *Please don't park here. / There's a park near our house* (EX-021-200).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + correct irregular plural.
14. **Other versions:** *A park is important for children.* · *Parks are good for everyone.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…for childrens.* → ✅ *children* · ❌ *The parks are important for the children.* (general → no *the*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Kids need parks.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "im-POR-tunt" · "CHIL-drun".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective + for + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Libraries are important for students.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *child*? → **Answer:** *children*.

### EX-021-706 — "The fountain in the square is beautiful."

1. **Meaning:** The water feature in the town square is lovely. ⛲
2. **Situation:** Sightseeing in an old town.
3. **Main lesson:** subject noun + place phrase → *is*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *in the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The fountain in the square** (S) → **is** → **beautiful**.
6. **Subject:** head **fountain**.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *beautiful*.
10. **Word by word:** *fountain* 📦 — structure that throws water into the air · *in the square* — *square* 📍 = open area in a town.
11. **How the rules work together:** head *fountain* singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *There is a market in the main square every Sunday* (EX-021-485).
13. **Why this is correct:** head-noun agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *The fountains in the square are beautiful.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The fountain in the square are beautiful.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *…on the square.* (UK usually *in the square*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *That fountain in the square is gorgeous.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FOWN-tin".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + in the + place + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The statue in the park is famous.*
21. **Quick check:** Head noun? → **Answer:** *fountain*.

### EX-021-707 — "The police are here."

1. **Meaning:** Police officers have arrived. 🚓👮
2. **Situation:** After a car accident in the street.
3. **Main lesson:** *the police* is always **plural** → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the police* + *are* + place word.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The police** (S) → **are** → **here**.
6. **Subject:** *The police*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *here*.
10. **Word by word:** *the police* 👮 — the group of officers (no *-s*, but plural meaning) · *here*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *police* = plural noun without *-s* → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** nouns with no *-s* but plural meaning (*people, children*) ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** *police* always takes a plural verb.
14. **Other versions:** *A police officer is here.* (one person → *is*) · *The police are coming.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The police is here.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *A police came.* → ✅ *A police officer came.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The police are here now.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "puh-LEES".
19. **Pattern:** **The police + are + …**
20. **More examples:** *The police are looking for the thief.*
21. **Quick check:** *The police is* or *are*? → **Answer:** *are*.

### EX-021-708 — "Rome is an ancient city."

1. **Meaning:** Rome is a very, very old city. 🏛️🇮🇹
2. **Situation:** A history lesson.
3. **Main lesson:** proper noun subject + *an* + adjective + common noun.
4. **Grammar used:** city name + *is an* + adjective + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Rome** (S) → **is** → **an ancient city** (B).
6. **Subject:** *Rome* (proper noun).
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *an ancient city*.
10. **Word by word:** *Rome* — capital, no *the* · *an* — before the vowel sound of *ancient* · *ancient* — thousands of years old · *city* (C).
11. **How the rules work together:** proper noun (no article) + *be* + *an* + adj + common noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Paris is a beautiful city* (EX-021-025).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct article before a vowel sound.
14. **Other versions:** *Rome and Athens are ancient cities.* (plural: *city → cities*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Rome is a ancient city.* → ✅ *an ancient* · ❌ *The Rome is…* → ✅ *Rome*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Rome's one of the oldest cities in the world.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *R*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "AYN-shunt".
19. **Pattern:** **City + is an/a + adjective + city.**
20. **More examples:** *Tokyo is a modern city. · Cairo is an old city.*
21. **Quick check:** *a ancient* or *an ancient*? → **Answer:** *an ancient*.

### EX-021-709 — "The neighbourhood is safe."

1. **Meaning:** The area where we live doesn't have much crime. 🏘️🛡️
2. **Situation:** Looking for a flat to rent.
3. **Main lesson:** long noun (*neighbour* + *-hood*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The neighbourhood** (S) → **is** → **safe**.
6. **Subject:** *The neighbourhood*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *safe* — not dangerous.
10. **Word by word:** *neighbourhood* 📍 — the area around your home (*neighbour* + *-hood* = noun ending for a group/state, like *childhood*) · *safe*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our neighbours make jam* (EX-021-565) — *neighbour* is a person; *neighbourhood* is a place.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *Is the neighbourhood safe?* · *It's a safe neighbourhood.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The neighbour is safe.* (= the person) → ✅ *neighbourhood* (the area) · ❌ *The neighbourhood is safety.* → ✅ *safe* (*safety* is a noun)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's a really safe area.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *neighbourhood* / US *neighborhood*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NAY-ber-hood".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The area is quiet. · The village is friendly.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun or adjective: *safety*? → **Answer:** noun (*safe* is the adjective).

## Step B — City nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-711 — "The new library is huge. Have you seen it?"

1. **Meaning:** The new library is very big; have you visited it? 📚🏛️
2. **Situation:** Chatting with a neighbour.
3. **Main lesson:** *the new library* → *it* (object).
4. **Grammar used:** *the new* + noun + *is* + adj · *Have you seen it?*
5. **Sentence structure:** **The new library** (S) → **is** → **huge** · **Have** → **you** (S) → **seen** (V) → **it** (O)?
6. **Subject:** *The new library* · *you*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *seen*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Have* (present perfect question).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *huge* · *it*.
10. **Word by word:** *library* 📍 · *huge* · *Have you seen* — "at any time until now?" · *it* — the library.
11. **How the rules work together:** place → *it*; present perfect asks about experience up to now ([L-101](../../02-intermediate-grammar/tenses/lesson-101-present-perfect-form.md) later).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The library has thousands of books* (EX-021-028).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *The new parks are huge. Have you seen them?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Have you seen her?* → ✅ *it* · ❌ *Did you saw it?* → ✅ *Did you see it?* / *Have you seen it?*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Have you been to the new library? It's massive!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "LY-bruh-ree" (not "LY-berry").
19. **Pattern:** **The new + noun + is + adj. Have you seen it?**
20. **More examples:** *The new bridge is amazing. Have you seen it?*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *the library*? → **Answer:** *it*.

### EX-021-712 — "The street musicians are very good. People give them money."

1. **Meaning:** The musicians who play outside are great; people give them coins. 🎸🪙
2. **Situation:** Walking through a busy city centre.
3. **Main lesson:** *street musicians* → *them* as the indirect object.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + plural noun + *are very* + adj · *People give them money.*
5. **Sentence structure:** **The street musicians** (S) → **are** → **very good** · **People** (S) → **give** (V) → **them** (O1) → **money** (O2).
6. **Subject:** *The street musicians* (head **musicians**) · *People*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *give*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very good* · *them* (who receives) · *money* (what).
10. **Word by word:** *street* — describes where they play · *musicians* 👩 — people who play music · *People* — irregular plural (no *-s*) · *money* — U.
11. **How the rules work together:** *people* is plural → *give* (no *-s*); plural → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our neighbours make jam. They give us a jar every year* (EX-021-565).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + two objects in the right order.
14. **Other versions:** *People give money to them.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *People gives them money.* → ✅ *give* · ❌ *People give them moneys.* → ✅ *money*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The buskers here are brilliant — people throw them coins.* (*busker* = street musician)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "myoo-ZISH-unz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + adj. People give them + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The waiters are friendly. Customers give them tips.*
21. **Quick check:** *People give* or *People gives*? → **Answer:** *People give*.

### EX-021-713 — "My town has a castle. Tourists visit it every day."

1. **Meaning:** There's a castle in my town, and visitors come to see it daily. 🏰📸
2. **Situation:** Describing your hometown.
3. **Main lesson:** *a castle* (first mention) → *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *has a* + noun · plural noun + verb + *it* + *every day*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My town** (S) → **has** (V) → **a castle** (O) · **Tourists** (S) → **visit** (V) → **it** (O) → **every day**.
6. **Subject:** *My town* · *Tourists*.
7. **Verb:** *has* · *visit*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a castle* · *it* · *every day*.
10. **Word by word:** *town* 📍 · *has* · *a castle* — new info → *a* · *Tourists* — plural · *it* — the castle.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *has*; plural subject → *visit* (no *-s*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *We visited three museums and a castle* (EX-021-648).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *My town has two castles. Tourists visit them every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Tourists visits it.* → ✅ *visit* · ❌ *My town have a castle.* → ✅ *has*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There's a castle in my town — it's full of tourists every day.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KAH-sl" (silent *t*).
19. **Pattern:** **My + place + has a + noun. Plural noun + verb + it + every day.**
20. **More examples:** *Our city has a zoo. Families visit it at weekends.*
21. **Quick check:** *Tourists visit* or *visits*? → **Answer:** *visit*.

### EX-021-714 — "Mrs Brown is our mayor. Everyone likes her."

1. **Meaning:** Mrs Brown is the leader of our town, and all people like her. 👩‍💼🏙️

4. **Grammar used:** title + name + *is our* + noun · *Everyone likes her.*

6. **Subject:** *Mrs Brown* · *Everyone*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *likes*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *our mayor* · *her*.
10. **Word by word:** *Mrs* — title for a married woman (also *Ms*) · *mayor* 👩 — leader of a town/city · *Everyone* — singular verb (*likes*) · *her*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *everyone* takes a singular verb; woman → *her*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Mr Ali is my neighbour. I like him* (EX-021-038).

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Everyone like her.* → ✅ *likes* · ❌ *Everyone likes she.* → ✅ *her*

17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK: *Mrs* (no full stop) · US: *Mrs.*
18. **Pronunciation:** "MIS-iz" · "MAIR" (UK) / "MAY-er" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **Title + name + is our + noun. Everyone likes her/him.**
20. **More examples:** *Dr Khan is our doctor. Everyone trusts her.*
21. **Quick check:** *Everyone like* or *Everyone likes*? → **Answer:** *likes*.

### EX-021-715 — "These buildings are very tall. Some of them have fifty floors."

1. **Meaning:** These buildings are very high; a few have 50 levels. 🏢🏢
2. **Situation:** A tour of a modern city.
3. **Main lesson:** *these buildings* → *some of them*.
4. **Grammar used:** *These* + plural noun + *are very* + adj · *Some of them have* + number + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **These buildings** (S) → **are** → **very tall** · **Some of them** (S) → **have** (V) → **fifty floors** (O).
6. **Subject:** *These buildings* · *Some of them*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *have*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very tall* · *fifty floors*.
10. **Word by word:** *These* + plural · *buildings* 📍 · *Some of them* — a part of the group (*of* + object pronoun) · *floors* — levels.

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A village has one shop…* (second mention → *The*) · ❌ *The village have…* → ✅ *has*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There's just one shop, a school and a little church.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** commas between list items; *and* before the last.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WUN-SHOP".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + has one + noun, a + noun and a + adj + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The island has one hotel, a café and a small beach.*
21. **Quick check:** Why *the village* now? → **Answer:** it was already mentioned.

---

### EX-021-746 — "On Sundays, all the villagers meet in the square."

1. **Meaning:** Every Sunday, everyone from the village comes together in the square. 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦⛲
2. **Situation:** Last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** *all the* + plural noun (*villagers*) as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *On* + plural day, + *all the* + plural noun + verb + *in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **On Sundays,** → **all the villagers** (S) → **meet** (V) → **in the square** (P).
6. **Subject:** *all the villagers*.
7. **Verb:** *meet*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in the square*.
10. **Word by word:** *On Sundays* — every Sunday · *all the* · *villagers* 👩 — people who live in a village (*village* + *-er*) · *meet* — no *-s* (plural).
11. **How the rules work together:** plural subject → base verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *On Saturdays, there is always a queue outside the shop* (EX-021-596).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + time phrase.
14. **Other versions:** *Every Sunday, the whole village meets in the square.* (*the whole village* → singular *meets*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…all the villagers meets…* → ✅ *meet* · ❌ *…all villagers the…* → ✅ *all the villagers*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *On Sundays, everyone gets together in the square.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *S* in *Sundays*; comma after the time phrase.
18. **Pronunciation:** "VIL-i-jerz".
19. **Pattern:** **On + days, all the + plural noun + verb + in the + place.**
20. **More examples:** *On Fridays, all the students eat lunch outside.*
21. **Quick check:** Person noun made from *village*? → **Answer:** *villager*.

## Step J — Real writing: a letter to the council

> ✉️ *Dear Sir or Madam, the **streetlights** in Park Road do not work. The **road** is very dangerous for **children** at **night**. Yours faithfully, J. Okafor*

### EX-021-747 — "The streetlights in Park Road do not work."

1. **Meaning:** The lights along Park Road are broken. 💡❌
2. **Situation:** A formal complaint letter to the town council.
3. **Main lesson:** plural head noun + street name → plural verb in formal writing.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *in* + street name + *do not* + verb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The streetlights in Park Road** (S) → **do not work** (V).
6. **Subject:** head **streetlights**.
7. **Verb:** *work*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *do not* (full form — more formal than *don't*).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in Park Road* (part of the subject).
10. **Word by word:** *streetlights* — compound (*street* + *lights*) · *in Park Road* — UK often *in* for roads in letters (*on* is also fine) · *work* — function.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural head → *do not*; formal writing avoids short forms.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The lift is broken* (EX-021-658).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + formal style.
14. **Other versions:** *The streetlight outside number 12 does not work.* (singular → *does*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The streetlights … does not work.* → ✅ *do not* · ❌ *…in the Park Road.* → ✅ *in Park Road*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The streetlights on Park Road aren't working.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** formal letters: *do not* (not *don't*); capital letters in road names.
18. **Pronunciation:** "STREET-lyts".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + in + road name + do not work.**
20. **More examples:** *The traffic lights at the crossroads do not work.*
21. **Quick check:** *don't* or *do not* in a formal letter? → **Answer:** *do not*.

### EX-021-748 — "The road is very dangerous for children at night."

1. **Meaning:** At night, the dark road can hurt children. 🌙⚠️🧒
2. **Situation:** The council letter continues.
3. **Main lesson:** subject noun + plural noun after *for* + time phrase.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is very* + adj + *for* + plural noun + *at night*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The road** (S) → **is** → **very dangerous** → **for children** (P) → **at night**.
6. **Subject:** *The road*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very dangerous* · *for children* · *at night*.
10. **Word by word:** *dangerous* — can cause harm (noun: *danger*) · *for children* — general → no *the* · *at night*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → no article; *at night* fixed phrase.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Parks are important for children* (EX-021-705).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear, formal reason.
14. **Other versions:** *The road is not safe for children after dark.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…dangerous for the children in the night.* → ✅ *for children at night* · ❌ *The road is very danger.* → ✅ *dangerous*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's really dangerous for kids at night.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop; a formal letter ends with *Yours faithfully* (when you don't know the name).
18. **Pronunciation:** "DAYN-jer-us".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is very + adj + for + plural noun + at night.**
20. **More examples:** *The bridge is very slippery for cyclists in winter.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun form of *dangerous*? → **Answer:** *danger*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-749 — "Let's walk to the square. / It's a short walk to the square."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Let's go to the square on foot. (2) Going to the square on foot doesn't take long. 🚶
2. **Situation:** Deciding how to get to the square.
3. **Main lesson:** *walk* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *Let's* + verb + *to the* + noun · *It's a* + adj + noun + *to the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **(we)** → **walk** (V) → **to the square** · (2) **It** (S) → **'s** → **a short walk** (B) → **to the square**.
6. **Subject:** (1) *Let's* = *let us* · (2) *It*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *walk* · (2) *'s*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *to the square* · *a short walk*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *walk* after *Let's* → verb · (2) *a short walk* — after *a* + adjective → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** *Let's* + base verb; *a* (+ adjective) + noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Please taste the sauce. / This sauce has a strange taste* (EX-021-600).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *We walked to the square.* (verb) · *Let's go for a walk.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Let's to walk…* → ✅ *Let's walk* · ❌ *It's short walk.* → ✅ *a short walk*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's only a five-minute walk.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WAWK" (the *l* is silent).
19. **Pattern:** **Let's + verb + to the + place / It's a + adj + noun + to the + place**
20. **More examples:** *Let's drive there. / It's a long drive.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a short walk*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-750 — "This house is very old. / The new building will house fifty families."

1. **Meaning:** (1) This home is very old. (2) The new building will give homes to 50 families. 🏠 / 🏢
2. **Situation:** A town plan for new homes.
3. **Main lesson:** *house* as a noun (1) and a verb (2) — with a **different sound**!
4. **Grammar used:** *This* + noun + *is very* + adj · *the new* + noun + *will* + verb + number + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **This house** (S) → **is** → **very old** · (2) **The new building** (S) → **will house** (V) → **fifty families** (O).
6. **Subject:** *This house* · *The new building*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *house*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (2) *will*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very old* · *fifty families*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *house* after *This* → noun · (2) *house* after *will* → verb (= give a home to) · *families* — *-y* → *-ies*.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the word class; here pronunciation changes too.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I'll toast the bread. / I eat toast for breakfast* (EX-021-599).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *The museum houses many old paintings.* (verb)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The new building will houses…* → ✅ *will house* · ❌ *…fifty familys.* → ✅ *families*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The new block will have homes for fifty families.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** noun *house* = "HOWSS" (s sound) · verb *house* = "HOWZ" (z sound)!
19. **Pattern:** **This + noun + is + adj / The + noun + will + verb + number + plural noun**
20. **More examples:** *I use a computer. / The use of phones is not allowed.* (verb "YOOZ", noun "YOOS")
21. **Quick check:** How is the verb *house* pronounced? → **Answer:** "HOWZ" (with a *z* sound).
