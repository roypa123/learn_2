**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Body & health nouns to watch:** irregular plurals *foot → feet, tooth → teeth* ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)). **U** nouns: *health, medicine* (in general), *sleep, exercise* (activity), *hair* (on your head), *advice*. Illnesses: *a cold, a headache, a cough, a fever* (C, with *a*) but *flu* / *the flu* (no *a*). We use **my / your / his** with body parts, not *the*: *My head hurts*.

**New words in this part:** headache · health · medicine · bitter · dentist · sore · waiting room · pill · cream · skin · stick · temperature · cough · allergy · flu · patient · operation · shoulder · lung · cut · pain · cabinet · surgery · side effect · diet · throat · tablet · appointment · knee · swollen · wound · bandage · rest.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-801 — "My head hurts."

1. **Meaning:** I feel pain in my head. 🤕
2. **Situation:** You tell a friend why you're quiet.
3. **Main lesson:** A body-part noun as the subject, with *my*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + verb-s.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My head** (S) → **hurts** (V).
6. **Subject:** *My head*.
7. **Verb:** *hurts* (head = *it* → *-s*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:** *My* — body parts use possessives, not *the* · *head* 📦 · *hurts* — gives pain.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → verb + *-s*; body part → *my*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Babies cry* (EX-021-001) — the shortest pattern: noun + verb.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + V; correct possessive.
14. **Other versions:** *My feet hurt.* (plural → no *-s*) · *Does your head hurt?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The head hurts.* (for your own head) → ✅ *My head* · ❌ *My head hurt.* (now) → ✅ *hurts*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've got a headache.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HED" · "HURTS".
19. **Pattern:** **My + body part + hurts.**
20. **More examples:** *My back hurts. · My arm hurts.*
21. **Quick check:** *The head hurts* or *My head hurts*? → **Answer:** *My head hurts*.

### EX-021-802 — "Health is more important than money."

1. **Meaning:** Being well matters more than being rich. 💪❤️ > 💰
2. **Situation:** A grandparent gives advice.
3. **Main lesson:** two **U** idea nouns compared.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is more* + adjective + *than* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Health** (S) → **is** → **more important** → **than money**.
6. **Subject:** *Health*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *more important than money*.
10. **Word by word:** *health* 💭 — the state of your body (U) · *more important* — comparative of a long adjective · *than* · *money* — U.
11. **How the rules work together:** U nouns in general → no article; long adjectives use *more*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Time is money* (EX-021-012).
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*; correct comparative.
14. **Other versions:** *Nothing is more important than health.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The health is…* (general → *Health*) · ❌ *…importanter than…* → ✅ *more important than*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Your health matters more than money.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HELTH" (tongue between teeth for *th*).
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is more + adjective + than + U noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Love is more important than money.*
21. **Quick check:** Adjective from *health*? → **Answer:** *healthy*.

### EX-021-803 — "Her eyes are blue."

1. **Meaning:** The colour of her eyes is blue. 👀💙
2. **Situation:** Describing someone.
3. **Main lesson:** plural body part → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *Her* + plural noun + *are* + colour.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Her eyes** (S) → **are** → **blue**.
6. **Subject:** *Her eyes*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *blue*.
10. **Word by word:** *Her* · *eyes* — plural (two eyes) · *blue*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My grandmother has beautiful grey hair* (EX-021-128).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *She has blue eyes.* (adjective before the noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Her eyes is blue.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *She has eyes blue.* → ✅ *blue eyes*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's got blue eyes.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "AYZ" (rhymes with *size*).
19. **Pattern:** **Her + plural body part + are + colour.**
20. **More examples:** *His eyes are brown. · My nails are short.*
21. **Quick check:** Another way: *She has ___ eyes.* → **Answer:** *blue*.

### EX-021-805 — "The medicine is bitter."

1. **Meaning:** The medicine tastes strong and unpleasant. 💊😖
2. **Situation:** A child won't take their medicine.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun *medicine* (the liquid) + taste adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The medicine** (S) → **is** → **bitter**.
6. **Subject:** *The medicine*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *bitter* — strong, not sweet.
10. **Word by word:** *medicine* 📦 — U here (the stuff); C for kinds (*two medicines*) · *bitter*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Honey is sweet* (EX-021-558) — taste adjective.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *This medicine tastes bitter.* · *Is the medicine sweet?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The medicines is bitter.* → ✅ *The medicine is* · ❌ *The medicine is bitterly.* → ✅ *bitter*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This medicine tastes horrible!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MED-suhn" (UK often two syllables) · "BIT-er".
19. **Pattern:** **The + U noun + is + taste adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The coffee is bitter. · The lemon is sour.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite of *bitter* for taste? → **Answer:** *sweet*.

### EX-021-807 — "Exercise is good for you."

1. **Meaning:** Moving your body (sport, walking) makes you healthy. 🏃‍♀️💪
2. **Situation:** A doctor's advice.
3. **Main lesson:** *exercise* = U (activity in general).
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is good for* + pronoun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Exercise** (S) → **is** → **good** → **for you** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Exercise*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *good* · *for you*.
10. **Word by word:** *exercise* 💭 — physical activity (U) · *good for you* — healthy · *you* — people in general.
11. **How the rules work together:** U → *is*; *exercise* is C when it means a school task.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *These exercises are difficult. I don't understand them* (EX-021-215) — C, school tasks.
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Doing exercise every day is good for you.* · *Do some exercise.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Exercises are good for your health.* (for activity → *Exercise is*) · ❌ *Make exercise.* → ✅ *Do exercise* / *Take exercise*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Exercise is really good for you.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "EK-ser-syz".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is good/bad for you.**
20. **More examples:** *Sleep is good for you. · Stress is bad for you.*
21. **Quick check:** *make exercise* or *do exercise*? → **Answer:** *do exercise*.

1. **Meaning:** My back hurts a little; it feels painful. 🧍‍♂️😣
2. **Situation:** After moving furniture.
3. **Main lesson:** body part + *sore*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My back** (S) → **is** → **sore**.
6. **Subject:** *My back*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *sore* — painful.
10. **Word by word:** *back* 📦 — the back part of your body (also a direction word!) · *sore*.
11. **How the rules work together:** body part with *my*; singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My head hurts* (EX-021-801) — verb; here *be* + adjective.

## Step B — Health nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-811 — "Your hands are dirty. Wash them before dinner."

1. **Meaning:** Your hands aren't clean; clean them before we eat. 🙌🧼
2. **Situation:** A parent talks to a child.
3. **Main lesson:** *your hands* → *them*.
4. **Grammar used:** *Your* + plural noun + *are* + adj · imperative + *them* + *before* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Your hands** (S) → **are** → **dirty** · **(You)** → **Wash** (V) → **them** (O) → **before dinner** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Your hands* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *Wash*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *dirty* · *them* · *before dinner*.
10. **Word by word:** *hands* — plural · *Wash* — command · *them* — the hands · *before dinner* — meal, no article.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural object → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *These maps are old. Don't use them* (EX-021-615).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun agrees.
14. **Other versions:** *Your face is dirty. Wash it.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Wash it.* (for hands) → ✅ *them* · ❌ *…before the dinner.* → ✅ *before dinner*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Go and wash your hands before dinner!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WOSH-thum".
19. **Pattern:** **Your + plural noun + are + adj. Verb + them + before + meal.**
20. **More examples:** *Your shoes are wet. Take them off.*
21. **Quick check:** Pronoun for *hands*? → **Answer:** *them*.

### EX-021-812 — "The nurse was very kind. She held my hand."

1. **Meaning:** The nurse was nice; she took my hand to help me feel calm. 👩‍⚕️🤝
2. **Situation:** Remembering a blood test.
3. **Main lesson:** *the nurse* → *she*; body part object with *my*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *was very* + adj · *She* + past verb + *my* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The nurse** (S) → **was** → **very kind** · **She** (S) → **held** (V) → **my hand** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The nurse* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *was* · *held* (past of *hold*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very kind* · *my hand*.
10. **Word by word:** *nurse* 👩 · *kind* · *held* — irregular past · *my hand*.
11. **How the rules work together:** woman → *she*; body part → *my*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Marta is a nurse at a big hospital* (EX-021-346).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun + past.
14. **Other versions:** *The doctor was very kind. He explained everything.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She held the my hand.* → ✅ *my hand* · ❌ *She holded…* → ✅ *held*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The nurse was lovely — she held my hand.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NURS" · "HELD".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person + was very + adj. She + past verb + my + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The teacher was very patient. She helped me.*
21. **Quick check:** Past of *hold*? → **Answer:** *held*.

### EX-021-813 — "These pills are strong. Take them after meals."

1. **Meaning:** These tablets have powerful medicine; swallow them after you eat. 💊🍽️
2. **Situation:** A pharmacist gives instructions.
3. **Main lesson:** *these pills* → *them*; plural noun after *after*.
4. **Grammar used:** *These* + plural noun + *are* + adj · *Take them after* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **These pills** (S) → **are** → **strong** · **(You)** → **Take** (V) → **them** (O) → **after meals** (P).
6. **Subject:** *These pills* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *Take*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *strong* · *them* · *after meals*.
10. **Word by word:** *pills* 📦 — small round medicine (also *tablets*) · *Take* — for medicine (not *eat* or *drink*) · *meals* — breakfast, lunch, dinner.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *them*; we **take** medicine.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The medicine is bitter* (EX-021-805).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct verb collocation + pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *This pill is strong. Take it after breakfast.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Eat them after meals.* → ✅ *Take them* · ❌ *…after the meals.* (general → *after meals*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They're strong, so take them after food.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PILZ" · "MEELZ".
19. **Pattern:** **These + plural noun + are + adj. Take them + after + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *These drops are for your eyes. Use them twice a day.*
21. **Quick check:** *eat* or *take* medicine? → **Answer:** *take*.

### EX-021-814 — "My grandfather has a bad leg. He walks with a stick."

1. **Meaning:** One of my grandfather's legs is weak or hurt; he uses a walking stick. 👴🦯
2. **Situation:** Describing a family member.
3. **Main lesson:** *a* + adj + body part; *he* + *with a* + noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *has a bad* + noun · *He* + verb-s + *with a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My grandfather** (S) → **has** (V) → **a bad leg** (O) · **He** (S) → **walks** (V) → **with a stick** (P).
6. **Subject:** *My grandfather* · *He*.
7. **Verb:** *has* · *walks*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a bad leg* · *with a stick*.
10. **Word by word:** *a bad leg* — one leg that is hurt (*a*, because he has two) · *with* — using · *stick* 📦 — walking stick.
11. **How the rules work together:** one of two → *a bad leg*; man → *he*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My grandfather doesn't drink tea* (EX-021-046).
13. **Why this is correct:** articles + agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandmother has a bad back. She walks slowly.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…has bad leg.* → ✅ *a bad leg* · ❌ *He walks by a stick.* → ✅ *with a stick*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandad's got a bad leg, so he uses a stick.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GRAND-fah-ther".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + has a bad + body part. He + verb-s + with a + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My uncle has a bad knee. He walks with a stick.*
21. **Quick check:** *walk by a stick* or *walk with a stick*? → **Answer:** *with a stick*.

### EX-021-815 — "The doctor gave me some cream. I put it on my skin twice a day."

1. **Meaning:** The doctor gave me a medicine you rub on; I use it on my skin two times every day. 🧴
2. **Situation:** Explaining treatment for a rash.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun *cream* (with *some*) → *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *gave me some* + U noun · *I put it on my* + noun + frequency.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The doctor** (S) → **gave** (V) → **me** (O1) → **some cream** (O2) · **I** (S) → **put** (V) → **it** (O) → **on my skin** (P) → **twice a day**.
6. **Subject:** *The doctor* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *gave* · *put* (same in present and past).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *me* · *some cream* · *it* · *on my skin* · *twice a day*.
10. **Word by word:** *some* — with U nouns · *cream* — U · *it* — U nouns use *it* · *skin* — U/C · *twice a day* — 2 times each day.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → *some* + *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *some* + U noun: *The children are hungry. Give them some bread* (EX-021-042).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct quantity word + pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *The doctor gave me some tablets. I take them twice a day.* (plural → *them*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…gave me a cream.* (possible for one tube/type; general → *some cream*) · ❌ *I put them on…* (for cream) → ✅ *it*

> ✉️ *Dear Ms Hill, I'm sorry, but my **daughter** has a high **temperature** today. She will stay at **home** until **Thursday**. Kind regards, Ana Silva*

### EX-021-847 — "My daughter has a high temperature today."

1. **Meaning:** My daughter has a fever today. 👧🌡️
2. **Situation:** An email to a teacher.
3. **Main lesson:** *a high temperature* — adjective inside an illness phrase.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *has a* + adj + noun + *today*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My daughter** (S) → **has** (V) → **a high temperature** (O) → **today**.
6. **Subject:** *My daughter*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a high temperature* · *today*.
10. **Word by word:** *daughter* 👧 — silent *gh* · *has* · *a high temperature* — temperatures are *high/low*, not *big*.
11. **How the rules work together:** she → *has*; *a* + adj + noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Do you have a temperature?* (EX-021-817).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct collocation.
14. **Other versions:** *My son has a bad cough today.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…has a big temperature.* → ✅ *high* · ❌ *My daughter have…* → ✅ *has*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's running a temperature.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** formal email: full sentences, no slang.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DAW-ter".
19. **Pattern:** **My + person + has a high + noun + today.**
20. **More examples:** *My husband has a bad headache today.*
21. **Quick check:** *big* or *high* temperature? → **Answer:** *high*.

### EX-021-848 — "She will stay at home until Thursday."

1. **Meaning:** She won't go to school; she'll be at home up to Thursday. 🏠📅
2. **Situation:** The email continues.
3. **Main lesson:** *at home* (no *the*) + *until* + day.
4. **Grammar used:** *She will* + verb + *at home until* + day.
5. **Sentence structure:** **She** (S) → **will stay** (V) → **at home** (P) → **until Thursday** (P).
6. **Subject:** *She*.
7. **Verb:** *stay*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *will*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at home* · *until Thursday*.
10. **Word by word:** *stay* — remain · *at home* — fixed, no article · *until* — up to that time · *Thursday* — capital.
11. **How the rules work together:** *at home / to school / to bed* → no article (fixed phrases).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *These days, more people are cycling to work* (EX-021-776) — another no-article phrase.
13. **Why this is correct:** fixed phrase + future.
14. **Other versions:** *She will be back at school on Friday.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…stay at the home…* → ✅ *at home* · ❌ *…until thursday.* → ✅ *Thursday*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She'll be off school till Thursday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *T*; email ends *Kind regards* (friendly formal).
18. **Pronunciation:** "THURZ-day".
19. **Pattern:** **She will stay at home until + day.**
20. **More examples:** *He will stay in bed until tomorrow.*
21. **Quick check:** *at the home* or *at home*? → **Answer:** *at home*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-849 — "You should rest. / The doctor told me to get some rest."

1. **Meaning:** (1) You need to relax and not work. (2) The doctor said I need some time to relax. 🛋️
2. **Situation:** Advice after an illness.
3. **Main lesson:** *rest* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *You should* + verb · *The* + noun + *told me to get some* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **You** → **should rest** (V) · (2) **The doctor** (S) → **told** (V) → **me** (O) → **to get some rest**.
6. **Subject:** (1) *You* · (2) *The doctor*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *rest* · (2) *told*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *should*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *me* · *some rest*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *rest* after *should* → verb · (2) *some rest* — after *some* → noun (U).
11. **How the rules work together:** after a modal → verb; after *some* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Babies need a lot of sleep* (EX-021-810) — *sleep* noun; *rest* works the same way.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *I need to rest.* (verb) · *I need a rest.* (C — a short break)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *You should to rest.* → ✅ *should rest* · ❌ *…get some rests.* → ✅ *some rest*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Get some rest!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "REST" (noun and verb the same).
19. **Pattern:** **You should + verb / get some + U noun**
20. **More examples:** *You should sleep. / Get some sleep.*
21. **Quick check:** In *some rest*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-850 — "Can you hand me the towel? / Give me your hand."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Please pass me the towel. (2) Put your hand in mine (let me hold it). 🤲
2. **Situation:** (1) After a shower; (2) helping someone up a step.
3. **Main lesson:** *hand* as a verb (1, = pass) and a C noun (2, body part).
4. **Grammar used:** *Can you* + verb + *me the* + noun? · *Give me your* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Can** → **you** (S) → **hand** (V) → **me** (O1) → **the towel** (O2)? · (2) **(You)** → **Give** (V) → **me** (O1) → **your hand** (O2).
6. **Subject:** (1) *you* · (2) hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *hand* · (2) *Give*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *Can*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *me* · *the towel* · *your hand*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *hand* after *Can you* → verb · (2) *your hand* — after *your* → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** after a modal + subject → verb; after a possessive → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Your hands are dirty. Wash them before dinner* (EX-021-811).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Can you pass me the salt?* · *Raise your hand.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Can you hands me the towel?* → ✅ *hand* · ❌ *Give me you hand.* → ✅ *your hand*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Pass me the towel?* · *Here, take my hand.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HAND" (noun and verb the same).
19. **Pattern:** **Can you + verb + me the + noun? / Give me your + noun**
20. **More examples:** *Can you head the ball?* (verb) */ My head hurts.* (noun)
21. **Quick check:** In *hand me the towel*, is *hand* a noun or a verb? → **Answer:** verb.
