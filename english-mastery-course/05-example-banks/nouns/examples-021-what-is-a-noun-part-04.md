**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

**New home words in this part:** washing machine · balcony · curtains · heating · jar · carpet · hall · tiles · mirror · cushion · remote control · switch · drawer · lift · rubbish · goldfish.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-151 — "My brother's room is messy."

1. **Meaning:** The room that belongs to my brother is untidy — things are everywhere. 🧦👕📚
2. **Situation:** Your mother asks you to call your brother to clean his room.
3. **Main lesson:** A possessive noun (*brother's*) + the head noun (*room*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun's + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My brother's room** (S) → **is** → **messy**.
6. **Subject:** *My brother's room* — head noun **room** (the thing we describe).
7. **Verb:** *is* — singular (one room).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *messy* — adjective (untidy).
10. **Word by word:** *My* · *brother's* — belongs to my brother (*'s* = possession, [L-037](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-037-possessive-s.md)) · *room* 📍 · *messy* — not tidy.
11. **How the rules work together:** in *X's Y*, **Y** is the head noun; the verb agrees with **Y**.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The children's toys…* (EX-021-133).
13. **Why this is correct:** singular head → *is*; correct possessive.
14. **Other versions:** *My brothers' room is messy.* (two brothers, one room — apostrophe after s) · *Is your brother's room messy?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My brother room is messy.* → ✅ *brother's* · ❌ *The room of my brother is messy.* (understandable but unnatural) → ✅ *My brother's room*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My brother's room is a total mess!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe before **s**: *brother's*.
18. **Pronunciation:** *brother's* = "BRUTH-erz" (same as *brothers*).
19. **Pattern:** **My + person's + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *My sister's car is new. · My dad's office is small.*
21. **Quick check:** Head noun? → **Answer:** *room*.

### EX-021-152 — "The washing machine is noisy."

1. **Meaning:** The machine that washes clothes makes a lot of noise. 🧺🔊
2. **Situation:** You can't hear the TV because the washing machine is on.
3. **Main lesson:** A **compound** noun of two words as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The washing machine** (S) → **is** → **noisy**.
6. **Subject:** *The washing machine* — head **machine**.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *noisy* — making a lot of noise.
10. **Word by word:** *washing* — describes which machine (used for washing) · *machine* 📦 · *noisy* — from the noun *noise* + *-y*.
11. **How the rules work together:** two words = one thing; the second word is the head.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *dishwasher* (one word, EX-021-117) vs *washing machine* (two words) — English has both styles.
13. **Why this is correct:** singular head → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *The washing machines in the laundry are noisy.* · *Is the washing machine on?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The machine washing is noisy.* → ✅ *washing machine* · ❌ *The washing machine is noise.* → ✅ *noisy* (adjective)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The washing machine's so loud!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two words, no hyphen.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress the first word: ***WASH**ing machine*; "ma-SHEEN".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The coffee machine is broken. · The alarm clock is loud.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun → adjective: *noise* → ? → **Answer:** *noisy*.

### EX-021-153 — "Grandpa reads the newspaper every morning."

1. **Meaning:** Each morning, my grandfather reads the newspaper. 📰👴
2. **Situation:** Describing your grandfather's habits.
3. **Main lesson:** Family name-noun (S) + compound noun (O) + time noun.
4. **Grammar used:** name + verb-s + *the* + noun + *every* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Grandpa** (S) → **reads** (V) → **the newspaper** (O) → **every morning** (time).
6. **Subject:** *Grandpa* — used as a name → capital **G**.
7. **Verb:** *reads* — -s for one person.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the newspaper* (O) · *every morning* (time).
10. **Word by word:** *Grandpa* · *reads* · *newspaper* 📦 — *news* + *paper*, one word · *every morning* — each morning.
11. **How the rules work together:** S-V-O-Time; *every* + singular noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Grandma knits scarves* (EX-021-103).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct agreement and order.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandpa reads the paper every morning.* (*the paper* = the newspaper) · *Does Grandpa read the news online?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Grandpa read the newspaper every morning.* → ✅ *reads* · ❌ *…every mornings.* → ✅ *every morning*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Grandpa always reads the paper with his coffee.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one word: *newspaper*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "NYOOZ-pay-per" (UK) / "NOOZ-pay-per" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **Family name + verb-s + the + noun + every + time.**
20. **More examples:** *Mum drinks coffee every morning. · Dad walks the dog every evening.*
21. **Quick check:** One word or two: *news paper*? → **Answer:** one: *newspaper*.

### EX-021-154 — "Our flat has a balcony."

1. **Meaning:** Our apartment has a small outside area attached to it. 🏢🪴
2. **Situation:** Describing your home to a new friend.
3. **Main lesson:** Place noun (S) + another place noun (O).
4. **Grammar used:** *our* + noun + *has a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our flat** (S) → **has** (V) → **a balcony** (O).
6. **Subject:** *Our flat*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a balcony*.
10. **Word by word:** *flat* 📍 (UK; US *apartment*) · *balcony* 📍 — a platform outside an upstairs window, with a wall or rail.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular subject → *has*; C singular object → *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Our garden has an apple tree* (EX-021-105).
13. **Why this is correct:** S-V-O with correct article.
14. **Other versions:** *Our flat doesn't have a balcony.* · *Does your flat have a balcony?* · Plural: *balconies* (y → ies).
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Our flat has balcony.* → ✅ *a balcony* · ❌ *Our flat have a balcony.* → ✅ *has*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We've got a little balcony.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BAL-kuh-nee".
19. **Pattern:** **Our + home + has a/an + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Our house has a garage. · Their flat has a lift.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *balcony*? → **Answer:** *balconies*.

### EX-021-155 — "The baby's bottle is on the table."

1. **Meaning:** The bottle that belongs to the baby is on the table. 🍼
2. **Situation:** A parent asks where the baby's milk bottle is.
3. **Main lesson:** Possessive noun + head noun (S) + noun after *on*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun's + noun + *is on the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The baby's bottle** (S) → **is** → **on the table**.
6. **Subject:** *The baby's bottle* — head **bottle**.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *on the table* — place.
10. **Word by word:** *baby's* — belongs to the baby · *bottle* 📦 · *table* 📦 (P).
11. **How the rules work together:** possessive *'s* + head noun; place phrase.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My brother's room* (EX-021-151).
13. **Why this is correct:** singular head → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *The babies' bottles are on the table.* (several babies) · *Where's the baby's bottle?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The bottle of the baby…* (unnatural) → ✅ *The baby's bottle* · ❌ *The babys bottle…* → ✅ *baby's*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The baby's bottle's on the table.* (two *'s* — possessive + *is*!)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** apostrophe in *baby's*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BAY-beez BOT-ul".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person's + noun + is on the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The dog's bowl is in the kitchen. · The teacher's bag is on the chair.*
21. **Quick check:** In *The baby's bottle's on the table*, what does each *'s* mean? → **Answer:** first = possession, second = *is*.

### EX-021-156 — "My parents are at work."

1. **Meaning:** My mother and father are at their jobs now.
2. **Situation:** A neighbour asks if your parents are home.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural family noun + fixed phrase *at work* (no article).
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + plural noun + *are at* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My parents** (S) → **are** → **at work**.
6. **Subject:** *My parents*.
7. **Verb:** *are* (plural).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *at work* — place (fixed phrase).
10. **Word by word:** *parents* 👩 · *at work* — at their workplace; *work* here is a U noun, no article.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*; *at work / at home / at school* — no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Are your parents at home?* (EX-021-120).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement and fixed phrase.
14. **Other versions:** *My mum is at work.* · *Are your parents at work?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My parents are at the work.* → ✅ *at work* · ❌ *My parents is at work.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They're both at work.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "at-WURK".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are at work/home/school.**
20. **More examples:** *The children are at school. · My grandparents are at home.*
21. **Quick check:** Article or not: *at ___ work*? → **Answer:** no article.

### EX-021-157 — "The curtains are blue."

1. **Meaning:** The pieces of cloth that cover the windows are blue. 🪟💙
2. **Situation:** Describing your bedroom.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun usually used in the **plural** (*curtains* — two pieces).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The curtains** (S) → **are** → **blue**.
6. **Subject:** *The curtains*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *blue* — adjective.
10. **Word by word:** *curtains* 📦 — cloth that hangs at a window (usually a pair) · *blue* — colour adjective.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural noun → *are*; colour adjective after *be* (no -s).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** adjectives don't change: ❌ *blues* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement; adjective unchanged.
14. **Other versions:** *The curtain is torn.* (one curtain) · *Are the curtains new?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The curtains is blue.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The curtains are blues.* → ✅ *blue*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We've got blue curtains in the bedroom.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KUR-tunz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + colour.**
20. **More examples:** *The walls are white. · The towels are green.*
21. **Quick check:** Make singular. → **Answer:** *The curtain is blue.*

### EX-021-158 — "My cousin has twin daughters."

1. **Meaning:** My cousin has two daughters who were born at the same time. 👧👧
2. **Situation:** Talking about your extended family.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun (*twin*) used like an adjective before a plural noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *has* + noun + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My cousin** (S) → **has** (V) → **twin daughters** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My cousin*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *twin daughters* — head **daughters**.
10. **Word by word:** *cousin* 👩 · *twin* — describes the daughters (noun used as a describer → stays singular) · *daughters* 👩 — female children.
11. **How the rules work together:** in *twin daughters*, only the head noun is plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *two apple trees* — not *apples trees* (EX-021-105).
13. **Why this is correct:** describer noun stays singular.
14. **Other versions:** *My cousin has twins.* (*twins* as the head noun) · *Does your cousin have twin boys?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…has twins daughters.* → ✅ *twin daughters* · ❌ *…has a twin daughters.* → ✅ no *a*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My cousin's got twin girls.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DAW-terz" (the **gh** is silent).
19. **Pattern:** **Person + has + twin + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *She has twin sons. · They have twin brothers.*
21. **Quick check:** Silent letters in *daughter*? → **Answer:** *gh*.

### EX-021-159 — "The heating doesn't work."

1. **Meaning:** The system that makes the house warm is broken. 🥶🔥❌
2. **Situation:** A cold winter morning; you call the landlord.
3. **Main lesson:** An uncountable noun made from a verb (*heat* → *heating*) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *doesn't* + verb.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The heating** (S) → **doesn't work** (V).
6. **Subject:** *The heating* — the heating system.
7. **Verb:** *work* (= function correctly).
8. **Helping verbs:** *doesn't* (singular subject).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:** *heating* 📦 — the system for heating a building (U, no -s) · *doesn't work* — is not functioning.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun = singular → *doesn't*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *the cooking* (EX-021-113) — verb-ing used as a noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement with a U noun.
14. **Other versions:** *The heating isn't working.* (now, temporary) · *Does the heating work?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The heating don't work.* → ✅ *doesn't* · ❌ *The heatings…* → ✅ *heating*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The heating's broken — it's freezing!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "HEE-ting".
19. **Pattern:** **The + system noun + doesn't work.**
20. **More examples:** *The lighting is too dark. · The air conditioning doesn't work.*
21. **Quick check:** Countable or uncountable: *heating*? → **Answer:** uncountable.

### EX-021-160 — "The sugar is in the blue jar."

1. **Meaning:** You can find the sugar inside the blue glass container. 🫙
2. **Situation:** A visitor is making tea in your kitchen.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun (S) + C noun with an adjective after *in*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + U noun + *is in the* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The sugar** (S) → **is** → **in the blue jar**.
6. **Subject:** *The sugar* — specific sugar (in this kitchen) → *the*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in the blue jar* — place.
10. **Word by word:** *sugar* 📦 (U) · *blue* · *jar* 📦 — a glass container with a lid.
11. **How the rules work together:** U nouns can take *the* when specific.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** U nouns with *the* for specific things ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** specific U noun → *the*; singular verb.
14. **Other versions:** *Sugar is bad for your teeth.* (general → no *the*) · *Is the sugar in the jar?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The sugars are in the jar.* → ✅ *The sugar is…* · ❌ *…in blue jar.* → ✅ *in the blue jar*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Sugar's in the blue jar.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** ⚠️ *sugar* = "SHUG-er" (the **s** sounds like **sh**!).
19. **Pattern:** **The + U noun + is in the + adjective + container.**
20. **More examples:** *The rice is in the white bag. · The coffee is in the red tin.*
21. **Quick check:** General or specific: *Sugar is sweet.* → **Answer:** general (no *the*).

## Step B — Home nouns and pronouns

### EX-021-161 — "My wife and I have three children. We live in a small town."

1. **Meaning:** My wife and I are parents of three children; our home is in a small town.
2. **Situation:** Introducing yourself at a parents' meeting.
3. **Main lesson:** Compound subject (*My wife and I*) → **We**; plural object noun.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + *and I* + *have* + number + plural noun · *We* + verb + *in a* + adj + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My wife and I** (S) → **have** → **three children** (O) · **We** (S) → **live** → **in a small town**.
6. **Subject:** *My wife and I* · *We* (= my wife and I).
7. **Verb:** *have* · *live* (plural → no -s).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *three children* · *in a small town*.
10. **Word by word:** *wife* 👩 · *I* — subject form, last · *children* — irregular plural · *town* 📍.
11. **How the rules work together:** two people → plural verb → *we*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Tom and I are neighbours. We…* (EX-027-005).
13. **Why this is correct:** subject form *I*, plural agreement, clear pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *My husband and I have a son. We live in Rome.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Me and my wife have…* → ✅ *My wife and I* · ❌ *…three childrens.* → ✅ *children* · ❌ *My wife and I has…* → ✅ *have*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My wife and I have got three kids.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WYF" (*wife*); plural *wives* "WYVZ".
19. **Pattern:** **My + person + and I + have + number + plural noun. We + verb…**
20. **More examples:** *My brother and I have two dogs. We walk them every day.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *wife*? → **Answer:** *wives*.

### EX-021-162 — "The lamp is old, but it still works."

1. **Meaning:** The lamp isn't new, but it continues to work. 💡
2. **Situation:** Your friend wants to throw away your old lamp.
3. **Main lesson:** Noun → *it* in the second part; *but* for contrast.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adj + *, but* + *it* + *still* + verb-s.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The lamp** (S) → **is** → **old** · **, but** · **it** (S) → **still** → **works** (V).
6. **Subject:** *The lamp* · *it* (= the lamp).
7. **Verb:** *is* · *works*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *old* · *still* — continuing until now.
10. **Word by word:** *lamp* 📦 · *old* · *but* · *it* · *still* — goes **before** the main verb · *works* — functions.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → *it*; *still* before a normal verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The paint is still wet* (EX-021-150) — *still* after *be*.
13. **Why this is correct:** correct pronoun and adverb position.
14. **Other versions:** *The lamps are old, but they still work.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…but it works still.* (possible but less natural) → ✅ *it still works* · ❌ *…but he still works.* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's old, but it still works fine.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *but*.
18. **Pronunciation:** stress *STILL*: *it STILL works*.
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adjective, but it still + verb-s.**
20. **More examples:** *The car is old, but it still runs. · The phone is cheap, but it still takes good photos.*
21. **Quick check:** Where does *still* go with a normal verb? → **Answer:** before it (*still works*).

### EX-021-163 — "My son lost his shoes. He can't find them anywhere."

1. **Meaning:** My son doesn't know where his shoes are; he looked everywhere. 👟❓
2. **Situation:** It's time for school and you're late!
3. **Main lesson:** Person noun → *He*; plural thing noun → *them*.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + past verb + *his* + plural noun · *He can't* + verb + *them* + *anywhere*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My son** (S) → **lost** (V) → **his shoes** (O) · **He** (S) → **can't find** (V) → **them** (O) → **anywhere**.
6. **Subject:** *My son* · *He*.
7. **Verb:** *lost* (past of *lose*) · *find*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't* (= cannot — not able).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *his shoes* · *them* (= the shoes) · *anywhere* — in any place (negatives).
10. **Word by word:** *son* 👩 · *lost* · *his* — belongs to him · *shoes* 📦 · *them* · *anywhere*.
11. **How the rules work together:** person → *he*; plural thing → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I can't find them* ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct pronouns for each noun.
14. **Other versions:** *My daughter lost her bag. She can't find it anywhere.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *He can't find they.* → ✅ *them* · ❌ *He can't find it.* (for shoes) → ✅ *them* · ❌ *…lose his shoes yesterday* → ✅ *lost*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *He's lost his shoes again!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** *find them* → "FYN-dem".
19. **Pattern:** **Noun + lost + his/her + noun. He/She can't find + it/them.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad lost his keys. He can't find them.*
21. **Quick check:** *them* refers to? → **Answer:** the shoes.

### EX-021-164 — "Our neighbour's cat comes into our garden. It sleeps on our car."

1. **Meaning:** The cat that belongs to our neighbour enters our garden and sleeps on top of our car. 🐈🚗
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend a funny story.
3. **Main lesson:** Possessive + animal noun → *It*.
4. **Grammar used:** possessive + noun + verb-s + *into our* + noun · *It* + verb-s + *on our* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Our neighbour's cat** (S) → **comes** → **into our garden** · **It** (S) → **sleeps** → **on our car**.
6. **Subject:** *Our neighbour's cat* (head *cat*) · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *comes* · *sleeps*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *into our garden* (movement) · *on our car* (place).
10. **Word by word:** *neighbour's* — belongs to the neighbour · *cat* 🐾 · *into* — movement to the inside · *garden* · *It* — not our pet → *it* · *car* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** someone else's animal we don't know well → *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** pets you know → *he/she*; other animals → *it* ([L-027](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-027-subject-pronouns.md#72-choosing-the-right-pronoun)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct possessive and pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *Our neighbours' cats come into our garden. They sleep on our car.* (two neighbours, many cats)

11. **How the rules work together:** each clause has its own S-V-O; *the* = specific things in this house.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Dad washes the car* (EX-021-107).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement and spelling.
14. **Other versions:** *The parents clean the kitchen.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The father hoover the carpets…* → ✅ *hoovers* · ❌ *…the mother washs…* → ✅ *washes*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Dad does the hoovering and Mum does the windows.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *and* (two full clauses).
18. **Pronunciation:** "HOO-verz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + person + verb-s + the + plural noun, and the + person + verb-s + the + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The boy waters the plants, and the girl feeds the cat.*
21. **Quick check:** US word for *hoover*? → **Answer:** *vacuum*.

---

### EX-021-194 — "The children tidy their rooms and feed the goldfish."

1. **Meaning:** The children make their rooms neat, and they give food to the goldfish. 🐟
2. **Situation:** The story ends.
3. **Main lesson:** One subject, two verbs; *goldfish* (same form plural).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + verb + *their* + plural + *and* + verb + *the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The children** (S) → **tidy** (V1) → **their rooms** (O1) → **and** → **feed** (V2) → **the goldfish** (O2).
6. **Subject:** *The children* — for both verbs.
7. **Verb:** *tidy* · *feed* (plural → no -s).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *their rooms* · *the goldfish*.
10. **Word by word:** *tidy* — make neat (also an adjective: *a tidy room*) · *their rooms* — each child's room → plural · *goldfish* 🐾 — plural = *goldfish* (like *fish*).
11. **How the rules work together:** one subject can have two verbs joined by *and*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *fish* — same form ([L-025](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-025-irregular-plurals.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement; correct plural *goldfish*.
14. **Other versions:** *The boy tidies his room and feeds the goldfish.* (y → ies)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…feed the goldfishes.* → ✅ *goldfish* · ❌ *The children tidies…* → ✅ *tidy*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The kids tidy up and feed the fish.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma — one subject, two verbs.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TY-dee"; "GOHLD-fish".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + verb + their + noun + and + verb + the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The students clean their desks and close the windows.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *goldfish*? → **Answer:** *goldfish*.

## Step J — Home nouns in real writing: a flat advert

> ***For rent:** two-bedroom **flat** in the city **centre**. The **flat** has a modern **kitchen**, a **balcony** and free **parking**. **Pets** are welcome. Call 07 123 456.*

### EX-021-195 — "For rent: two-bedroom flat in the city centre."

1. **Meaning:** A flat with two bedrooms, in the middle of the city, is available to rent. 🏢🔑
2. **Situation:** An advert online.
3. **Main lesson:** Advert style: nouns without articles; a compound adjective (*two-bedroom*).
4. **Grammar used:** heading + number-noun adjective + noun + *in the* + compound noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **For rent:** (heading) → **two-bedroom flat** (main noun phrase) → **in the city centre** (place).
6. **Subject:** none — adverts often drop the verb and articles to save space.
7. **Verb:** none (full sentence: *A two-bedroom flat is for rent…*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** place phrase.
10. **Word by word:** *rent* 💭 — money you pay to live in a place · *two-bedroom* — hyphen; *bedroom* stays **singular** before the noun · *flat* · *city centre*.
11. **How the rules work together:** number + noun as an adjective → hyphen, no -s.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *a twenty-three-year-old woman* ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)) — same idea.
13. **Why this is correct:** normal advert/headline style.
14. **Other versions (full sentence):** *A two-bedroom flat is available to rent in the city centre.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *two-bedrooms flat* → ✅ *two-bedroom flat* · ❌ *two bedroom flat* (no hyphen) — very common, but the hyphen is more correct
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There's a two-bed flat for rent downtown.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** colon after the heading; hyphen in *two-bedroom*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TOO-BED-room FLAT".
19. **Pattern:** **number-noun + noun** (*three-bedroom house, five-star hotel*).
20. **More examples:** *a three-day trip · a ten-minute walk*
21. **Quick check:** *a two-bedrooms flat* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *a two-bedroom flat*.

---

### EX-021-196 — "The flat has a modern kitchen, a balcony and free parking."

1. **Meaning:** The flat includes a new-style kitchen, a balcony, and a place to park a car at no cost.
2. **Situation:** The same advert.
3. **Main lesson:** A list of three objects — C, C, U.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *has a* + adj + noun + *, a* + noun + *and* + adj + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The flat** (S) → **has** (V) → **a modern kitchen, a balcony and free parking** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The flat*.
7. **Verb:** *has*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** three objects.
10. **Word by word:** *modern* — new, up to date · *kitchen* (C → *a*) · *balcony* (C → *a*) · *free* — no cost · *parking* 💭 (U → no *a*).
11. **How the rules work together:** each item keeps its own article rule.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** lists ([L-087](../../01-beginner-grammar/punctuation/lesson-087-commas-in-lists-and-capital-letters.md)); U vs C ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct articles in a list.
14. **Other versions:** *The flat has two bathrooms and a garden.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…and a free parking.* → ✅ *free parking* · ❌ *…modern kitchen, balcony…* → ✅ *a* before each C noun
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's got a modern kitchen, a balcony and free parking.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** commas between list items.
18. **Pronunciation:** "MOD-ern".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + has a + noun, a + noun and + U noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The hotel has a pool, a gym and free Wi-Fi.*
21. **Quick check:** Why no *a* before *free parking*? → **Answer:** *parking* is uncountable.

---

### EX-021-197 — "Pets are welcome."

1. **Meaning:** You may bring your animals to live in the flat. 🐶🐱
2. **Situation:** The end of the advert.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural noun used in general as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Pets** (S) → **are** → **welcome**.
6. **Subject:** *Pets* — in general → no article.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *welcome* — adjective (accepted, wanted).
10. **Word by word:** *pets* 🐾 — animals that live with people · *welcome*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → no *the*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Dogs bark* — general plural (EX-021-013).
13. **Why this is correct:** general statement form.
14. **Other versions:** *No pets.* (common sign) · *Are pets allowed?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The pets are welcome.* (sounds like specific pets) → ✅ *Pets are welcome.* · ❌ *Pets are welcomed.* (possible but less common here)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Pets are fine.* / *Pets allowed.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WEL-kum".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + welcome/allowed.**
20. **More examples:** *Children are welcome. · Visitors are welcome.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite sign? → **Answer:** *No pets.*

## Step K — Noun or verb? Home words

### EX-021-198 — "We garden every weekend. / Our garden is small."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Every weekend we work in our garden. (2) Our garden isn't big.
2. **Situation:** Talking about hobbies.
3. **Main lesson:** *garden* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *We* + verb + time · *our* + noun + *is* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **We** → **garden** (V) → **every weekend** · (2) **Our garden** (S) → **is** → **small**.
6. **Subject:** *We* · *Our garden*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *garden* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) time · (2) *small*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *garden* after *We* → verb (= do gardening) · (2) *garden* after *Our* → noun 📍.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the job.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** [L-021 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#75-same-word-noun-or-verb).
13. **Why this is correct:** each in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *My grandmother loves gardening.* (-ing noun = the activity)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *We garden in every weekend.* → ✅ *every weekend* (no *in*) · ❌ *Our gardens is small.* → ✅ *garden is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We do a bit of gardening at the weekend.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "GAR-dun".
19. **Pattern:** **We + verb + every weekend / Our + noun + is + adjective**
20. **More examples:** *We fish on Sundays. / The fish is fresh.*
21. **Quick check:** In *We garden*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** verb.

---

### EX-021-199 — "I'll dust the shelves. / There's dust on the shelves."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I will clean the dust off the shelves. (2) There is dust (fine dirt) on the shelves.
2. **Situation:** Cleaning before guests arrive.
3. **Main lesson:** *dust* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I'll* + verb + *the* + plural · *There's* + U noun + *on the* + plural.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **'ll dust** (V) → **the shelves** (O) · (2) **There's** → **dust** → **on the shelves**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · real subject *dust*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *'ll dust* (*will dust*) · (2) *'s* (is).
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *'ll* (= will).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the shelves* (O / place).
10. **Word by word:** (1) *dust* after *I'll* → verb (= remove dust) · (2) *dust* after *There's* → noun (U: tiny dirt) · *shelves* (f → ves).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides; U noun → *There's* (singular).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *There's sand in my shoes* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md) bank) — U noun with *There's*.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its natural position.
14. **Other versions:** *The shelves are dusty.* (adjective *dusty*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *There are dusts on the shelves.* → ✅ *There's dust* · ❌ *I'll dust on the shelves.* → ✅ *I'll dust the shelves.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *These shelves need a good dust!* (noun = a cleaning)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *I'll* = I will.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DUST".
19. **Pattern:** **I'll + verb + the + noun / There's + U noun + on the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *I'll brush the floor. / There's a brush in the cupboard.*
21. **Quick check:** Adjective from *dust*? → **Answer:** *dusty*.

---

### EX-021-200 — "Please don't park here. / There's a park near our house."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Please don't leave your car in this place. (2) There's a public green area near our house. 🚗🚫 / 🌳
2. **Situation:** A sign in front of a garage / describing your area.
3. **Main lesson:** *park* as a verb (1) and a noun (2) — completely different meanings!
4. **Grammar used:** negative instruction · *There's a* + noun + *near our* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Please don't park** (V) → **here** · (2) **There's** → **a park** → **near our house**.
6. **Subject:** hidden *you* · real subject *a park*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *park* (leave a car) · (2) *'s* (is).
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *don't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *here* · (2) *near our house*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *park* after *don't* → verb · (2) *park* after *a* → noun 📍 (a green public place) · *near* — close to.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides the job **and** the meaning.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I drink water / I water the plants* ([L-008](../../00-foundations/03-words-and-meaning/lesson-008-things-actions-describing-words.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its natural position.
14. **Other versions:** *Where can I park? · Let's go to the park.* · *a car park* (UK) = *a parking lot* (US)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please not park here.* → ✅ *don't park* · ❌ *There's park near our house.* → ✅ *a park*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *No parking!* (common sign) · *There's a nice park just round the corner.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PARK" (same for both).
19. **Pattern:** **Please don't + verb + here / There's a + noun + near + place**
20. **More examples:** *Please don't smoke here. / There's a smoke alarm in the kitchen.*
21. **Quick check:** UK *car park* = US ___? → **Answer:** *parking lot*.
