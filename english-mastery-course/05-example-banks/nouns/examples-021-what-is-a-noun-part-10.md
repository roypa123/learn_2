**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

**New words in this part:** savings · delivery · rent · parcel · discount · refund · account · tax · loyalty card · taste · second-hand · choice · square · order · tracking number · jar.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-451 — "The bank is closed."

1. **Meaning:** The bank isn't open now. 🏦🚫
2. **Situation:** You arrive at the bank on Sunday.
3. **Main lesson:** A money-place noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The bank** (S) → **is** → **closed**.
6. **Subject:** *The bank*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *closed* — not open.
10. **Word by word:** *bank* 📍 — a place that keeps money (also *a river bank* = the side of a river!) · *closed*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Is the bank open?* (EX-021-009).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The banks are closed on Sundays.* · *Is the bank closed?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Bank is closed.* → ✅ *The bank* · ❌ *The bank is close.* → ✅ *closed* (*close* = near, or the verb)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The bank's shut.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KLOHZD".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + open/closed.**
20. **More examples:** *The post office is closed. · The pharmacy is open.*
21. **Quick check:** *close* or *closed* for the state? → **Answer:** *closed*.

### EX-021-452 — "Savings are important."

1. **Meaning:** Money you keep for the future matters a lot. 🐷💰
2. **Situation:** A parent gives money advice to a teenager.
3. **Main lesson:** A noun that is usually **plural** for money (*savings*).
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Savings** (S) → **are** → **important**.
6. **Subject:** *Savings* — money saved (plural form, plural verb).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *important*.
10. **Word by word:** *savings* 💭 — money you have saved (*save* + *-ing* + *s*) · *important*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural form → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** always-plural nouns (*clothes, jeans*) ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#77-special-nouns-first-look)).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural noun + plural verb.
14. **Other versions:** *My savings are in the bank.* · *She spent all her savings.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Savings is important.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *a saving* (for money) → ✅ *savings*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's good to have some savings.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SAY-vingz".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *My clothes are new. · The stairs are steep.*
21. **Quick check:** *Savings is* or *Savings are*? → **Answer:** *are*.

### EX-021-453 — "Delivery is free."

1. **Meaning:** You don't pay to have the things sent to your house. 🚚🆓
2. **Situation:** A note on an online shop.
3. **Main lesson:** U noun (*delivery* as a service) as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Delivery** (S) → **is** → **free**.
6. **Subject:** *Delivery*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *free* — costs nothing.
10. **Word by word:** *Delivery* 💭 — bringing things to your home (U as a service; C *a delivery* = one parcel/visit) · *free*.
11. **How the rules work together:** service in general → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *free* = no cost / available ([D-001](../../06-spoken-english/daily-conversations/dialogue-001-meeting-someone-new.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *We have a delivery at three.* (C) · *Is delivery free?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The delivery is free* — fine for one specific order · ❌ *Deliveries is free.* → ✅ *Delivery is free.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Free delivery!* (common on websites)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "di-LIV-uh-ree".
19. **Pattern:** **Service noun + is + free.**
20. **More examples:** *Parking is free. · Entry is free.*
21. **Quick check:** Verb from *delivery*? → **Answer:** *deliver*.

### EX-021-456 — "Online shopping is easy."

1. **Meaning:** Buying things on the internet is simple. 💻🛒
2. **Situation:** Explaining to a grandparent.
3. **Main lesson:** Adjective + **-ing noun** (gerund, U) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** adj + -ing noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Online shopping** (S) → **is** → **easy**.
6. **Subject:** *Online shopping* — head **shopping** (the activity).
7. **Verb:** *is* (U → singular).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *easy*.
10. **Word by word:** *online* — adjective · *shopping* — a noun here (the activity of buying) · *easy*.
11. **How the rules work together:** activity -ing nouns are U → singular verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *the cooking, the ironing* — -ing nouns (EX-021-113).
13. **Why this is correct:** U subject → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Shopping is fun.* · *Is online shopping safe?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Online shopping are easy.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *An online shopping…* → ✅ no *an*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Shopping online is so easy.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "ON-lyn SHOP-ing".
19. **Pattern:** **Adjective + -ing noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Online banking is quick. · Night driving is difficult.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *shopping* a verb here? → **Answer:** No — a noun (the subject).

### EX-021-457 — "The discount is twenty percent."

1. **Meaning:** The price is reduced by 20%. 🏷️
2. **Situation:** A shop assistant explains a sale.
3. **Main lesson:** Idea noun (*discount*) + a percentage.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + number + *percent*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The discount** (S) → **is** → **twenty percent**.
6. **Subject:** *The discount*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *twenty percent* — complement.
10. **Word by word:** *discount* 💭 — money taken off a price · *percent* — % (no -s: *twenty percent*; UK also *per cent*).
11. **How the rules work together:** *percent* doesn't take -s after a number.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** numbers ([L-016](../../00-foundations/06-first-words/lesson-016-numbers-0-100.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct form.
14. **Other versions:** *There's a 20% discount on shoes.* · *Is there a discount?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…twenty percents.* → ✅ *twenty percent* · ❌ *The discount are…* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's twenty percent off.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *20%* or *twenty percent*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DIS-kownt"; "per-SENT".
19. **Pattern:** **The discount is + number + percent.**
20. **More examples:** *The tax is ten percent. · The tip is fifteen percent.*
21. **Quick check:** *twenty percents* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *twenty percent*.

### EX-021-458 — "Coins are heavy."

1. **Meaning:** Metal money weighs a lot (when you have many). 🪙
2. **Situation:** Your pocket is full of coins.
3. **Main lesson:** C money noun (plural) vs U *money*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Coins** (S) → **are** → **heavy**.
6. **Subject:** *Coins* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *heavy*.
10. **Word by word:** *coins* 📦 — round metal money (C) · *heavy*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *money* U, *coins* C plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *money / coin* pair ([L-026 §7.5](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md#75-countable--uncountable-pairs-with-similar-meanings)).
13. **Why this is correct:** plural C → *are*.
14. **Other versions:** *This coin is old.* · *Money is heavy* ❌ (strange — say *coins*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Coins is heavy.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *a money* → ✅ *a coin*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My pocket's full of coins.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KOYNZ".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Notes are light. · Bags are heavy.*
21. **Quick check:** C or U: *coin*? *money*? → **Answer:** *coin* = C · *money* = U.

### EX-021-459 — "The customer wants a refund."

1. **Meaning:** The customer wants their money back for something they bought. 💸↩️
2. **Situation:** A shop assistant tells the manager.
3. **Main lesson:** Person noun (S) + idea noun (O).
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + *a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The customer** (S) → **wants** (V) → **a refund** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The customer*.
7. **Verb:** *wants*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a refund*.
10. **Word by word:** *customer* · *wants* · *refund* 💭 — money given back (C: *a refund*).
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *wants*; C → *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Tomorrow, I will return the dress* (EX-021-427).
13. **Why this is correct:** S-V-O with article.
14. **Other versions:** *Can I have a refund?* · *The customers want refunds.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The customer want…* → ✅ *wants* · ❌ *…wants refund.* → ✅ *a refund*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She wants her money back.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** noun "REE-fund" (stress first); verb *re-FUND*.
19. **Pattern:** **The + person + wants a/an + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The guest wants a towel. · The child wants a drink.*
21. **Quick check:** Stress in the noun *refund*? → **Answer:** first: *RE-fund*.

## Step B — Pronouns

### EX-021-461 — "I ordered a new lamp online. It arrived yesterday."

1. **Meaning:** I bought a lamp on the internet; it came yesterday. 💡📦
2. **Situation:** Showing a friend your new lamp.
3. **Main lesson:** *a new lamp* → *It*.
4. **Grammar used:** *I ordered a* + adj + noun + adverb · *It arrived* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **ordered** → **a new lamp** (O) → **online** · **It** (S) → **arrived** → **yesterday**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *It* (= the lamp).
7. **Verb:** *ordered* · *arrived*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a new lamp* · *online* · *yesterday*.
10. **Word by word:** *ordered* — asked to buy · *lamp* · *online* — on the internet (adverb here) · *It*.
11. **How the rules work together:** first mention *a*, then *it*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I bought a new phone. It was expensive.* (EX-021-415).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *I ordered new lamps. They arrived yesterday.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…It arrive yesterday.* → ✅ *arrived* · ❌ *…He arrived…* → ✅ *It*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Got a new lamp online — it came yesterday.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "OR-derd".
19. **Pattern:** **I ordered a + adj + noun online. It arrived + time.**
20. **More examples:** *I ordered a book. It came today.*
21. **Quick check:** What does *It* refer to? → **Answer:** the lamp.

### EX-021-462 — "My sister loves expensive shoes. She buys them every month."

1. **Meaning:** My sister likes costly shoes a lot; she buys some every month. 👠
2. **Situation:** Talking about family habits.
3. **Main lesson:** Person noun → *She*; plural object → *them*.
4. **Grammar used:** noun + verb-s + adj + plural · *She* + verb-s + *them* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My sister** → **loves** → **expensive shoes** (O) · **She** → **buys** → **them** (O) → **every month**.
6. **Subject:** *My sister* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *loves* · *buys*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *expensive shoes* · *them* · *every month*.
10. **Word by word:** *expensive* · *shoes* (general) · *them* = shoes · *every month*.
11. **How the rules work together:** two pronouns, two different references.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My sister has two cats. She loves them.* ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md) bank).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronouns.
14. **Other versions:** *My brother loves video games. He buys them online.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *She buys it every month.* (for plural) → ✅ *them* · ❌ *…every months.* → ✅ *every month*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *She's obsessed with expensive shoes!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BYZ-them" → "BYZ-em".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + loves + adj + plural. She/He buys them + time.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad loves old books. He buys them at markets.*
21. **Quick check:** *them* = ? → **Answer:** expensive shoes.

### EX-021-463 — "The tickets are very cheap. Let's buy them now."

1. **Meaning:** The tickets don't cost much; I suggest we buy them now. 🎟️
2. **Situation:** Friends looking at concert tickets online.
3. **Main lesson:** Plural → *them*; *Let's* suggestion.
4. **Grammar used:** plural + *are very* + adj · *Let's* + verb + *them* + *now*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The tickets** (S) → **are** → **very cheap** · **Let's** → **buy** → **them** (O) → **now**.
6. **Subject:** *The tickets* · *Let's* (= we).
7. **Verb:** *are* · *buy*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none (*Let's* is a suggestion form).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very cheap* · *them* · *now*.
10. **Word by word:** *tickets* · *cheap* (things) · *Let's* = let us · *them*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *tickets* (things) → *cheap*; plural → *them*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Let's* ([L-028 §7.8](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md#78-let-me--lets)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct adjective and pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *The ticket is cheap. Let's buy it.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Let's to buy them.* → ✅ *Let's buy* · ❌ *The tickets are very low.* → ✅ *cheap* (things) / *The prices are low.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *They're so cheap — let's get them!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LETS BY-em".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural + are cheap. Let's buy them.**
20. **More examples:** *The strawberries look fresh. Let's buy them.*
21. **Quick check:** *Let's to buy* or *Let's buy*? → **Answer:** *Let's buy*.

### EX-021-465 — "These oranges are sweet. Where did you buy them?"

1. **Meaning:** These oranges taste sweet; I'm asking where you bought them. 🍊
2. **Situation:** Eating fruit at a friend's house.
3. **Main lesson:** *these* + plural → *them* in a past question.
4. **Grammar used:** *These* + plural + *are* + adj · *Where did you* + verb + *them*?
5. **Sentence structure:** **These oranges** (S) → **are** → **sweet** · **Where** → **did** → **you** → **buy** → **them**?
6. **Subject:** *These oranges* · *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *buy* (base form after *did*).
8. **Helping verbs:** *did* (past question).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *sweet* · *them*.
10. **Word by word:** *oranges* (sound /ɪz/) · *sweet* — adjective (taste) · *them*.
11. **How the rules work together:** past question: *did* + base verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *did* questions ([L-065](../../01-beginner-grammar/verbs/lesson-065-past-simple-negative-and-questions.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct question form.
14. **Other versions:** *This orange is sweet. Where did you buy it?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Where did you bought them?* → ✅ *buy* · ❌ *Where you bought them?* → ✅ *Where did you buy…?*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *These are lovely — where'd you get them?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "OR-in-jiz" (3 syllables).
19. **Pattern:** **These + plural + are + adj. Where did you buy them?**
20. **More examples:** *These shoes are nice. Where did you get them?*
21. **Quick check:** After *did*, *buy* or *bought*? → **Answer:** *buy*.

## Step C — Questions and negatives

### EX-021-466 — "Can I have a refund, please?"

1. **Meaning:** I'm asking politely for my money back.
2. **Situation:** Returning a broken item.

18. **Pronunciation:** "a-REE-fund".
19. **Pattern:** **Please send me a/an + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Please send me a copy. · Please send me an invoice.*
21. **Quick check:** UK formal close after *Dear Sir or Madam*? → **Answer:** *Yours faithfully*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-499 — "I'd like to order a pizza. / Your order is ready."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I want to ask for a pizza (to buy). (2) The food you asked for is ready. 🍕
2. **Situation:** On the phone with a pizza shop / at the counter.
3. **Main lesson:** *order* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I'd like to* + verb + *a* + noun · *Your* + noun + *is* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **'d like to order** (V) → **a pizza** (O) · (2) **Your order** (S) → **is** → **ready**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *Your order*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *order* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *'d* (would).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *a pizza* · (2) *ready*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *order* after *to* → verb · (2) *order* after *Your* → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I'd like a glass of water* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** each in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *Can I take your order?* (waiter) · *We ordered Chinese food.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I'd like order a pizza.* → ✅ *to order* · ❌ *Your order are ready.* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Order for Ana!* (a shop calling your name)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "OR-der" (same for noun and verb).
19. **Pattern:** **I'd like to + verb + a + noun / Your + noun + is ready**
20. **More examples:** *I'd like to book a table. / Your booking is confirmed.*
21. **Quick check:** In *Can I take your order?*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-500 — "I pay the rent on the first of the month. / The pay at my new job is good."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I give the rent money on the 1st day of each month. (2) The money I get at my new job is good.
2. **Situation:** Talking about money and work.
3. **Main lesson:** *pay* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2) — and *rent* as a noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *I* + verb + *the* + noun + date · *The* + U noun + *at my* + adj + noun + *is* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **pay** (V) → **the rent** (O) → **on the first of the month** · (2) **The pay at my new job** (S) → **is** → **good**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *The pay at my new job* (head **pay**).
7. **Verb:** (1) *pay* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *the rent* · date · (2) *good*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *pay* after *I* → verb · *the first of the month* — date phrase · (2) *pay* after *The* → noun (U: money for work, similar to *salary*).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides; U noun *pay* → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My rent is high* (EX-021-454) · *rent* noun/verb.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *The pay is low.* · *I paid the bill.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The pays are good.* → ✅ *The pay is good.* · ❌ *…on first of the month.* → ✅ *on the first of the month*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The money's good at my new job.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PAY" (same for noun and verb).
19. **Pattern:** **I pay the + noun + on the + date / The pay + is + adjective**
20. **More examples:** *I rent a flat. / The rent is high.*
21. **Quick check:** In *The pay is good*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun (U).
