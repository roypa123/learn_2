# Example Bank 021 · Part 13 — What Is a Noun? (EX-021-601 to EX-021-650)

**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Travel nouns to watch:** *luggage, baggage, traffic, travel, information* are **U** (no *a*, no *-s*): ❌ *a luggage, two luggages* → ✅ *some luggage, two bags* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)). **by + transport** has no article: *by bus, by train, by car*.

**New words in this part:** platform · passport · luggage · bridge · seat · traffic · ferry · suitcase · flight attendant · passenger · umbrella · pilot · ticket office · driving licence · journey · hostel · metro · car park · row · bus stop · petrol · view · tower · return ticket · lorry · postcard · castle.

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-601 — "The platform is crowded."

1. **Meaning:** There are a lot of people on the platform. 🚉🧍🧍🧍
2. **Situation:** Rush hour at the train station.
3. **Main lesson:** A travel-place noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The platform** (S) → **is** → **crowded**.
6. **Subject:** *The platform*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *crowded* — full of people.
10. **Word by word:** *the* — the one here · *platform* 📍 — the place where you wait for the train · *crowded* — adjective.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Every day, the train is full* (EX-021-056).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The platforms are crowded.* · *Is the platform crowded?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Platform is crowded.* → ✅ *The platform* · ❌ *The platform is crowd.* → ✅ *crowded* (*crowd* is a noun)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The platform's packed!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PLAT-form" · "KROW-did".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The station is busy. · The airport is quiet.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun inside *crowded*? → **Answer:** *crowd*.

### EX-021-602 — "My passport is in my bag."

1. **Meaning:** You can find my passport inside my bag. 🛂👜
2. **Situation:** At the airport, checking your things.
3. **Main lesson:** Two thing nouns: one as the subject, one after a preposition.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is in my* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My passport** (S) → **is** → **in my bag** (P).
6. **Subject:** *My passport*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *in my bag*.
10. **Word by word:** *My* — belongs to me · *passport* 📦 — travel document with your photo · *in* — inside · *bag* 📦.
11. **How the rules work together:** *my* + noun (no *the*); noun after *in* → **P**.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The keys are on the table* (EX-021-004) — noun after a preposition.
13. **Why this is correct:** clear S + *be* + place.
14. **Other versions:** *Our passports are in my bag.* · *Is your passport in your bag?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My the passport…* → ✅ *My passport* · ❌ *…in the my bag.* → ✅ *in my bag*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Passport's in my bag — don't worry.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PAHS-port" (UK) / "PAS-port" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is in my + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My ticket is in my pocket. · My phone is in my coat.*
21. **Quick check:** *my the bag* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *my bag*.

### EX-021-603 — "The airport is huge."

1. **Meaning:** The airport is very, very big. ✈️🏢
2. **Situation:** Arriving at a big international airport.
3. **Main lesson:** place noun + strong adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The airport** (S) → **is** → **huge**.
6. **Subject:** *The airport*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *huge* — very big.
10. **Word by word:** *airport* 📍 — compound noun (*air* + *port*): where planes come and go · *huge*.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The price of a cup of coffee at the airport is ridiculous* (EX-021-487).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The airport is small.* · *Airports are busy places.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The airport is very huge.* → ✅ *huge* / *really huge* (*very* doesn't go well with strong adjectives) · ❌ *The air port* → ✅ *airport* (one word)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This airport is massive!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *airport* — one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "AIR-port" · "HYOOJ".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + strong adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The station is enormous. · The hotel is tiny.*
21. **Quick check:** *very huge* or *really huge*? → **Answer:** *really huge*.

### EX-021-604 — "Taxis are expensive in London."

1. **Meaning:** In London, taking a taxi costs a lot of money. 🚕💷
2. **Situation:** Travel advice for a visitor.
3. **Main lesson:** plural C noun in general + a proper noun after *in*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective + *in* + city.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Taxis** (S) → **are** → **expensive** → **in London** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Taxis* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *expensive* · *in London*.
10. **Word by word:** *taxis* — *taxi* + *s* · *expensive* · *London* — proper noun (capital, no *the*).
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → no *the*; plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Cheese is expensive here* (EX-021-552) — U noun → *is*.
13. **Why this is correct:** plural + *are*; city name without *the*.
14. **Other versions:** *A taxi is expensive in London.* · *Taxis aren't cheap in London.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The taxis are expensive in London.* (only if you mean specific taxis) → ✅ *Taxis* · ❌ *…in the London.* → ✅ *in London*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Cabs are pricey in London.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *L*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TAK-seez".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are + adjective + in + city.**
20. **More examples:** *Trains are fast in Japan. · Buses are cheap in Mexico.*
21. **Quick check:** *in the London* — correct? → **Answer:** No: *in London*.

### EX-021-606 — "My luggage is heavy."

1. **Meaning:** My bags and suitcases weigh a lot. 🧳😓
2. **Situation:** Carrying bags to the hotel.
3. **Main lesson:** *luggage* is a **U** noun → *is*, no *-s*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My luggage** (S) → **is** → **heavy**.
6. **Subject:** *My luggage*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *heavy*.
10. **Word by word:** *luggage* 📦 — all the bags you travel with (U) · *heavy*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U noun → singular verb, even if it means many bags.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** U nouns: *furniture, information, advice* ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *My bags are heavy.* (C → *are*) · *I have two pieces of luggage.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My luggages are heavy.* → ✅ *My luggage is heavy.* · ❌ *a luggage* → ✅ *a bag* / *a piece of luggage*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My bags weigh a ton.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LUG-ij".
19. **Pattern:** **My + U noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The furniture is old. · The information is wrong.*
21. **Quick check:** How do you count luggage? → **Answer:** *pieces of luggage* / *bags*.

### EX-021-608 — "The seats are comfortable."

1. **Meaning:** The seats feel nice to sit in. 💺😊
2. **Situation:** On a new train.
3. **Main lesson:** plural noun subject → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.

### EX-021-609 — "Traffic is terrible today."

1. **Meaning:** There are too many cars on the roads today. 🚗🚙🚕
2. **Situation:** Arriving late to work.
3. **Main lesson:** *traffic* is U → *is*, no article in general.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective + time word.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Traffic** (S) → **is** → **terrible** → **today**.
6. **Subject:** *Traffic*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *terrible* · *today*.
10. **Word by word:** *traffic* 💭 — all the cars, buses etc. on the road (U) · *terrible* · *today*.
11. **How the rules work together:** U → singular verb, no *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My luggage is heavy* (EX-021-606) — another U noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *The traffic is terrible today.* (also very common) · *There's a lot of traffic.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Traffics are terrible.* → ✅ *Traffic is* · ❌ *There are many traffics.* → ✅ *There is a lot of traffic.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The traffic's a nightmare today.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TRAF-ik".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is + adjective + time.**
20. **More examples:** *Weather is bad today.* (more often *The weather*)
21. **Quick check:** *many traffic* or *a lot of traffic*? → **Answer:** *a lot of traffic*.

## Step B — Travel nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-611 — "My suitcase is too small. I can't close it."

1. **Meaning:** My suitcase doesn't have enough space, so I can't shut it. 🧳😤
2. **Situation:** Packing for a holiday.
3. **Main lesson:** *my suitcase* → *it* (object).
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is too* + adj · *I can't* + verb + *it*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My suitcase** (S) → **is** → **too small** · **I** (S) → **can't close** (V) → **it** (O).
6. **Subject:** *My suitcase* · *I*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *close*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *too small* · *it*.
10. **Word by word:** *suitcase* 📦 — a big bag with a handle for travel (C, unlike *luggage*) · *too* — more than OK · *close* (verb) · *it* — the suitcase.
11. **How the rules work together:** thing → *it*; *can't* + base verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *This pan is too heavy. I can't lift it* (EX-021-564).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun; modal + base.
14. **Other versions:** *My suitcases are too small. I can't close them.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I can't close him.* → ✅ *it* · ❌ *I can't closing it.* → ✅ *close*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My case won't shut!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *suitcase* — one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SOOT-kays".
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is too + adj. I can't + verb + it.**
20. **More examples:** *My bag is too full. I can't zip it.*
21. **Quick check:** Is *suitcase* C or U? → **Answer:** C (*a suitcase, two suitcases*).

### EX-021-612 — "The flight attendants are very kind. They help the passengers."

1. **Meaning:** The people who work on the plane are nice; they help the travellers. 👩‍✈️🧑‍🤝‍🧑
2. **Situation:** Describing a good flight.
3. **Main lesson:** noun + noun subject (*flight attendants*) → *they*; plural object *passengers*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + plural noun + *are very* + adj · *They help the* + plural noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The flight attendants** (S) → **are** → **very kind** · **They** (S) → **help** (V) → **the passengers** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The flight attendants* (head **attendants**) · *They*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *help*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very kind* · *the passengers*.
10. **Word by word:** *flight* — describes the type · *attendants* 👩 — helpers · *passengers* 👩 — people travelling.
11. **How the rules work together:** head noun plural → *are*, *they*; plural *they* → *help* (no *-s*).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The waiters are friendly. They speak three languages* (EX-021-512).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun and verb agree.
14. **Other versions:** *The pilot is very calm. He talks to the passengers.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They helps the passengers.* → ✅ *help* · ❌ *The flights attendants* → ✅ *flight attendants* (first noun stays singular)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The cabin crew were lovely.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** *flight attendant* — two words.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FLYT-uh-TEN-dunts" · "PAS-in-jerz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + plural noun + are + adj. They + verb + the + plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The bus drivers are friendly. They help old people.*
21. **Quick check:** Which noun is the head in *flight attendants*? → **Answer:** *attendants*.

### EX-021-613 — "I left my umbrella on the train. I can't find it now."

1. **Meaning:** I forgot my umbrella on the train, and now I don't have it. ☂️🚆
2. **Situation:** Arriving home in the rain.
3. **Main lesson:** *my umbrella* → *it*; *an* before a vowel sound.
4. **Grammar used:** *I left my* + noun + *on the* + noun · *I can't find it now.*
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **left** (V) → **my umbrella** (O) → **on the train** (P) · **I** → **can't find** (V) → **it** (O) → **now**.
6. **Subject:** *I* (both).
7. **Verb:** *left* (past of *leave*) · *find*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *can't*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my umbrella* · *on the train* · *it* · *now*.
10. **Word by word:** *left* — forgot to take · *umbrella* 📦 (*an umbrella*) · *on the train* — transport you go inside but stand up in → *on*.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → *it*; *on* for trains, buses, planes.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *a/an* mean "one" ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)); we say *an* before a vowel sound — *an umbrella, an airport* (full lesson: [L-032](../../01-beginner-grammar/articles-and-determiners/lesson-032-a-and-an.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear pronoun; right preposition.
14. **Other versions:** *I left my bag in the taxi.* (cars and taxis → *in*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I forgot my umbrella in the train.* → ✅ *on the train* · ❌ *I leaved…* → ✅ *left*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've left my umbrella on the train!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "um-BREL-uh".
19. **Pattern:** **I left my + noun + on the + transport. I can't find it.**
20. **More examples:** *I left my phone on the bus.*
21. **Quick check:** *in the train* or *on the train*? → **Answer:** *on the train*.

### EX-021-614 — "My uncle is a pilot. He flies to Asia every week."

1. **Meaning:** My uncle's job is flying planes; every week he goes to Asia. 👨‍✈️✈️
2. **Situation:** Talking about family jobs.
3. **Main lesson:** job noun after *be* (**B**) + *he*; continent name.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is a* + job · *He* + verb-s + *to* + place + *every week*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My uncle** (S) → **is** → **a pilot** (B) · **He** (S) → **flies** (V) → **to Asia** (P) → **every week**.
6. **Subject:** *My uncle* · *He*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *flies*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a pilot* · *to Asia* · *every week*.
10. **Word by word:** *uncle* 👩 · *a pilot* — job with *a* · *flies* — *fly* → *flies* (consonant + *y*) · *Asia* — proper noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** job after *be* needs *a*; he → verb-*s*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My father is a pilot* (EX-021-003).
13. **Why this is correct:** article with jobs; 3rd-person *-s*.
14. **Other versions:** *My aunt is a pilot. She flies to Africa.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My uncle is pilot.* → ✅ *a pilot* · ❌ *He flys…* → ✅ *flies*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My uncle's a pilot — he's in Asia every week.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *A* in *Asia*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PY-lut" · "AY-zhuh".
19. **Pattern:** **My + person + is a + job. He + verb-s + to + place + every week.**
20. **More examples:** *My cousin is a driver. He drives to Berlin every day.*
21. **Quick check:** *He flys* or *He flies*? → **Answer:** *flies*.

### EX-021-615 — "These maps are old. Don't use them."

1. **Meaning:** These maps have old information, so don't follow them. 🗺️⚠️
2. **Situation:** A guide talks to hikers.
3. **Main lesson:** *these* + plural noun → *them*.
4. **Grammar used:** *These* + plural noun + *are* + adj · *Don't* + verb + *them*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **These maps** (S) → **are** → **old** · **(You)** → **Don't use** (V) → **them** (O).
6. **Subject:** *These maps* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *use*.

1. **Meaning:** He spends so much time in his lorry that it feels like another house. 🚛🏠
2. **Situation:** Last line of the story.
3. **Main lesson:** two nouns linked by *be*: subject and complement (**B**).
4. **Grammar used:** *His* + noun + *is his second* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **His lorry** (S) → **is** → **his second home** (B).
6. **Subject:** *His lorry*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *his second home*.
10. **Word by word:** *His* — Leo's · *lorry* · *second* — number 2 in order · *home* 💭📍 — place where you live and feel comfortable.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun **is** noun → the second noun describes the first.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Time is money* (EX-021-012) — noun + *is* + noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + noun complement.
14. **Other versions:** *The office is my second home.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *His lorry is his two home.* → ✅ *second* · ❌ *His lorry is a his second home.* → ✅ *his second home*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *He practically lives in his lorry.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SEK-und-HOHM".
19. **Pattern:** **His + noun + is his second + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The library is my second home.*
21. **Quick check:** Noun after *is*? → **Answer:** *home* (in *his second home*).

## Step J — Real writing: a holiday postcard

> 📮 *Dear Mum, the **hotel** is lovely and the **beach** is beautiful. We visited three **museums** and a **castle**. Wish you were here! Love, Sam*

### EX-021-647 — "The hotel is lovely and the beach is beautiful."

1. **Meaning:** The hotel is very nice, and the beach is very pretty. 🏨🏖️
2. **Situation:** Writing a postcard home.
3. **Main lesson:** two short clauses, each with its own noun subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adj + *and* + *the* + noun + *is* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The hotel** (S) → **is** → **lovely** · **and** · **the beach** (S) → **is** → **beautiful**.
6. **Subject:** *The hotel* · *the beach*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *lovely* · *beautiful*.
10. **Word by word:** *hotel* 📍 · *lovely* · *and* — joins two clauses · *beach* 📍 — sand next to the sea · *beautiful*.
11. **How the rules work together:** each subject has its own verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The food was excellent, but the service was slow* (EX-021-547).
13. **Why this is correct:** two complete clauses joined by *and*.
14. **Other versions:** *The hotel and the beach are lovely.* (one subject with two nouns → *are*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The hotel is lovely and the beach beautiful.* (OK in writing, but beginners: keep *is*) · ❌ *The hotel and the beach is lovely.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The hotel's lovely and the beach is gorgeous.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** no comma needed before *and* in a short sentence.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BEECH" (long *ee*).
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is + adj and the + noun + is + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *The food is great and the people are friendly.*
21. **Quick check:** *The hotel and the beach ___ lovely.* → **Answer:** *are*.

### EX-021-648 — "We visited three museums and a castle."

1. **Meaning:** We went to see three museums and one castle. 🏛️🏰
2. **Situation:** Second line of the postcard.
3. **Main lesson:** plural noun + singular noun in one object.
4. **Grammar used:** *We* + past verb + number + plural noun + *and a* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **We** (S) → **visited** (V) → **three museums and a castle** (O).
6. **Subject:** *We*.
7. **Verb:** *visited*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *three museums and a castle*.
10. **Word by word:** *visited* — went to see · *three museums* — number + plural · *a castle* — one (C) · *castle* 📍 — old strong building for kings/lords.
11. **How the rules work together:** each noun keeps its own number: *three* + plural, *a* + singular.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *A bottle of water, a sandwich and an apple…* (EX-021-085) — list of nouns.
13. **Why this is correct:** correct counting words.
14. **Other versions:** *We visited a museum and two castles.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…three museum…* → ✅ *museums* · ❌ *…and castle.* → ✅ *and a castle*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *We saw three museums and a castle!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** in *castle*, the *t* is silent.
18. **Pronunciation:** "myoo-ZEE-umz" · "KAH-sl" (UK) / "KA-sl" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **We visited + number + plural noun + and a + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *We saw two churches and a palace.*
21. **Quick check:** Is the *t* in *castle* spoken? → **Answer:** No.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-649 — "We will travel by train. / Travel is my passion."

1. **Meaning:** (1) We're going to go by train. (2) Going to new places is the thing I love most. 🚆❤️
2. **Situation:** Talking about trips and hobbies.
3. **Main lesson:** *travel* as a verb (1) and a U noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *We will* + verb + *by* + noun · U noun + *is my* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **We** → **will travel** (V) → **by train** · (2) **Travel** (S) → **is** → **my passion** (B).
6. **Subject:** (1) *We* · (2) *Travel*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *travel* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *will* · (2) none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *by train* · *my passion*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *travel* after *will* → verb · (2) *Travel* at the start before *is* → noun (U) · *passion* 💭 — strong love.
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides: after a helping verb → verb; subject slot → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *It was a long journey* (EX-021-629) — *a travel* ❌ because *travel* is U; use *a journey/a trip*.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *I love travelling.* · *Air travel is expensive.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I had a nice travel.* → ✅ *a nice trip* · ❌ *Travels is my passion.* → ✅ *Travel is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm crazy about travel.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "TRAV-ul" (same for noun and verb).
19. **Pattern:** **We will + verb + by + transport / U noun + is my + noun**
20. **More examples:** *We will cook tonight. / Cooking is my hobby.*
21. **Quick check:** *a travel* or *a trip*? → **Answer:** *a trip*.

### EX-021-650 — "Please book a room for us. / I'm reading a good book on the train."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Please reserve a hotel room for us. (2) I'm reading a nice book while travelling by train. 🏨📖
2. **Situation:** Planning a trip; then on the train.
3. **Main lesson:** *book* as a verb (= reserve) and a C noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *Please* + verb + *a* + noun + *for us* · *I'm reading a* + adj + noun + *on the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **(You)** → **book** (V) → **a room** (O) → **for us** · (2) **I** → **'m reading** (V) → **a good book** (O) → **on the train** (P).
6. **Subject:** (1) hidden *you* · (2) *I*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *book* · (2) *reading*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (2) *'m* (*am*).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a room* · *for us* · *a good book* · *on the train*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *book* right after *Please* → verb (reserve) · (2) *a good book* — after *a* + adjective → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** after *a* (+ adjective) → noun; command position → verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *I left my umbrella on the train* (EX-021-613) — *on the train*.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *I booked a table for two.* (verb) · *This book is great.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Please book us a room in the train.* → ✅ *on the train* (if needed) · ❌ *I'm reading a good books.* → ✅ *a good book*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Can you book us a room?* · *I've got a great book for the train.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BUK" (noun and verb the same).
19. **Pattern:** **Please + verb + a + noun + for us / I'm reading a + adj + noun**
20. **More examples:** *Please park the car. / The park is near the hotel.*
21. **Quick check:** In *Please book a room*, is *book* a noun or a verb? → **Answer:** verb (= reserve).
