**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

> 🔁 **Sports & music nouns:** sports and games have **no article** (*play football, play chess*). Musical instruments usually take **the** (*play the guitar, play the piano*). *Music, sport* (UK, in general), *free time, fun* are **U**. *A match, a game, a goal, a team* are **C**.

**New words in this part:** match · player · hobby · photography · chess · referee · weekend · guitar · score · violin · fan · instrument · singer · goal · cup · hide-and-seek · marathon · surf · swimmer · collection · coin · outdoor · court · shed · stands · captain · final · comic · draw · cards · fishing · club · beginner · play (theatre).

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-901 — "Football is popular in Brazil."

1. **Meaning:** Many people in Brazil like football. ⚽🇧🇷
2. **Situation:** A geography lesson about sports.
3. **Main lesson:** A sport noun (U, no article) as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** sport noun + *is* + adjective + *in* + country.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Football** (S) → **is** → **popular** → **in Brazil** (P).
6. **Subject:** *Football*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *popular* · *in Brazil*.
10. **Word by word:** *Football* 💭 — sport (US *soccer*), no *the* · *popular* · *Brazil* — proper noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** sports are U → no article, singular verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My brother is tall. He plays basketball* (EX-021-033).
13. **Why this is correct:** U + *is*; no article with sports.
14. **Other versions:** *Cricket is popular in India.* · *Is football popular in your country?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The football is popular in Brazil.* → ✅ *Football* · ❌ *Football are popular.* → ✅ *is*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Brazilians love football.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *B*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "FUT-bawl".
19. **Pattern:** **Sport + is popular in + country.**
20. **More examples:** *Ice hockey is popular in Canada.*
21. **Quick check:** US word for *football* (the round-ball game)? → **Answer:** *soccer*.

### EX-021-902 — "The match is exciting."

1. **Meaning:** The game we are watching is thrilling. ⚽😃
2. **Situation:** Watching football on TV.
3. **Main lesson:** C event noun + *-ing* adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The match** (S) → **is** → **exciting**.
6. **Subject:** *The match*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *exciting*.
10. **Word by word:** *match* — a game between two teams (C; plural *matches*) · *exciting* — gives a feeling of excitement.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*; *-ing* adjective describes the thing that causes the feeling.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Life in a big city is exciting but tiring* (EX-021-740).
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *I'm excited about the match.* (*-ed* = how a person feels)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The match is excited.* → ✅ *exciting* · ❌ *The matchs…* → ✅ *matches*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *What a game!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** plural *matches* (*-ch* → *-es*).
18. **Pronunciation:** "MACH" · "ik-SY-ting".
19. **Pattern:** **The + event noun + is + -ing adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The film is boring. · The book is interesting.*
21. **Quick check:** The match is ___ / I am ___ (excited/exciting)? → **Answer:** *exciting* / *excited*.

3. **Main lesson:** plural person noun → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The players** (S) → **are** → **tired**.

9. **Object / complement / extras:** *tired*.
10. **Word by word:** *players* 👩 — people who play (*play* + *-er* + *s*) · *tired*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*; *-ed* adjective for feelings.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My muscles are tired* (EX-021-855).

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The players is tired.* → ✅ *are* · ❌ *The players are tiring.* (= they make others tired)

18. **Pronunciation:** "PLAY-erz".
19. **Pattern:** **The + plural noun + are + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *The fans are happy. · The coaches are angry.*
21. **Quick check:** Person noun from *play*? → **Answer:** *player*.

2. **Situation:** Introducing yourself in a class.
3. **Main lesson:** U activity noun after *is* (**B**).
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My hobby** (S) → **is** → **photography** (B).
6. **Subject:** *My hobby*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.

10. **Word by word:** *hobby* — free-time activity (C; *hobbies*) · *photography* — the art of taking photos (U).
11. **How the rules work together:** noun **is** noun; U after *be* → no *a*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Time is money* (EX-021-012) — noun *is* noun.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + noun.
14. **Other versions:** *My hobbies are photography and cooking.* (plural → *are*)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My hobby is a photography.* → ✅ *photography* · ❌ *My hobby is photo.* → ✅ *photography* (the activity)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm really into photography.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "fuh-TOG-ruh-fee" (stress moves: *PHOto*, *phoTOgraphy*).
19. **Pattern:** **My hobby is + U activity noun.**
20. **More examples:** *My hobby is gardening. · Her hobby is cooking.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *hobby*? → **Answer:** *hobbies*.

### EX-021-907 — "Weekends are too short."

1. **Meaning:** Saturday and Sunday finish too fast. 📅😅
2. **Situation:** Monday morning at work.
3. **Main lesson:** plural time noun in general → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are too* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Weekends** (S) → **are** → **too short**.
6. **Subject:** *Weekends*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *too short*.
10. **Word by word:** *weekends* — compound (*week* + *end*) · *too* — more than you want · *short*.
11. **How the rules work together:** general plural → no article; plural → *are*.

17. **Writing & punctuation:** *weekend* — one word.

19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are too + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Holidays are too short. · Exams are too long.*

### EX-021-908 — "My guitar is old, but it sounds great."

1. **Meaning:** My guitar isn't new, but its sound is excellent. 🎸
2. **Situation:** Talking about your instrument.
3. **Main lesson:** instrument noun → *it*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *is* + adj, *but it* + verb-s + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My guitar** (S) → **is** → **old** · **but** · **it** (S) → **sounds** (V) → **great**.
6. **Subject:** *My guitar* · *it*.
7. **Verb:** *is* · *sounds*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *old* · *great*.
10. **Word by word:** *guitar* · *sounds* — verb (sense verb + adjective) · *great*.
11. **How the rules work together:** sense verbs (*sound, look, taste, smell*) + **adjective**.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The lamp is old, but it still works* (EX-021-162).
13. **Why this is correct:** pronoun + sense verb pattern.
14. **Other versions:** *My piano is new, and it sounds beautiful.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…it sounds greatly.* → ✅ *great* · ❌ *…but he sounds great.* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's an old guitar, but it sounds amazing.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** comma before *but*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "gi-TAR" (stress on 2nd syllable).
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is + adj, but it + sense verb + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *This soup is cold, but it tastes good.*
21. **Quick check:** *sounds great* or *sounds greatly*? → **Answer:** *sounds great*.

### EX-021-909 — "The score is two–one."

1. **Meaning:** One team has 2 points/goals and the other has 1. ⚽ 2:1
2. **Situation:** Someone asks about a match.
3. **Main lesson:** *score* as a noun + numbers.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + number–number.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The score** (S) → **is** → **two–one** (B).
6. **Subject:** *The score*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *two–one*.
10. **Word by word:** *score* — the number of points/goals · *two–one* — said "two one"; *0* = *nil* (UK) / *zero* (US).
11. **How the rules work together:** singular → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The population of the city is about two million* (EX-021-738) — noun + *is* + number.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + number.
14. **Other versions:** *It's two–nil to Arsenal.* · *What's the score?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The score are two–one.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *The score is two to zero.* (UK says *two–nil*)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's two–one.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** written *2–1* (with a dash).
18. **Pronunciation:** "TOO-WUN" · *nil* "NIL".
19. **Pattern:** **The score is + number–number.**
20. **More examples:** *The score is three–nil. · The final score was one–one.*
21. **Quick check:** UK word for *zero* in football scores? → **Answer:** *nil*.

## Step B — Free-time nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-911 — "I love this song. I listen to it every day."

1. **Meaning:** I really like this song; I hear it daily. 🎵❤️
2. **Situation:** Sharing music with a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** *this song* → *it* after *listen to*.
4. **Grammar used:** *I love this* + noun · *I listen to it* + time.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **love** (V) → **this song** (O) · **I** → **listen** (V) → **to it** (P) → **every day**.
6. **Subject:** *I* (both).
7. **Verb:** *love* · *listen*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *this song* · *to it* · *every day*.
10. **Word by word:** *song* · *listen to* — always *to* before the thing · *it* — the song.
11. **How the rules work together:** *listen* + *to* + object pronoun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The guidebook is very useful. I take it everywhere* (EX-021-661).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct preposition + pronoun.
14. **Other versions:** *I love these songs. I listen to them every day.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I listen it every day.* → ✅ *listen to it* · ❌ *I listen to him.* (for a song) → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *This song's on repeat!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "LIS-un" (silent *t*).
19. **Pattern:** **I love this + noun. I listen to it + time.**
20. **More examples:** *I love this podcast. I listen to it on the bus.*
21. **Quick check:** *listen it* or *listen to it*? → **Answer:** *listen to it*.

### EX-021-912 — "My sister plays the violin. She practises every evening."

1. **Meaning:** My sister plays the violin, and she practises every evening. 🎻
2. **Situation:** Talking about family hobbies.
3. **Main lesson:** *play the* + instrument; person → *she*.
4. **Grammar used:** *My* + noun + *plays the* + noun · *She* + verb-s + *every evening*.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My sister** (S) → **plays** (V) → **the violin** (O) · **She** (S) → **practises** (V) → **every evening**.
6. **Subject:** *My sister* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *plays* · *practises*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the violin* · *every evening*.
10. **Word by word:** *the violin* — instruments take *the* · *practises* — UK verb (*practice* = noun); US uses *practices* for both.
11. **How the rules work together:** instruments → *the*; sports → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *When I was young, I played tennis every day* (EX-021-877) — sport, no article.
13. **Why this is correct:** article rule + 3rd-person *-s*.
14. **Other versions:** *My brother plays the drums.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My sister plays violin.* (common in US, but standard: *the violin*) · ❌ *She practise…* → ✅ *practises*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *My sister plays the violin — she practises every night.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK: *practice* (noun) / *practise* (verb).
18. **Pronunciation:** "vy-uh-LIN" · "PRAK-tis-iz".
19. **Pattern:** **Person + plays the + instrument. She + verb-s + time.**
20. **More examples:** *My dad plays the piano. He plays every Sunday.*
21. **Quick check:** *play the violin* or *play violin* (standard UK)? → **Answer:** *play the violin*.

### EX-021-913 — "Our fans are amazing. They sing for ninety minutes."

6. **Subject:** *Our fans* · *They*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *sing*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *amazing* · *for ninety minutes*.
10. **Word by word:** *fans* · *amazing* · *for* — length of time · *ninety minutes*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *they* + base verb.

15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *They sings…* → ✅ *sing* · ❌ *…during ninety minutes.* → ✅ *for ninety minutes* (length)

17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.

### EX-021-914 — "I have two tickets for the concert. Do you want one?"

1. **Meaning:** I've got two concert tickets; would you like one of them? 🎟️🎟️🎤
2. **Situation:** Inviting a friend.
3. **Main lesson:** *one* = one ticket (replaces a C noun).
4. **Grammar used:** *I have* + number + plural noun + *for the* + noun · *Do you want one?*
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **have** (V) → **two tickets** (O) → **for the concert** (P) · **Do** → **you** (S) → **want** (V) → **one** (O)?
6. **Subject:** *I* · *you*.
7. **Verb:** *have* · *want*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Do*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *two tickets* · *for the concert* · *one*.
10. **Word by word:** *tickets* · *for the concert* · *one* — one of the tickets.
11. **How the rules work together:** *one* replaces *a ticket* (C singular).
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *This city has many parks. My favourite one is Hyde Park* (EX-021-764).
13. **Why this is correct:** *one* avoids repeating the noun.
14. **Other versions:** *Do you want one of them?* · *Do you want it?* (only if there's one specific ticket)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Do you want it?* (for one of two) → ✅ *one* · ❌ *…two ticket…* → ✅ *tickets*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I've got a spare ticket for the concert — want it?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "KON-sert".
19. **Pattern:** **I have + number + plural noun + for the + event. Do you want one?**
20. **More examples:** *I made some cookies. Do you want one?*
21. **Quick check:** What does *one* mean here? → **Answer:** one ticket.

7. **Verb:** *meets*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *every Wednesday* · *in Room 5*.
10. **Word by word:** *chess* — type · *club* — group (singular → *meets*) · *every Wednesday* · *Room 5* — capital, no article.
11. **How the rules work together:** head singular → verb-*s*; noun + number → no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Your seat is … in row twelve* (EX-021-634).
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement + no article.
14. **Other versions:** *The football club trains on Mondays.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The chess club meet…* → ✅ *meets* · ❌ *…in the Room 5.* → ✅ *in Room 5*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Chess club's on Wednesdays, Room 5.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** capital *R* in *Room 5* on signs.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHES-KLUB".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + club + meets every + day + in Room + number.**
20. **More examples:** *The book club meets every Friday in the library.*
21. **Quick check:** *in the Room 5* or *in Room 5*? → **Answer:** *in Room 5*.

### EX-021-948 — "Beginners are welcome!"

1. **Meaning:** People who are new to chess can join too! 🙌
2. **Situation:** The notice continues.
3. **Main lesson:** plural noun (*verb + -er + s*) as the subject → *are*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Beginners** (S) → **are** → **welcome**.
6. **Subject:** *Beginners*.
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *welcome* — adjective: you are happily accepted.
10. **Word by word:** *beginners* — *begin* + **n** + *-er* + *s* (double *n*) · *welcome*.
11. **How the rules work together:** plural → *are*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *She is an excellent swimmer* (EX-021-929) — *-er* person nouns with double letters.
13. **Why this is correct:** agreement.
14. **Other versions:** *Everyone is welcome!* (*everyone* → singular)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Beginners are welcomed.* (on signs, *welcome* is the adjective) · ❌ *Beginers…* → ✅ *Beginners*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *You don't need to be good — beginners are welcome!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **!** on friendly notices.
18. **Pronunciation:** "bih-GIN-erz".
19. **Pattern:** **Plural noun + are welcome!**
20. **More examples:** *Children are welcome! · Dogs are welcome.*
21. **Quick check:** Spelling: *beginers* or *beginners*? → **Answer:** *beginners*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-949 — "Let's dance! / She learned a new dance."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Let's move to the music! (2) She learned new dance steps. 💃
2. **Situation:** At a party; then at a dance class.
3. **Main lesson:** *dance* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *Let's* + verb! · *She* + past verb + *a new* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **(we)** → **dance** (V) · (2) **She** (S) → **learned** (V) → **a new dance** (O).
6. **Subject:** (1) *Let's* = let us · (2) *She*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *dance* · (2) *learned*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (2) *a new dance*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *dance* after *Let's* → verb · (2) *a new dance* — after *a* + adjective → noun.
11. **How the rules work together:** after *Let's* → verb; after *a* (+ adj) → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Let's walk to the square. / It's a short walk…* (EX-021-749).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *They danced all night.* (verb) · *The salsa is a Latin dance.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Let's to dance!* → ✅ *Let's dance!* · ❌ *She learned new dance.* → ✅ *a new dance*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Come on, let's dance!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *learned* or *learnt*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "DAHNS" (UK) / "DANS" (US).
19. **Pattern:** **Let's + verb! / She learned a new + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *Let's swim! / Let's go for a swim.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a new dance*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.

### EX-021-950 — "Can you play the piano? / We saw a play at the theatre."

1. **Meaning:** (1) Do you know how to make music on the piano? (2) We watched a drama on stage at the theatre. 🎹 / 🎭
2. **Situation:** (1) At a friend's house; (2) talking about last night.
3. **Main lesson:** *play* as a verb (1) and a C noun (2: a drama).
4. **Grammar used:** *Can you* + verb + *the* + noun? · *We* + past verb + *a* + noun + *at the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **Can** → **you** (S) → **play** (V) → **the piano** (O)? · (2) **We** (S) → **saw** (V) → **a play** (O) → **at the theatre** (P).
6. **Subject:** *you* · *We*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *play* · (2) *saw*.
8. **Helping verbs:** (1) *Can*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *the piano* · *a play* · *at the theatre*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *play* after *Can you* → verb · (2) *a play* — after *a* → noun (a drama performed by actors).
11. **How the rules work together:** after a modal + subject → verb; after *a* → noun.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My sister plays the violin* (EX-021-912) — instruments with *the*.
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *She plays tennis.* (verb) · *Shakespeare wrote many plays.* (noun)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Can you play piano?* (common in US, but standard UK: *the piano*) · ❌ *We saw play at the theatre.* → ✅ *a play*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Do you play the piano?* · *We went to see a play last night.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** UK *theatre* / US *theater*.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PLAY" (noun and verb the same) · "THEER-uh-ter".
19. **Pattern:** **Can you + verb + the + instrument? / We saw a + noun + at the + place.**
20. **More examples:** *I can't play cards. / The play was three hours long.*
21. **Quick check:** In *a play at the theatre*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.
