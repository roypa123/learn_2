**Short reminders:** **Noun** = naming word (👩 person · 🐾 animal · 📍 place · 📦 thing · 💭 idea). **Positions:** **S** subject · **O** object · **B** after *be* · **P** after a preposition. **Head noun** decides the verb ([L-021 §7.4](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-021-what-is-a-noun.md#74-noun-phrases)).

**Shopping words in this part:** price · receipt · cash · wallet · sale · bill · change · queue · size · shop assistant · market · credit card · cash machine · bargain · checkout · fitting room · bakery · shopping centre.

> 🔁 **Money nouns:** *money, cash, change* (U — no *a*, no -s) vs *a coin, a note, a dollar, a pound, a price, a bill* (C) ([L-026](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-026-countable-and-uncountable-nouns.md#75-countable--uncountable-pairs-with-similar-meanings)).

## Step A — One noun, short sentences

### EX-021-401 — "The shop is busy."

1. **Meaning:** There are many people in the shop right now. 🛍️👥
2. **Situation:** You arrive at a shop on Saturday and see a crowd.
3. **Main lesson:** A **shop** noun as the subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The shop** (S) → **is** → **busy**.
6. **Subject:** *The shop*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *busy* — full of people (for places).
10. **Word by word:** *shop* 📍 (US also *store*) · *busy* — for a place: crowded; for a person: having a lot to do.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular noun → *is*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *busy* for people (D-001) — here for a place.
13. **Why this is correct:** S + *be* + adjective.
14. **Other versions:** *The shops are busy.* · *Is the shop busy?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Shop is busy.* → ✅ *The shop* · ❌ *The shop is busily.* → ✅ *busy*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *It's really busy in here!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "BIZ-ee".
19. **Pattern:** **The + place + is + busy/quiet.**
20. **More examples:** *The market is busy. · The café is quiet.*
21. **Quick check:** Opposite of *busy* (for a place)? → **Answer:** *quiet*.

### EX-021-402 — "Prices are high."

1. **Meaning:** Things cost a lot of money (in general). 💸
2. **Situation:** Two people complaining about the cost of living.
3. **Main lesson:** A plural money noun (general) + *high/low*.
4. **Grammar used:** plural noun + *are* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Prices** (S) → **are** → **high**.
6. **Subject:** *Prices* (in general).
7. **Verb:** *are*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *high*.
10. **Word by word:** *prices* 💭 — amounts of money things cost (C) · *high*.
11. **How the rules work together:** *price* → *high/low*; *things* → *expensive/cheap*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My salary is low* (EX-021-305).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct adjective for prices.
14. **Other versions:** *The prices in this shop are low.* · *Are prices high in your country?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Prices are expensive.* → ✅ *high* · ❌ *Prices is high.* → ✅ *are*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Everything's so expensive now!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "PRY-siz" (2 syllables).
19. **Pattern:** **Prices + are + high/low.**
20. **More examples:** *Rents are high. · Taxes are low.*
21. **Quick check:** *expensive* or *high* for prices? → **Answer:** *high*.

### EX-021-403 — "The receipt is in the bag."

1. **Meaning:** The paper that shows what you bought and paid is inside the bag. 🧾
2. **Situation:** You want to return something to a shop.
3. **Main lesson:** A thing noun with a **silent letter** as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + *is in the* + noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The receipt** (S) → **is** → **in the bag**.
6. **Subject:** *The receipt*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** place.
10. **Word by word:** *receipt* 📦 — ⚠️ silent **p**: "ri-SEET" · *bag*.
11. **How the rules work together:** S + *be* + place.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** silent letters ([L-006](../../00-foundations/02-letters-and-sounds/lesson-006-letters-and-sounds.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct form.
14. **Other versions:** *Do you have the receipt?* · *The receipts are in my wallet.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The receipt are…* → ✅ *is* · ❌ saying "ri-SEPT" → ✅ "ri-SEET"
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Keep the receipt!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** spelling: *re-ce-i-p-t* (not *reciept*).
18. **Pronunciation:** "ri-SEET".
19. **Pattern:** **The + noun + is in the + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The tickets are in my pocket. · The money is in the drawer.*
21. **Quick check:** Silent letter in *receipt*? → **Answer:** *p*.

### EX-021-404 — "Cash is useful."

1. **Meaning:** Having paper money and coins is helpful (e.g., at markets). 💵
2. **Situation:** Advice before travelling to a small village.
3. **Main lesson:** U money noun as subject.
4. **Grammar used:** U noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Cash** (S) → **is** → **useful**.
6. **Subject:** *Cash*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *useful*.
10. **Word by word:** *cash* 📦 — notes and coins (U, no *a*, no -s) · *useful* — helpful.
11. **How the rules work together:** U → singular, no article.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Money isn't everything* (EX-021-310).
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun + *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Do you have any cash?* · *I pay in cash.* (*in cash* — no article)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *A cash is useful.* → ✅ *Cash* · ❌ *Cashes are useful.* → ✅ *Cash is useful.*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Bring some cash, just in case.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "KASH".
19. **Pattern:** **U noun + is + adjective.**
20. **More examples:** *Water is important. · Information is useful.*
21. **Quick check:** *pay in cash* or *pay in a cash*? → **Answer:** *in cash*.

### EX-021-405 — "The supermarket sells everything."

1. **Meaning:** You can buy all kinds of things at the supermarket. 🛒
2. **Situation:** A new neighbour asks where to shop.
3. **Main lesson:** Compound place noun (one word) + pronoun object.
4. **Grammar used:** *the* + noun + verb-s + pronoun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **The supermarket** (S) → **sells** (V) → **everything** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The supermarket*.
7. **Verb:** *sells*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *everything*.
10. **Word by word:** *supermarket* 📍 — *super* + *market*, one word · *sells* · *everything* — all things.
11. **How the rules work together:** singular place → *sells*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The factory makes cars* (EX-021-306).
13. **Why this is correct:** S-V-O.
14. **Other versions:** *The supermarkets sell everything.* · *Does the supermarket sell batteries?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *The super market…* → ✅ *supermarket* · ❌ *The supermarket sell…* → ✅ *sells*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *You can get anything at the supermarket.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** one word.
18. **Pronunciation:** "SOO-per-mar-kit".
19. **Pattern:** **The + shop + sells + everything / plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The bakery sells bread. · The chemist's sells medicine.*
21. **Quick check:** One word or two: *super market*? → **Answer:** one.

### EX-021-406 — "My wallet is empty."

1. **Meaning:** There's no money in my wallet. 👛❌
2. **Situation:** At a café, you can't pay.
3. **Main lesson:** Thing noun + adjective.
4. **Grammar used:** *my* + noun + *is* + adjective.
5. **Sentence structure:** **My wallet** (S) → **is** → **empty**.
6. **Subject:** *My wallet*.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *empty*.
10. **Word by word:** *wallet* 📦 — a small case for money and cards (UK *purse* = a woman's small money bag; US *purse* = handbag) · *empty*.
11. **How the rules work together:** S + *be* + adjective.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *The fridge is empty* (EX-021-109).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct form.
14. **Other versions:** *My wallet is full of receipts.* · *Is your wallet empty?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *My wallet empty.* → ✅ *is empty* · ❌ *My wallet is emptied.* (= someone emptied it)
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I'm broke!* (= I have no money, informal)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "WOL-it".
19. **Pattern:** **My + noun + is + empty/full.**
20. **More examples:** *My bag is empty. · My phone battery is empty.*
21. **Quick check:** Informal word for "I have no money"? → **Answer:** *broke*.

### EX-021-409 — "Here is your change."

1. **Meaning:** This is the money I give back to you because you paid too much. 🪙
2. **Situation:** A shop assistant returns coins after you pay with a big note.
3. **Main lesson:** *change* as an **uncountable** money noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *Here is your* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** **Here** → **is** → **your change** (S, after the verb).
6. **Subject:** *your change* — comes after *is* in this *Here is…* pattern.
7. **Verb:** *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** none.
10. **Word by word:** *Here* — at this place · *change* 📦 — money returned (U: no *a*, no -s); also coins in general: *Do you have any change?*
11. **How the rules work together:** *Here is* + singular/U; *Here are* + plural.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Here you are* ([L-028](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** U noun → *is*.
14. **Other versions:** *Here are your keys.* (plural) · *Keep the change!* (= you can keep the extra money)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Here are your change.* → ✅ *is* · ❌ *Here is your changes.* → ✅ *change*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *There you go — your change.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHAYNJ".
19. **Pattern:** **Here is + your + U/singular noun / Here are + your + plural.**
20. **More examples:** *Here is your receipt. · Here are your tickets.*
21. **Quick check:** *Here is* or *Here are* + *your keys*? → **Answer:** *Here are*.

## Step B — Shopping nouns with pronouns

### EX-021-411 — "I like this jacket. Can I try it on?"

1. **Meaning:** I like this jacket; may I put it on to see if it fits? 🧥
2. **Situation:** In a clothes shop.
3. **Main lesson:** *this jacket* → *it* inside a phrasal verb.
4. **Grammar used:** *I like this* + noun · *Can I try it on?*
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **like** → **this jacket** (O) · **Can** → **I** → **try** → **it** (O) → **on**?
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *like* · *try on*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Can* (permission).
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *this jacket* · *it* (= the jacket).
10. **Word by word:** *jacket* 📦 · *try on* — put on clothes to test the size · *it* goes **between** *try* and *on*.
11. **How the rules work together:** pronoun object in the middle of a phrasal verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Turn it off* ([L-028 §7.7](../../01-beginner-grammar/pronouns/lesson-028-object-pronouns.md#77-phrasal-verbs-turn-it-off-not-turn-off-it)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct pronoun position.
14. **Other versions:** *Can I try on this jacket?* / *Can I try this jacket on?* (noun → both orders)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Can I try on it?* → ✅ *try it on* · ❌ *Can I try him on?* → ✅ *it*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Can I try this on?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **?**
18. **Pronunciation:** "TRY-it-ON" (linked).
19. **Pattern:** **I like this + noun. Can I try it on?**
20. **More examples:** *I like these shoes. Can I try them on?*
21. **Quick check:** Plural version with *shoes*? → **Answer:** *Can I try them on?*

### EX-021-412 — "These jeans are too small. Do you have them in a bigger size?"

1. **Meaning:** These jeans don't fit — they're too small; do you have the same jeans in a larger size? 👖
2. **Situation:** Speaking to a shop assistant from the fitting room.
3. **Main lesson:** Always-plural noun (*jeans*) → *them*; *size* (C).
4. **Grammar used:** *These* + plural + *are too* + adj · *Do you have them in a* + adj + noun?
5. **Sentence structure:** **These jeans** (S) → **are** → **too small** · **Do** → **you** → **have** → **them** (O) → **in a bigger size**?
6. **Subject:** *These jeans* · *you*.
7. **Verb:** *are* · *have*.
8. **Helping verbs:** *Do*.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *too small* · *them* · *in a bigger size*.
10. **Word by word:** *jeans* — always plural → *these, are, them* · *too small* — more small than you want · *size* (C) · *bigger*.
11. **How the rules work together:** always-plural nouns need plural partners.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My new jeans are too long* ([L-023](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md)).
13. **Why this is correct:** all partners plural.
14. **Other versions:** *This shirt is too small. Do you have it in a bigger size?*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *This jeans is…* → ✅ *These jeans are* · ❌ *…have it in a bigger size?* → ✅ *them*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Have you got these in a size up?*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "JEENZ".
19. **Pattern:** **These + plural noun + are too + adj. Do you have them in a + adj + size?**
20. **More examples:** *These shoes are too big. Do you have them in a smaller size?*
21. **Quick check:** *jeans* — singular or plural partners? → **Answer:** plural.

### EX-021-413 — "The shop assistant was very helpful. She found my size."

1. **Meaning:** The person working in the shop helped me a lot; she found clothes in my size.
2. **Situation:** Telling a friend about your shopping.

5. **Sentence structure:** **The shop assistant** (S) → **was** → **very helpful** · **She** → **found** → **my size** (O).
6. **Subject:** *The shop assistant* · *She*.
7. **Verb:** *was* · *found* (past of *find*).
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *very helpful* · *my size*.
10. **Word by word:** *shop assistant* 👩 (UK; US *sales clerk*) · *helpful* · *found* · *size*.
11. **How the rules work together:** noun → pronoun; past forms.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *My colleague Ana is very helpful* (EX-021-311).
13. **Why this is correct:** clear reference.
14. **Other versions:** *The shop assistants were very helpful. They found my size.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…She finded my size.* → ✅ *found* · ❌ *The shop assistant she was…* → ✅ no *she*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *The lady in the shop was really helpful.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "uh-SIS-tunt".
19. **Pattern:** **The + job + was very + adj. She/He found my + noun.**
20. **More examples:** *The waiter was very kind. He found us a table.*
21. **Quick check:** Past of *find*? → **Answer:** *found*.

### EX-021-415 — "I bought a new phone. It was expensive."

1. **Meaning:** I got a new phone, and it cost a lot. 📱💰
2. **Situation:** Showing friends your new phone.
3. **Main lesson:** *a new phone* → *It*, past.
4. **Grammar used:** *I bought a* + adj + noun · *It was* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **bought** → **a new phone** (O) · **It** (S) → **was** → **expensive**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *It*.
7. **Verb:** *bought* · *was*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *a new phone* · *expensive*.
10. **Word by word:** *bought* · *phone* · *It* = the phone · *expensive* — for **things** (not prices).
11. **How the rules work together:** *things* are expensive/cheap; *prices* are high/low.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Prices are high* (EX-021-402).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct adjective for a thing.
14. **Other versions:** *I bought new headphones. They were cheap.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *It was high.* → ✅ *expensive* (for a thing) · ❌ *I buyed…* → ✅ *bought*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Got a new phone — it cost a fortune!*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.

19. **Pattern:** **The staff are + adj and the prices are + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *The rooms are clean and the food is delicious.*
21. **Quick check:** *fair* sounds like which other word? → **Answer:** *fare*.

### EX-021-448 — "I bought two shirts and a pair of jeans."

1. **Meaning:** I bought two shirts and one pair of jeans. 👕👕👖
2. **Situation:** The same review.
3. **Main lesson:** Number + plural C noun + *a pair of* + always-plural noun.
4. **Grammar used:** *I bought* + number + plural + *and a pair of* + plural.
5. **Sentence structure:** **I** → **bought** → **two shirts and a pair of jeans** (O).
6. **Subject:** *I*.
7. **Verb:** *bought*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** two objects.
10. **Word by word:** *shirts* (C) · *a pair of* — used for one item of always-plural clothes · *jeans*.
11. **How the rules work together:** count always-plural nouns with *pair(s) of*.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *a pair of jeans* ([L-023 §7.7](../../01-beginner-grammar/nouns/lesson-023-singular-and-plural-nouns.md#77-special-nouns-first-look)).
13. **Why this is correct:** correct counting.
14. **Other versions:** *I bought three pairs of socks.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *…and a jeans.* → ✅ *a pair of jeans* · ❌ *…two pair of jeans.* → ✅ *two pairs of jeans*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I got two shirts and some jeans.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** full stop.
18. **Pronunciation:** "a PAIR-uv-JEENZ".
19. **Pattern:** **I bought + number + plural + and a pair of + always-plural noun.**
20. **More examples:** *She bought a dress and two pairs of shoes.*
21. **Quick check:** Plural of *a pair of jeans*? → **Answer:** *two pairs of jeans*.

## Step K — Noun or verb?

### EX-021-449 — "I often shop online. / The shop on the corner is closed."

1. **Meaning:** (1) I often buy things on the internet. (2) The shop at the corner of the street is shut.
2. **Situation:** Talking about shopping habits.
3. **Main lesson:** *shop* as a verb (1) and a noun (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I often* + verb + adverb · *the* + noun + *on the* + noun + *is* + adj.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **often** → **shop** (V) → **online** · (2) **The shop on the corner** (S) → **is** → **closed**.
6. **Subject:** *I* · *The shop on the corner*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *shop* · (2) *is*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** (1) *online* · (2) *closed*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *shop* after *I often* → verb (= buy things) · (2) *shop* after *The* → noun · *on the corner* — at the corner (outside → *on*).
11. **How the rules work together:** position decides; *often* before the main verb.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *in the corner* (inside a room) vs *on the corner* (of a street) (EX-021-383).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its position.
14. **Other versions:** *We go shopping on Saturdays.* (*shopping* = noun activity)
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *I shop often online.* → ✅ *I often shop online.* · ❌ *The shop in the corner* (of a street) → ✅ *on the corner*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *I do most of my shopping online.*
17. **Writing & punctuation:** two sentences.
18. **Pronunciation:** "on-LYN".
19. **Pattern:** **I often + verb + online / The + noun + on the corner + is + adj.**
20. **More examples:** *I often cook at home. / The cook is French.*
21. **Quick check:** Street corner — *in* or *on*? → **Answer:** *on*.

### EX-021-450 — "I need to change my shoes. / Keep the change!"

1. **Meaning:** (1) I must put on different shoes. (2) You can keep the extra money I gave you!
2. **Situation:** (1) Before going out in the rain. (2) Paying a taxi driver.
3. **Main lesson:** *change* as a **verb** (1) and a **U noun** with a different meaning (2).
4. **Grammar used:** *I need to* + verb + *my* + plural · instruction + *the* + U noun.
5. **Sentence structure:** (1) **I** → **need to change** (V) → **my shoes** (O) · (2) (*You*) **Keep** → **the change** (O) → **!**
6. **Subject:** *I* · hidden *you*.
7. **Verb:** (1) *need to change* · (2) *Keep*.
8. **Helping verbs:** none.
9. **Object / complement / extras:** *my shoes* · *the change*.
10. **Word by word:** (1) *change* after *to* → verb (= replace) · (2) *change* after *the* → noun (U: money given back); also *a change* (C) = something different: *We need a change.*
11. **How the rules work together:** position **and** context decide job and meaning.
12. **Remember from before 🔁:** *Here is your change* (EX-021-409).
13. **Why this is correct:** each word in its natural position.
14. **Other versions:** *Things change. · A change of plan.*
15. **Wrong ❌ → ✅:** ❌ *Keep the changes!* → ✅ *Keep the change!* (money = U) · ❌ *I need change my shoes.* → ✅ *need to change*
16. **Natural spoken English:** *Keep the change!* (very common with taxis and tips)
17. **Writing & punctuation:** **!** for a friendly instruction.
18. **Pronunciation:** "CHAYNJ" (same for noun and verb).
19. **Pattern:** **I need to + verb + my + noun / Keep the + noun!**
20. **More examples:** *I need to charge my phone. / There's no charge for delivery.* (*charge* noun = cost)
21. **Quick check:** In *Keep the change*, noun or verb? → **Answer:** noun.
